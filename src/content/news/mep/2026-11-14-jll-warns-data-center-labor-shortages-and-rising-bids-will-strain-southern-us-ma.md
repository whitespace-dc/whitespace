body_en: |
  Data center developers face steeper construction bids and intensifying competition for specialized workers in 2027, particularly in Southern US markets where demand for new facilities and power infrastructure is already straining workforce capacity, according to commercial real estate and construction advisory firm JLL.

  In its 2027 US Construction Perspective, JLL forecasts national tender prices — the rates contractors submit when bidding for projects — will rise 4.2% in 2027, up from 3.1% in 2026 and above the pre-2020 norm of 2% to 3%. The projection covers the broader construction market, not data centers exclusively.

  The South is expected to contribute more to nonresidential construction growth than any other US region and to carry the heaviest workload per worker by 2028, driven by data centers, power infrastructure projects, and public civil works, JLL said.

  ## Workforce scarcity beyond wage rates

  Matthew Giddings, JLL's executive director of technical services, said developers frequently underestimate the full cost of assembling qualified crews. "The challenge isn't just finding workers. It's finding qualified workers in the right location at the right time," he told Data Center Knowledge. "The budget impact extends well beyond wage rates."

  Developers are already paying overtime premiums and covering travel, lodging, and relocation costs to bring specialists into markets that lack sufficient local labor. Giddings cautioned that simply adding workers does not automatically improve output: expanding crews can increase costs without improving productivity if designs, materials, and construction sequencing are not well coordinated.

  JLL said emerging data center markets may lack the workforce capacity to support multiple hyperscale developments — large-scale facilities typically built by major cloud and technology companies — running simultaneously. Modern campuses increasingly incorporate on-site substations, utility interconnections, and dedicated power generation, requiring thousands of workers across multiyear construction programs.

  ## Labor planning must precede groundbreaking

  Giddings said the timing of workforce planning is as important as the planning itself. "You can't assume that labor will simply be available once a project is ready to break ground," he said. "Workforce planning needs to happen alongside site selection, permitting, and utility strategy."

  Building relationships early with trade schools, community colleges, and apprenticeship programs can develop local labor capacity over time. Developers who wait until construction is imminent risk competing for workers who are already committed to other projects.

  Power delivery creates a parallel staffing constraint. Utilities require crews to build transmission lines and substations; developers need electrical trades to construct and energize the facilities themselves. Giddings said those two labor pools are not interchangeable. JLL documented cases where facilities were substantially complete and power was available, but facilities still could not open because qualified commissioning personnel — the specialists who test and certify systems before operation — were unavailable. Those facilities remained months away from operation as a result.

  Giddings urged developers to integrate utility work, equipment procurement, construction, and commissioning within a single coordinated schedule to reduce that risk.

  ## Contractor uncertainty is embedded in bids

  JLL's outlook identified metals, energy-intensive products, and associated building packages as the primary sources of material cost pressure. Tender prices typically trail input cost increases by three to six months, meaning cost increases that materialized through late 2026 will continue to push bids higher in early 2027.

  Contractors are also pricing in project risk that they cannot confidently quantify. "When a contractor is asked to commit to a guaranteed price or delivery date before design is fully developed or procurement risks are understood, they're going to account for that uncertainty in their pricing," Giddings said.

  Early contractor involvement, clearer procurement plans, and experienced owner's project management teams can make pricing more predictable, according to JLL. Established data center contractors with large existing order books can afford to be selective about which projects they take on, which limits an owner's ability to transfer unresolved risks to the contractor at low cost.

  Prefabricated electrical assemblies and standardized cooling infrastructure can reduce the volume of on-site labor required, but JLL noted that manufacturers of prefabricated components face their own staffing and capacity constraints. Giddings said repeatable designs deployed consistently across a campus tend to deliver more value than one-off prefabrication decisions made in isolation.

  ## Acceleration spending must target the actual constraint

  For campuses built to support artificial intelligence workloads, bringing a facility online sooner can justify significant additional expenditure, given the revenue potential of earlier operation. Giddings said developers should weigh acceleration costs against financing expenses, customer commitments, equipment deployment timelines, and revenue lost through delayed occupancy.

  The critical variable is whether additional spending can actually change the opening date. "Adding overtime or manpower doesn't help if the true constraint is transformer delivery, utility energization, or commissioning," Giddings said. "Successful acceleration strategies target the actual bottlenecks rather than simply increasing construction spend."

  The Southern US is expected to sustain the highest construction workload per worker in the country through 2028, with data center and power infrastructure activity remaining the primary drivers of that demand, according to JLL.
body_zh: |
  商業不動產與建築顧問公司JLL表示，2027年資料中心開發商將面臨更高的工程投標報價與日益激烈的專業人力競爭，尤以美國南部市場最為嚴峻——當地對新設施與電力基礎建設的強勁需求，已對現有勞動力造成沉重負荷。

  JLL在其《2027年美國建築市場展望》報告中預測，全國標案報價（即承包商競標工程時所提出的費率）將於2027年上漲4.2%，高於2026年的3.1%，也超出2020年前2%至3%的正常水準。上述預測涵蓋整體建築市場，並非單指資料中心。

  JLL指出，南部地區對非住宅建築成長的貢獻預計將超越全美其他地區，且到2028年時每名工人所承擔的工作量將居全國之冠，主要驅動力來自資料中心、電力基礎建設工程及公共土木工程。

  ## 人力短缺不僅是薪資問題

  JLL技術服務部門執行董事Matthew Giddings表示，開發商往往低估了組建合格施工團隊的全部成本。他向《Data Center Knowledge》表示：「挑戰不只是找到工人，而是要在對的地點、對的時間找到合格的工人。預算衝擊遠遠不止於薪資層面。」

  由於當地勞動力供給不足，開發商已開始支付加班費，並負擔專業人員的差旅、住宿及搬遷費用，才能將人才引進市場。Giddings警告，單純增加工人並不會自動提升產出：若設計、材料與施工順序欠缺妥善協調，擴大施工團隊只會墊高成本，而非改善生產效率。

  JLL指出，部分新興資料中心市場可能缺乏同時支援多個超大規模開發案——即通常由主要雲端與科技公司興建的大型設施——所需的勞動力。現代化園區日益整合現場變電站、公用事業互聯及專屬發電設施，需要數千名工人跨越多年施工期持續投入。

  ## 勞動力規劃須先於動工

  Giddings表示，勞動力規劃的時機與規劃本身同等重要。他說：「你不能假設，只要專案準備好動工，工人自然就會到位。勞動力規劃必須與選址、取得許可及公用事業策略同步推進。」

  提早與職業學校、社區大學及學徒計畫建立合作關係，能夠逐步培養當地勞動力。若開發商等到動工在即才開始籌措人力，恐將面臨與其他專案競搶工人的窘境，而那些工人可能早已另有承諾。

  電力供應在人力配置上也面臨類似的平行制約。電力公司需要施工團隊興建輸電線路與變電站；開發商則需要電氣工種來建置設施並完成通電作業。Giddings表示，這兩個勞動力池無法相互替代。JLL記錄了多起案例：設施主體工程已大致完工，電力也已就緒，卻仍無法開業——原因在於負責在正式運作前測試與驗證系統的試運轉專業人員無人可調。這些設施因此在完工後又延宕數月才得以運作。

  Giddings敦促開發商將公用事業工程、設備採購、施工及試運轉整合進統一的協調排程，以降低此類風險。

  ## 承包商的不確定性已反映於報價

  JLL的展望報告指出，金屬、能源密集型產品及相關建築套組，是材料成本壓力的主要來源。標案報價通常落後於原料成本上漲三至六個月，意味著2026年底前已出現的成本漲幅，將持續推高2027年初的投標報價。

  承包商也將難以準確量化的專案風險納入報價之中。Giddings說：「當一名承包商被要求在設計尚未完成或採購風險尚未釐清的情況下，就承諾保證價格或交付日期，他們必然會將這種不確定性反映在報價裡。」

  JLL表示，提早讓承包商介入、明確採購計畫，以及配備經驗豐富的業主專案管理團隊，有助於讓報價更具可預測性。擁有龐大既有訂單的資深資料中心承包商，往往能夠選擇性接案，使業主難以用低成本將未解決的風險轉嫁給承包商。

  預製電氣組件與標準化冷卻基礎設施可減少現場所需的人力規模；但JLL指出，預製元件製造商本身也面臨人力與產能瓶頸。Giddings表示，在整個園區一致採用可複製設計，往往比零散、個案式的預製決策更能創造實質效益。

  ## 加速支出須瞄準真正的瓶頸

  對於為支援人工智慧工作負載而興建的園區而言，考量提前運作的收益潛力，讓設施提早上線可為額外支出提供正當理由。Giddings表示，開發商應將加速所需的成本，與融資費用、客戶承諾、設備部署時程及延遲入駐所損失的收益一併衡量。

  關鍵變數在於，增加支出是否真的能改變開業日期。Giddings說：「如果真正的制約在於變壓器交貨、公用事業通電或試運轉，那麼增加加班或人力並沒有幫助。成功的加速策略，是針對實際瓶頸下手，而不是單純增加建設支出。」

  根據JLL的報告，美國南部預計至2028年都將維持全國最高的每人施工工作量，資料中心與電力基礎建設活動仍將是推動該需求的主要驅力。
