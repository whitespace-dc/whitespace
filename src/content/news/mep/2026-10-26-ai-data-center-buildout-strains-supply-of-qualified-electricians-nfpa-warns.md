body_en: |
  The surge in artificial intelligence data center construction is intensifying demand for qualified electricians at a time when the skilled trades face a supply shortfall that has been building for decades, the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) warned in analysis published October 25, 2026.

  Writing in Data Center Dynamics, NFPA's Corey Hannahs — a third-generation master electrician with more than 30 years in the field — said demand for electricians to build and maintain AI data centers cannot be met without urgently expanding the pipeline of trained workers.

  ## Tech giants invest billions as labor supply lags

  Companies including Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google parent Alphabet, Microsoft Azure, Meta, and Oracle are investing billions of dollars in data center construction to meet AI demand, Hannahs wrote. He noted that being first to market typically delivers the highest return on investment, adding competitive pressure on construction timelines that skilled labor shortages are now threatening.

  Recognizing the dependency, Meta and Google have each recently invested millions of dollars in skilled trades training programs, Hannahs said, describing the spending as evidence that those companies understand data center expansion and electrician supply are inseparable.

  ## What electricians do in a data center

  Data centers require electrical infrastructure to power lighting, outlets, networks, and the silicon-based servers at the core of AI workloads, Hannahs said. That infrastructure must be built and maintained by qualified electricians — a term with a specific regulatory meaning under U.S. electrical codes and standards.

  Three NFPA documents collectively define those standards: NFPA 70, known as the National Electrical Code (NEC), which governs electrical installations; NFPA 70B, the Standard for Electrical Equipment Maintenance; and NFPA 70E, the Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace. NFPA refers to the three documents together as the Electrical Cycle of Safety (ECoS).

  The NEC and the ECoS standards require that certain electrical installations and servicing tasks be performed by "qualified persons" — individuals who have received proper training, not simply those capable of running conduit or pulling wire, Hannahs said. A qualified electrician, he wrote, applies accurate load calculations, questions designs that appear incorrect, and delivers electrical systems that minimize risk and support long-term reliability.

  ## Decades of underinvestment in vocational training

  The skilled labor shortage did not emerge suddenly, Hannahs argued. He attributed it to decades of secondary education and parental guidance that directed students toward four-year college degrees while framing skilled trades as a fallback rather than a deliberate career choice. The removal of exploratory vocational classes from public school funding deepened the gap, he said.

  The result is a shortage the industry must now address under time pressure. "We must do the work we've neglected for the past three decades: find them, inspire them, and train them," Hannahs wrote.

  ## Maintenance adds to long-term demand

  The need for qualified electricians does not end once a data center is built. Electrical systems degrade over time, Hannahs said, and the high power demands of AI data centers place additional stress on electrical infrastructure, creating sustained demand for qualified maintenance workers long after construction crews move on.

  ## Competing sectors compound the pressure

  Demand for electricians extends beyond data centers. Hannahs cited manufacturing, healthcare, renewable energy projects, and electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure as sectors simultaneously competing for the same limited pool of skilled workers, further tightening supply.

  ## Training timelines under scrutiny

  Traditional electrician apprenticeship programs take four to five years to complete. Hannahs said the industry should be open to exploring new training methods that could supplement or shorten that timeline, though he did not specify what form those methods should take. He called on large data companies to require qualified electricians — not merely electricians — in project contracts, arguing that the capital investment those facilities represent warrants electrical systems built and maintained to the highest standard.
body_zh: |
  人工智慧資料中心的建設熱潮，正值技術工人供給積弱數十年之際，進一步加劇了對合格電工的需求。美國國家消防協會（NFPA）於2026年10月25日發布的分析報告中發出警示。

  NFPA的科里·漢納斯（Corey Hannahs）在業界媒體《Data Center Dynamics》撰文指出，若不緊急擴大培訓人才的管道，AI資料中心建設與維護所需的合格電工需求將無從滿足。漢納斯本人是第三代持照電工師父，在業界深耕逾30年。

  ## 科技巨頭砸下數十億，勞動力供給卻遠遠跟不上

  漢納斯在文中指出，亞馬遜雲端服務（AWS）、Google母公司字母控股（Alphabet）、微軟Azure、Meta與甲骨文（Oracle）均斥資數十億美元興建資料中心，以因應AI帶來的龐大需求。他強調，搶先進入市場通常能帶來最高的投資報酬，這使得工期的競爭壓力更加沉重，而目前技術工人短缺的問題，正嚴重威脅建設時程。

  有鑑於此，Meta與Google近期各自投入數百萬美元，支持技術工人培訓計畫。漢納斯認為，這筆支出清楚說明兩家公司已意識到：資料中心的擴張與電工人才的供給，是密不可分的兩件事。

  ## 電工在資料中心扮演的角色

  資料中心需要完整的電氣基礎設施，為照明、電源插座、網路以及作為AI運算核心的矽基伺服器提供電力。漢納斯說明，這些基礎設施的建設與維護，必須由合格電工負責——「合格」一詞在美國電氣規範與標準中具有明確的法規意義，並非泛指任何能夠布管或拉線的人員。

  規範合格電工標準的文件共有三份，均出自NFPA：管轄電氣裝設的《NFPA 70》，即《美國國家電氣規範》（NEC）；規範電氣設備維護的《NFPA 70B》；以及規範職場電氣安全的《NFPA 70E》。NFPA將此三份文件合稱為「電氣安全循環」（Electrical Cycle of Safety，ECoS）。

  NEC與ECoS標準要求特定電氣裝設及維修作業須由「合格人員」執行。漢納斯表示，所謂合格電工，能精確進行負載計算、對看似有誤的設計提出質疑，並交付能將風險降至最低、支持長期穩定運行的電氣系統。

  ## 職業訓練數十年的積欠

  漢納斯認為，技術工人短缺並非一夕之間形成。他將原因歸結於數十年來的中等教育政策與家長觀念，持續引導學生走向四年制大學，同時將技術職業定位為退而求其次的選擇，而非主動的職涯規劃。公立學校從課程預算中刪除探索性職業課程，更進一步加深了這道缺口。

  其結果，是一場業界如今必須在時間壓力下正面迎擊的短缺危機。漢納斯寫道：「我們必須完成過去三十年來一直疏於做的工作：找到他們、激勵他們、培訓他們。」

  ## 維護需求帶來長期用人壓力

  合格電工的需求並不會在資料中心竣工後就告終。漢納斯指出，電氣系統會隨時間老化，而AI資料中心的高功率需求更對電氣基礎設施造成額外負荷，使得施工團隊離場後，對合格維護人員的需求仍長期持續存在。

  ## 多個產業搶才，供給壓力雪上加霜

  電工需求並不限於資料中心。漢納斯指出，製造業、醫療照護、再生能源計畫以及電動車（EV）充電基礎設施，正同時向同一批有限的技術工人伸出橄欖枝，進一步壓縮供給空間。

  ## 培訓時程面臨審視

  傳統的電工學徒訓練計畫需耗費四至五年才能完成。漢納斯表示，業界應對可能補充或縮短現有培訓時程的新訓練方式保持開放態度，但未具體說明應採取何種形式。他呼籲大型資料公司在工程合約中明確要求採用合格電工——而非僅是一般電工——並主張，這些設施所代表的龐大資本投入，理應擁有依最高標準建設與維護的電氣系統。
