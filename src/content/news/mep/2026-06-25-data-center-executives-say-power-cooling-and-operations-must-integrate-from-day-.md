  ## Power, cooling, and operations convergence now a design requirement for AI data centers

  Senior executives at three data center infrastructure suppliers told Data Center Frontier on June 24 that rising rack densities and the complexity of AI workloads have made siloed infrastructure design unworkable, and that electrical, mechanical, and operational systems must now be treated as a single coordinated environment from the earliest planning stages.

  The remarks came in Data Center Frontier's Q2 2026 Executive Roundtable, which asked panelists how tightly integrated power, cooling, and facility operations must become to support the next generation of AI deployments. The panel included Steve Altizer, President and CEO of Compu Dynamics; Joe Capes, VP at Trane Technologies and head of its LiquidStack liquid cooling unit; and Robert Danforth, Director of Engineering, Simulation, and Advanced Development at Rehlko, a power systems company.

  ## The case for integration starting at planning

  Altizer said the shift is categorical: integration can no longer be retrofitted after equipment and building decisions are made. "Integration has to be foundational. It has to start at the first planning conversation," he said.

  In prior data center generations, he said, mechanical, electrical, IT, and operations teams could work in parallel and assemble their work later, because load profiles were more predictable and facilities had margin to absorb change. He cited the introduction of ChatGPT as a rough dividing line, saying there was "very little change to absorb" before it. AI workloads have removed that margin.

  Altizer said a change in rack density — the amount of compute power packed into a standard equipment cabinet — now simultaneously affects electrical distribution, structural requirements, thermal strategy, commissioning procedures, service access, and day-to-day operations. "These are no longer independent decisions. They are all part of one performance system," he said.

  ## Cooling, facility design, and operations as mutually dependent variables

  Capes, whose role spans both Trane Technologies and LiquidStack — a company specializing in liquid cooling systems for high-density servers — described the same interdependence from the thermal side. Cooling affects compute performance and reliability, he said; facility design affects cooling performance; and operational decisions affect both. "The projects that are being executed well are the ones where everyone is working from the same plan, and communicating efficiently," Capes said.

  Liquid cooling, which circulates water or another fluid directly to server components rather than relying on air, is increasingly relevant at rack densities that conventional air systems cannot handle. Capes did not specify density thresholds, but the source article notes that racks are climbing "into the hundreds of kilowatts" in leading AI deployments.

  Danforth of Rehlko framed the same issue around the behavior of AI workloads themselves. Changes in how AI systems use compute can simultaneously affect power delivery, thermal performance, energy storage systems, controls, and facility operations, he said. "The question isn't whether a generator, battery system, cooling solution, or control platform performs well on its own. It's whether all of those systems can work together effectively under highly dynamic conditions."

  ## Simulation and digital twin tools emerging as integration aids

  Danforth pointed to simulation and digital twin technologies — software models that replicate a physical facility's behavior before it is built — as a response to the coordination challenge. These tools allow operators to understand how system interactions play out before infrastructure is deployed and to identify potential problems early in the design process, he said. He cited growing industry attention to these approaches without naming specific products or vendors.

  ## Modular and hybrid campus models as a structural response

  Altizer acknowledged that AI workloads are not uniform, noting that training clusters, inference nodes — servers optimized for running trained AI models rather than building them — enterprise AI environments, and edge sites can each have different requirements for density, cooling architecture, network connectivity, security, and serviceability. A single fixed design cannot address all of them.

  He said many companies are responding with modular approaches or hybrid models that integrate turnkey modular AI capacity into larger campus environments. At the campus level, he described a strategy of standardizing backbone infrastructure — utility power feeds, central cooling capacity, and network pathways — while allowing the IT environment and its associated critical infrastructure components to evolve as workload requirements change. "The goal is not modularity for its own sake," Altizer said. "The goal is to support the next generation of AI deployments without forcing every hardware change to become a major redesign."

  He also noted that as AI systems move toward pod-scale platforms — large, pre-integrated computing units that bundle servers, networking, and sometimes cooling — the boundary between IT equipment and facility infrastructure becomes harder to draw.

  ## What the shift means for the industry

  All three executives described the same directional change: the data center industry is moving away from optimizing discrete subsystems toward designing and operating entire infrastructure stacks as unified systems. Danforth said reliability in AI deployments will increasingly be determined by how effectively the full infrastructure stack performs as a coordinated system, not by the performance of any single component.

  The practical implication, according to Altizer, is that procurement and engineering processes that have historically involved separate teams working independently on power, cooling, and IT must be restructured. He said AI infrastructure "cannot be planned as a collection of disparate systems" and must instead be "designed as one coordinated environment, from the utility backbone all the way to the IT rack."

  Danforth said the use of simulation and digital twin tools before infrastructure is deployed will become more important as AI deployments grow larger and more complex.

  ## 電力、冷卻與營運整合，已成AI資料中心的設計必要條件

  三家資料中心基礎設施供應商的高階主管於6月24日向Data Center Frontier表示，機架密度的持續攀升以及AI工作負載的複雜性，已讓各系統各自為政的基礎設施設計方式難以為繼。他們強調，電氣、機械與營運系統如今必須從最早的規劃階段起，就被視為一個統一協調的整體來對待。

  上述談話出自Data Center Frontier舉辦的2026年第二季高層圓桌論壇，主題聚焦於電力、冷卻與設施營運需要整合到何種程度，才能支撐下一世代的AI部署。與談者包括Compu Dynamics總裁兼執行長Steve Altizer、Trane Technologies副總裁暨旗下液冷品牌LiquidStack負責人Joe Capes，以及電力系統公司Rehlko工程、模擬暨先進開發總監Robert Danforth。

  ## 整合必須從規劃之初著手

  Altizer直言，這波轉變是根本性的：整合工作已無法在設備選型與建築決策塵埃落定後再來補救。「整合必須成為基礎，必須從第一次規劃對話就開始，」他說。

  他指出，在過去幾個世代的資料中心建置中，機械、電氣、IT與營運團隊可以各自平行作業、最後再將成果拼湊整合，因為負載曲線相對可預測，設施也有足夠餘裕吸收變動。他以ChatGPT問世作為概略的分水嶺，說在那之前「幾乎沒有什麼變動需要消化」。AI工作負載的到來，已將這道緩衝空間徹底抹去。

  Altizer說，機架密度——即塞入一個標準設備機櫃的運算能力——一旦改變，就會同時牽動電氣配電、結構承重需求、熱管理策略、試運轉程序、維修動線與日常營運。「這些不再是各自獨立的決策，它們全都是同一套效能系統的一部分，」他說。

  ## 冷卻、設施設計與營運三者相互依存

  Capes的職責橫跨Trane Technologies與LiquidStack——後者專精於高密度伺服器的液冷系統——他從熱管理的角度描述了同樣的相互依存關係。他說，冷卻影響運算效能與可靠性，設施設計影響冷卻效能，而營運決策則同時牽動兩者。「執行成效良好的專案，都是每個人依循同一份計畫、溝通順暢的專案，」Capes說。

  液冷技術將水或其他流體直接循環至伺服器元件，而非仰賴氣冷，在傳統氣冷系統已無力應付的機架密度下，其重要性與日俱增。Capes未提及具體的密度門檻，但原報導指出，在主要的AI部署場景中，機架功率正攀升至「數百千瓦」的水準。

  Rehlko的Danforth則從AI工作負載本身的行為模式切入，描述相同的課題。他說，AI系統使用運算資源的方式一旦改變，可能同時影響電力輸送、散熱效能、儲能系統、控制平台與設施營運。「問題不在於發電機、電池系統、冷卻方案或控制平台各自能否表現良好，而在於所有這些系統能否在高度動態的條件下有效協同運作。」

  ## 模擬與數位孿生工具，成為整合的新興輔助利器

  Danforth點名模擬與數位孿生技術——即在實體設施建成前，先以軟體模型複製其運行行為——作為因應協調挑戰的解方。他說，這些工具讓營運商能在基礎設施部署之前，就了解各系統之間的互動情形，並在設計流程的早期找出潛在問題。他提到業界對這類工具的關注日益升溫，但未點名具體產品或廠商。

  ## 模組化與混合園區模式，提供結構性因應之道

  Altizer承認AI工作負載並非鐵板一塊：訓練叢集、推論節點——即針對執行已訓練AI模型而非訓練模型所優化的伺服器——企業AI環境與邊緣站點，在密度、冷卻架構、網路連線、安全性與維護性上，各有各的需求。任何一種固定設計都無法通吃。

  他說，許多企業正以模組化方式或混合模式來因應——將交鑰匙模組化AI容量整合進更大型的園區環境。在園區層級，他描述的策略是將骨幹基礎設施標準化——包括公用電力饋線、中央冷卻容量與網路通道——同時讓IT環境及其配套的關鍵基礎設施元件，隨著工作負載需求的演變而彈性調整。「目標不是為了模組化而模組化，」Altizer說，「目標是在不強迫每次硬體異動都得進行大規模重新設計的前提下，支撐下一世代的AI部署。」

  他也指出，隨著AI系統朝向Pod規模平台演進——即將伺服器、網路設備、有時還包含冷卻系統捆綁在一起的大型預整合運算單元——IT設備與設施基礎設施之間的界線將愈來愈難以劃清。

  ## 這波轉變對產業的深遠意涵

  三位高層描述的方向性轉變如出一轍：資料中心產業正從優化各個獨立子系統，轉向將整個基礎設施堆疊設計並運營為統一系統。Danforth說，AI部署的可靠性將愈來愈取決於完整基礎設施堆疊作為協調系統的整體效能，而非任何單一元件的表現。

  就實際影響而言，Altizer指出，長久以來由電力、冷卻、IT各自獨立作業的採購與工程流程，必須重新整頓。他說，AI基礎設施「不能被規劃成一堆各自為政的系統拼湊體」，而必須「從公用事業骨幹一路到IT機架，設計為一個統一協調的環境」。

  Danforth則表示，隨著AI部署規模日益龐大、複雜度持續提升，在基礎設施部署前先行運用模擬與數位孿生工具，將成為愈來愈不可或缺的環節。
