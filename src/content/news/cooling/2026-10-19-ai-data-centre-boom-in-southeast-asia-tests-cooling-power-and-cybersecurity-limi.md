body_en: |
  The artificial intelligence-driven expansion of data centres across Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia is straining cooling systems, power grids and cybersecurity defences, according to industry executives who spoke at Tech Week Singapore on September 29 and 30.

  Malaysia leads the three markets with 1,318 megawatts (MW) of operational data centre capacity and 5,438 MW in its development pipeline, according to Pritesh Swamy, senior director at Cushman & Wakefield's Asia Pacific Data Centre Group. A megawatt is a unit of electrical power capacity. Singapore has approximately 1,068 MW operational, 232 MW under development and a further 200 MW allocated under its second Data Centre Call for Application, a government process for assigning new capacity to qualifying operators. Indonesia has 468 MW operational and nearly 2,000 MW in development.

  ## Cooling technology and tropical climate constraints

  AI servers generate considerable heat and require energy-intensive cooling to prevent overheating, said Srini Rao, head of ecosystems at Novagate Cloud. The latest AI installations use liquid cooling, a system in which a water-based coolant circulates through equipment to absorb heat. The coolant can operate at temperatures up to 45 degrees Celsius, reducing the electricity otherwise required to chill it to lower temperatures, Rao said.

  Closed-loop systems recirculate the same coolant continuously, releasing heat into outdoor air rather than evaporating water. That configuration can cut water consumption "to almost zero," Rao said.

  That efficiency is harder to achieve in tropical climates. Indonesia's high ambient outdoor temperatures limit how much heat can be shed into the air, requiring additional mechanical cooling and making near-zero water consumption unlikely, Rao said, though he added that substantial savings remained achievable.

  Data centres in cooler climates can draw on outdoor air to assist cooling when temperatures are sufficiently low, an option with limited application in tropical locations. The trade-off illustrates a broader challenge: reducing water consumption does not necessarily lower a facility's overall environmental footprint if additional electricity is required to compensate.

  ## Singapore's moratorium and Johor's emergence as a regional market

  Limited land and electricity in Singapore shaped the early geography of regional expansion. Singapore imposed a moratorium on new data centre construction in 2019, prompting operators to establish facilities in Johor, Malaysia, which offered available land, power and fibre connectivity within close range of Singapore's business networks. Singapore lifted the moratorium in 2022 and established a capacity allocation process, initially assigning 80 MW to four operators and allocating a further 200 MW in a second round this year.

  "While Johor started as a spillover location for Singapore, over time, Johor has become a market on its own and has begun serving the greater Southeast Asia region," Swamy said.

  The shift has expanded attention to the wider SIJORI corridor, a cross-border development area comprising Singapore, Johor and Indonesia's Riau Islands. The Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone provides a framework for cross-border cooperation. Operators are also evaluating Batam, Bintan and Karimun in the Riau Islands as potential future sites. Jakarta is growing as a separate, independent data centre market, while the Riau Islands remain in early stages of development and are expected to benefit from connectivity with Singapore in a pattern similar to Johor's, Swamy said.

  ## Indonesia: power grid and workforce constraints

  Indonesia's nearly 2,000 MW development pipeline is the region's largest volume of planned capacity, but electricity availability and grid distribution are insufficient to support its full build-out, Swamy said. The country also faces a shortage of workers with the experience required to manage large-scale data centre construction and operations.

  "Talent is also challenging, as it is very difficult to find an expert who can spearhead the construction or operation stage," Swamy said.

  ## Physical infrastructure as a cybersecurity target

  Security executives at a Tech Week Singapore panel on AI-related risks identified data centre physical supporting systems — generators, cooling equipment and networked control hardware — as attack targets separate from the data stored within facilities.

  Kevin O'Leary, head of security and resiliency, network and edge at Kyndryl ASEAN, said attackers are directing attention toward supporting infrastructure rather than data directly. "With everything run by the internet, the hackers won't be attacking the data directly. They will be looking at what's supporting it: the systems, the generators," O'Leary said. Compromising the cooling system, he added, would stop an entire facility.

  Ashish Thapar, vice-president and head of cybersecurity for Asia Pacific at NTT DATA, pointed to remotely controlled systems and internet-connected devices as particular points of exposure, and said the risk is especially acute for data centres supporting hospitals and other organisations that cannot tolerate service interruptions.

  Research cited by Vijay Vaidyanathan, regional vice-president of solutions engineering for Asia-Pacific and Japan at Claroty, found that 23 percent of 750,000 data centres examined contained internet-connected assets with risky outbound access, with power and cooling equipment among the most exposed categories. Those findings indicate that protecting operational technology — the hardware and software controlling physical systems — has become a distinct security requirement alongside conventional information security.

  ## Regional outlook

  The three markets are at different stages of maturity. Singapore is managing capacity through a structured allocation process while development extends across the SIJORI corridor. Malaysia holds the region's largest pipeline at more than 5,400 MW under development. Indonesia's nearly 2,000 MW planned build-out represents the largest single volume of unbuilt capacity in the region; whether it moves forward will depend on whether the country's power infrastructure, workforce and operational resilience can keep pace with investment, Swamy said.
body_zh: |
  人工智慧驅動的資料中心擴張浪潮席捲馬來西亞、新加坡與印尼，正對冷卻系統、電力網路及資安防線構成沉重壓力。這是9月29日至30日出席「Tech Week Singapore」論壇的業界高層共同指出的現況。

  根據世邦魏理仕亞太資料中心部門資深總監Pritesh Swamy的說法，馬來西亞以1,318百萬瓦（MW）的在運資料中心容量及5,438 MW的開發管線，領跑三大市場。MW為電力容量單位。新加坡目前約有1,068 MW投入運營，另有232 MW正在建設中，並在政府第二輪「資料中心申請徵集」程序下額外獲配200 MW；該程序用於將新增容量核配給符合資格的業者。印尼則有468 MW在運，近2,000 MW正處於開發階段。

  ## 冷卻技術與熱帶氣候的制約

  Novagate Cloud生態系統主管Srini Rao表示，AI伺服器產生大量熱能，需要耗電量極高的冷卻設備以防止過熱。最新的AI機房採用液冷技術，透過水基冷卻液在設備間循環流動以吸收熱量。Rao指出，冷卻液可在攝氏45度的條件下運行，從而降低將其冷卻至更低溫度所需的電力消耗。

  封閉式循環系統將同一批冷卻液持續循環再用，把熱量排放至室外空氣，而非蒸發水分。Rao表示，這種配置可將耗水量「幾乎降至零」。

  然而在熱帶氣候下，這種效率難以完全實現。Rao指出，印尼室外環境溫度偏高，限制了向空氣排熱的能力，必須加設機械冷卻設備，使耗水量趨近於零的目標幾乎無從達成——不過他也補充，仍可實現相當可觀的節水效益。

  氣候較涼爽地區的資料中心，在室外溫度夠低時可引入自然氣流輔助冷卻，但這種做法在熱帶地區的適用性極為有限。此一取捨揭示了一項更普遍的挑戰：若需動用額外電力來補償，減少耗水量未必能降低設施的整體環境足跡。

  ## 新加坡禁令與柔佛崛起為區域市場

  新加坡土地與電力資源有限，深刻影響了區域擴張的早期地理布局。新加坡於2019年頒布新建資料中心禁令，促使業者轉往馬來西亞柔佛設點——柔佛擁有充裕的土地、電力與光纖連接，且距離新加坡的商業網路近在咫尺。新加坡於2022年解除禁令，建立容量核配機制，最初向四家業者分配80 MW，今年第二輪再核配200 MW。

  Swamy表示：「柔佛最初是作為新加坡的溢出承接地，但隨著時間推移，柔佛已自成一方市場，並開始服務更廣泛的東南亞地區。」

  這股趨勢也將目光引向更廣義的新柔廖走廊（SIJORI corridor）——一個涵蓋新加坡、柔佛及印尼廖內群島的跨境發展區域。柔佛—新加坡特別經濟區為跨境合作提供了制度框架。業者也正在評估廖內群島的巴淡島、民丹島及卡里蒙島，作為潛在的未來選址。雅加達正發展為獨立的資料中心市場；廖內群島目前仍處於早期開發階段，預計將循著與柔佛類似的模式，藉由與新加坡的連接受益，Swamy說。

  ## 印尼：電網與人才的雙重瓶頸

  Swamy指出，印尼近2,000 MW的開發管線是區域內規模最大的計畫容量，然而電力供應與電網分配不足，無法支撐其全面建設；該國同時面臨具備大型資料中心建設與運營經驗之人才嚴重短缺的問題。

  Swamy表示：「人才方面同樣是一大挑戰，要找到能夠主導建設或運營階段的專業人才，非常困難。」

  ## 實體基礎設施成為資安攻擊目標

  在Tech Week Singapore一場聚焦AI相關風險的論壇上，資安高層指出，資料中心的實體支援系統——包括發電機、冷卻設備及聯網控制硬體——已成為獨立於設施內部儲存資料之外的攻擊目標。

  Kyndryl東南亞資安韌性、網路與邊緣業務主管Kevin O'Leary表示，攻擊者正將矛頭指向支援基礎設施，而非直接鎖定資料本身。「由於一切都透過網路運行，駭客不會直接攻擊資料，他們會鎖定支撐資料的系統：那些系統、那些發電機，」O'Leary說。他補充，一旦冷卻系統遭到入侵，整座設施將陷入癱瘓。

  NTT DATA亞太區資安業務副總裁暨負責人Ashish Thapar則點出遠端控制系統與連網設備是特別脆弱的暴露點，並表示這項風險對支援醫院及其他無法承受服務中斷之機構的資料中心而言尤為嚴峻。

  Claroty亞太暨日本區解決方案工程區域副總裁Vijay Vaidyanathan引述的研究顯示，在受檢的75萬座資料中心中，有23%含有具高風險對外存取的連網資產，電力與冷卻設備屬於暴露程度最高的類別。這些發現表明，保護營運技術（OT）——即控制實體系統的硬體與軟體——已成為有別於傳統資訊安全的獨立安全需求。

  ## 區域前景展望

  三大市場目前各處於不同的成熟階段。新加坡透過結構化的容量核配程序管理供給，同時開發活動延伸至新柔廖走廊。馬來西亞坐擁區域最大開發管線，規模逾5,400 MW。印尼近2,000 MW的計畫建設量，代表區域內尚未動工的最大單一容量體量；Swamy表示，能否如期推進，將取決於印尼的電力基礎設施、人才供給及運營韌性能否跟上投資的腳步。
