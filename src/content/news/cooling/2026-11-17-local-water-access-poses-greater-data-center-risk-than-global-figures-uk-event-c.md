body_en: |
  Industry representatives at W.Media's inaugural Data Center Meet-up in Manchester concluded that headline global water-use statistics obscure the more material question of how facilities interact with local water supply, competing demands, and community trust. Data centers account for 0.01 percent of global water consumption and 0.03 percent in the UK, but those aggregate figures tell operators little about site-specific risk, participants said.

  The event, held in Manchester last week, brought together members of the Greater North data center community for panel discussions and networking focused on water sustainability, with participants drawn from operators, engineering firms, and industry associations.

  ## Why global figures can mislead

  Lizabeth Hood of the International Data Center Authority (IDCA), who delivered the keynote, said a data center's water footprint extends beyond what flows through its on-site cooling systems. The electricity used to power a facility carries its own embedded water footprint, with the magnitude depending on the generation source. Nuclear power, Hood noted, can require significant water volumes for cooling, and that consideration extends to on-site generation options including small modular reactors (SMRs), compact nuclear units proposed in some plans for dedicated data center power supply.

  Hood also pointed to uneven freshwater distribution within the UK. While the country has adequate overall availability, supply varies significantly between regions, shaped by population growth, industrial activity, and changing weather patterns. Those variations mean operators cannot rely on national averages when assessing local site conditions.

  ## Cooling technology trade-offs

  A panel moderated by John Booth, Technical Director at the National Data Center Association (NDCA), examined how cooling choices shape water consumption. Panelists were Hood; Lizzy McDowell of Kao Data; and Luke Armstrong of Black and White Engineering.

  Armstrong described how closed-loop liquid cooling, dry coolers, and hybrid systems can reduce or eliminate operational water use. Dry cooling, which rejects heat to ambient air without consuming water, was identified as an increasingly viable option as cooling technology advances and tolerable operating temperatures rise. However, dry cooling can increase electricity demand, and equipment footprint and capital cost must be weighed. The right configuration, panelists said, depends on local climate, water availability, and power supply characteristics.

  Reclaimed water, treated wastewater reused for cooling, was raised as a further option to reduce reliance on freshwater where infrastructure exists.

  A planned 40 megawatt (MW) data center in Stockport was cited as a concrete illustration. The facility, expected to use closed-loop liquid cooling, is anticipated to consume water roughly equivalent to the annual use of five families of four, a comparison the panel offered to put the figures in relatable terms.

  ## Measuring both direct and indirect use

  The panel discussed Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE), a standard metric that quantifies how much water a data center consumes per unit of energy delivered to IT equipment. WUE captures direct on-site use but does not automatically account for the water embedded in grid electricity. Panelists said both dimensions should be considered for a complete picture of a facility's water impact.

  ## Community engagement and transparency

  Building trust with local communities was identified as a challenge distinct from, but inseparable from, the technical one. McDowell said concerns about water use, electricity draw, noise, and new construction need to be addressed through early engagement and evidence, and that such engagement cannot end once planning permission is granted.

  Kao Data has appointed a community liaison manager to maintain an ongoing relationship with residents near its facilities. The company also operates the Kao Seed Fund, which supports local not-for-profit projects based on needs identified by the communities themselves. McDowell referenced the "Data Centers Explained" website, developed by Kao Data in association with techUK and the Data Center Alliance, as an effort to make basic industry information accessible to the public.

  ## Waste heat recovery as a local benefit

  The discussion extended to ways data centers can contribute positively to host communities. Waste heat recovery was highlighted: panelists noted that Nordic projects have demonstrated how excess heat from data centers can supply homes and local heating networks. McDowell said Kao Data aims to recover at least 25 percent of waste heat from its facilities, though achieving that target depends on collaboration with local authorities and other partners.

  ## Implications for industry planning

  Panelists and audience questions pointed to the same conclusion: responsible siting and operation requires coordination across operators, developers, designers, utilities, regulators, local authorities, and communities. As AI-driven workloads increase infrastructure density and heat output, the panel argued, the industry's ability to demonstrate local accountability, not just favorable global averages, will be central to maintaining public and regulatory support for new development.

  The conversation is scheduled to continue at the Manchester Cloud and Data Center Convention on July 8, 2027.
body_zh: |
  W.Media 在曼徹斯特舉辦首屆資料中心聚會（Data Center Meet-up），與會業界代表結論認為，全球用水量的概括統計數字，掩蓋了資料中心如何與在地水資源供應、競爭性需求及社區信任互動等更為實質的問題。資料中心佔全球用水量的 0.01%，在英國則為 0.03%，但參與者表示，這些總體數字對於評估個別場址的風險幾乎毫無助益。

  此次活動上週在曼徹斯特舉行，匯聚了大北方（Greater North）資料中心社群的成員，共同就水資源永續議題進行座談與交流，出席者涵蓋營運商、工程顧問公司及產業公會。

  ## 為何全球數字可能產生誤導

  國際資料中心權威機構（IDCA）的 Lizabeth Hood 擔任主題演講嘉賓，她指出，資料中心的用水足跡不僅限於場址內冷卻系統的直接用水，還延伸至電力本身所蘊含的內嵌用水足跡，其大小取決於發電來源。Hood 特別提到，核能發電在冷卻過程中可能需要大量用水，這一點同樣適用於場址自建的發電選項，包括小型模組化反應爐（SMR）——這種緊湊型核能裝置已出現在部分專為資料中心供電的規劃方案中。

  Hood 也點出英國境內淡水分布不均的問題。儘管英國整體淡水供應充足，但受人口成長、工業活動及天氣型態變化影響，各地區供水情況差異顯著。這些差異意味著，營運商在評估個別場址條件時，不能僅憑全國平均數字作出判斷。

  ## 冷卻技術的取捨

  座談由全國資料中心協會（NDCA）技術總監 John Booth 主持，探討冷卻方式的選擇如何影響用水量。座談成員包括 Hood、Kao Data 的 Lizzy McDowell，以及 Black and White Engineering 的 Luke Armstrong。

  Armstrong 說明，密閉迴路液冷、乾式冷卻機及混合系統均可降低乃至消除營運用水量。乾式冷卻藉由將廢熱排放至周圍空氣而不消耗水資源，隨著冷卻技術進步、設備可承受的操作溫度提高，此方案的可行性日益提升。然而，乾式冷卻可能增加電力需求，設備佔地面積與建置成本也須列入考量。座談成員表示，最適合的配置取決於在地氣候、水資源可用性及電力供應特性。

  以再生水（即經過處理後再利用於冷卻的廢水）取代淡水，亦被提及為可行選項之一，前提是當地須具備相應的基礎設施。

  座談以斯托克波特（Stockport）一座規劃中的 40 百萬瓦（MW）資料中心為具體案例加以說明。該設施預計採用密閉迴路液冷，全年用水量預估約相當於五戶四口之家的年度用水總量——座談以此對比讓數字更具體易懂。

  ## 同時衡量直接與間接用水

  座談討論了用水效率指標（WUE），這是一項衡量資料中心每單位輸送至 IT 設備的能源所消耗水量的標準指標。WUE 能反映場址內的直接用水，但不會自動計入電網供電中所蘊含的內嵌用水。座談成員表示，要完整呈現設施的用水影響，兩個面向均應納入考量。

  ## 社區溝通與資訊透明

  建立在地社區信任，被視為一項與技術挑戰截然不同、卻又密不可分的課題。McDowell 表示，社區對用水量、耗電量、噪音及新建工程的疑慮，必須透過及早溝通與實證數據加以回應，而這樣的溝通不能在取得建設許可後便告終止。

  Kao Data 設有專職社區聯絡主任，負責與設施附近居民維繫持續的互動關係。該公司亦設立「Kao Seed Fund」，依社區自行提出的需求，資助當地非營利項目。McDowell 並提及「Data Centers Explained」網站——由 Kao Data 與 techUK 及資料中心聯盟（Data Center Alliance）共同開發，旨在讓一般大眾能輕鬆取得業界基本資訊。

  ## 廢熱回收作為在地貢獻

  討論進一步延伸至資料中心如何為所在社區帶來正面貢獻。廢熱回收受到特別關注：座談成員指出，北歐多個項目已示範如何將資料中心的廢熱用於供應住宅暖氣與在地供熱網路。McDowell 表示，Kao Data 的目標是回收旗下設施至少 25% 的廢熱，但達成此目標有賴於與地方政府及其他合作夥伴的協作。

  ## 對產業規劃的啟示

  座談成員及現場提問均指向同一個結論：負責任的選址與營運，需要營運商、開發商、設計師、公用事業業者、主管機關、地方政府及社區之間的跨界協調。座談認為，隨著 AI 驅動的工作負載持續提升基礎設施密度與散熱量，產業展現在地責任擔當的能力——而非僅靠有利的全球平均數字——將是維繫大眾與監管機構對新建項目支持的核心所在。
