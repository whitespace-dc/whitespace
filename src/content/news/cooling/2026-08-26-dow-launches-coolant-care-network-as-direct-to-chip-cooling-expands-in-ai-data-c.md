body_en: |
  Dow has launched the Coolant Care Network, a structured fluid-management service aimed at data center operators deploying direct-to-chip liquid cooling at scale in artificial intelligence (AI) and high-performance computing (HPC) environments, the company said.

  The network combines laboratory testing, data analysis, expert interpretation, and mitigation support across both primary and secondary cooling loops, according to Chuck Carn, Senior Director of Global Product Management at Dow, speaking to Electronics Cooling Magazine. It covers management of Dow's DOWFROST LC and DOWFROST HD Heat Transfer Fluids.

  ## Why coolant management is becoming a reliability variable

  As direct-to-chip liquid cooling moves from pilot projects into broader AI and HPC deployments, even small changes in coolant chemistry or cleanliness can directly affect system performance, equipment protection, and uptime, Carn said. Direct-to-chip cooling loops operate under stringent performance and reliability requirements that leave little room for deviations in coolant quality or system operation, he said.

  Thermal reliability in these environments depends not only on effective heat removal, but also on maintaining fluid stability and integrity over time, Carn said. That dependency is driving a shift toward structured, continuous monitoring programs in place of ad-hoc fluid management.

  ## What the Coolant Care Network provides

  Rather than requiring operators to manage multiple vendors independently, the network connects operators with Dow-approved laboratories and service providers supported by Dow's technical guidance, Carn said. When testing identifies a problem, Dow specialists interpret the results and issue a report that provides an overall system status alongside actionable corrective recommendations.

  The response process is tiered by severity. If coolant falls outside recommended operating limits, Dow provides guidance on which additives to introduce and at what dosage rates to bring the fluid back within specifications, Carn said. When the issue involves contamination or commissioning residues, guidance focuses on targeted mitigation carried out by approved service providers. When fluid health has declined significantly, Dow provides recommendations on adjusting glycol concentration, improving filtration, and increasing monitoring frequency.

  ## Commissioning and baseline sampling

  Carn outlined a staged approach to establishing fluid-health baselines before a system enters routine operation. On the system side, he recommended confirming compatibility between the coolant and all wetted materials, validating mechanical integrity, and completing thorough cleaning and flushing before startup to remove fabrication debris, residues, and other contaminants that can restrict flow or damage components. Long-term fluid health relies heavily on proper commissioning from the outset, he said.

  After coolant installation and initial circulation, engineers should collect a representative sample and analyze it against applicable fluid specifications and original equipment manufacturer (OEM) recommended operating ranges, Carn said. Key parameters at this stage include concentration, appearance, pH, reserve alkalinity, inhibitor concentrations, metals content, and contaminants that may have been introduced during commissioning. These results establish the baseline for the newly commissioned system, enabling changes in fluid chemistry, cleanliness, or system conditions to be identified and addressed early.

  ## Designing sampling access points

  Sampling points should be located in well-circulated sections of the cooling loop, away from dead legs or stagnant low-flow zones that can produce misleading results, Carn said. The points should be readily accessible and designed to minimize contamination during collection. Collecting samples from the same locations after system fill, following maintenance events, and during routine operation enables meaningful trend analysis and early identification of changes in coolant health, he said.

  ## Key parameters in routine testing

  The test results Carn identified as most useful fall into two categories. The first covers fluid performance: concentration and composition, appearance and clarity, pH, and inhibitor health as measured through reserve alkalinity. The second covers system interaction: dissolved metals, contamination levels, and particulate presence, which can provide early signals of corrosion, material degradation, or cleanliness problems.

  Evaluating these parameters together allows operators to move beyond a simple pass/fail result and track trends in fluid condition over time, Carn said. Dow specialists then translate those results into recommendations that identify root causes and define the most appropriate corrective actions.

  ## Fluid characteristics beyond thermal properties

  Beyond thermal conductivity and specific heat, Carn identified additional fluid properties that affect long-term system reliability. These include corrosion protection, thermal and chemical stability, and compatibility with the metals, elastomers, and polymers used throughout the system. Fluid purity also matters, as contaminants can lead to fouling, scaling, or deposit formation over time, he said. Resistance to oxidation and degradation under continuous thermal stress, biostability, and leak detectability were cited as further factors that support consistent operation and simplify maintenance.

  ## Industry significance

  The Coolant Care Network's launch signals that coolant chemistry management is moving from an ancillary concern to a recognized element of the thermal reliability stack as direct-to-chip systems expand into production-scale AI and HPC environments. Dow said the network is designed to reduce operational risk, improve consistency, and support cooling system reliability and uptime across the full life of the system.

  Dow said the network's integrated approach simplifies management by consolidating laboratory testing, data analysis, and expert guidance under a single coordinated program spanning both primary and secondary cooling loops.
body_zh: |
  陶氏（Dow）宣布推出「冷卻液照護網路」（Coolant Care Network），這是一套針對資料中心業者的結構化流體管理服務，適用於在人工智慧（AI）與高效能運算（HPC）環境中大規模部署直接對晶片液冷系統的場合，陶氏表示。

  根據陶氏全球產品管理資深總監查克‧卡恩（Chuck Carn）接受《Electronics Cooling Magazine》採訪時的說明，這套網路整合了實驗室檢測、資料分析、專家解讀及矯正支援，覆蓋主迴路與次迴路兩大冷卻迴路。服務範圍涵蓋陶氏旗下 DOWFROST LC 與 DOWFROST HD 熱傳流體的全面管理。

  ## 冷卻液管理成為可靠性的關鍵變因

  卡恩表示，隨著直接對晶片液冷從試點專案擴展至更大規模的 AI 與 HPC 部署，冷卻液化學成分或潔淨度的細微變化，都可能直接影響系統效能、設備保護及正常運行時間。他說，直接對晶片冷卻迴路在效能與可靠性方面的要求極為嚴格，冷卻液品質或系統運行若出現任何偏差，幾乎沒有容忍空間。

  卡恩指出，這些環境中的熱管理可靠性，不僅取決於有效的散熱能力，也有賴於長期維持流體的穩定性與完整性。這項依賴性正促使業界從臨時性的流體管理，轉向結構化、持續監測的管理模式。

  ## 冷卻液照護網路的服務內容

  卡恩說，這套網路無需業者自行分頭應對多家廠商，而是透過陶氏的技術指導，將業者與陶氏認可的實驗室及服務提供商串聯起來。當檢測發現問題時，陶氏專家會解讀結果並發出報告，提供整體系統狀態評估及具體可行的矯正建議。

  應對流程依嚴重程度分級。卡恩表示，若冷卻液超出建議操作範圍，陶氏會指引業者應添加哪些添加劑、以何種劑量使流體回歸規格範圍。若問題涉及汙染或試運轉殘留物，指引重點則在於由認可服務提供商執行的定點矯正措施。當流體健康狀況顯著下滑時，陶氏將提供調整乙二醇濃度、改善過濾及提高監測頻率的建議。

  ## 試運轉與基準採樣

  卡恩說明了在系統進入例行運行前建立流體健康基準的分階段做法。在系統端，他建議先確認冷卻液與所有接觸材料的相容性，驗證機械完整性，並在啟動前徹底清洗與沖洗，以去除製造殘留物、雜質及其他可能阻塞流道或損壞元件的汙染物。他說，長期流體健康有賴於從一開始就確實做好試運轉。

  卡恩表示，完成冷卻液注入及初始循環後，工程師應採集具代表性的樣本，並依適用的流體規格及原始設備製造商（OEM）建議操作範圍進行分析。此階段的關鍵參數包括：濃度、外觀、pH 值、緩衝鹼度、緩蝕劑濃度、金屬含量，以及試運轉過程中可能引入的汙染物。這些結果將建立新試運轉系統的健康基準，使後續流體化學成分、潔淨度或系統狀態的變化得以及早識別並處理。

  ## 採樣接取點的設計

  卡恩表示，採樣點應設置於冷卻迴路中循環良好的區段，避開死角或低流速的滯流區，以免產生誤導性結果。採樣點應便於接取，並在設計上將採集過程中的汙染風險降至最低。在系統注液後、每次維護事件後，以及例行運行期間，皆從相同位置採集樣本，可實現有意義的趨勢分析，並及早發現冷卻液健康狀況的變化，他說。

  ## 例行檢測的關鍵參數

  卡恩認為最具參考價值的檢測結果分為兩大類。第一類涵蓋流體效能：濃度與成分、外觀與清澈度、pH 值，以及透過緩衝鹼度衡量的緩蝕劑健康狀況。第二類涵蓋系統交互作用：溶解金屬、汙染程度及顆粒物含量，這些指標可提供腐蝕、材料劣化或潔淨度問題的早期預警信號。

  卡恩表示，綜合評估這些參數，可讓業者超越單純的合格／不合格判斷，長期追蹤流體狀況的趨勢。陶氏專家再將這些結果轉化為建議，找出根本原因，並界定最適當的矯正行動。

  ## 熱傳導性能以外的流體特性

  除導熱係數與比熱之外，卡恩還點出其他影響系統長期可靠性的流體特性。這些包括：防腐蝕保護、熱穩定性與化學穩定性，以及與系統中所有金屬、彈性體和聚合物材料的相容性。他說，流體純度同樣不可忽視，因為汙染物日積月累可能導致積垢、水垢或沉積物的形成。此外，卡恩還列舉了在持續熱應力下的抗氧化與抗劣化能力、生物穩定性，以及洩漏可偵測性等因素，這些特性均有助於維持穩定運行並簡化維護作業。

  ## 產業意義

  冷卻液照護網路的推出，標誌著隨著直接對晶片系統擴展至生產規模的 AI 與 HPC 環境，冷卻液化學管理正從次要考量晉升為熱管理可靠性架構中公認的重要一環。陶氏表示，這套網路旨在降低營運風險、提升一致性，並在系統整個生命週期中支援冷卻系統的可靠性與正常運行時間。

  陶氏指出，這套網路的整合式做法，將實驗室檢測、資料分析與專家指導整合於單一協調計畫之下，涵蓋主迴路與次迴路，從而簡化管理作業。
