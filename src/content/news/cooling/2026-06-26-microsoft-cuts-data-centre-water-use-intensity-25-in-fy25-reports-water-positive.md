body_en: |
  Microsoft reduced the water-use intensity of its data centres by 25% in fiscal year 2025 and replenished more water than it withdrew across its global operations during the same period, the company said, marking two milestones toward its 2030 sustainability targets.

  The company's average water use effectiveness (WUE) — a metric that expresses litres of water consumed per kilowatt-hour of energy used — fell to 0.27 L/kWh in 2025, down from 2.3 L/kWh in the early 2000s, a reduction of nearly 90% over roughly two decades, according to Microsoft. The 25% improvement in FY25 alone moves the company toward a stated goal of cutting water-use intensity by 40% by 2030 and achieving water-positive status — meaning it returns more water to local watersheds than it consumes — by the same year.

  ## Cooling system changes drive the reduction

  About 90% of Microsoft's owned data centre fleet now operates on cooling systems that use little or no water during normal operations, the company said. The shift began in 2008 when Microsoft adopted direct air cooling with evaporative assist across its portfolio, replacing traditional cooling towers that evaporate water continuously regardless of outdoor conditions.

  Under the evaporative-assist approach, water enters the cooling process only when outdoor temperatures exceed 29.4 degrees Celsius. The practical effect varies sharply by climate: sites in Northern Europe require no water for cooling over a full year, while facilities in Dublin and Amsterdam use water less than 5% of the time. Data centres in Virginia need cooling water roughly 10% of the year, and those in Phoenix require it up to 40% of the time.

  A data centre design introduced in 2024 for artificial intelligence workloads goes further, using a closed-loop direct-to-chip cooling system that recirculates water without evaporation, eliminating water consumption during normal operations entirely. Direct-to-chip cooling delivers coolant to processors directly, allowing operators to manage heat at the component level without relying on air-side evaporation.

  ## Operational tuning at existing sites

  Alongside new builds, Microsoft said it has been refining settings and controls at older facilities to reduce unnecessary cooling water use. Real-time weather data, operational analytics and regular audits are used to compare actual consumption against expected performance benchmarks.

  In Phoenix, those measures produced a 23% year-on-year improvement in WUE in FY25, according to the company. Microsoft said it is now applying the same optimisation program to direct-evaporatively cooled data centres across its global portfolio.

  ## Alternative water sources

  In markets where demand is highest, Microsoft has expanded the use of recycled, reused and non-potable water. In Quincy, Washington, 74% of water used at its data centres comes from such sources; in Singapore, the figure is 99%; and in San Antonio, Texas, it is 79%, the company said.

  Rainwater harvesting systems are operating at selected data centres in the Netherlands, Sweden and Ireland, with additional installations planned in Canada, the United Kingdom, Finland, Italy, South Africa and Austria. At planned data centres in Quebec, rainwater collection is expected to reach up to 1.5 million litres a year depending on local rainfall, which would offset on-site withdrawals.

  Some facilities also use on-site treatment systems that allow the same water to be recycled multiple times for cooling, reducing dependence on municipal supplies, particularly where freshwater resources are under strain.

  ## Community infrastructure investment

  Beyond its own facilities, Microsoft has funded water and wastewater infrastructure upgrades in communities where its data centres operate. Where facility expansion requires local utility improvements, the company said it covers those costs in full so local ratepayers are not burdened.

  Near its data centre in Leesburg, Virginia, Microsoft is funding more than $25 million in water and sewer improvements. Since 2020, the company said it has invested more than $500 million in more than 75 water and wastewater infrastructure projects globally.

  The company is also pursuing water replenishment work beyond its own sites. In the Phoenix area and parts of Nevada, it is partnering with FIDO Tech and local utilities on leak detection in ageing water distribution systems. In the Midwest, it is working with The Nature Conservancy to restore oxbow wetlands — former river bends cut off from the main channel — which help recharge groundwater and reduce flood risk.

  ## Industry significance

  Data centre water consumption has drawn increasing scrutiny from communities and policymakers as demand for cloud computing and AI services has risen. Operators face pressure to justify water withdrawals, particularly in arid regions such as the southwestern United States where supplies are already constrained.

  Microsoft's results indicate that a combination of evaporative-assist cooling, closed-loop direct-to-chip systems for AI workloads, operational optimisation and alternative water sourcing can reduce water intensity at scale. Judy Priest, who leads technology strategy, innovation and research for Microsoft's cloud and AI infrastructure, and Steve Solomon, an engineering leader in the same division, said the approach is designed to reduce the water intensity of digital infrastructure as data centre demand continues to grow.

  With 90% of its owned fleet already on low- or no-water cooling and its 2024 AI data centre design eliminating operational water use entirely, Microsoft said it is extending the same optimisation measures to evaporatively cooled sites globally as it works toward its 2030 intensity and water-positive targets.
body_zh: |
  微軟宣布，在2025財年將旗下資料中心的用水強度降低了25%，並於同期在全球營運中的水資源回補量超過了實際取用量，達成邁向2030年永續目標的兩項里程碑。

  該公司的平均水資源使用效率（WUE）——衡量每千瓦時能源所消耗公升水量的指標——在2025年降至每千瓦時0.27公升，相較於2000年代初期的每千瓦時2.3公升，在近20年間已累計減少將近90%。微軟表示，光是2025財年就達成的25%改善幅度，使公司更接近其既定目標：在2030年前將用水強度削減40%，並達到「水正平衡」狀態——即回補至當地流域的水量超過本身的消耗量。

  ## 冷卻系統改革帶動用水強度下降

  微軟表示，旗下自有資料中心艦隊中約90%現已採用在正常運作下幾乎不耗水或完全不耗水的冷卻系統。這一轉型始於2008年，當時微軟在其資料中心組合中全面導入「蒸發輔助直接風冷」技術，取代了傳統冷卻塔——後者無論戶外氣候如何，都會持續蒸發消耗大量冷卻水。

  在蒸發輔助方案下，冷卻水僅在戶外氣溫超過攝氏29.4度時才會投入使用。實際效果因地而異，差異相當顯著：北歐地區的資料中心在整年運作中完全不需要冷卻用水；都柏林與阿姆斯特丹的設施用水頻率不到全年的5%；維吉尼亞州的資料中心約有10%的時間需要冷卻用水；而鳳凰城的設施則有高達40%的時間需要用水降溫。

  2024年推出的一款專為人工智慧工作負載設計的資料中心方案更進一步，採用閉迴路直接對晶片冷卻系統，透過循環利用冷卻液而無需蒸發，在正常運作下徹底杜絕了用水消耗。直接對晶片冷卻技術直接將冷卻劑輸送至處理器，讓運營商得以在元件層級直接管控熱量，無需依賴氣側蒸發散熱。

  ## 現有設施的運營調校

  除了新建設施外，微軟表示也持續精調舊有設施的各項設定與控制參數，以減少不必要的冷卻用水。公司運用即時天氣資料、運營分析數據及定期稽核，將實際用水量與預期效能基準加以比對。

  在鳳凰城，上述措施在2025財年帶來了WUE年減23%的成效。微軟表示，目前正將同一套優化方案推廣至全球各地採用直接蒸發冷卻的資料中心。

  ## 替代水源的應用

  在需求最為旺盛的市場，微軟已大幅擴展再生水、循環再利用水及非飲用水的使用。該公司表示，在華盛頓州昆西，其資料中心有74%的用水來自此類水源；在新加坡，這一比例高達99%；在德克薩斯州聖安東尼奧則為79%。

  荷蘭、瑞典及愛爾蘭等地的特定資料中心已建置雨水收集系統，加拿大、英國、芬蘭、義大利、南非及奧地利等地亦已規劃後續建置。在魁北克計畫中的資料中心，預計每年可收集多達150萬公升的雨水（視當地降雨量而定），可用於抵消現地的取水量。

  部分設施也設置了現場水處理系統，讓同一批水能夠在冷卻流程中被多次循環使用，減少對市政供水的依賴，在淡水資源日益緊張的地區尤具重要性。

  ## 社區基礎設施投資

  除自身設施之外，微軟也在其資料中心所在社區資助了供水與污水基礎設施的升級工程。當設施擴建需要改善地方公用事業系統時，該公司表示將全額承擔相關費用，以免增加當地居民的負擔。

  在維吉尼亞州利斯堡的資料中心附近，微軟正資助逾2,500萬美元的供水與污水改善工程。自2020年以來，該公司表示已在全球超過75項供水與污水基礎設施計畫中投入逾5億美元。

  微軟也積極推動超出其自身設施範疇的水資源補充工作。在鳳凰城地區及內華達州部分地區，公司正與FIDO Tech及當地公用事業業者合作，對老舊輸配水管網進行滲漏偵測。在美國中西部，微軟則與大自然保護協會攜手，致力於復育牛軛湖濕地——即遭河道截斷、不再與主流相連的廢棄河灣——此舉有助於地下水回補並降低洪水風險。

  ## 產業意義

  隨著雲端運算與人工智慧服務的需求持續攀升，資料中心的用水問題受到社區與政策制定者日益嚴格的審視。業者正面臨必須合理說明取水量的壓力，尤其是在美國西南部等供水本已吃緊的乾旱地區更是如此。

  微軟的成果表明，透過結合蒸發輔助冷卻、針對AI工作負載的閉迴路直接對晶片冷卻系統、運營優化以及替代水源多管齊下，確實能夠在規模化的條件下有效降低用水強度。負責微軟雲端與AI基礎設施技術策略、創新與研究的Judy Priest，以及同一部門的工程主管Steve Solomon表示，這套方案旨在於資料中心需求持續增長的同時，降低數位基礎設施的用水強度。

  在旗下自有資料中心艦隊已有90%採用低耗水或零耗水冷卻系統、2024年推出的AI資料中心設計更在正常運作時完全消除用水消耗的基礎上，微軟表示將繼續把相同的優化措施推廣至全球各地的蒸發冷卻設施，持續朝2030年的用水強度削減目標及水正平衡目標邁進。
