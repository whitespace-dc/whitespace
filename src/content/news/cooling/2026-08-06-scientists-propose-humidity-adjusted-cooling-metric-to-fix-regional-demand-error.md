body_en: |
  Atmospheric scientists at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa have published research challenging the cooling degree days (CDD) metric — the standard tool used by utility companies, grid operators, energy planners, and financial markets to estimate air conditioning and refrigeration energy demand — saying it produces systematic regional biases by ignoring humidity.

  The study, published in Nature Communications, introduces a replacement called effective cooling degree days (ECDD), which incorporates a simplified model of the refrigeration cycle to capture how both temperature and humidity affect the efficiency of a cooling system. Applying the new metric to 50 years of North American weather data, the researchers found that cooling efficiency across the continent has declined by 2–4% per decade since 1971. The conventional metric, they report, overstates demand in some regions and understates it in others.

  ## What cooling degree days measure and why the metric is used

  A cooling degree day is a unit that represents one degree of average daily temperature above a reference threshold, typically 65°F (18°C). Utilities and grid operators sum these units over a season to estimate how much electricity customers will need to run cooling systems. Financial markets also trade futures contracts based on CDD readings to hedge against unusually hot summers.

  The existing metric treats every degree of heat as requiring the same amount of energy to cool, regardless of atmospheric conditions. The University of Hawaiʻi study argues this assumption is flawed: cooling systems become less efficient as temperatures rise, and humidity compounds the problem because the system must also remove moisture from the air, not only lower the temperature.

  "Estimates of cooling degree days are used everywhere," said Jake Casselman, an atmospheric sciences postdoctoral researcher at UH Mānoa's School of Ocean and Earth Science and Technology (SOEST). "Utility companies, grid operators, energy planners, and engineers use them to anticipate electricity demand, and financial markets trade futures on them to hedge against unusually hot summers. If that yardstick is biased in ways that depend on a region's climate, then the planning decisions built on it are biased too. That can mean building the wrong amount of power generation in the wrong place, or misjudging where the grid is most at risk during a heat wave."

  ## How the new metric works

  The ECDD metric embeds a simplified refrigeration-cycle model that estimates how efficiently a cooling system can remove heat under varying atmospheric conditions. By incorporating both temperature and humidity, it produces a demand estimate that reflects the actual thermodynamic work a system must perform rather than treating outdoor temperature as a uniform proxy.

  The researchers applied the metric to five decades of North American weather data and then used climate projections from 19 models to assess future demand changes, mapping the results onto the US electricity grid and weighting them by population.

  ## Regional divergence: the humidity tug-of-war

  One of the study's central findings is that humidity creates sharply different outcomes across regions — an effect the temperature-only metric cannot capture.

  In humid regions, rising heat and moisture reinforce each other, increasing the energy burden beyond what temperature alone would indicate. In arid regions such as the desert southwest, the researchers found that as air grows drier, the relative ease of cooling partially offsets the penalty of higher temperatures, keeping efficiency steady or, in some locations, improving it.

  "Seeing these two effects pull in opposite directions across the continent, what we ended up calling a 'tug-of-war,' is something a temperature-only view would never reveal," Casselman said.

  ## Future demand: northwest, Great Lakes, and mid-Atlantic face steepest increases

  Using projections from the 19 climate models under a high-emissions scenario, the team identified the regions where cooling-related electricity demand is projected to rise most sharply. The northwest, Great Lakes, and mid-Atlantic stand out, with some grid regions in those areas projected to see cooling-related electricity demand more than double by mid-century.

  "Our results show that the regions facing the steepest future increases in cooling demand are the northwest, Great Lakes, and mid-Atlantic, where some grid regions are projected to see cooling-related electricity demand more than double by mid-century in this high-emissions worst-case scenario," said Christina Karamperidou, a SOEST atmospheric sciences professor and co-author of the study.

  ## Implications for grid planning and financial markets

  The findings carry direct consequences for infrastructure investment and grid reliability. Planning built on the conventional CDD metric may have directed generation capacity to the wrong locations or underestimated risk during heat events in humid corridors. Financial instruments tied to CDD readings may similarly reflect a distorted picture of demand risk.

  The study's authors argue that replacing the temperature-only metric with one grounded in the physics of refrigeration would give planners, operators, and market participants a more accurate basis for decisions about where to site generation, how to size transmission, and how to hedge seasonal demand.

  The researchers plan to apply the effective cooling degree days framework to future climate projections across additional grid regions as model data becomes available.

  ## 學者提出濕度調整版冷卻指標，修正各地用電需求估算偏差

  夏威夷大學馬諾阿分校的大氣科學家發表研究，對「冷卻度日」（Cooling Degree Days，CDD）指標提出質疑。這項指標長期以來是電力公司、電網業者、能源規劃人員及金融市場用來估算空調與冷凍系統用電需求的標準工具，但研究人員指出，由於該指標完全忽略濕度因素，導致各地估算結果出現系統性偏差。

  這篇研究發表於《自然通訊》（Nature Communications），提出了一種名為「有效冷卻度日」（Effective Cooling Degree Days，ECDD）的替代指標。新指標內建一套簡化的冷凍循環模型，能同時考量溫度與濕度對冷卻系統效率的影響。研究團隊將新指標套用在北美五十年的氣象資料上，發現自1971年以來，北美大陸整體冷卻效率每十年下滑2至4%。他們也指出，傳統指標在部分地區會高估需求，在其他地區則會低估。

  ## 冷卻度日的定義與用途

  所謂「冷卻度日」，是指某日平均氣溫超出基準溫度（通常為華氏65度，即攝氏約18度）的度數單位。電力公司與電網業者會將一整季的度數加總，藉此估算用戶運行冷卻設備所需的電力。金融市場也會根據CDD數據交易期貨合約，以對沖異常炎熱夏季帶來的風險。

  現行指標假設每增加一度的熱量，所需的冷卻能耗都相同，不考慮大氣條件的差異。夏威夷大學的研究認為這個假設有根本性的缺陷：冷卻系統的效率會隨溫度上升而下降，而濕度更會加劇問題，因為系統不僅需要降低氣溫，還必須同時除去空氣中的水分。

  「冷卻度日的估算值被廣泛使用，」夏威夷大學馬諾阿分校海洋暨地球科學技術學院（SOEST）大氣科學系博士後研究員傑克．卡塞爾曼（Jake Casselman）表示，「電力公司、電網業者、能源規劃人員和工程師都用它來預測電力需求，金融市場也拿它交易期貨、對沖酷熱夏季的風險。如果這把尺本身就因地區氣候而存在偏差，那麼建立在上面的所有規劃決策也會跟著出錯。這可能導致發電容量建錯地方，或在熱浪來襲時低估電網最脆弱的環節在哪裡。」

  ## 新指標的運作原理

  ECDD指標內嵌了一套簡化的冷凍循環模型，能在不同大氣條件下估算冷卻系統排除熱量的效率。透過同時納入溫度與濕度，它所得出的需求估算值，反映的是系統實際必須付出的熱力學功，而非單純以室外氣溫作為統一的替代指標。

  研究團隊將這套指標套用於北美五十年的氣象資料，再使用19個氣候模型的推估結果評估未來需求變化，並將結果對應到美國電網地圖上，同時依人口加權計算。

  ## 地區分歧：濕度引發的「拔河效應」

  這項研究的核心發現之一，是濕度會在不同地區造成截然不同的結果——這種效應是單純以溫度為依據的指標完全無法捕捉的。

  在潮濕地區，熱度與濕度相互強化，使能源負擔遠超過單看溫度所能預測的程度。而在西南部沙漠等乾燥地區，研究人員發現，隨著空氣愈來愈乾燥，冷卻作業的相對容易度部分抵銷了高溫帶來的懲罰，使效率得以維持穩定，在某些地點甚至有所提升。

  「看到這兩種效應在整個大陸上朝相反方向拉扯——也就是我們所說的『拔河效應』——是單靠溫度視角永遠不可能發現的，」卡塞爾曼說。

  ## 未來需求：西北部、五大湖區與大西洋中部面臨最大增幅

  研究團隊利用19個氣候模型在高排放情境下的推估，找出冷卻相關電力需求預計增幅最大的地區。西北部、五大湖區與大西洋中部格外突出，部分電網區域預計到本世紀中葉，冷卻相關電力需求將超過現在的兩倍。

  「我們的結果顯示，未來冷卻需求增幅最陡峭的地區是西北部、五大湖區與大西洋中部，在這個高排放最壞情境下，部分電網區域預計到本世紀中葉冷卻相關電力需求將增加一倍以上，」SOEST大氣科學系教授、本研究共同作者克里斯蒂娜．卡蘭佩里杜（Christina Karamperidou）表示。

  ## 對電網規劃與金融市場的影響

  這些研究結果對基礎設施投資與電網可靠度具有直接影響。以傳統CDD指標為依據的規劃，可能將發電容量配置到錯誤的地點，或在潮濕走廊地帶的熱浪期間低估風險。與CDD數據掛鉤的金融工具，同樣可能反映出一幅扭曲的需求風險圖像。

  這項研究的作者主張，以植基於冷凍物理學的指標取代單純依賴溫度的舊指標，將為規劃人員、電網業者與市場參與者提供更精確的決策基礎，包括：發電廠應設在何處、輸電規模應如何設定，以及如何對沖季節性需求風險。

  研究團隊計畫在未來取得更多模型資料後，將有效冷卻度日框架進一步套用於更多電網區域的氣候推估研究。
