body_en: |
  Airsys, a data center cooling company, has led a pre-seed investment round in ThermAI, a UK startup that installs GPU compute nodes inside homes and diverts the waste heat to provide free heating and hot water to residents, marking the cooling firm's first entry into what it calls circular heat reuse.

  Airsys announced the deal and said it will act as both lead investor and technical partner to ThermAI. Augur VC and angel investors also participated in the round. Full financial terms were not disclosed. ThermAI had previously raised more than £20,000 (approximately $26,450) in earlier angel investment.

  ## How the system works

  ThermAI's residential product, HeatBridge, is built around a unit called the LiquidDock server cassette. The cassette uses Airsys' LiquidPack spray-cooling technology, which sprays an electrically non-conductive fluid directly onto server hardware inside a sealed unit rather than submerging the servers in fluid. The process recovers heat as outlet water at temperatures of up to 60°C (140°F).

  Each installation pairs a custom six-GPU ThermAI Node, assembled by contract manufacturer 2CRSi using Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs, with the HeatBridge unit. An Airsys heat exchanger moves the recovered heat into a home water circuit, where Sunamp heat batteries store it until the household needs warmth or hot water. Households receive the heat at no cost. Paying compute customers access the GPU capacity remotely through ThermAI's FlightDeck cloud platform.

  "We're designing the computing platform and the heating system together, so heat reuse is part of the infrastructure from the start," said Christopher Smart, founder and CEO of ThermAI. "Our aim is to make that work as a commercially viable model that can scale."

  ## Target market and rollout

  Airsys said ThermAI's initial deployments will focus on social housing, where residents are more likely to experience fuel poverty, particularly in winter. ThermAI is targeting placement in 100 homes with UK social housing providers in 2027.

  The startup has also secured a letter of intent from Professor Jonathan Hirst of the University of Nottingham's School of Chemistry to take part in a compute pilot. Hirst's group uses GPU-based workloads for research including drug discovery and molecular dynamics simulations.

  "ThermAI's approach is a highly sustainable one that would benefit local communities," Professor Hirst said. "Initiatives like this could play an important role in maintaining and improving the sustainability of organizations such as ours."

  ## Airsys' rationale

  For Airsys, the ThermAI deal is its first investment in a circular heat reuse model. The company frames the residential pilot as a proof of concept it expects to scale: Airsys said the same spray-cooling technology could one day recover heat from full data center deployments and feed it into district heating networks, in which heat from large facilities is piped to multiple buildings.

  "Heat reuse is the next frontier for thermal infrastructure, and ThermAI shows what it looks like when you design for circularity from the server up," said Yunshui Chen, founder and CEO of Airsys. "If we can prove this circular model in a single household, the same approach can scale to data centers feeding district heating."

  Dan Chapman, president of Augur VC, said in a statement that "ThermAI brings together a commercial opportunity in GPU computing and a practical way to make homes more affordable to heat" and that the firm sees "strong potential in the model."

  ## A crowded and uneven market

  ThermAI enters a field with multiple active competitors and a history of exits. French startup Qarnot was an early mover in the space but has since shifted mostly to larger-scale deployments focused on heating networks. UK company Heata operates a similar residential program in partnership with cloud provider Civo. French firm Hestiia uses recycled chips in its boiler product. Thermify is running a pilot with UK energy network operator UKPN, while Deep Green focuses on heating swimming pools rather than homes. US electrical panel manufacturer SPAN has developed a distributed data center product that uses underutilized electrical capacity in homes and commercial spaces to run on-site Nvidia GPUs.

  Several companies that entered the space have since left it. Germany's Cloud&Heat has pivoted to edge data center products. Dutch startup Nerdalize and New York-based Exergy have both exited the field.

  ## What it means for the industry

  The investment is notable for drawing an established data center cooling supplier into the residential heat reuse model as a hands-on technical partner rather than a passive backer. Airsys has stated that the circular approach it is piloting at the household level is intended to inform how the company applies heat reuse at data center scale, with district heating networks as the stated longer-term objective.

  ThermAI's next disclosed milestone is deploying units in 100 UK social housing homes with social housing providers in 2027.

  冷卻設備商Airsys日前領投英國新創ThermAI的種子前輪融資。ThermAI的商業模式是將GPU運算節點安裝於住宅內，並將廢熱轉供免費暖氣與熱水給住戶，標誌著Airsys首度跨足其所稱的「循環熱回收」領域。

  Airsys宣布這項交易，並表示將同時擔任ThermAI的領投方及技術合作夥伴。創投公司Augur VC與天使投資人亦參與本輪融資，惟詳細財務條款並未揭露。ThermAI先前已透過天使投資籌得逾2萬英鎊（約合2萬6,450美元）。

  ## 系統運作方式

  ThermAI的住宅產品「HeatBridge」以一款名為「LiquidDock伺服器卡匣」的裝置為核心。該卡匣採用Airsys的LiquidPack噴霧冷卻技術——在密封裝置內，以不導電液體直接噴灑於伺服器硬體上，而非將伺服器浸泡於液體中。此程序可回收出水口最高達攝氏60度（華氏140度）的熱能。

  每套安裝方案包含一個訂製的六GPU ThermAI節點——由合約製造商2CRSi以輝達RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell伺服器版GPU組裝——以及一個HeatBridge裝置。Airsys的熱交換器將回收熱能導入住宅熱水迴路，再由Sunamp熱電池儲存，待住戶有暖氣或熱水需求時使用。住戶免費獲得這些熱能，而付費運算客戶則可透過ThermAI的FlightDeck雲端平台，遠端存取GPU算力。

  ThermAI創辦人暨執行長Christopher Smart表示：「我們從一開始就將運算平台與暖氣系統整合設計，讓廢熱回收成為基礎設施的一部分。我們的目標是建立一套具商業可行性、能夠規模化的模式。」

  ## 目標市場與部署計畫

  Airsys表示，ThermAI的初期部署將聚焦於社會住宅，因為這類住戶更容易面臨能源貧困問題，尤其在冬季。ThermAI計劃於2027年在英國社會住宅業者的協助下，於100戶住宅完成設備部署。

  這家新創公司也已取得諾丁漢大學化學學院Jonathan Hirst教授的意向書，確認其將參與一項運算試驗計畫。Hirst教授的研究團隊以GPU算力從事藥物探索及分子動力學模擬等研究。

  Hirst教授表示：「ThermAI的做法高度永續，能為在地社區帶來實質效益。這類計畫在協助維護及提升我們這類機構永續性方面，可望扮演重要角色。」

  ## Airsys的策略考量

  對Airsys而言，投資ThermAI是其首度涉足循環熱回收模式。該公司將這項住宅試驗定位為可望規模化的概念驗證：Airsys表示，相同的噴霧冷卻技術未來或許能從完整的資料中心部署中回收熱能，並將其導入區域供熱網路——亦即將大型設施的熱能透過管道輸送至多棟建築。

  Airsys創辦人暨執行長Yunshui Chen表示：「熱能回收是熱基礎設施的下一個前沿，ThermAI示範了從伺服器端起步、以循環設計為本的具體樣貌。若我們能在單一住戶中驗證這套循環模式，相同的做法便能擴展至資料中心，進而供應區域供熱網路。」

  Augur VC總裁Dan Chapman在一份聲明中表示，「ThermAI將GPU運算的商業機會，與讓住宅暖氣更負擔得起的實際方法結合在一起」，並表示該公司對此模式「看好其強大潛力」。

  ## 競爭激烈且發展不均的市場

  ThermAI進入的是一個競爭者眾多、但也不乏退場案例的領域。法國新創Qarnot是該領域的先行者，但現已轉型，主要聚焦於以供熱網路為主的大規模部署。英國公司Heata與雲端服務商Civo合作，經營類似的住宅方案。法國公司Hestiia在其鍋爐產品中使用回收晶片。Thermify正在與英國電力網路運營商UKPN進行試驗，而Deep Green則專注於加熱游泳池，而非住宅供暖。美國電力面板製造商SPAN則開發了一款分散式資料中心產品，利用住宅與商業空間中閒置的電力容量，在現場運行輝達GPU。

  也有數家曾進軍此領域的業者已先後退出。德國的Cloud&Heat已轉型，專注於邊緣資料中心產品；荷蘭新創Nerdalize與紐約的Exergy均已退出這一領域。

  ## 對產業的意義

  這筆投資值得關注之處，在於它將一家具規模的資料中心冷卻設備供應商，以實際技術合作夥伴而非被動出資方的角色引入住宅熱回收模式。Airsys表示，其在住戶層面試驗的循環方法，旨在為公司如何在資料中心規模上應用熱回收提供借鑒，並以區域供熱網路作為明確的長期目標。

  ThermAI下一個已公開的里程碑，是在2027年與英國社會住宅業者合作，在100戶社會住宅中完成設備部署。
