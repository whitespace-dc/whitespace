body_en: |
  A Legionnaires' disease outbreak in the Swiss canton of Basel-Stadt has killed one person and sent 25 of 26 confirmed patients to hospital, with authorities shutting down two cooling towers identified as likely sources of the bacterial infection.

  Nine patients remain in inpatient treatment as of the latest reporting, and seven required intensive care, according to the Department of Health. The outbreak has been concentrated in the central Kleinbasel district, though isolated cases have also occurred in other parts of the canton.

  ## Cooling towers shut down, remediation under way

  Investigators focused on cooling towers after the geographic pattern of infections suggested one or more common sources. Cooling towers — which dissipate heat from industrial or commercial processes by evaporating water — can harbour *Legionella pneumophila*, the bacterium that causes Legionnaires' disease, when water temperatures and maintenance conditions allow it to multiply. The bacteria can then spread via airborne water droplets inhaled by people nearby.

  Of ten facilities tested in the vicinity of the Kleinbasel cluster, eight showed exceedances of the regulatory limit for legionella bacteria. Two towers that exhibited elevated levels of *Legionella pneumophila* specifically were shut down on Friday, July 31. Both are located on the roof of an office building on Rebgasse, in the heart of the Altstadt district, and are undergoing remediation.

  ## Authorities extend outreach to unregistered facilities

  The Department of Health has asked operators of larger industrial plants that use wet cooling systems and are not subject to mandatory reporting requirements to contact the Cantonal Laboratory voluntarily. That request signals concern that additional unregistered towers in the area may not yet have been tested.

  The outbreak underscores the public health risk posed by inadequately maintained wet cooling infrastructure in dense urban environments, where aerosols from rooftop towers can reach large numbers of people. With remediation ongoing and outreach to non-reporting operators under way, authorities have not yet indicated whether the two shut-down towers account for all confirmed cases or whether additional sources remain under investigation.

  瑞士巴塞爾城市州（Basel-Stadt）爆發退伍軍人症疫情，已造成一人死亡，26例確診病患中有25人住院治療，衛生主管機關鎖定兩座冷卻塔為細菌感染的可能源頭，並已下令關閉。

  根據衛生局最新公布的數據，目前仍有9名患者留院治療，其中7人曾需要進加護病房。此次疫情集中在巴塞爾市中心的「小巴塞爾」（Kleinbasel）區，但全州其他地區亦出現零星個案。

  ## 冷卻塔遭勒令停用　消毒作業進行中

  調查人員根據感染案例的地理分布研判，疫情源頭可能集中於一處或數處共同感染地點，因此將調查重心轉向冷卻塔。冷卻塔是工業或商業設施用以散熱的裝置，透過水分蒸發來降低系統溫度；當水溫與維護條件適合細菌滋生時，便可能孳生引發退伍軍人症的嗜肺性退伍軍人桿菌，細菌隨後藉由空氣中的水霧飛沫傳播，由附近人員吸入而感染。

  調查人員對小巴塞爾群聚感染地帶周邊共十處設施進行採樣，其中八處的退伍軍人桿菌含量超過法定標準。兩座嗜肺性退伍軍人桿菌濃度特別偏高的冷卻塔，已於7月31日（星期五）正式停用。這兩座冷卻塔均位於雷布加塞街（Rebgasse）一棟辦公大樓的屋頂，地點就在舊城區（Altstadt）的核心地帶，目前正接受全面消毒整治。

  ## 當局擴大通報　主動聯繫未登記設施

  衛生局已要求轄區內使用濕式冷卻系統、且不在強制通報規範內的大型工業廠區業者，主動與州立實驗室（Cantonal Laboratory）聯繫接受檢測。這項呼籲顯示，當局擔憂該區域可能仍有其他尚未建檔的冷卻塔尚未完成採樣。

  此次疫情再度凸顯了在人口稠密的都市環境中，維護不善的濕式冷卻設施所構成的公共衛生風險——屋頂冷卻塔產生的氣霧飛沫，可能波及大量居民。目前消毒整治作業仍在進行中，針對未通報業者的主動外展工作也尚未結束；對於這兩座已停用的冷卻塔是否足以解釋全部確診案例、抑或是否仍有其他感染源有待釐清，當局尚未作出最終定論。
