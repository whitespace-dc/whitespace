body_en: |
  Evaporative cooling systems at data centers can consume between 20,000 and 50,000 gallons of water per day for every megawatt of IT load, according to a report by Media Analytics Ltd., a figure that has elevated water use alongside power demand as the central concern for communities evaluating new facilities. Despite that consumption profile, operators continue to deploy evaporative technology, citing lower upfront costs and energy efficiency advantages that closed-loop alternatives cannot fully match.

  ## Water consumption at scale

  The Media Analytics Ltd. figures translate to significant volumes at the scale of modern data center campuses. A 10 MW facility would consume between 200,000 and 500,000 gallons of water per day under those parameters. A 100 MW site — the scale of many hyperscale campuses currently under construction — could use 2 million to 5 million gallons daily. A 1 GW facility could consume between 20 million and 50 million gallons per day, depending on design and operating conditions.

  Water concerns have become a common argument against new data center construction, particularly in drought-prone regions, where consumption figures have circulated widely in neighborhood forums and on social media.

  ## How evaporative cooling works and why it persists

  Evaporative cooling transfers heat from a data center to water circulating through a cooling tower or similar heat-rejection system. As a portion of that water evaporates, it carries heat away; the remaining water is collected and recirculated. The process allows large volumes of heat to be rejected with relatively low electrical energy input.

  Matt Green, CEO of HVAC firm Brucker, identifies first cost as a primary reason the technology remains in use. An open-loop evaporative system can cost 60% to 70% less upfront than a closed-loop alternative with comparable heat-dissipation capacity, he said. That gap is driven largely by the copper, aluminum, and other materials required in the dry coolers used in closed-loop designs.

  Brendan Mark, CEO of Capture H2O, a cooling and heating water treatment provider, frames the retention of evaporative systems around energy performance. Water-cooled systems are approximately 25% to 40% more energy-efficient than closed-loop air-cooled alternatives, he said. "So, there's a tradeoff," Mark said. "Yes, you are using less water, but you are using more energy."

  Ashlesa Mohapatra, quality manager at ServerLIFT LLC, a manufacturer of data center hardware-handling equipment, estimates that evaporative cooling can use 10% to 35% less electricity during peak summer conditions than closed-loop alternatives, though the exact savings depend on climate and system design.

  ## The closed-loop alternative and its tradeoffs

  A closed-loop system rejects heat by blowing air over tubes containing a circulating fluid, eliminating most evaporative water loss. Green argues that over the operating life of a data center, which typically runs around the clock, closed-loop systems carry a lower total cost once water use and ongoing operating expenses are accounted for.

  Mark disputes the thermal equivalence. "In a closed-loop system, you're literally blowing air over a set of tubes that has some sort of fluid running through it," he said. "That tube is not going to be able to reject the heat as effectively as evaporative cooling."

  Mohapatra adds that while cooling water in evaporative systems does not contact IT hardware directly, water-based systems introduce maintenance requirements and mechanical wear that should be included in total cost calculations alongside capital and energy figures.

  ## How climate and geography alter the calculation

  Evaporative systems depend on ambient conditions to function efficiently. In hot, dry environments such as Arizona, water evaporates readily into low-humidity air, making the technology more effective. In humid climates such as Orlando, Florida, the ambient air already holds substantial moisture, reducing evaporation rates and forcing the system to work harder — and consume more energy — to achieve equivalent cooling.

  "Evaporative works on the principle that the water actually evaporates," Mohapatra said. "So, when it's already humid out there, and there's a lot of moisture in the air, it takes more energy to pull the water and evaporate it into the atmosphere."

  In regions with abundant water supply and lower water-stress risk, operators may find the operational tradeoffs easier to justify, Mohapatra said, even if environmental and political concerns remain.

  ## Industry direction

  Green expects open-loop evaporative cooling to decline as processors generate more heat and cooling requirements intensify. "I think we will, with the new chipsets that we're seeing, be fundamentally shifted to almost exclusively closed-loop cooling systems," he said, adding that open-loop systems will gradually disappear. He attributed continued use of evaporative technology partly to institutional familiarity — operators staying with proven systems rather than accepting the perceived risk of changing approaches.

  Mark takes a less definitive position, saying water-cooled systems will remain part of the industry conversation. He anticipates the debate will become more nuanced as operators develop greater precision about how, where, and under what conditions water is being consumed.
body_zh: |
  據市場研究機構Media Analytics Ltd.一份報告指出，資料中心蒸發冷卻系統每百萬瓦（MW）IT負載每日耗水可達兩萬至五萬加侖，這項數字使用水議題與電力需求並列，成為社區評估新建設施時的核心疑慮。儘管耗水量如此之大，業者仍持續部署蒸發冷卻技術，理由是其前期成本較低，且能源效率優勢難以被閉迴路替代方案完全取代。

  ## 用水規模

  Media Analytics Ltd.的數據換算至現代資料中心園區的規模後，代表著相當可觀的耗水量。以一座10 MW的設施為例，依上述參數，每日耗水量將介於20萬至50萬加侖之間。100 MW的站點——目前許多正在興建的超大規模園區均達此規模——每日用水可達200萬至500萬加侖。若是1 GW的設施，視設計與運行條件而定，每日耗水量將在2,000萬至5,000萬加侖之間。

  用水疑慮已成為反對新建資料中心的常見論點，尤其在乾旱易發地區，相關耗水數據在社區論壇和社群媒體上廣泛流傳。

  ## 蒸發冷卻的運作原理及其持續普及的原因

  蒸發冷卻透過冷卻水塔或類似散熱系統中的循環水，將資料中心的熱量帶走。當部分水蒸發時，熱量隨之散逸；剩餘的水則被收集並重新循環利用。這項程序能以相對低的電能輸入，排除大量熱量。

  HVAC廠商Brucker執行長Matt Green指出，初始成本是此技術持續受到採用的主因。他表示，開迴路蒸發系統的前期成本，比具有同等散熱能力的閉迴路替代方案低60%至70%。這項差距主要源自閉迴路設計中乾式冷卻器所需的銅、鋁及其他材料成本。

  冷卻與加熱水處理廠商Capture H2O執行長Brendan Mark，則從能源效能的角度闡釋蒸發系統持續受到青睞的原因。他表示，水冷系統的能源效率比閉迴路氣冷替代方案高約25%至40%。「所以這是一種取捨，」Mark說，「是的，你確實用了較少的水，但卻耗費了更多能源。」

  資料中心硬體搬運設備製造商ServerLIFT LLC品質經理Ashlesa Mohapatra估計，在夏季尖峰條件下，蒸發冷卻比閉迴路替代方案可節省10%至35%的電力，但確切節省幅度取決於氣候與系統設計。

  ## 閉迴路替代方案及其取捨

  閉迴路系統透過將空氣吹過含有循環流體的管路來散熱，幾乎消除了蒸發性水損耗。Green認為，若將資料中心通常全年不間斷的運作壽命、水資源使用及持續運營費用一併納入計算，閉迴路系統的總持有成本實際上更低。

  Mark對此提出異議。「在閉迴路系統中，你實際上是把空氣吹過一組管路，管路裡有某種流體流通，」他說，「那根管路的散熱效果，終究無法與蒸發冷卻相提並論。」

  Mohapatra則補充指出，雖然蒸發系統的冷卻水不會直接接觸IT硬體，但水基系統帶來的維護需求與機械磨損，同樣應納入總成本計算，與資本支出和能源數據一併考量。

  ## 氣候與地理條件如何左右效益計算

  蒸發系統有效運作有賴於環境條件的配合。在亞利桑那州等炎熱乾燥的環境中，水分能迅速蒸發進入低濕度的空氣，使這項技術更具效率。在佛羅里達州奧蘭多等潮濕氣候中，環境空氣本已飽含水分，蒸發率因此降低，系統必須更費力——也消耗更多能源——才能達到同等的冷卻效果。

  「蒸發冷卻的原理，是讓水真正蒸發，」Mohapatra說，「所以當外面已經很潮濕、空氣中充滿水氣時，就需要消耗更多能源，才能將水抽出並蒸發至大氣中。」

  Mohapatra表示，在水資源豐沛、水資源壓力風險較低的地區，即使環境與政治方面的疑慮依然存在，業者在衡量這樣的運營取捨時可能更為從容。

  ## 產業走向

  Green預期，隨著處理器發熱量持續攀升、冷卻需求日益加劇，開迴路蒸發冷卻將逐漸式微。「我認為，隨著我們所見到的新一代晶片組，整個產業將從根本上轉向幾乎全面採用閉迴路冷卻系統，」他說，並補充表示開迴路系統將逐漸退出舞台。他將蒸發技術持續受到採用，部分歸因於制度慣性——業者寧願沿用已驗證的系統，而不願承擔改換方法所帶來的潛在風險。

  Mark的立場則較為審慎，認為水冷系統仍將持續出現在產業討論之中。他預期，隨著業者對水資源消耗的方式、地點與條件掌握得更加精確，這場論辯將走向更細膩、更具針對性的發展。
