body_en: |
  GSMA convened Asia-Pacific government officials, telecom operators, technology companies, and standards bodies in Shanghai to address how regional networks must be upgraded for AI services, with participants calling for millisecond-level latency, terabit-level bandwidth, and coordinated cross-border infrastructure.

  The gathering centered on a shared view that existing connectivity approaches are insufficient for AI training, inference, and industrial deployment. Si Han Bo Chen, Head of GSMA Greater China, said in opening remarks that existing approaches were no longer sufficient for the next stage of AI deployment and that Asia-Pacific had moved from being a technology follower to taking a leading role in new infrastructure and computing-network integration.

  ## China's three-tier infrastructure plan

  China outlined its national planning model at the roundtable. He Xiaowen, Deputy Director-General of the Department of Planning at China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, described a three-tier national structure covering national, regional, and urban layers, alongside high-speed direct links between regions.

  He said China is advancing all-optical networks, 400G and 800G transmission speeds (the capacity to carry 400 or 800 gigabits of data per second over a single optical channel), SRv6 (Segment Routing over IPv6, a protocol for controlling how traffic moves across networks), and intelligent computing orchestration to reduce computing silos — isolated processing pools that cannot easily share resources — across the country. He also pointed to a "millisecond" computing action aimed at achieving millisecond-level interconnection between computing centres, access to computing resources, and delivery of computing applications.

  He called for stronger interoperability and resource sharing across Asia-Pacific. "Computing power empowers the future, networks connect us all, and synergy achieves win-win results," he said.

  ## Technical requirements

  Huawei argued that AI is driving specific, quantifiable network demands. Zhai Haipeng, Vice President of Huawei's Optical Business Product Line, said AI is pushing networks toward latency of 5 milliseconds to 1 millisecond and reliability of 99.999 percent to 99.9999 percent, alongside greater bandwidth. Latency is the time it takes data to travel between two network points.

  Zhai called for regional alignment around the ITU-T ION-2030 architecture — ITU-T is the standards-setting arm of the International Telecommunication Union — and identified three priorities for Asia-Pacific: coordination of cross-border submarine and land cables, deterministic low-latency networks built around 1-, 5-, and 20-millisecond latency tiers, and an ultra-gigabit capacity strategy.

  AI service providers put bandwidth requirements even higher. Daniel Shi, Global Business Account Executive at MiniMax, an AI model developer, said future AI networks would need terabit-per-second-level bandwidth, millisecond-level latency, and strong security. "The black magic of the AI field must be held in the hands of the good guys," Shi said.

  ## Standards and inclusion

  Dr Atsuko Okuda, ITU Regional Director for Asia and the Pacific, said telecom operators across the region are likely to face major infrastructure upgrades as AI drives demand for data and computing. She pointed to fibre expansion, backbone upgrades, and denser 5G deployments as likely components of that work.

  Okuda said countries should refer to the more than 200 AI-related standards developed at the ITU when deploying AI systems. She also said less-connected communities should not be excluded from the benefits of digital change as infrastructure investment accelerates.

  ## National policy dimensions

  Thailand and Sri Lanka each framed the network upgrade as a matter of national economic policy. Chaichanok Chidchob, Thailand's Minister of Digital Economy and Society, said resilient digital and mobile connectivity underpins the AI economy and linked it directly to the country's development strategy.

  Eranga Weeraratne, Sri Lanka's Deputy Minister of Digital Economy, said smaller economies must decide whether they will only consume AI or also produce it in selected areas. He said Sri Lanka had chosen a selective production route, supported by regional partnerships and closer integration between computing and network infrastructure.

  ## Operators as AI partners

  Several participants argued that telecom operators should play a larger role in AI deployment than simply carrying traffic. Wu Jiong, Marketing Deputy General Manager at Mobile Cloud, said China Mobile is building Intelligent Computing Centres and all-optical backbone infrastructure as part of a plan to coordinate cloud, edge, and terminal resources, and aims to improve access to different types of computing through scheduling and optimisation. "In the AI era, security is not an option, it is a mandatory question," Wu said.

  MiniMax's Shi said deeper cooperation between AI developers and operators could include resource exchange and wholesale distribution arrangements as AI demand rises, positioning operators as commercial partners rather than infrastructure-only providers.

  ## Commercial pressure

  Liu Zhen, Vice President of Kuaishou Group, linked low-latency, reliable computing-network infrastructure to commercial success in short video, e-commerce, and AI services, and argued that business decisions should be closely tied to user demand.

  CTM Macau described the challenge from a smaller market perspective. Ebel Cham, VP Commercial at CTM Macau, said telecom services remain the foundation of AI development and outlined the company's plan to build an "AI Hub" by aggregating AI models, computing resources, and applications through common billing and platform interfaces.

  Zhang Chao, Vice President of Kunzhen Zhenyu and President of its Overseas Business Division, stated the commercial case directly. "Computing power is directly equal to revenue, and tokens have become the new commodity of the AI era," Zhang said. Tokens are the discrete units AI models use to process and generate text; their production depends on computing and network capacity.

  ## Significance

  The roundtable reflects a shift in how Asia-Pacific's telecoms industry is framing network investment: away from general-purpose connectivity and toward infrastructure built specifically for AI workloads. Agreement across government ministries, equipment vendors, standards bodies, and AI model developers on specific latency and reliability targets indicates that regional planning is moving from broad aspiration to defined technical objectives.

  Sri Lanka's position on selective AI production illustrates the divergence taking shape among smaller economies as infrastructure costs and technical requirements for AI services increase.

  Huawei's Zhai identified the coordination of cross-border submarine and land cable infrastructure, a deterministic low-latency network strategy built around 1-, 5-, and 20-millisecond latency circles, and an ultra-gigabit capacity program as the concrete priorities Asia-Pacific must act on to meet the demands the Shanghai discussions identified.
body_zh: |
  GSMA日前於上海召集亞太地區政府官員、電信業者、科技公司及標準機構，共同探討區域網路如何升級才能支援AI服務。與會者一致呼籲，網路須達到毫秒級延遲、兆位元每秒（Tbps）級頻寬，並建立跨境基礎設施協調機制。

  此次論壇圍繞一項共識展開：現有連線方式已不足以應對AI訓練、推論與工業化部署的需求。GSMA大中華區負責人陳思瀚博在開幕致詞中指出，現有方式已無法滿足AI部署下一階段的要求，亞太地區已從技術跟隨者的角色，躍升為新型基礎設施與算網融合的領跑者。

  ## 中國三層次基礎設施規劃

  中國在此次圓桌論壇上說明了其國家規劃模式。中國工業和資訊化部規劃司副司長何小文描述了一套涵蓋國家、區域與城市三個層級的全國性架構，並輔以各區域間的高速直聯鏈路。

  何小文表示，中國正積極推進全光網路，以及400G與800G傳輸速率（即每條光學通道可承載400或800吉位元資料的能力）、SRv6（IPv6分段路由，一種控制網路流量路徑的協定），以及智慧算力編排，以消除全國各地的算力孤島——即無法輕易共享資源的孤立運算池。他並提到「毫秒」算力行動，目標是在算力中心互聯、算力資源存取以及算力應用交付等層面，實現毫秒級互通。

  他呼籲亞太地區加強互通性與資源共享，並表示：「算力賦能未來，網路連結萬物，協同共創雙贏。」

  ## 技術需求

  華為認為，AI正在催生具體且可量化的網路需求。華為光產品線副總裁翟海鵬表示，AI正推動網路朝5毫秒至1毫秒的延遲（即資料在兩個網路節點之間傳輸所需的時間）及99.999%至99.9999%的可靠性邁進，同時頻寬需求亦大幅攀升。

  翟海鵬呼籲亞太地區統一對齊國際電信聯盟電信標準化部門（ITU-T）的ION-2030架構，並點出三大優先事項：跨境海底電纜與陸地電纜的協調整合、以1毫秒、5毫秒及20毫秒三個延遲等級為基礎構建確定性低延遲網路，以及超吉位元容量策略。

  AI服務供應商對頻寬的要求更為積極。AI模型開發商MiniMax全球商業客戶主管Daniel Shi表示，未來AI網路將需要兆位元每秒（Tbps）級別的頻寬、毫秒級延遲，以及強健的資安保障。「AI領域的黑魔法，必須掌握在善意之人手中，」他說。

  ## 標準與共融

  ITU亞太地區主任奧田敦子（Dr Atsuko Okuda）表示，隨著AI帶動資料與算力需求，區域內電信業者極可能面臨大規模基礎設施升級。她指出，光纖擴建、骨幹網路升級以及更密集的5G部署，將是此項工作的可能組成要素。

  奧田敦子表示，各國在部署AI系統時，應參考ITU制定的逾200項AI相關標準。她並強調，隨著基礎設施投資加速，連線較為薄弱的社群不應被排除在數位變革的紅利之外。

  ## 國家政策面向

  泰國與斯里蘭卡均將網路升級定位為國家經濟政策要務。泰國數位經濟與社會部部長柴查諾·奇德喬（Chaichanok Chidchob）表示，穩固的數位與行動連線能力是AI經濟的根基，並將其直接連結至該國的發展策略。

  斯里蘭卡數位經濟副部長伊蘭加·維拉拉特納（Eranga Weeraratne）表示，規模較小的經濟體必須決定，究竟只是AI的消費者，還是要在特定領域成為AI的生產者。他說，斯里蘭卡已選擇走有選擇性的生產路線，並藉由區域夥伴關係，以及算力與網路基礎設施更緊密的整合來加以支撐。

  ## 電信業者作為AI合作夥伴

  多名與會者主張，電信業者在AI部署中應扮演比單純傳輸流量更重要的角色。移動雲公司市場行銷副總裁吳炯表示，中國移動正打造智算中心與全光骨幹網路基礎設施，作為協調雲端、邊緣與終端資源整體計畫的一環，目標是透過調度與優化，提升不同類型算力的可及性。「在AI時代，資安不是選項，而是必答題，」他說。

  MiniMax的Daniel Shi表示，AI開發商與電信業者之間更深層的合作，可望涵蓋資源交換與批發分銷安排，以因應AI需求的持續成長，讓電信業者成為商業夥伴，而非單純的基礎設施供應商。

  ## 商業壓力

  快手集團副總裁劉震將低延遲、高可靠的算網基礎設施與短影音、電子商務及AI服務的商業成功相連結，並主張商業決策應與使用者需求緊密扣合。

  澳門CTM則從較小市場的角度闡述此一挑戰。CTM澳門商務副總裁艾貝爾·陳（Ebel Cham）表示，電信服務仍是AI發展的根基，並概述了公司透過整合AI模型、算力資源與應用程式，並藉由共通帳務與平台介面打造「AI Hub」（AI樞紐）的計畫。

  坤珍振宇副總裁暨海外業務事業部總裁張超則直接道出商業邏輯：「算力直接等於營收，token已成為AI時代的新大宗商品。」所謂token，是AI模型用以處理與生成文字的最小單位，其生產仰賴算力與網路容量。

  ## 重要意義

  此次圓桌論壇反映出亞太電信產業看待網路投資的視角正在轉變：從通用型連線，走向專為AI工作負載打造的基礎設施。政府部會、設備廠商、標準機構與AI模型開發商就延遲與可靠性的具體指標取得共識，顯示區域規劃正從宏觀願景走向明確的技術目標。

  斯里蘭卡選擇性AI生產的立場，則彰顯出隨著AI服務的基礎設施成本與技術門檻持續攀升，規模較小的經濟體之間正浮現的分歧。

  華為翟海鵬指出亞太地區必須具體落實的三大優先行動，以回應上海論壇所揭示的需求：協調跨境海底電纜與陸地電纜基礎設施、以1毫秒、5毫秒及20毫秒三個延遲圓圈為核心建立確定性低延遲網路策略，以及推出超吉位元容量計畫。
