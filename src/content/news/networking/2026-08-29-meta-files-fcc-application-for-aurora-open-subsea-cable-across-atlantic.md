body_en: |
  Meta has filed with the U.S. Federal Communications Commission for regulatory approval of Aurora, a transatlantic subsea cable system designed to carry approximately 497 terabits per second across 24 fibre pairs at 20.7 Tbps each. The company has asked the FCC to rule by November 2026.

  The filing's significance lies less in the system's capacity than in its architecture. Aurora is structured as an open cable system, meaning that owners of individual fibre pairs will be able to deploy and operate their own submarine line terminal equipment (SLTE) — the electronics that convert signals onto the fibre — rather than sharing a common set managed by a single operator or consortium. Meta's filing also indicates the company is considering selling dark fibre, meaning unlit, unactivated fibre capacity, into the secondary market, which would allow carriers or other buyers to purchase access without Meta surrendering ownership of the cable itself.

  ## What open cable means and why it matters

  Traditional subsea cable systems bundle the physical fibre together with the electronics that activate it under a single operator or consortium structure. Customers buying capacity on such systems take on that operator's choice of equipment vendor and are tied to consortium-wide decisions about when and how to upgrade.

  Open cable systems separate physical infrastructure from electronics. Each fibre-pair owner selects, deploys, and manages its own terminal gear independently. That allows one owner to upgrade to newer coherent optics technology without requiring every other participant to do the same — a meaningful operational advantage as artificial intelligence workloads drive traffic patterns that diverge from legacy telecommunications demand.

  ## Hyperscalers as infrastructure builders

  Aurora reflects a structural shift in who builds and owns international subsea capacity. According to the source reporting, Google holds stakes in around 34 subsea systems, Meta in roughly 19 to 20, and Microsoft and Amazon together in approximately ten more. Hyperscalers' share of international bandwidth consumption has grown from close to nothing in 2010 to an estimated 71 to 75 percent today.

  Three factors are driving that cohort toward open designs, according to the analysis. First, ownership economics: hyperscalers are now financing and building routes themselves rather than negotiating wholesale capacity from telco-led consortia, and separating cable ownership from terminal equipment gives them tighter control over cost and schedule. Second, monetisation: the Aurora filing's reference to a dark fibre secondary market shows Meta can recover costs from carriers or other hyperscalers without ceding primary control of the asset. Third, technology refresh: independent terminal equipment ownership lets individual fibre-pair holders upgrade on their own timeline rather than waiting for consortium-wide coordination.

  ## Comparable builds

  Aurora is not an isolated case. V.tal's announced US-Brazil subsea system was built explicitly with open cable technology to serve both hyperscalers and carriers on separate terms. A Microsoft-led consortium with Lightstorm is applying the same hyperscaler-adjacent build pattern to an India-Southeast Asia route.

  ## What comes next

  Meta's request for an FCC decision by November 2026 sets a near-term regulatory milestone for Aurora. The deadline falls shortly after Capacity Europe 2026, an industry conference scheduled for 13 to 15 October in London where subsea financing and infrastructure ownership form part of the agenda.

  此次申請的意義，不在於系統容量的大小，而在於其架構設計。Aurora採用開放式電纜系統架構，各光纖對的擁有者可自行部署並操作海底線路終端設備（SLTE）——亦即負責將訊號轉換至光纖的電子設備——而無需共用由單一營運商或財團統一管理的設備。Meta的申請文件也透露，該公司正考慮將暗纖（即未點亮、尚未啟用的光纖容量）銷售至次級市場，讓電信業者或其他買家得以購買使用權，而Meta本身則仍保有電纜的所有權。

  ## 開放式電纜的定義與重要性

  傳統海底電纜系統將實體光纖與啟用光纖所需的電子設備捆綁在一起，由單一營運商或財團統一管理。在這類系統上購買容量的客戶，只能沿用該營運商所選擇的設備廠商，並受制於財團在升級時機與方式上的集體決策。

  開放式電纜系統則將實體基礎設施與電子設備分離，各光纖對的擁有者可獨立選擇、部署並管理自己的終端設備。如此一來，某一擁有者便可升級至更新的同調光學技術，而毋須等待所有參與者同步跟進——這在人工智慧工作負載驅動流量模式、使其日益偏離傳統電信需求的背景下，具有相當實質的營運優勢。

  ## 超大規模業者成為基礎設施建設者

  Aurora反映出一個結構性轉變：國際海底容量的建設者與擁有者正在改變。根據相關報導，Google目前持有約34個海底系統的股份，Meta持有約19至20個，微軟與亞馬遜合計則持有約10個。超大規模業者在國際頻寬消耗中所占的比重，已從2010年的接近零，攀升至今日估計的71至75%。

  分析指出，有三大因素驅動這一群體轉向開放式設計。第一是所有權經濟：超大規模業者如今自行融資並建設路由，而非向電信業者主導的財團協商批發容量；將電纜所有權與終端設備分離，讓他們得以更嚴格掌控成本與時程。第二是商業化變現：Aurora申請文件中提及的暗纖次級市場，顯示Meta可藉此向電信業者或其他超大規模業者回收成本，同時不必放棄資產的主要控制權。第三是技術更新：獨立的終端設備所有權，讓各光纖對的持有者得以按照自身時程進行升級，不必等待財團層級的協調。

  ## 類似建設案例

  Aurora並非孤例。V.tal宣布的美國至巴西海底系統，即明確採用開放式電纜技術，以便在不同條款下分別服務超大規模業者與電信業者。由微軟主導、聯合Lightstorm組成的財團，也正將同樣的超大規模業者主導建設模式，應用於印度至東南亞的路由上。

  ## 後續發展

  Meta要求FCC於2026年11月前就Aurora作出裁定，為該計畫設下近期監管里程碑。這一期限恰好落在2026年Capacity Europe研討會結束後不久——該會議預定於10月13日至15日在倫敦舉行，議程中即涵蓋海底融資與基礎設施所有權等議題。
