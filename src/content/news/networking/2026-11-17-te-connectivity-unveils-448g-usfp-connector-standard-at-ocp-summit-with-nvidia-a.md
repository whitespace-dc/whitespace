body_en: |
  TE Connectivity announced a new 448G front-panel transceiver connector standard, called USFP, at the October 2026 OCP Global Summit, developed in partnership with NVIDIA and Cisco. The announcement came as the industry faces competing approaches to reaching 448G SerDes — Serializer/Deserializer — speeds, which define per-lane data transfer rates and directly affect how efficiently power is used to move data at scale in AI data centers.

  Brian Payne, Vice President of Product Management for TE Connectivity's Digital Data Networks (DDN) division, presented a 3D-printed prototype of the USFP connector during the summit. Payne said the company designed the USFP to maintain a form factor similar to existing QSFP — Quad Small Form-factor Pluggable — connectors, while separating signal integrity and power delivery pathways within the housing. The separation, Payne said, keeps signal integrity performance high as transceiver power requirements increase.

  ## 448G Connectivity and Thermal Management

  The USFP design includes provision for cold plate liquid cooling, allowing heat to be extracted from the transceiver as power levels rise in future generations, according to Payne. He said thermal and signal integrity challenges are the central obstacles industry faces in moving from current front-panel standards to 448G, and that TE believes the USFP design balances those constraints without over-indexing on any single factor.

  Payne described two competing models in the market for reaching 448G, without identifying them by name. TE's partnership with NVIDIA and Cisco on the USFP standard was announced on the Monday of the OCP Summit week, Payne said.

  ## Copper Backplanes and Midplanes

  Beyond front-panel connectivity, TE Connectivity displayed copper backplane and midplane products at its booth, which Payne described as a critical enabling technology for rack-scale AI infrastructure. The midplane architecture he demonstrated allows compute sleds and power delivery to slide in from one side while GPU resources connect through the midplane, physically separating compute and GPU within a rack. Copper backplanes handle connectivity on the rear side of the chassis.

  Payne said TE is one of a small number of companies able to manufacture copper backplane solutions in the volumes required by hyperscale AI deployments, calling it a technically demanding product that must be produced at massive scale.

  ## Optics Investment and RAM Photonics Acquisition

  TE Connectivity also highlighted its optics roadmap at the summit. Earlier in 2026, the company acquired RAM Photonics, an optics firm it is integrating into its product portfolio. Payne said TE is investing in co-packaged optics — where optical components are integrated directly with switch silicon to shorten electrical signal paths — as well as co-packaged copper and other approaches to extending optical connectivity deeper into AI systems.

  Payne acknowledged the industry has discussed co-packaged optics for more than a decade, but said the physics constraints created by increasing lane counts are making the transition more urgent. As more data lanes are required per chip, the maximum distance copper can effectively carry those signals decreases, he said, pointing to that dynamic as the driver behind TE's optics investment now.

  ## Context: DDN Growth and OCP Membership

  TE Connectivity's DDN division has recorded significant growth over the past three years, Payne said, attributing the expansion to demand for AI infrastructure buildout. The company has been a member of the Open Compute Project since 2017. Payne joined TE Connectivity approximately one month before the October 2026 summit, arriving from Dell, where he spent 26 years.

  TE Connectivity's OCP Summit booth was organized around three interconnected system-level racks covering optical interconnect, sidecar power, and copper IT, reflecting the company's stated strategy of addressing power delivery and connectivity across all layers of AI infrastructure from near-chip packaging through to the network switch.

  The company said the USFP standard, the copper backplane portfolio, and the RAM Photonics-derived optics products are all in active deployment or development for large-scale AI infrastructure customers.
body_zh: |
  TE Connectivity於2026年10月OCP全球峰會上，宣布與輝達（NVIDIA）及思科（Cisco）共同開發全新448G前面板收發器連接器標準，命名為USFP。此次發表正值業界對於如何達成448G SerDes（序列器／解序列器）速度各有主張之際——SerDes定義每通道資料傳輸速率，直接影響AI資料中心在大規模資料搬移時的供電效率。

  TE Connectivity數位資料網路（DDN）事業部產品管理副總裁布萊恩．培恩（Brian Payne）在峰會上展示了USFP連接器的3D列印原型。培恩表示，該公司設計USFP時刻意維持與現有QSFP（四通道小型可插拔）連接器相近的外觀尺寸，同時在外殼內部將訊號完整性路徑與供電路徑分離。他指出，這種分離設計能在收發器功耗需求持續提升的情況下，維持高水準的訊號完整性表現。

  ## 448G連接技術與散熱管理

  根據培恩的說明，USFP設計內建冷板液冷支援，可在未來世代功耗提升時，直接從收發器抽取熱能。他表示，業界在從現行前面板標準邁向448G的過程中，散熱管理與訊號完整性是兩大核心障礙，而TE認為USFP設計能在不過度偏重任一因素的前提下，同時兼顧這兩項限制。

  培恩在未點名的情況下，描述了市場上達成448G的兩種競爭模式。他表示，TE與輝達及思科在USFP標準上的合作關係，於OCP峰會週的週一正式對外公布。

  ## 銅製背板與中板

  除前面板連接技術之外，TE Connectivity也在其展位展示了銅製背板與中板產品，培恩將其定位為機架規模AI基礎設施的關鍵賦能技術。他所展示的中板架構，允許運算托盤與供電模組從機架一側插入，同時讓GPU資源透過中板進行連接，在機架內部實現運算單元與GPU的物理分隔；銅製背板則負責機箱背面的連接工作。

  培恩表示，在能以超大規模AI部署所需量產規模製造銅製背板解決方案的業者中，TE是少數幾家之一，並稱其為技術難度極高、同時又必須大量生產的產品。

  ## 光學投資與RAM Photonics收購

  TE Connectivity在峰會上也重點介紹了其光學產品路線圖。2026年稍早，該公司收購了光學廠商RAM Photonics，目前正積極將其整合至整體產品組合之中。培恩表示，TE正投資共封裝光學（co-packaged optics）——即將光學元件直接整合於交換器晶片，以縮短電訊號傳輸路徑——以及共封裝銅纜等其他方式，致力將光學連接延伸至AI系統的更深層。

  培恩承認，業界討論共封裝光學已逾十年，但他指出，通道數不斷增加所帶來的物理限制，正使這一技術轉型愈發迫切。他說，每顆晶片所需的資料通道越多，銅纜能有效傳輸這些訊號的最大距離就越短，並以此動態趨勢作為TE現階段加碼投入光學領域的核心驅動力。

  ## 背景：DDN事業部成長與OCP會員資格

  培恩表示，TE Connectivity的DDN事業部過去三年錄得顯著成長，並將此一擴張歸因於AI基礎設施建置需求的強勁拉動。該公司自2017年起便是開放運算專案（Open Compute Project，OCP）的成員。培恩約於2026年10月峰會召開前一個月加入TE Connectivity，此前他曾在戴爾（Dell）服務長達26年。

  TE Connectivity在OCP峰會的展位圍繞三個相互連接的系統級機架進行規劃，分別涵蓋光學互連、側掛式供電與銅製IT架構，呼應該公司所揭示的整體策略——從近晶片封裝到網路交換器，全面解決AI基礎設施各層的供電傳輸與連接問題。

  該公司表示，USFP標準、銅製背板產品組合，以及源自RAM Photonics的光學產品，目前皆已進入為大規模AI基礎設施客戶積極部署或開發的階段。
