body_en: |
  Arista Networks reported $3.036 billion in second-quarter 2026 revenue, the first time the networking vendor has surpassed $3 billion in a single quarter, representing a 37.7 percent year-on-year increase and a 12.1 percent sequential gain from the first quarter of 2026.

  To illustrate the scale of that growth: Arista generated $2.32 billion in revenue for the entire calendar year 2020, the company said. Chief Executive Jayshree Ullal attributed the performance to "significant demand," with Arista's Ethernet switch line playing a central role. That product has grown from single-digit cumulative customer counts in 2024 to more than 100 customers.

  Following the quarter, Arista raised its third-quarter 2026 revenue guidance to approximately $3.3 billion and lifted its full-year forecast to $12.6 billion, implying roughly 40 percent annual growth.

  ## Supply chain overhaul

  Despite the strong results, Ullal acknowledged that "industry-wide supply tightness and rising component costs persist," but said Arista had taken "individual and aggressive proactive steps" to address the constraints.

  The most visible signal of that effort is a near-tripling of Arista's multi-year purchase commitments, which rose to $9.7 billion from $3.6 billion a year ago. Arista also hired Eugenia Corrales, formerly chief executive of Nefeli Networks, to lead global operations.

  Chief Operating Officer Todd Nightingale said the company has "secured multi-year agreements with leading vendors of strategic components, qualified new suppliers in key areas to limit risk, and built out supply chains for next-gen AI technologies." Memory supply is locked in for 2026, with visibility extending into 2027 for DDR4 (fourth-generation double data rate synchronous dynamic RAM), DDR5, and NAND (non-volatile flash) memory, Nightingale said. He added that Arista has increased resilience by expanding vendor qualification across its supply base.

  On the product side, Arista debuted 1.6-terabit-per-second switches in June 2026, with shipments planned for early 2027. Analysts at William Blair said the launch creates "a path for sustained high growth" over the next year.

  ## Distributed compute as a growth market

  With supply largely stabilized, Arista's leadership used the earnings call to position scale-across networking as an emerging revenue driver alongside the more established scale-out segment. Scale-out refers to cluster-to-cluster interconnectivity; scale-across, also called distributed compute networking, links geographically dispersed facilities so operators can treat them as a unified compute pool.

  Ullal said a "constant state of scarcity for power, space, and compute" is pushing customers to distribute workloads across existing, disparate sites rather than concentrate them in a single location. Demand is currently concentrated among what she called "cloud and AI titans" and a growing set of neoclouds, with enterprise adoption trailing behind.

  ## Positioning against Nvidia

  Asked about Arista's competitive stance against Nvidia, whose NVLink proprietary interconnect has long dominated scale-up networking (the high-speed links within a single GPU cluster), Ullal said Arista "does better" than NVLink in scale-out and scale-across configurations. She said Arista is targeting scale-up specifically where non-Nvidia accelerators are in use.

  She pointed to Google's TPUs (tensor processing units, chips designed for machine-learning workloads) as a growing training platform and cited a range of inference accelerators being developed by Arista partners, including custom chips built by cloud customers in-house. "Where it's non-Nvidia, Arista will be excited and will be working more closely both in the scale-up and scale-out, in some cases to build custom racks with their custom processors so that we can better tune our network with the behavior for their inference or training engines," Ullal said.

  Ullal drew a parallel to InfiniBand, the high-speed interconnect protocol that was displaced by Ethernet in data center networking, arguing a similar transition in scale-up could play out over a two-to-three-year horizon. "I expect us to do much better there," she said.

  Arista's 1.6-terabit switches are scheduled to ship in early 2027.
body_zh: |
  Arista Networks 公布2026年第二季營收達30.36億美元，為該公司首度單季突破30億美元，較去年同期成長37.7%，較2026年第一季環比增長12.1%。

  為彰顯此一成長規模，公司特別指出，Arista 2020年全年營收不過23.2億美元。執行長 Jayshree Ullal 將亮眼業績歸因於「強勁需求」，其中乙太網路交換機產品線扮演核心角色——這條產品線的累計客戶數從2024年的個位數，如今已成長至逾100家。

  財報發布後，Arista 隨即上調第三季營收指引至約33億美元，全年預測亦上修至126億美元，隱含年增幅約達40%。

  ## 供應鏈大規模重組

  儘管業績亮眼，Ullal 坦言「整體產業供應吃緊與零組件成本上漲的問題依然存在」，但表示 Arista 已採取「個別且積極主動的步驟」加以因應。

  最具體的行動，是 Arista 多年期採購承諾近乎翻三倍——從一年前的36億美元大幅躍升至97億美元。公司亦延攬前 Nefeli Networks 執行長 Eugenia Corrales 主掌全球營運。

  營運長 Todd Nightingale 表示，公司已「與關鍵戰略零組件的主要廠商簽訂多年期協議，並在要害領域認證新供應商以降低風險，同時為下一代 AI 技術建立完整供應鏈」。記憶體供應方面，2026年的備貨已獲保障，DDR4（第四代雙倍資料速率同步動態隨機存取記憶體）、DDR5 及 NAND（非揮發性快閃記憶體）的能見度更延伸至2027年。Nightingale 並指出，公司透過擴大供應基礎的廠商認證範圍，進一步提升供應韌性。

  產品面上，Arista 於2026年6月推出每秒1.6兆位元交換機，預計2027年初開始出貨。William Blair 分析師表示，此次產品發布為 Arista 創造了「未來一年持續高速成長的路徑」。

  ## 分散式運算成新成長引擎

  供應面問題大致穩定後，Arista 管理層在法說會上積極布局，將「橫向擴展（scale-across）」網路定位為繼「向外擴展（scale-out）」之外的新興營收動能。所謂向外擴展，指的是叢集與叢集之間的互聯；橫向擴展——亦稱分散式運算網路——則是將地理上分散的設施相互連結，讓營運商得以將其視為統一的運算資源池。

  Ullal 表示，「電力、空間與運算資源長期供不應求」，正促使客戶將工作負載分散至既有的各地異質設施，而非集中於單一地點。目前需求主要集中在她所稱的「雲端與 AI 巨頭」以及快速崛起的新興雲端業者，企業端的採用則相對落後。

  ## 與輝達正面較量

  被問及 Arista 對輝達的競爭態勢時——輝達的 NVLink 專有互連技術長期主導「向上擴展（scale-up）」網路，即單一 GPU 叢集內的高速連結——Ullal 表示，在向外擴展與橫向擴展的應用場景中，Arista 的表現「優於」NVLink。她說，Arista 在向上擴展領域的目標，明確鎖定採用非輝達加速器的客群。

  她以 Google 的 TPU（張量處理器，專為機器學習工作負載設計的晶片）為例，指其已成為日益重要的訓練平台，並提及 Arista 合作夥伴旗下多款推論加速器，其中包括雲端客戶自行研發的客製晶片。Ullal 表示：「凡是採用非輝達產品的場合，Arista 都充滿熱忱、願意更深入合作，無論是向上擴展或向外擴展，甚至在某些情況下攜手打造搭載客製處理器的定製機架，以便針對客戶的推論或訓練引擎，更精準地調校網路行為。」

  Ullal 進一步以 InfiniBand 為前車之鑑——這種高速互連協定曾是資料中心網路的要角，最終被乙太網路取而代之——她認為向上擴展領域可能在兩到三年內上演類似的典範轉移。「我預期我們在那個領域會有更亮眼的表現，」她說。
