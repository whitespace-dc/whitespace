body_en: |
  Globe Telecom President and Chief Executive Officer Carl Raymond R. Cruz on Wednesday called for continued network investment and infrastructure co-building among Philippine telecommunications companies, rejecting consolidation as the primary answer to industry-wide financial pressure — a position that puts him at odds with PLDT Inc. and Smart Communications, Inc. Chairman Manuel V. Pangilinan, who last month said the current competitive landscape may be unsustainable.

  "We need to continue investing; otherwise, it's not only the industry but the country, we will get left behind," Cruz told reporters at the BusinessWorld Forecast 2027 forum in Manila. "It is not really consolidating but co-build where we can, and if there are no competition concerns, we should. We are competitors, not enemies."

  ## The consolidation debate

  Cruz's remarks were a direct response to Pangilinan's comments at PLDT's financial briefing earlier this month. Pangilinan argued that the financial strain facing each major carrier makes the status quo unsustainable. "This is untenable for each of us. We have to consolidate. Or one or two of us, we have to give up at some point," Pangilinan said.

  Cruz did not endorse merger activity but pointed to joint network development and infrastructure sharing as an alternative framework. He cited the Konektadong Pinoy Act — a law that lapsed into effect in 2025 and is designed to lower barriers to entry in the data transmission industry, promote competition, and facilitate infrastructure sharing and network expansion — as the legal vehicle for that approach. "I think the KP law opens the opportunity for that, in terms of co-build and sharing. So, that is an opportunity, obviously," he said.

  ## Industry financials

  The financial results of the Philippines' major carriers underscore why the consolidation question has surfaced. For the nine months ended September 2026, Globe's attributable net income declined 6.9% to P16.47 billion from P17.69 billion in the same period a year earlier, weighed down by higher net interest expenses and the absence of nonrecurring gains from tower sales booked in the prior year. Globe's third-quarter net income, however, rose on a quarterly basis.

  PLDT's third-quarter attributable net income fell 15.4% to P5.23 billion from P6.18 billion a year earlier. The company also expects its telecommunications core income to decline for the full year.

  Converge ICT Solutions, Inc. is maintaining its full-year revenue growth target of 4% to 6% despite an 11.9% decline in third-quarter attributable net income, citing sustained demand for data services. DITO CME Holdings Corp., which holds an indirect interest in DITO Telecommunity Corp., narrowed its third-quarter attributable net loss to P5.80 billion, but its nine-month attributable net loss widened to P17.09 billion from P9.65 billion a year earlier as expenses increased and revenues declined.

  Cruz acknowledged the pressure is real and broad. "If you take a look at the financials of all key players, there is pressure," he said. "So, for me, maybe too much and at the same time not enough. But it really depends on how the economy progresses."

  ## Capital requirements and government incentives

  Cruz framed the investment challenge as a macroeconomic problem as much as a competitive one. Telecommunications infrastructure requires sustained, large-scale capital expenditure, and he said government support — particularly for geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, known in the Philippines as GIDA zones — could ease the burden on carriers. "If we are able to share specifically with the government also helping out with incentives like GIDA, then that would be great for the industry," he said.

  ## Significance

  The divergence between Cruz and Pangilinan reflects a broader unresolved question for the Philippine telecommunications sector: whether co-investment frameworks enabled by the Konektadong Pinoy Act can generate enough capital efficiency to relieve financial pressure without the market-structure changes that consolidation would bring. Regulatory and competition-law constraints on consolidation were implicitly acknowledged by Cruz when he conditioned co-building on the absence of competition concerns.

  DITO CME's widening nine-month net loss of P17.09 billion, against a narrower quarterly figure, represents the most acute financial stress point among the major players heading into 2027.

  菲律賓Globe電信總裁兼執行長卡爾．雷蒙德．R．克魯茲（Carl Raymond R. Cruz）週三呼籲菲律賓各電信業者持續投資網路、推動基礎建設共建，明確拒絕以整併作為紓解全產業財務壓力的主要解方——此一立場與PLDT公司及智慧通訊（Smart Communications）董事長曼紐爾．V．潘吉里南（Manuel V. Pangilinan）相左。潘吉里南上個月曾表示，現行競爭態勢恐怕難以為繼。

  克魯茲在馬尼拉「BusinessWorld 2027展望論壇」接受媒體訪問時表示：「我們必須持續投資；否則，不只是這個產業，整個國家都將落後。」他補充說：「真正需要的，不是整併，而是在可行的地方共建；只要不涉及競爭疑慮，我們就應該這樣做。我們是競爭者，不是敵人。」

  ## 整併之爭

  克魯茲的言論是對潘吉里南本月稍早在PLDT財務說明會上發言的直接回應。潘吉里南認為，各大電信商所承受的財務壓力已使現狀難以為繼。他說：「對我們每一家而言，這都是難以持續的局面。我們必須整併，否則遲早會有一兩家不得不退出市場。」

  克魯茲並未認同合併收購的方向，而是提出聯合網路建設與基礎設施共享作為替代框架。他援引《互聯菲律賓法》（Konektadong Pinoy Act）——這部法律於2025年自動生效，旨在降低資料傳輸業的進入門檻、促進競爭、推動基礎設施共享與網路擴建——作為推動上述做法的法律依據。他表示：「我認為《互聯菲律賓法》在共建與共享方面開創了機會，這顯然是個契機。」

  ## 產業財務表現

  菲律賓主要電信業者的財務數字，正是整併議題浮上檯面的根本原因。截至2026年9月底止的前九個月，Globe歸屬股東的稅後淨利年減6.9%，從176.9億披索降至164.7億披索，主要受淨利息費用增加、以及前一年入帳的鐵塔出售非經常性收益消失所拖累。不過，Globe第三季淨利相較前一季仍呈現環比成長。

  PLDT第三季歸屬股東的稅後淨利年減15.4%，從61.8億披索降至52.3億披索，公司並預期全年電信核心收益將下滑。

  Converge ICT Solutions儘管第三季歸屬股東的稅後淨利年減11.9%，仍維持全年營收成長4%至6%的目標，理由是資料服務需求持續穩健。DITO CME控股公司（持有DITO Telecommunity間接股權）第三季歸屬股東的稅後淨損雖收窄至58億披索，但前九個月歸屬股東的稅後淨損因支出增加、營收下滑，從96.5億披索大幅擴大至170.9億披索。

  克魯茲承認，財務壓力確實廣泛存在。他說：「只要看看各大業者的財務數字，就可以看出壓力的存在。對我而言，或許是太多了，同時又不夠——但這真的取決於整體經濟的走向。」

  ## 資本需求與政府誘因

  克魯茲將投資挑戰定性為總體經濟問題，而非單純的競爭問題。電信基礎設施需要持續且大規模的資本支出，他表示，政府的支持——特別是針對菲律賓所謂「地理上偏遠弱勢地區」（GIDA，Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas）所提供的誘因——有助於減輕業者的負擔。他說：「如果能夠共建，同時政府也透過GIDA等誘因提供協助，那對整個產業來說將是一大助益。」

  ## 意義

  克魯茲與潘吉里南之間的分歧，折射出菲律賓電信業一個懸而未決的核心問題：《互聯菲律賓法》所賦予的共同投資框架，能否在不改變市場結構的前提下，創造足夠的資本效率以紓解財務壓力？克魯茲以「不涉及競爭疑慮」作為推動共建的前提條件，也間接承認了整併在法規與競爭法層面所面臨的限制。

  DITO CME前九個月淨損擴大至170.9億披索，儘管單季虧損已有所收窄，仍代表各主要業者中財務壓力最為嚴峻的一環，這股壓力正持續延伸至2027年。
