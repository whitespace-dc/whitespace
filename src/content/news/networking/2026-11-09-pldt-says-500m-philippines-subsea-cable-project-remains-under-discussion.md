body_en: |
  A proposed $500-million subsea fiber-optic network that would link the Philippine archipelago from Batanes to Palawan remains under discussion, with the three participating carriers still assessing build costs, investment returns, and downstream demand before committing, a senior PLDT executive said on Saturday.

  PLDT Senior Vice-President and Enterprise Business Head Patricio S. Pineda III, speaking to reporters on the sidelines of a forum, confirmed the project has not advanced to a final investment decision. "It is still being discussed," he said, adding that the fundamental case for the network had not weakened. "The Philippines is a punch above its weight in terms of usage from a digital standpoint. So, that will still be needed."

  The Philippine Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) disclosed in August that PLDT, Globe Telecom, and Converge ICT Solutions had jointly proposed a nationwide subsea fiber-optic network valued at roughly 30.63 billion Philippine pesos ($500 million). The plan calls for cables running from Batanes in the far north to Palawan in the southwest, with branches extending to the Visayas island group and Mindanao, the country's southern island.

  ## Why the decision is taking time

  Pineda identified return on investment as the central variable. "We just have to really figure out the build costs and the ability for that to recover. So, hopefully, the RoI figures do not get so much of a whack around," he said.

  Beyond construction economics, the partners are evaluating whether enterprise customers and government agencies will generate sufficient demand to justify the outlay. "The macro part is really much more around the demand, maybe more enterprise usage — like how soon are companies, public sector able to pull the trigger on their big projects and their spending," Pineda said.

  He acknowledged that broader economic conditions could cut either way. Uncertainty and softer growth tend to push companies toward spending restraint, he said, but clear business cases for digital transformation can still prompt investment even in a cautious environment. "When digital transformation and certain things become strong business cases, there's a lot of incentive as well to pull the trigger — so that you're ready when the economy begins to pick up," he said.

  ## VITRO REIT IPO pushed to 2027

  Separately, Pineda said PLDT remains prepared to list VITRO REIT, Inc. — the proposed real estate investment trust (REIT) vehicle for its data center assets — once market conditions improve. PLDT announced in October that it had deferred the IPO to 2027, citing prevailing market conditions and rising interest rates. Pineda said the earliest likely window is the first quarter of 2027.

  VITRO REIT is intended to hold assets belonging to VITRO, the data center business of PLDT subsidiary ePLDT, Inc. VITRO currently operates 11 data centers across the Philippines with a combined capacity of nearly 100 megawatts (MW) — enough to power a significant cluster of large commercial facilities.

  ## Hyperscaler interest and currency effects

  Despite the cautious tone on capital allocation, Pineda described enterprise demand, including from hyperscalers — large cloud providers such as the major U.S. and Asian technology platforms — as strong. "I think it is already in the year-around hyperscalers having interest here in the region. We are still very bullish," he said.

  The weakening Philippine peso presents a mixed picture for PLDT's enterprise segment, he added. Dollar-denominated revenues convert at a more favorable rate, boosting reported income, but the same currency dynamic raises the cost of imported connectivity and information and communications technology equipment. "The big part could be our projects, connectivity-wise and ICT where we are importing," he said.

  ## What it means for Philippine connectivity

  The proposed subsea network is designed to close a persistent infrastructure gap in an archipelago of more than 7,600 islands, where inter-island connectivity remains uneven and many communities rely on wireless links rather than fiber backhaul. A decision to proceed would represent one of the largest domestic telecommunications infrastructure investments in Philippine history. No timeline for a final investment decision was given.

  ## PLDT：菲律賓5億美元海底電纜計畫仍在洽談中

  一項由PLDT、Globe Telecom及Converge ICT Solutions三家電信業者共同提出、連結菲律賓群島各地的海底光纖網路計畫，目前仍在討論之中。三方業者正就建設成本、投資回報及下游需求進行評估，尚未作出最終投資承諾。PLDT一名高層主管於上週六表示。

  PLDT資深副總裁兼企業業務負責人Patricio S. Pineda III在一場論壇場外接受媒體採訪時，確認本計畫尚未進入最終投資決策階段。「目前仍在討論中，」他說，並補充表示這項網路建設的基本需求並未減弱。「菲律賓在數位應用方面的表現遠超其規模，因此這項建設仍有其必要性。」

  菲律賓資訊通訊技術部（DICT）於今年8月披露，PLDT、Globe Telecom與Converge ICT Solutions三家業者已聯合提議建設一套全國性海底光纖網路，估值約為306.3億菲律賓披索（約5億美元）。計畫路線從最北端的巴丹省（Batanes）延伸至西南端的巴拉望島（Palawan），並設有分支延伸至維薩亞斯群島（Visayas）及菲律賓南部大島民答那峨島（Mindanao）。

  ## 決策為何遲遲未定

  Pineda指出，投資回報率是核心變數。「我們必須真正釐清建設成本，以及能否回收投資。希望投資回報率的數字不要差太多，」他說。

  除了建設經濟效益之外，三方業者也在評估企業客戶與政府機關能否產生足夠需求，以支撐這筆龐大支出。「宏觀面的關鍵更在於需求——也許是更多企業端的使用，例如企業與公部門何時能夠在重大專案與支出上拍板定案，」Pineda說。

  他也坦言，整體經濟環境的影響可能正反兼具。不確定性與經濟成長趨緩往往促使企業縮減支出；但若數位轉型具備清晰的商業效益，即便在謹慎的環境下仍可能促成投資。「當數位轉型與特定事務形成強烈的商業論據，就會有很大的誘因去拍板——好讓自己在經濟回升時已做好準備，」他說。

  ## VITRO REIT上市延至2027年

  另外，Pineda表示，PLDT準備好在市場條件改善後，將旗下資料中心資產的不動產投資信託（REIT）載體VITRO REIT, Inc.掛牌上市。PLDT於10月宣布，以市場條件不佳及利率上升為由，將IPO推遲至2027年。Pineda表示，最早可能的時間窗口為2027年第一季。

  VITRO REIT預計持有PLDT子公司ePLDT旗下資料中心業務VITRO的資產。VITRO目前在菲律賓各地共運營11座資料中心，總容量接近100百萬瓦（MW），足以供應大型商業設施群集的用電需求。

  ## 超大規模雲端業者的興趣與匯率效應

  儘管在資本配置上態度審慎，Pineda仍形容企業端需求強勁，包括來自超大規模雲端業者——即美國及亞洲各大科技平台等大型雲端服務供應商——的詢問。「我認為超大規模業者對本地區的興趣已是全年持續不斷的常態，我們依然非常看好，」他說。

  他補充指出，菲律賓披索走軟對PLDT企業業務帶來喜憂參半的影響。以美元計價的收入在換算時可獲得較有利的匯率，拉高帳面收益；但同樣的匯率變動也推高了進口連網設備及資通訊（ICT）器材的採購成本。「影響最大的部分可能是我們的專案——包括連網建設與ICT方面的進口採購，」他說。

  ## 對菲律賓網路連線的意義

  這項擬議中的海底網路旨在填補菲律賓群島長期存在的基礎設施缺口。菲律賓由逾7,600座島嶼組成，島際連線至今仍參差不齊，許多地區仍依賴無線連線而非光纖回程網路。若決定推進，此計畫將是菲律賓史上規模最大的國內電信基礎設施投資之一。目前各方尚未給出最終投資決定的時程。
