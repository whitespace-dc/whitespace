body_en: |
  The European Union will provide an €11.1 million (US$12.2 million) grant to extend the Medusa Submarine Cable System's terrestrial reach from Port Said, Egypt, into Jordan, while the European Investment Bank (EIB) has committed a separate loan of up to €60 million (US$65.8 million) to fund the cable's second Mediterranean phase, according to a project press release.

  The two instruments address different parts of the same system. The EU grant finances the land-based link connecting Medusa's existing Egyptian landing station to Jordan. The EIB loan — a debt instrument rather than a grant — supports the broader second phase of the undersea cable across the Mediterranean region.

  ## What Medusa is and where it stands

  Medusa is an approximately 8,000-kilometer submarine cable system with landing stations at 21 points. It connects Portugal, Spain, France, Italy, Malta, Greece, and Cyprus on the northern shore of the Mediterranean to Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt, and Syria on the southern and eastern shores. Jordan will become the newest addition once the terrestrial extension is complete.

  The cable is engineered with 24 fiber pairs, each carrying a capacity of 20 terabits per second (Tbps), and is designed for a minimum operational life of 25 years. The first phase — linking France, Morocco, and Tunisia — is complete and already carrying traffic, the press release said. The eastern segment, which includes the connection to Egypt and the planned Jordanian extension, is targeted to be ready for service by 2027.

  AFR-IX Telecom, a telecommunications operator active in Portugal, the United States, and several African countries, also supports the project alongside the EIB.

  ## EU funding history for the project

  The new €11.1 million grant is not the EU's first financial commitment to Medusa. The bloc had previously allocated €40 million (US$43.9 million) through its Neighbourhood Investment Platform to build a dedicated research and education subnetwork running alongside the main commercial cable on both shores of the Mediterranean. The latest grant specifically addresses the Jordan extension and is separate from that earlier commitment.

  ## Research and education rationale

  Officials framed the Jordan extension primarily around academic and scientific connectivity. Medusa Chief Operating Officer Damien Bertrand said the system was conceived to bring the two shores of the Mediterranean closer through digital connectivity, and that the new EU support would enable universities and research institutions across Europe, North Africa, and Jordan to collaborate and access shared digital resources.

  EU Commissioner for the Mediterranean Dubravka Šuica cited SESAME — a particle physics research facility in Jordan — along with Jordanian universities as direct beneficiaries, saying faster connections would make it easier for researchers and scientists to share data and work on joint projects with European peers. Šuica also referenced potential commercial and public-service benefits for Jordan.

  ## Strategic framing

  The EU has positioned Medusa within its Global Gateway strategy, described in the press release as a framework for supporting sustainable and trusted infrastructure and building international partnerships. The project is also listed under the Pact for the Mediterranean.

  EIB Vice President Gelsomina Vigliotti said reliable digital connections were essential for innovation, economic resilience, and cooperation, and that the investment was intended to bring Europe and its Mediterranean partners closer together.

  ## What comes next

  With the first phase operational and the EIB loan in place for phase two, the eastern segment of Medusa — including the Egypt-to-Jordan terrestrial extension — is scheduled to reach commercial service by 2027.

  ## 歐盟撥款1.22億元　Medusa海底電纜從埃及向約旦延伸

  根據專案新聞稿，歐盟將提供1,110萬歐元（約1.22億美元）補助款，將Medusa海底電纜系統的陸上段從埃及塞得港延伸至約旦；歐洲投資銀行（EIB）則另行承諾提供最高6,000萬歐元（約6.58億美元）貸款，資助該海底電纜的地中海第二期工程。

  這兩項資金工具針對的是同一系統的不同部分。歐盟補助款用於興建連接Medusa現有埃及登陸站至約旦的陸上段鏈路；歐洲投資銀行的貸款則屬債務工具而非補助，支援的是跨地中海地區更廣泛的海底電纜第二期工程。

  ## Medusa電纜的規模與現況

  Medusa是一套全長約8,000公里的海底電纜系統，共設有21個登陸站。它將地中海北岸的葡萄牙、西班牙、法國、義大利、馬爾他、希臘及賽普勒斯，與南岸及東岸的摩洛哥、阿爾及利亞、突尼西亞、利比亞、埃及及敘利亞相互連接。陸上延伸段完工後，約旦將成為最新加入的落點。

  該電纜採用24對光纖設計，每對傳輸容量為每秒20兆位元（20 Tbps），設計最低使用壽命為25年。新聞稿指出，第一期工程——連接法國、摩洛哥及突尼西亞——已竣工並開始承載流量。包含埃及連接及計畫中的約旦延伸段在內的東段，預計於2027年正式投入服務。

  在歐洲投資銀行之外，活躍於葡萄牙、美國及多個非洲國家的電信業者AFR-IX Telecom也是本專案的支持夥伴。

  ## 歐盟對本專案的資助歷程

  此次1,110萬歐元補助款並非歐盟對Medusa的首次財務投入。歐盟此前已透過「鄰近地區投資平台」撥付4,000萬歐元（約4.39億美元），用於在地中海兩岸的主幹商用電纜旁建置一條專屬的研究與教育次網路。最新這筆補助款專門針對約旦延伸段，與先前的承諾互相獨立。

  ## 學術研究的核心考量

  官員們將此次約旦延伸工程的主要訴求定位於學術與科研連結。Medusa營運長達米安·貝特朗（Damien Bertrand）表示，這套系統的初衷是透過數位連結拉近地中海兩岸的距離，而歐盟最新的支持將使歐洲、北非及約旦各地的大學與研究機構得以協作並共享數位資源。

  負責地中海事務的歐盟委員杜布拉芙卡·蘇伊察（Dubravka Šuica）特別點名約旦的粒子物理研究設施SESAME以及當地各大學為直接受益方，並指出更快速的連線將有助於研究人員與科學家與歐洲夥伴共享資料、推動聯合計畫。蘇伊察也提及此一連線對約旦在商業及公共服務方面的潛在效益。

  ## 戰略定位

  歐盟將Medusa納入其「全球門戶」（Global Gateway）戰略框架，新聞稿將該戰略描述為支持可持續、可信賴基礎設施並建立國際夥伴關係的政策架構。本專案亦列於「地中海公約」（Pact for the Mediterranean）之下。

  歐洲投資銀行副行長潔索米娜·維里歐帝（Gelsomina Vigliotti）表示，可靠的數位連結對於創新、經濟韌性與合作至關重要，此項投資旨在拉近歐洲與地中海夥伴之間的距離。

  ## 後續展望

  隨著第一期工程正式上線、歐洲投資銀行的第二期貸款到位，Medusa東段——包括從埃及至約旦的陸上延伸段——預計將於2027年達到商業服務水準。
