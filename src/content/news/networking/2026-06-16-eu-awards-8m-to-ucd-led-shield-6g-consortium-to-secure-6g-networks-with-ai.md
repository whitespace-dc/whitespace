  University College Dublin (UCD) has been selected to lead Shield-6G, an €8 million European Union project to build artificial intelligence-driven cybersecurity into the foundations of 6G mobile networks before the first commercial deployments begin.

  Funded under the EU's Horizon Europe Smart Networks and Services Joint Undertaking (SNS JU), Shield-6G was the sole awardee under its Horizon Europe funding call. UCD will coordinate 19 international partners drawn from academic research institutions, multinational industrial companies, and specialised small and medium-sized enterprises.

  The European Commission has charged the consortium with establishing foundational security, reliability, and resilience guidelines for 6G networks. The project's core deliverable is an AI-driven cyber threat intelligence platform intended to move beyond basic network reliability toward what its architects describe as an "unprecedented" standard of systemic resilience.

  ## How the system is designed to work

  Shield-6G's proposed architecture combines automated zero-touch security orchestration — security responses that require no human intervention — with a suite of privacy-preserving analytics tools. Those tools include federated learning, a technique that trains AI models across distributed data without centralising it; secure multi-party computation; and differential privacy. Together, they are designed to allow multiple network operators and stakeholders to share threat intelligence and enable networks to self-heal against cascading vulnerabilities without transmitting raw, sensitive data between parties.

  That capability addresses a specific constraint in the European regulatory environment, where cross-border data flows between telecom operators face increasing legal scrutiny while those same operators must cooperate against shared infrastructure threats.

  "6G will be far more than the next step in mobile connectivity; it will form the intelligent digital nervous system of future society, connecting people, industries, critical infrastructure and autonomous systems," said Madhusanka Liyanage, director of UCD NetsLab and lead of the Shield-6G consortium. "Through Shield-6G, we are working to ensure that security, trust and resilience are embedded at the very foundation of this future."

  ## Standards work already under way at ETSI

  Shield-6G enters a standards environment already moving on AI's structural role in 6G. Ultan Mulligan, chief services officer at the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) — the body responsible for producing technical telecommunications standards across Europe — told Capacity earlier this year that AI will not simply be a feature layer added to 6G. "AI is going to have a very significant input, evolving every bit as fast as, if not faster than, 6G networks," Mulligan said. "It will very much be a driving force behind 6G."

  On security specifically, Mulligan said: "Assurance in the quality and performance of AI systems and security in the data — whether for training, telemetry, digital twins, or similar — is crucial." He added: "It's an absolutely key component that must be addressed from the beginning." ETSI published its first standard on cybersecurity for AI systems at the end of last year, and Mulligan indicated further standards would follow as both the threat landscape and the technology itself continue to evolve.

  ## The financial scale behind the security decisions

  The infrastructure that will depend on security architecture being defined now is projected to be substantial. Dell'Oro Group research published this month forecasts that cumulative 6G radio access network (RAN) revenue during the first six years of the commercial deployment cycle will exceed $100 billion, with wireless capital expenditure over the same period projected to exceed $500 billion.

  Those projections place the significance of pre-commercial security work such as Shield-6G in financial terms: the standards and architectural guidelines established before the first 6G networks go live will underpin more than half a trillion dollars in infrastructure spending over the technology's opening commercial phase.

  ETSI's Mulligan indicated that additional cybersecurity standards for AI systems are in development, with the pace of that work expected to track the evolution of both 6G networks and the AI systems embedded within them.

  ## 歐盟撥款800萬歐元　支持都柏林大學學院領軍Shield-6G聯盟以AI鞏固6G網路資安

  愛爾蘭都柏林大學學院（University College Dublin，UCD）獲選主導Shield-6G計畫，這是一項總經費達800萬歐元的歐盟專案，旨在6G行動網路首批商業部署啟動之前，便將人工智慧驅動的資安防護機制深植於網路基礎架構之中。

  Shield-6G由歐盟「地平線歐洲」計畫下的「智慧網路與服務聯合事業」（SNS JU）提供資金，是該期地平線歐洲徵件中唯一的得獎計畫。都柏林大學學院將統籌協調19個來自不同背景的國際夥伴，涵蓋學術研究機構、跨國工業企業，以及專業中小型企業。

  歐盟委員會賦予該聯盟一項核心任務：為6G網路建立奠基性的安全、可靠度與韌性指導方針。計畫的主要成果是一套AI驅動的網路威脅情資平台，設計目標超越基本的網路可靠度，要打造出計畫架構師所形容的「前所未有」的系統性韌性標準。

  ## 系統設計架構

  Shield-6G提出的架構，結合了「無需人工介入即可自動執行資安應對」的零接觸資安協調機制，以及一套保護隱私的分析工具組。這些工具包括：聯合學習（federated learning）——一種在分散式資料環境中訓練AI模型、無需集中資料的技術；安全多方運算；以及差分隱私。這些工具合力設計，讓多個網路營運商與利害關係人得以共享威脅情資，並使網路能夠在不傳輸原始敏感資料的前提下，自我修復以抵禦連鎖漏洞。

  這項能力正是針對歐洲監管環境中的一項特殊限制而設計——在歐洲，電信營運商之間的跨境資料流動正面臨日益嚴格的法律審查，而這些營運商又必須合力應對共同的基礎設施威脅。

  「6G的意義將遠超行動連線的下一個世代；它將成為未來社會的智慧數位神經系統，連結人類、產業、關鍵基礎設施與自主系統，」都柏林大學學院NetsLab主任、Shield-6G聯盟主持人Madhusanka Liyanage表示。「透過Shield-6G，我們致力確保安全、信任與韌性在這個未來的最底層便已深深紮根。」

  ## ETSI的標準制定工作已然展開

  Shield-6G進入的是一個已開始積極推進AI在6G架構性角色標準化的環境。歐洲電信標準協會（ETSI）——負責制定歐洲全域電信技術標準的機構——首席服務長Ultan Mulligan今年稍早接受《Capacity》雜誌訪問時表示，AI在6G中不僅僅是附加的功能層。「AI將發揮極為深遠的影響，其演進速度不亞於——甚至可能快過——6G網路本身，」Mulligan說。「它將是推動6G發展的核心力量之一。」

  在資安面向，Mulligan指出：「對AI系統品質與效能的保證，以及對資料的安全保障——無論是用於訓練、遙測、數位孿生或類似用途——都至關重要。」他補充道：「這是一個必須從一開始就正視的絕對關鍵要素。」ETSI已於去年底發布了首項針對AI系統資安的標準，Mulligan也表示，隨著威脅環境與技術本身持續演進，後續還將陸續推出更多標準。

  ## 資安決策背後的龐大財務規模

  目前正在制定的資安架構所要守護的基礎設施，其規模預計將相當可觀。Dell'Oro Group本月發布的研究預測，在6G商業部署週期的頭六年，累計6G無線接取網路（RAN）營收將超過1,000億美元，同期無線資本支出則預計超過5,000億美元。

  這些預測數字，讓Shield-6G等商轉前資安工作的重要性得以具體呈現：在6G網路正式上線之前所確立的標準與架構指導方針，將支撐這項技術在開放商業階段超過5兆新台幣（逾5,000億美元）的基礎設施投資。

  ETSI的Mulligan也表示，針對AI系統的額外資安標準目前正在研擬之中，相關工作的推進步調，預計將配合6G網路與其中所嵌入AI系統的演進同步加速。
