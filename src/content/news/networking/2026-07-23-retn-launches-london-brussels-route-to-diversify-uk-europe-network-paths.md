body_en: |
  ## New route and infrastructure

  REDN, the independent global network service provider, has launched a London-Brussels internet route designed to reduce UK dependence on the Amsterdam and Paris transit hubs through which the majority of the country's international traffic currently flows.

  The route is built on newly constructed Rotterdam-Brussels dark fibre, which RETN said will form the foundation of its own dense wavelength-division multiplexing (DWDM) network in Belgium. DWDM is a technology that multiplies the capacity of a single fibre cable by carrying multiple data signals simultaneously on different wavelengths of light. Owning that underlying infrastructure gives RETN direct operational control over the Belgian segment of the path.

  ## Physical diversity inside London

  Within London, the route uses a distinct exit path that avoids the Docklands egress corridor — the heavily trafficked east London passage that serves the data centre clusters concentrated in that area. The route also departs from a different landing station than RETN's previously announced low-latency CrossChannel route, which exits from Slough. That combination means UK traffic can use the new Brussels path without sharing physical infrastructure with either of RETN's existing major exit points.

  ## Why the route was built

  REDN said the investment responds to the concentration of international traffic across a limited number of established UK-Europe routes, and to growing industry awareness of vulnerabilities affecting both subsea cable systems and terrestrial fibre infrastructure. By routing through Brussels instead of Amsterdam or Paris, the company said customers gain an alternative path that improves service continuity when congestion or outages affect the traditional transit points.

  Tony O'Sullivan, RETN's chief executive, said in a statement: "Building resilient networks is no longer simply about adding capacity — it's about creating genuine physical diversity. Customers need unique alternatives that strengthen business continuity."

  ## Significance

  The launch adds a third distinct physical corridor for RETN's UK-to-continental-Europe traffic, sitting alongside its existing routes through the established northern European hubs. For the wider market, the move reflects a pattern in which carriers are investing in geographic route diversity rather than simply adding bandwidth on existing paths, in response to heightened concern about the concentration of critical traffic across a small number of cable systems and landing stations.

  ## 新路由與基礎建設

  身為獨立全球網路服務供應商的RETN，推出了倫敦─布魯塞爾網際網路路由，旨在降低英國對阿姆斯特丹與巴黎兩大轉接樞紐的依賴。目前，英國絕大多數的國際流量均流經這兩個節點。

  新路由以全新建設的鹿特丹─布魯塞爾暗光纖為骨幹。RETN表示，這段暗光纖將成為其在比利時建設自有密集波長分波多工（DWDM）網路的根基。DWDM是一種技術，能讓單條光纖電纜透過不同光波長同時承載多個資料訊號，從而成倍提升傳輸容量。掌握這段底層基礎設施，使RETN得以對路徑中的比利時路段擁有直接的營運控制權。

  ## 倫敦境內的實體多元性

  在倫敦境內，新路由採用獨特的出口路徑，刻意繞開碼頭區（Docklands）出口走廊。碼頭區是東倫敦流量極為繁忙的通道，服務著當地密集的資料中心聚落。此外，新路由與RETN先前宣布的低延遲跨英吉利海峽路由採用不同的登陸站；後者從斯勞（Slough）出發。兩項差異加總，意味著英國流量可透過這條新的布魯塞爾路徑傳輸，完全不必與RETN現有兩條主要出口路徑共用任何實體基礎設施。

  ## 建設這條路由的原因

  RETN表示，此次投資是對兩個現象的回應：一是英國國際流量高度集中於少數幾條成熟英歐路由；二是業界對海底電纜系統與陸地光纖基礎設施脆弱性的關注持續升溫。該公司指出，改道經由布魯塞爾而非阿姆斯特丹或巴黎，客戶便擁有一條替代路徑，當傳統轉接點發生壅塞或中斷時，有助於維持服務不間斷。

  RETN執行長Tony O'Sullivan發表聲明表示：「建設具韌性的網路，已不再只是增加頻寬這麼簡單，而是要創造真正的實體多元性。客戶需要真正獨特的替代方案，以強化業務持續性。」

  ## 重要意義

  此次路由開通，為RETN的英國至歐洲大陸流量新增第三條獨立的實體走廊，與其現有途經北歐各主要樞紐的路由並列。就更廣泛的市場意義而言，此舉反映出電信業者正朝地理路由多元化方向投資的趨勢，不再只是在現有路徑上堆疊頻寬。這一現象的背景，是業界對關鍵流量過度集中於少數電纜系統與登陸站的隱憂日益加深。
