  ## FLAG Brings Chennai-Singapore Subsea Route Online This Month

  Subsea cable operator FLAG is preparing to enter commercial service on its Chennai-to-Singapore route this month, delivering a new direct undersea connection between India's southeastern coast and Southeast Asia's dominant internet exchange hub.

  ## Why This Route Matters for Data Center Planners

  - **South Asia–Southeast Asia corridor:** Chennai is India's primary subsea cable landing hub on the Bay of Bengal coast, while Singapore anchors regional internet exchange and hyperscale colocation. A direct FLAG route between the two tightens latency and diversifies path options for operators routing traffic between Indian data centers and Singapore-based cloud or exchange infrastructure.
  - **Redundancy and path diversity:** New route additions on high-traffic corridors reduce single-point-of-failure risk. Planners evaluating Chennai or Singapore colocation should factor this additional cable into resilience calculations alongside existing systems on the same corridor.
  - **Carrier and wholesale capacity:** FLAG has historically served as a carrier's carrier, meaning capacity on this route is likely to flow through to enterprise and hyperscale customers via transit and wavelength agreements rather than direct retail sales.

  ## Planning Implications

  For teams sizing connectivity budgets or evaluating latency SLAs between Indian operations and Singapore-based infrastructure, the FLAG Chennai-Singapore route enters as a new competitive option this month. Procurement teams should engage FLAG and their carrier partners to assess pricing and available capacity windows before end-of-quarter planning cycles close.
