body_en: |
  DE-CIX Chief Executive Officer Ivo Ivanov has warned that connectivity, not computing power, will be the principal obstacle for space-based data centres, arguing the industry has underestimated the difficulty of building predictable, resilient links between orbital infrastructure and terrestrial networks.

  Ivanov made the assessment as technology companies and space organisations examine whether placing AI data centre infrastructure in orbit could relieve pressure on terrestrial power supplies, cooling systems, and land availability. While he acknowledged those drivers are real, he said the debate has concentrated too narrowly on the compute side of the equation.

  "What's striking, however, is that much of the mainstream debate still focuses on compute rather than connectivity. AI doesn't create value in a vacuum. Every model, application, and agent depends on the constant exchange of data between users, enterprises, cloud platforms, and other AI systems. Moving compute into orbit may help address power and cooling challenges, but it doesn't remove the need for fast, resilient, and predictable networking. In fact, it makes it all the more vital," Ivanov said.

  ## What is drawing industry attention to orbital infrastructure

  Ivanov said demand for AI compute has renewed interest in space-based infrastructure because orbit could offer access to continuous solar power, natural radiative cooling, and room to scale beyond physical constraints faced on Earth. He noted the discussion has moved from speculation to active planning among major technology firms.

  "SpaceX's projected record-breaking IPO has obviously dominated headlines, but it's also brought one of the technology industry's most ambitious ideas into mainstream conversation — putting AI data centers into orbit," Ivanov said. "Whether those ambitions are realized tomorrow or years from now, the idea has already graduated from sci-fi fantasy to real infrastructure planning."

  He pointed to existing activity in the sector. Google's Project Suncatcher has been exploring space-based compute and energy generation. Amazon already operates satellite-to-cloud ground station infrastructure designed to connect orbital assets with terrestrial cloud environments. The European Space Agency (ESA) is backing the OFELIAS project, which brings together organisations including the German Aerospace Centre and DE-CIX to develop low-latency, laser-based satellite communications.

  ## The connectivity problem Ivanov identifies

  Ivanov's central argument is that lifting servers into orbit addresses only part of the infrastructure challenge. AI systems depend on continuous data exchange between applications, enterprises, edge locations, cloud platforms, and other AI models. That traffic pattern means network performance is a core requirement, not a secondary concern, he said.

  His assessment is that the commercial case for orbital data centres will rest not on the ability to launch hardware, but on whether the industry can integrate space-based compute into the broader digital network in a way that is transparent to users and applications.

  "Getting compute into orbit is one thing, but weaving together data centers on Earth, cloud platforms, edge locations, satellites, and eventually orbital compute into a single digital ecosystem capable of supporting AI at planetary scale is another thing entirely," Ivanov said.

  ## Laser links: capabilities and unresolved limits

  Ivanov addressed optical and laser-based communications, which are widely discussed as a means to achieve the high-capacity links that space-to-ground data transfer would require. Laser links can support far greater bandwidth than traditional radio communications, but they face environmental and operational constraints that have not been resolved.

  Cloud cover, atmospheric turbulence, and the complexity of handovers between satellites and ground stations remain open problems for operators building dependable connections. Those issues matter directly for proposed orbital data centre models, which assume a continuous flow of data between space-based processing and ground-based users and systems.

  "Elon Musk himself has noted that orbital infrastructure is only a few milliseconds away, but latency is only part of the equation. Predictability and resilience matter just as much. Optical and laser-based links are far more preferable to radio communications and can deliver enormous bandwidth, but they have to contend with cloud cover, atmospheric turbulence, and the complexities of satellite-to-ground handovers, which is why the ESA's OFELIAS initiative and others like it are so important," Ivanov said.

  ## What the assessment means for the industry

  Ivanov's remarks frame ground-based network infrastructure as a prerequisite rather than an afterthought for any commercially viable orbital data centre. The position implies that investment in interconnection and terrestrial network architecture will need to keep pace with, or precede, investment in space-based compute if AI workloads are to be served reliably from orbit.

  DE-CIX, as a major internet exchange point operator, is a direct participant in the ESA's OFELIAS project to develop the laser-based satellite communications that Ivanov identified as essential to solving the ground link problem. The project involves the German Aerospace Centre alongside DE-CIX.

  ## DE-CIX執行長：軌道資料中心最大瓶頸在網路，不在運算

  DE-CIX執行長伊沃·伊萬諾夫（Ivo Ivanov）警告，對於以太空為基地的資料中心而言，網路連線能力——而非運算效能——才是最根本的障礙。他認為，業界嚴重低估了在軌道基礎設施與地面網路之間打造可預期、具高韌性連線的難度。

  伊萬諾夫提出上述判斷之際，科技公司與太空機構正積極評估將人工智慧資料中心基礎設施部署至軌道的可行性，藉此紓解地面電力供應、冷卻系統與土地取得方面的壓力。他雖承認上述驅動因素確實存在，但指出目前的討論過度聚焦於運算這一端，視野失之偏狹。

  「然而令人矚目的是，主流討論至今仍把焦點放在運算，而非連線能力。AI並非在真空中創造價值——每一個模型、應用程式與智慧代理，都仰賴使用者、企業、雲端平台與其他AI系統之間持續不斷的資料交換。把運算移到軌道上，或許有助於解決電力與冷卻的挑戰，卻無法消除對快速、具韌性且可預期的網路的需求。事實上，這反而讓網路變得更加不可或缺，」伊萬諾夫說。

  ## 軌道基礎設施為何吸引業界目光

  伊萬諾夫表示，AI運算需求的爆發，讓業界重新對太空基礎設施燃起興趣，因為軌道環境可提供持續的太陽能、天然輻射冷卻，以及地球上難以企及的擴展空間。他指出，相關討論已從天馬行空的推測，演進為大型科技公司之間的實質規劃。

  「SpaceX預計創紀錄的IPO顯然主導了各方頭條，但這也讓科技業最宏大的構想之一——將AI資料中心送上軌道——進入了主流視野，」伊萬諾夫說。「不論這些抱負明天就能實現，還是需要數年時間，這個想法已然從科幻小說的幻想，升格為真實的基礎設施規劃。」

  他援引業界現有的具體行動：Google的「Project Suncatcher」正在探索太空運算與太空能源生產；Amazon已建置衛星對雲端的地面站基礎設施，旨在將軌道資產與地面雲端環境相互串聯；歐洲太空總署（ESA）則支持OFELIAS計畫，該計畫匯集了德國航太中心與DE-CIX等機構，共同研發低延遲的雷射衛星通訊技術。

  ## 伊萬諾夫所指出的網路連線問題

  伊萬諾夫的核心論點是：將伺服器送上軌道，只解決了基礎設施挑戰的一部分。AI系統仰賴應用程式、企業、邊緣位置、雲端平台與其他AI模型之間持續不斷的資料交換。他說，這種流量模式意味著網路效能是核心需求，而非次要考量。

  他的判斷是：軌道資料中心的商業可行性，並不取決於能否把硬體送上太空，而在於業界能否將太空運算無縫整合進更廣泛的數位網路，讓使用者與應用程式完全感知不到其中的差異。

  「把運算送上軌道是一回事，但要把地面資料中心、雲端平台、邊緣位置、衛星，乃至最終的軌道運算，編織成一個能夠在行星尺度上支撐AI運作的單一數位生態系，則是截然不同的另一回事，」伊萬諾夫說。

  ## 雷射連線的能力與尚待解決的限制

  伊萬諾夫針對光學與雷射通訊技術進行了深入探討。這類技術被廣泛視為實現太空對地面大容量資料傳輸的可行途徑。雷射連線所能支援的頻寬，遠超傳統無線電通訊，但在環境條件與操作層面，仍存在一些迄今未解的限制。

  雲層覆蓋、大氣湍流，以及衛星與地面站之間交接（handover）的複雜性，對於致力建立可靠連線的業者而言，依然是懸而未決的難題。這些問題直接影響所謂軌道資料中心的發展構想——因為該構想的前提，是太空運算與地面使用者及系統之間必須存在持續不斷的資料流動。

  「伊隆·馬斯克本人曾指出，軌道基礎設施距我們只有幾毫秒之遙，但延遲只是方程式的一部分。可預期性與韌性同樣至關重要。光學與雷射連線遠比無線電通訊更為理想，能夠提供龐大的頻寬，但它們必須應對雲層覆蓋、大氣湍流，以及衛星對地面交接的複雜性，這正是ESA的OFELIAS計畫與其他類似倡議如此重要的原因，」伊萬諾夫說。

  ## 這項評估對業界的意涵

  伊萬諾夫的論述，將地面網路基礎設施定位為軌道資料中心商業化的前提條件，而非事後才需考慮的附加項目。這一立場隱含的意義是：若要讓AI工作負載能夠可靠地從軌道上提供服務，對互連與地面網路架構的投資，必須與對太空運算的投資同步推進，甚至應當先行。

  DE-CIX身為重要的網際網路交換點（IXP）營運商，本身便是ESA的OFELIAS計畫的直接參與者，致力於研發伊萬諾夫所指出的雷射衛星通訊技術——他認為這正是解決地面連線問題的關鍵所在。該計畫的參與機構包括德國航太中心與DE-CIX。
