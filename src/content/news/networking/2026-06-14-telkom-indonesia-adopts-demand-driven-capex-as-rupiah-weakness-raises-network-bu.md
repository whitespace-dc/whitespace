  ## Telkom Indonesia adopts demand-driven capex as rupiah weakness raises network build costs

  PT Telkom Indonesia (Persero) Tbk. (ticker: TLKM) says it will apply disciplined, demand-driven capital expenditure (capex) and make greater use of existing infrastructure to manage rising construction costs driven by a weakening rupiah, while Indonesia's telecommunications network providers' association warned that imported equipment prices could rise by as much as 12 percent.

  Andri Herawan Sasoko, Telkom's vice president of corporate communication, said the state-controlled digital telecoms company monitors global conditions continuously, including the impact on the exchange rate and investment costs. "The impact can still be managed through mature investment planning and prudent investment management," Andri told Bisnis.com on Friday, June 12, 2026.

  ## Telkom's four-part response

  Telkom outlined four operational responses to currency-driven cost pressure. First, the company will prioritise network construction in areas with high demand, growth potential, and economic value. Second, it will optimise the use of existing infrastructure to improve investment efficiency rather than defaulting to new builds. Third, it will strengthen synergies and collaboration within the Telkom group of companies to maintain cost effectiveness. Fourth, it will manage investment selectively and in a measured way — including gradual adjustments to project timelines where necessary — without changing the company's strategic direction.

  Andri said Telkom is also maintaining healthy cash flow by applying a total expenditure (totex) efficiency strategy, an approach that covers both capital and operating spending, as part of its TLKM 30 transformation agenda.

  ## What is driving costs up

  The rupiah's weakness against the US dollar has raised the price of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) — the plastic sheeting used to protect underground cables — as well as a broad range of other imported inputs. Jerry Mangasas Swandy, chairman of Apjatel (Asosiasi Penyelenggara Jaringan Telekomunikasi, Indonesia's association of telecommunications network providers), said the depreciation is a concern for the entire information technology and telecoms industry because most network equipment and IT hardware sold in Indonesia still relies on imported components or subassemblies.

  Jerry said that over the past year equipment prices had stabilised after the global semiconductor shortage eased, but that currency weakness now risks reversing that improvement by pushing up import costs.

  ## Equipment categories at risk

  Apjatel identified two broad categories of equipment facing potential price increases.

  For general IT hardware, the affected items include:
  - Laptops and personal computers (PCs) built around imported components
  - Servers and data storage devices
  - Network equipment such as routers, switches, and firewalls
  - Supporting components including chipsets, memory modules, power supply units (PSUs), and peripherals

  Apjatel said industry participants estimate price increases in this segment at 5 to 10 percent, depending on how long the rupiah remains under pressure and on individual vendor procurement contracts.

  For the telecommunications sector specifically, where Apjatel said import dependence is even higher, the affected components include fiber optic (FO) cables and their accessories — patch cords, splitters, closures, and drop cores — as well as active network equipment such as optical line terminals (OLTs, the provider-side device that connects a fiber network to the internet backbone), optical network terminals and units (ONTs/ONUs, the customer-premises equipment that receives the fiber signal), optical distribution frames (ODFs), and optical distribution cabinets (ODCs). Metro and transport network equipment and other technical gear are also exposed. Apjatel estimated price adjustments in the telecom segment at 5 to 12 percent, depending on vendor, country of origin, and payment method.

  ## Labour costs and indirect pressures

  Jerry noted that labour costs for network construction — including fibre rollouts, maintenance, and customer installations — are not directly tied to the exchange rate. However, he said contractors and service providers frequently adjust their rates in practice because the tools and equipment they use are largely imported, because replacement equipment becomes more expensive, and because overall operating cost structures shift with exchange rate moves. "This could drive up total project costs, even though the proportion is smaller than the increase on equipment," he said.

  ## Industry mitigation measures

  Apjatel said the sector should respond to rupiah weakness through several measures: optimising inventory levels and procurement planning to reduce exposure to short-term price swings; negotiating with global vendors for more stable payment arrangements; increasing the use of domestically produced components that meet quality standards; and collaborating with the Indonesian government to maintain a healthy industry ecosystem amid exchange rate pressure.

  "Apjatel also encourages all members to maintain service quality and ensure telecommunications infrastructure development continues, because connectivity is a basic societal need and the foundation of the digital economy," Jerry said.

  ## Significance

  The combined picture from Telkom and Apjatel indicates that Indonesia's telecoms industry faces a structural cost challenge: the networks underpinning the country's digital economy are built largely from dollar-denominated imports, making domestic expansion plans sensitive to exchange rate movements that Indonesian operators cannot control. Telkom's response — prioritising high-value areas, extending the life of existing assets, and adjusting project timelines incrementally — signals that network rollout schedules may slow in lower-priority areas if the rupiah remains weak, even as the company maintains that its strategic direction is unchanged.

  Apjatel said it is encouraging members to maintain infrastructure build-out and service quality regardless of cost pressures, framing connectivity as a foundational economic requirement.

  ## 印尼電信採需求導向資本支出策略 應對盧比貶值推高建網成本

  PT Telkom Indonesia（Persero）Tbk.（股票代號：TLKM）表示，將採行紀律嚴明的需求導向資本支出（capex）策略，並更積極運用既有基礎設施管控建網成本的上漲壓力——這波壓力源於盧比持續走弱。與此同時，印尼電信網路業者協會也警告，進口設備價格漲幅最高可能達12%。

  印尼電信企業傳播副總裁 Andri Herawan Sasoko 表示，這家國有數位電信公司持續監測全球情勢，包括匯率波動對投資成本的影響。「透過成熟的投資規劃與審慎的投資管理，這些衝擊仍可有效控制，」Andri 於2026年6月12日（週五）接受《Bisnis.com》採訪時表示。

  ## 印尼電信的四大因應對策

  印尼電信針對匯率波動引發的成本壓力，提出四項營運面的因應措施。其一，優先在需求旺盛、成長潛力高、具備經濟價值的地區推進網路建設。其二，優化既有基礎設施的使用效率，以提升投資效益，而非一味新建。其三，強化集團內各子公司之間的協同合作，維持成本競爭力。其四，有選擇性且審慎地管控投資，視情況對部分專案時程做出漸進式調整，但不改變公司整體策略方向。

  Andri 表示，印尼電信亦透過推行「整體支出（totex）效率策略」維持健全的現金流——此一策略涵蓋資本支出與營運支出兩個面向，是公司「TLKM 30」轉型議程的重要一環。

  ## 成本上漲的根本原因

  盧比兌美元匯率走弱，已推升高密度聚乙烯（HDPE）的進口價格——HDPE 是一種用於保護地下電纜的塑膠材料——以及其他多種進口原物料的成本。印尼電信網路業者協會（Apjatel，Asosiasi Penyelenggara Jaringan Telekomunikasi）主席 Jerry Mangasas Swandy 表示，匯率貶值令整個資訊科技與電信產業感到憂慮，因為印尼市場上銷售的大多數網路設備與IT硬體，至今仍高度依賴進口零組件或半成品。

  Jerry 指出，過去一年間，隨著全球半導體短缺問題緩解，設備價格已逐漸趨穩；然而，盧比走弱如今恐將拉高進口成本，使這段時期的改善成果付諸東流。

  ## 面臨漲價風險的設備類別

  Apjatel 將可能受到價格衝擊的設備分為兩大類。

  就一般IT硬體而言，受影響的品項包括：
  - 採用進口零組件的筆記型電腦與個人電腦（PC）
  - 伺服器與資料儲存裝置
  - 路由器、交換器與防火牆等網路設備
  - 晶片組、記憶體模組、電源供應器（PSU）及週邊設備等支援性零組件

  Apjatel 表示，業界估計此類設備的漲幅約為5%至10%，實際幅度視盧比貶值持續時間以及各供應商的採購合約條款而定。

  就電信產業而言，Apjatel 指出該領域對進口的依賴程度更高，受影響的零組件涵蓋：光纖（FO）電纜及其配件——包括跳接線（patch cord）、分光器（splitter）、光纖接頭盒（closure）與入戶光纖（drop core）——以及光線路終端（OLT，設置於業者端、負責將光纖網路接入網際網路骨幹的設備）、光網路終端與光網路單元（ONT／ONU，設置於用戶端、負責接收光纖訊號的設備）、光纖配線架（ODF）與光纖配線箱（ODC）等主動式網路設備。都會網路與傳輸網路設備及其他技術性器材同樣面臨風險。Apjatel 估計電信設備的價格調幅約在5%至12%之間，依供應商、原產國及付款方式不同而有所差異。

  ## 人工成本與間接壓力

  Jerry 指出，網路工程的人工成本——包括光纖佈建、維運及用戶裝機作業——並非直接與匯率掛鉤。然而他表示，承包商與服務業者在實務上往往仍會調漲報價，原因在於他們所使用的工具與設備大多為進口品、替換設備的成本隨之上升，且整體營運成本結構也會因匯率變動而發生位移。「這可能推高整體專案成本，儘管其佔比小於設備漲價的部分，」他說。

  ## 業界的因應措施

  Apjatel 表示，面對盧比走弱，業界應從多個面向積極因應：優化庫存水位與採購規劃，降低對短期價格波動的暴露；與全球供應商協商更穩定的付款安排；提高符合品質標準的國產零組件使用比例；並與印尼政府協作，在匯率承壓期間維護健全的產業生態。

  「Apjatel 也鼓勵所有會員維持服務品質，確保電信基礎設施建設持續推進，因為連網是社會的基本需求，也是數位經濟的根基，」Jerry 說。

  ## 整體意義

  綜合印尼電信與Apjatel的訊息，印尼電信產業正面臨一項結構性成本挑戰：支撐全國數位經濟的網路基礎設施，絕大部分仰賴以美元計價的進口設備，使得國內擴網計畫對匯率波動高度敏感，而這恰恰是印尼業者無從掌控的外部因素。印尼電信的因應策略——優先布局高價值地區、延長既有資產使用年限、漸進式調整專案時程——已隱含一個訊號：若盧比持續疲弱，低優先順序地區的網路建設進度恐將放緩，即便公司對外強調整體策略方向不變。

  Apjatel 則表示，將持續鼓勵會員在成本壓力下仍堅持推進基礎設施建設、維護服務品質，並將連網能力定位為經濟發展不可或缺的基礎條件。
