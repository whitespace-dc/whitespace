body_en: |
  Four days after a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck Colombia on August 10, carrier Claro says 80 percent of its base stations and all fiber-optic nodes across the affected departments are operating normally, while rival Tigo continues stabilization efforts in a network environment still constrained by power outages and limited infrastructure access, according to reporting by Infobae cited by Data Center Dynamics.

  The hardest-hit departments are Chocó, Caldas, Quindío, Risaralda, and Valle del Cauca. Both Claro and Tigo have seen a decline in outage reports on the public monitoring platform Downdetector since the earthquake.

  ## Claro's recovery timeline

  Claro said its technical teams restored nearly half of all affected base stations within the first three days following the quake, attributing the pace to contingency protocols activated immediately after the event. By August 14, all fiber-optic nodes in the affected areas had returned to normal operation alongside 80 percent of base stations.

  To compensate for the loss of grid electricity — a primary obstacle to keeping network equipment running — Claro deployed 26 diesel power generators across three cities: Quibdó, the capital of Chocó department; Pereira in Risaralda; and Cali in Valle del Cauca. The generators are keeping backup systems operational at sites where commercial power remains unavailable.

  ## Tigo's ongoing stabilization work

  Tigo said its technical teams are conducting continuous monitoring and working to address three overlapping problems: power outages, physical damage to infrastructure, and a surge in network traffic driven by high demand for communications in the earthquake's aftermath. The carrier did not report a specific restoration percentage.

  Downdetector data for Tigo shows a scattered distribution of outage reports without the marked peaks seen on competing networks, which the source describes as consistent with gradual network stabilization, though isolated disruptions persist.

  ## Remaining obstacles

  Despite the progress, operators have identified three external factors that are slowing full service restoration: prolonged commercial power outages in some areas, difficulty reaching sites with damaged infrastructure, and constraints on fuel supplies needed to run the diesel generators keeping network nodes active. These conditions continue to limit operation of base stations and network nodes in certain locations.

  ## Outage profile by carrier

  Downdetector data collected through August 13 shows the distribution of reported service problems for each carrier:

  - **Claro:** broadband internet outages (46%), WiFi outages (27%), mobile internet outages (16%). Incidents peaked between 6:00 pm and 10:00 pm on August 12.
  - **Movistar:** broadband internet (30%), mobile signal (27%), mobile internet (26%), with higher outage concentration between 3:00 pm and 9:00 pm on August 13.
  - **Tigo:** mobile internet (31%), mobile signal (24%), broadband internet (22%).

  ## Government coordination and emergency data access

  Coordination with Colombian government agencies has been central to prioritizing repairs, the carriers said. Claro and Tigo have maintained direct communication with local authorities to identify critical areas and relay updates through official channels, which they said has allowed them to allocate technical resources more efficiently.

  In parallel, users of Claro, Tigo, Wom, and satellite provider Starlink in the five affected departments received free mobile data following the earthquake. Starlink also activated free service access across the affected areas and issued credits to allow users to reactivate lapsed subscriptions.

  ## What this means for affected regions

  The pace of recovery from a major seismic event carries direct consequences for emergency response, economic activity, and public safety coordination in the affected departments. The continued reliance on diesel generators to sustain network nodes underscores how closely telecommunications resilience in the region is tied to grid stability — and how quickly power infrastructure failures can cascade into connectivity outages. Full restoration remains contingent on resolving commercial electricity supply, clearing access routes to damaged sites, and maintaining fuel logistics for backup generation.

  ## 哥倫比亞地震四天後　Claro宣布八成基地台恢復運作

  8月10日規模7.4強震重創哥倫比亞後第四天，電信業者Claro宣布，受災各省八成基地台及全部光纖節點已恢復正常運作；競爭對手Tigo則持續展開網路穩定作業，在停電頻仍、基礎設施通道受限的環境下艱難應對。上述消息由Infobae報導，經Data Center Dynamics引述。

  此次受災最嚴重的省份包括喬科省（Chocó）、卡爾達斯省（Caldas）、金迪奧省（Quindío）、里薩拉爾達省（Risaralda）及考卡山谷省（Valle del Cauca）。地震發生後，Claro與Tigo在公開監測平台Downdetector上的故障通報數量均出現下滑趨勢。

  ## Claro的恢復時間軸

  Claro表示，地震發生後頭三天內，技術團隊已搶通近半數受影響基地台，並將這一速度歸功於事件發生後立即啟動的應急預案。截至8月14日，受災地區全部光纖節點均已恢復正常，基地台復原率達八成。

  為了因應電力中斷——這是維持網路設備運作的首要障礙——Claro在三座城市部署了26台柴油發電機，分別是喬科省省會基布多（Quibdó）、里薩拉爾達省的佩雷拉（Pereira），以及考卡山谷省的卡利（Cali）。在商業電力仍無法供應的站點，這批發電機負責維持備援系統的持續運轉。

  ## Tigo持續進行的穩定作業

  Tigo表示，技術團隊正進行全天候監控，並著手解決三項相互交疊的問題：電力中斷、基礎設施實體損毀，以及地震後民眾通訊需求暴增所帶來的網路流量激增。該業者並未公布具體的復原百分比。

  Downdetector針對Tigo的監測數據顯示，故障通報呈分散分布，未出現其他電信業者網路上可見的明顯尖峰，消息來源將此情況解讀為網路逐步穩定的跡象，但零星斷線問題仍持續存在。

  ## 尚待克服的障礙

  儘管整體進展可期，各業者仍點出三項拖慢全面恢復服務的外部因素：部分地區商業電力長期中斷、受損基礎設施所在地點難以進入，以及維持網路節點運作所需的柴油供應受到制約。上述情況持續限制特定地點基地台與網路節點的正常運作。

  ## 各業者故障概況

  根據Downdetector截至8月13日所蒐集的數據，各業者服務問題的通報分布如下：

  - **Claro：** 寬頻網路中斷（46%）、WiFi中斷（27%）、行動網路中斷（16%）。故障通報在8月12日下午6時至晚間10時之間達到尖峰。
  - **Movistar：** 寬頻網路中斷（30%）、行動訊號中斷（27%）、行動網路中斷（26%），故障集中於8月13日下午3時至晚間9時。
  - **Tigo：** 行動網路中斷（31%）、行動訊號中斷（24%）、寬頻網路中斷（22%）。

  ## 政府協調與緊急數據開放

  各業者表示，與哥倫比亞政府機關的協調合作是決定搶修優先順序的核心機制。Claro與Tigo持續與地方當局保持直接聯繫，協助確認關鍵受災區域，並透過官方管道發布最新消息，據稱這有助於更有效率地調配技術資源。

  與此同時，Claro、Tigo、Wom及衛星業者Starlink的用戶，凡位於五個受災省份者，均在地震後獲得免費行動數據。Starlink亦在受災地區全面啟動免費服務，並發放點數，允許用戶重新啟用已到期的訂閱方案。

  ## 對受災地區的意義

  大規模地震後的網路恢復速度，對受災省份的緊急救援、經濟活動及公共安全協調均具有直接影響。持續仰賴柴油發電機維持網路節點運作一事，清楚呈現出當地電信韌性與電網穩定度之間的高度連動關係，也說明電力基礎設施一旦失效，連鎖反應將如何迅速轉化為網路斷線危機。能否全面恢復服務，仍取決於商業電力供應的解決、通往受損站點道路的疏通，以及備援發電所需燃油物流的持續保障。
