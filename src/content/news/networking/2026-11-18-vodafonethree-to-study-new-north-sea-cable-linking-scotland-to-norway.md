body_en: |
  VodafoneThree announced on 10 November a feasibility study into a new subsea digital corridor connecting mainland Scotland and Shetland to Norway through the North Sea. Vodafone Business estimates the route could deliver up to £8.2bn in economic value over the next decade and protect £6.6m in economic activity for every day that disruption occurs on an alternative route.

  The study is scheduled to begin in summer 2027. No date for a potential transition from research to construction has been set.

  The announcement reflects a broader shift among telecoms operators toward the North Sea. Most of the UK's roughly 30 subsea telecommunications cables to mainland Europe land in England, leaving Scotland with a smaller share. Operators are increasingly assessing routes northward toward Scandinavia to improve geographic diversity and resilience.

  ## Related Projects and Existing Connections

  The November announcement follows VodafoneThree's June 2025 feasibility study into a cable connecting mainland Scotland to the Shetland Islands. The company said that link could support connectivity objectives in Shetland's Local Development Plan and the UK government's Shared Rural Network (SRN) programme, which aims to extend mobile coverage in rural areas.

  Nick Gliddon, VodafoneThree's business director, said the proposed UK-Norway corridor would strengthen physical links between the two countries, add "vital route diversity", and provide resilient connectivity for both nations' infrastructure. Gliddon added that the corridor could "unlock investment in data centres and AI, creating skilled jobs and wider economic opportunities."

  If VodafoneThree proceeds, its system would not be the first UK-Norway link. NO-UK already connects Stavanger, Norway, with Newcastle, England. The Tampnet North and Tampnet South systems form part of a larger offshore communications network with landing points in both countries. North Sea Connect links the UK to Denmark, and Midgard Infra's Verena system, expected in 2028, will provide another UK-Denmark connection between Scarborough and Esbjerg.

  ## Security and Regulatory Context

  "Reliable international connectivity is vital to the UK's defence," Gliddon said in an official statement. Cable damage incidents in the Baltic Sea between 2023 and 2025 raised international concern about subsea resilience, with attention on possible sabotage and anchor-dragging.

  In February 2025, the European Union released a Joint Communication setting out four goals for submarine cable security: reduce disruptions and their impact; detect threats; shorten response and recovery times; and deter hostile actors through sanctions and other measures.

  UK Defence Secretary John Healey announced on 9 April 2026 that the UK, Norway, and their allies had tracked Russian submarines whose mission was to survey cables in the North Atlantic and the UK Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ). The UK government cited that incident when it introduced a white paper on 22 October 2026 titled "Critical Undersea Infrastructure Security and Resilience: Proposals for modernising the UK's legislation." Any new North Sea cable would most likely fall under those proposed rules.

  ## Nordic Data Centre Demand

  Norway has attracted data centre operators with cheap power, available land, and a cool climate that reduces operating costs. Gliddon pointed to data centre and AI investment as among the potential benefits of the proposed corridor.

  The pace of growth has prompted regulatory intervention. Norway's state-owned grid operator Statnett announced on 9 November 2026 a pause in data centre connection reservations until 7 August 2027. On the same day, Norwegian ministers published new plans covering licensing, grid connection rules, and the possibility of state-owned sites. The Norwegian Water Resources and Energy Directorate has warned that further data centre growth could lead to higher electricity prices and a power deficit.

  Operator interest extends across the Nordic region. Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden have all attracted data centre investment driven by cooler climates, available land, and renewable energy. Iceland has emerged as a location for sustainable data centres in particular.

  Connecting to Norway through the North Sea could also position mainland Scotland and Shetland as data centre investment opportunities by pairing available land with high-capacity subsea connectivity.

  ## North Sea Infrastructure Characteristics

  The North Sea's history as an offshore oil and gas basin has produced an existing maritime ecosystem: ships, ports, seabed survey data, and the Tampnet network's connections to offshore platforms. That established infrastructure may lower the barrier for new cable projects.

  The sea's shallow waters make cables easier to lay and repair relative to deeper ocean routes, but also expose them to damage from anchors and fishing equipment in a region with extensive commercial fishing activity. The seabed is already populated with oil pipelines and power cables, which can complicate route planning and raise costs.

  Cable crossings in the North Sea are relatively short. The NO-UK cable runs approximately 700 kilometres; Tampnet North's greater length of roughly 1,751 kilometres reflects two Norwegian landing points rather than the sea's width. Shorter cable distances reduce latency, the delay between a signal being sent and received, giving North Sea routes a performance advantage over longer paths.

  The region's geographic position provides Scandinavia with relatively direct connections to major connectivity hubs in London and Amsterdam, adding strategic value to any new cable.

  ## What Comes Next

  New North Sea cables from Scotland would distribute the UK's subsea connectivity infrastructure more widely and add route diversity for traffic currently concentrated in English Channel cables. For the Nordic region, additional cable capacity would serve a data centre sector whose growth has already strained grid capacity in Norway.

  The feasibility study for the UK-Norway corridor is set to begin in summer 2027. VodafoneThree's earlier feasibility work on a mainland Scotland-to-Shetland link remains ongoing. Both projects, if they proceed to construction, would fall under the proposed UK regulatory framework published in October 2026.

  Norway's data centre grid reservation pause runs until 7 August 2027.
body_zh: |
  VodafoneThree於11月10日宣布，將針對一條穿越北海、連結蘇格蘭本島及謝德蘭群島至挪威的全新海底數位廊道展開可行性研究。Vodafone Business估計，這條路線在未來十年內可望創造高達82億英鎊的經濟效益，且每當替代路線發生中斷時，每天可保護達660萬英鎊的經濟活動不受影響。

  此研究預計於2027年夏季啟動，目前尚未設定從研究推進至動工建設的時程。

  這項宣布反映出電信業者正逐漸轉向北海布局的更廣泛趨勢。英國目前通往歐洲大陸的約30條海底電信電纜，大多在英格蘭登陸，蘇格蘭分得的比例相對較小。各業者正日益評估向北延伸、連結斯堪地那維亞的路線，以提升地理多樣性與韌性。

  ## 相關計畫與現有連線

  此次11月的宣布，是繼VodafoneThree於2025年6月針對連結蘇格蘭本島與謝德蘭群島的電纜展開可行性研究之後的進一步行動。該公司表示，那條鏈路可支援謝德蘭群島地方發展計畫的連線目標，以及英國政府旨在擴大偏鄉地區行動通訊覆蓋率的「共享農村網路」（Shared Rural Network，SRN）計畫。

  VodafoneThree業務總監尼克．格利頓（Nick Gliddon）表示，擬議中的英挪廊道將強化兩國之間的實體連結，增添「不可或缺的路由多樣性」，並為兩國的基礎設施提供具備韌性的連線能力。格利頓補充，這條廊道有望「帶動資料中心與人工智慧的投資，創造高技術就業機會及更廣泛的經濟發展機會」。

  若VodafoneThree決定推進，其系統將不會是第一條英挪海底電纜。NO-UK已將挪威的斯塔萬格（Stavanger）與英格蘭的新堡（Newcastle）相連。Tampnet North與Tampnet South系統則構成一個更大型離岸通訊網路的一部分，兩端皆在兩國各自設有登陸點。North Sea Connect將英國連結至丹麥；而Midgard Infra旗下的Verena系統預計於2028年完工，將在英國斯卡伯勒（Scarborough）與丹麥埃斯比約（Esbjerg）之間提供另一條英丹連線。

  ## 安全與法規背景

  格利頓在官方聲明中表示：「可靠的國際連線對英國的防衛至關重要。」2023年至2025年間，波羅的海接連發生電纜受損事件，引發國際社會對海底電纜韌性的高度關注，各方矛頭指向可能的蓄意破壞與拋錨拖曳行為。

  2025年2月，歐盟發布一份聯合通訊文件，訂立海底電纜安全的四大目標：減少中斷事件及其影響、偵測威脅、縮短應對與復原時間，以及透過制裁等措施嚇阻惡意行為者。

  英國國防大臣約翰．希利（John Healey）於2026年4月9日宣布，英國、挪威及其盟友已追蹤到俄羅斯潛艦，其任務為勘測北大西洋及英國專屬經濟區（EEZ）內的電纜。英國政府在2026年10月22日發布的白皮書中援引該事件，白皮書題為《關鍵海底基礎設施安全與韌性：英國法規現代化建議》。任何新的北海電纜，極有可能受這些擬議規範所管轄。

  ## 北歐資料中心需求

  挪威以廉價電力、充裕土地及有助於降低營運成本的涼爽氣候，吸引眾多資料中心業者進駐。格利頓特別指出，資料中心與人工智慧投資是擬議廊道潛在效益之一。

  然而，快速成長的態勢已促使主管機關出手干預。挪威國有電網業者Statnett於2026年11月9日宣布，暫停受理資料中心的電網接入預約申請，暫停期限至2027年8月7日。同一天，挪威各部會發布涵蓋許可申請、電網接入規則及國有廠址可行性的新計畫。挪威水資源暨能源局已警告，資料中心若繼續擴張，可能導致電價上漲並出現電力短缺。

  業者的布局興趣遍及整個北歐地區。丹麥、芬蘭、冰島、挪威及瑞典均憑藉涼爽氣候、充裕土地及再生能源，吸引了大量資料中心投資；冰島尤其以永續資料中心的發展重鎮而聲名大噪。

  透過北海連結挪威，也可望使蘇格蘭本島與謝德蘭群島憑藉充裕土地加上高容量海底連線的優勢，進而成為資料中心的投資熱點。

  ## 北海基礎設施特性

  北海長期作為離岸油氣開採重鎮，已形成一套完整的海事生態系：船隊、港口、海床勘測資料，以及Tampnet網路與離岸平台之間的連線。這套既有基礎設施有望降低新電纜計畫的進入門檻。

  北海水深較淺，使電纜鋪設與維修作業相對深海路線更為便利，但同時也令電纜更易在這片商業漁業活動頻繁的海域中，遭受拋錨及漁業作業損壞。海床上早已密布油管與電力電纜，這些設施將使路線規劃更加複雜，並可能推高工程成本。

  北海的電纜跨距相對較短。NO-UK電纜全長約700公里；Tampnet North長達約1,751公里，主要是因為在挪威設有兩個登陸點，而非北海本身的寬度所致。電纜距離越短，延遲（即訊號發送至接收之間的時間差）越低，使北海路線在效能上優於距離更長的替代路徑。

  此地區的地理位置，可讓斯堪地那維亞與倫敦、阿姆斯特丹等主要連線樞紐之間維持相對直接的連線，為任何新電纜增添了重要的戰略價值。

  ## 後續展望

  來自蘇格蘭的北海新電纜，將使英國的海底連線基礎設施分布更加廣泛，並為目前集中於英吉利海峽電纜的流量增添路由多樣性。對北歐地區而言，新增的電纜容量將服務一個成長速度已對挪威電網容量造成壓力的資料中心產業。

  英挪廊道的可行性研究預計於2027年夏季啟動。VodafoneThree稍早針對蘇格蘭本島至謝德蘭群島連線所進行的可行性研究仍在持續推進中。兩項計畫若推進至動工階段，均將受2026年10月發布的英國擬議監管框架所規範。

  挪威的資料中心電網接入預約暫停期限至2027年8月7日止。
