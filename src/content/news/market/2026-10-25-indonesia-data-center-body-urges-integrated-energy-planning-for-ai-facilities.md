body_en: |
  Indonesia's data center industry association has called on policymakers and developers to align AI data center construction with coordinated national planning for power generation, electricity grids, telecommunications networks, and water supply, warning that large-scale investment without parallel infrastructure development could leave facilities unable to operate as planned.

  Hendra Suryakusuma, chairman of the Indonesia Data Center Provider Organization (IDPRO), said AI data centers — particularly those running high-intensity computing workloads — require more complex power and cooling planning than conventional facilities, and that large-scale development has the potential to place significant additional demand on national electricity supply.

  "The construction of large-scale data centers also creates significant additional electricity needs. AI data centers, particularly those using high-intensity computing, require more complex power and cooling planning," Hendra told Bisnis.com on Saturday.

  ## Shifting investment criteria

  Hendra said the factors determining data center investment attractiveness have expanded beyond land availability, fiscal incentives, and construction costs. Global investors and customers, he said, are now also evaluating electricity reliability, the ability to supply low-carbon energy, international connectivity, regulatory certainty, operational security, and efficiency in energy and water use.

  Indonesia's underlying case for investment includes the scale of its domestic digital market, its geographic position, energy development opportunities, and growing demand for cloud and AI computing, Hendra said. But he said those advantages can only be realized if capacity development is carried out in step with power supply planning, grid infrastructure, telecommunications, and water resources.

  ## National roadmap

  IDPRO is backing the development of a national data center industry roadmap to serve as a reference for capacity development and supporting infrastructure provision. Hendra said such a roadmap should include projections for capacity requirements, energy availability, connectivity development, sustainability standards, workforce needs, and targets for domestic economic contribution.

  He called for long-term planning that brings together government, energy providers, zone managers, telecommunications operators, and industry associations. The goal, he said, is to ensure that data center construction proceeds in step with energy and telecommunications infrastructure development, so that investment announcements are not made before the supporting facilities are ready.

  "Indonesia needs to avoid a situation where investment has been announced but the supporting infrastructure cannot yet meet its operational requirements," Hendra said.

  ## Efficiency standards and carbon

  Hendra said the industry needs to adopt measurable energy and water efficiency standards, including Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) — a ratio measuring how efficiently a data center uses energy, where a lower figure means more of the total power consumed reaches computing equipment rather than overhead systems such as cooling — as well as Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) and carbon emissions metrics.

  He drew a distinction between operational efficiency and low-carbon energy sourcing, arguing the two are complementary but not interchangeable. A data center can achieve high efficiency while still generating substantial emissions if it draws from a high-carbon grid. For that reason, he said, planning for AI data centers must address both how efficiently a facility operates and the carbon intensity of its energy source.

  ## Regional competition

  Hendra said Indonesia is competing for data center investment alongside Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand, as facilities expand across Southeast Asia. He argued Indonesia should not compete primarily by offering the largest incentives or the lowest costs, describing that approach as less sustainable than building competitive advantage through investment certainty, infrastructure reliability, access to increasingly clean energy, human resource quality, and the scale of the domestic digital market.

  "Indonesia does not have to compete solely by offering the largest incentives or the lowest costs. A more sustainable competitive advantage is built through investment certainty, infrastructure reliability, access to increasingly clean energy, human resource quality, and the scale of the domestic digital market," he said.

  ## Domestic ecosystem

  Hendra said AI data center development should also be directed at strengthening Indonesia's national digital ecosystem. He said the presence of AI data centers should support domestic industry development, broaden access to AI technology for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), and improve the competency of Indonesia's workforce — not only provide computing capacity for global technology companies.

  IDPRO has framed AI data center development as the construction of a national ecosystem encompassing energy infrastructure, connectivity, sustainability, and domestic industry readiness. The national roadmap IDPRO is backing would set out capacity projections, energy availability targets, connectivity development plans, sustainability benchmarks, workforce requirements, and domestic economic contribution goals.
body_zh: |
  印尼資料中心產業公會呼籲政策制定者與開發商，將AI資料中心的建設與全國電力發電、輸配電網路、電信基礎建設及供水系統的整體規劃相互銜接，並警告若大規模投資缺乏配套基礎建設同步推進，可能導致設施無法依計畫正常運作。

  IDPRO主席亨德拉·蘇亞庫蘇馬（Hendra Suryakusuma）表示，AI資料中心——尤其是承載高強度運算工作負載的設施——在電力與冷卻規劃上遠比傳統設施複雜，大規模發展有可能對全國電力供應造成相當程度的額外需求。

  「大規模資料中心的建設同樣會帶來龐大的額外用電需求。AI資料中心，特別是採用高強度運算的設施，需要更為複雜的電力與冷卻規劃，」亨德拉週六接受《Bisnis.com》採訪時表示。

  ## 投資評估標準的轉變

  亨德拉指出，決定資料中心投資吸引力的因素，已從土地取得、財政優惠及建設成本等傳統考量向外延伸。他表示，全球投資人與客戶如今也會評估電力供應的穩定性、提供低碳能源的能力、國際連線品質、法規確定性、營運安全，以及能源與用水的使用效率。

  亨德拉說，印尼在吸引投資方面本具備若干基礎優勢，包括國內數位市場規模、地理位置、能源開發潛力，以及雲端與AI運算需求的持續成長。但他強調，唯有在產能發展的同時，同步推動電力供應規劃、電網基礎建設、電信及水資源建設，這些優勢才能真正轉化為實際效益。

  ## 全國發展路線圖

  IDPRO積極推動制定一份全國資料中心產業路線圖，作為產能發展與配套基礎建設供給的參考依據。亨德拉表示，這份路線圖應涵蓋產能需求預測、能源供應、連線建設、永續標準、人力資源需求，以及對國內經濟貢獻的目標。

  他呼籲推動長期規劃，將政府、能源供應商、園區管理方、電信業者及產業協會納入協作框架，確保資料中心的建設能與能源及電信基礎建設同步推進，避免在配套設施尚未就緒前便對外宣布投資計畫。

  「印尼必須避免出現這樣的局面：投資已宣布，配套基礎建設卻仍無法滿足其運作需求，」亨德拉說。

  ## 能源效率標準與碳排

  亨德拉表示，業界必須採用可量化的能源與用水效率標準，包括電能使用效率（PUE）——這是衡量資料中心能源使用效率的比值，數值越低，代表總用電量中越高比例實際流向運算設備，而非空調等輔助系統——以及水資源使用效率（WUE）與碳排放指標。

  他特別區分了運營效率與低碳能源來源兩個概念，主張兩者相輔相成、但不可相互取代。一座資料中心即便達到高效運營，若仍仰賴高碳電網，其碳排放量依然可觀。有鑑於此，他表示AI資料中心的規劃必須同時兼顧設施的運營效率與能源來源的碳排強度。

  ## 區域競爭態勢

  亨德拉指出，隨著東南亞資料中心持續擴張，印尼正與新加坡、馬來西亞及泰國競逐資料中心投資。他認為，印尼不應以提供最大優惠或最低成本作為主要競爭手段，形容這種策略的永續性遠不如透過投資確定性、基礎建設可靠度、日益潔淨的能源取得管道、人力資源素質，以及國內數位市場規模來建立競爭優勢。

  「印尼不必僅靠提供最大優惠或最低成本來競爭。更具永續性的競爭優勢，來自投資確定性、基礎建設可靠度、日益潔淨的能源取得管道、人力資源素質，以及國內數位市場的規模，」他說。

  ## 國內生態系統

  亨德拉表示，AI資料中心的發展也應致力於強化印尼的國家數位生態系統。他說，AI資料中心的落地應支持國內產業發展、擴大中小企業取得AI技術的管道，並提升印尼勞動力的專業能力——而不僅僅是為全球科技企業提供運算容量。

  IDPRO將AI資料中心的發展定位為建構一個涵蓋能源基礎建設、連線能力、永續發展與國內產業準備度的國家生態系統。IDPRO所推動的全國路線圖，將設定產能預測、能源供應目標、連線建設計畫、永續基準、人力需求，以及對國內經濟貢獻的目標。
