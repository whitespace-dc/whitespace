body_en: |
  The Philippine Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) has endorsed the US-led Pax Silica initiative as a pillar of the country's AI infrastructure strategy, even as the government and private sector continue to weigh the costs and benefits — and a leading business group warned that gains from a proposed $10 billion industrial district in Central Luzon must be enforced by contract, not assumed.

  ## DICT links Pax Silica to AI master plan

  DICT Secretary Henry Rhoel R. Aguda told reporters that Pax Silica is "part of our Philippine AI master plan" and that building fiber backbone is critical to the country's digital connectivity goals. The initiative would complement existing government efforts to expand connectivity.

  A data-center hub in Clark, in Central Luzon, is proposed at 400 megawatts of capacity. The DICT's Philippine AI Infrastructure master plan targets approximately 1.5 gigawatts of total data center capacity over the next seven years and aims to attract $30 billion in investment by 2033. The plan covers AI strategy, digital connectivity expansion, and the introduction of high-performance computing capability.

  ## Pax Silica's scope in the Philippines

  Pax Silica is a US-led initiative designed to strengthen technology supply chains and counter China's position in technology manufacturing. The Philippines is one of 23 signatories and joined the bloc in April.

  The Philippine component centers on a 1,620-hectare industrial and innovation district in Central Luzon, within the Luzon Economic Corridor, with an initial investment target of $10 billion. The district is designed to integrate semiconductor design, fabrication, and advanced packaging; artificial intelligence computing; critical mineral processing; energy and data infrastructure; research and development; logistics; and residential and commercial development.

  ## Clark hub's energy and water demands draw scrutiny

  The main objections raised by government assessors and the private sector center on the energy and water demands of building data centers and related infrastructure at scale in Central Luzon.

  Secretary Aguda said developers are required to establish their own power sources rather than draw from the national grid. He added that data centers under the plan will operate their own desalination facilities — converting saltwater into freshwater — and recycle water used to cool server equipment, with the potential to supply surrounding communities.

  ## Business lobby demands contractual safeguards

  The Management Association of the Philippines (MAP), a business group, stopped short of endorsing or opposing Pax Silica, instead urging the government to treat it as a strategic negotiation. MAP said the country "should neither approach Pax Silica with blind optimism nor reflexive opposition."

  "The critical issue is whether the agreements, governance structures, and implementation mechanisms can be designed in a manner that maximizes value to the Filipino people while effectively managing economic, social, environmental, and geopolitical risks," MAP said in a statement issued Thursday.

  MAP argued the initiative could help the Philippines reduce reliance on labor exports and consumption-led growth, and upgrade its value chain from raw or minimally processed resources toward semiconductors and advanced manufacturing. But those outcomes "cannot be assumed. They must be deliberately negotiated, contractually protected, and institutionally monitored," the group said.

  MAP called for agreements that encourage "progressive value addition in mineral processing, component manufacturing, technology development, advanced manufacturing, and research activities."

  ## Negotiations to determine whether targets are met

  If the Clark hub and the broader Pax Silica program are realized at the projected scale, the Philippines would move from services and low-value-added exports toward a semiconductor and AI infrastructure base. The DICT frames the Clark facility as one building block in a 1.5-gigawatt data center ecosystem targeted by 2033, underpinned by $30 billion in total investment.

  Whether those targets are reached depends on whether government and private-sector negotiations produce governance frameworks that satisfy the DICT's connectivity ambitions alongside MAP's demand for enforceable protections — a process both parties said is currently underway.
body_zh: |
  菲律賓資訊通訊技術部（DICT）已將美國主導的Pax Silica倡議，正式列為菲國AI基礎設施戰略的核心支柱，惟政府與民間仍持續評估中呂宋一座百億美元工業特區的成本效益。主要商業團體同步示警，各方期待的收益必須以合約明文保障，而非憑空假設。

  ## DICT將Pax Silica納入AI總體規劃

  DICT部長亨利·魯爾·阿古達（Henry Rhoel R. Aguda）向媒體表示，Pax Silica「是我們菲律賓AI總體規劃的一環」，並強調建設光纖骨幹網路對菲律賓數位連結目標至關重要。這項倡議將與政府現有的擴大連結計畫形成互補。

  克拉克位於中呂宋，擬建的資料中心樞紐計畫裝置容量達400百萬瓦。DICT的菲律賓AI基礎設施總體規劃目標，是在未來七年內使全國資料中心總容量達到約1.5吉瓦，並爭取在2033年前吸引300億美元投資。此規劃涵蓋AI策略、數位連結擴展，以及引進高效能運算能力等面向。

  ## Pax Silica在菲律賓的規模

  Pax Silica是美國主導的倡議，旨在強化科技供應鏈，並制衡中國在科技製造領域的地位。菲律賓是23個簽署國之一，於今年4月正式加入。

  菲律賓的項目以中呂宋呂宋經濟走廊內一處占地1,620公頃的產業暨創新特區為核心，初期投資目標為100億美元。該特區設計整合半導體設計、晶圓製造與先進封裝；人工智慧運算；關鍵礦產加工；能源與資料基礎設施；研究與開發；物流；以及住宅與商業開發等多元功能。

  ## 克拉克樞紐的能源與用水需求引發審查

  政府評估人員與民間業者提出的主要疑慮，集中於在中呂宋大規模興建資料中心及相關基礎設施，對能源供應與水資源所造成的龐大需求。

  阿古達部長表示，開發商必須自行建立電力來源，不得仰賴國家電網供電。他並補充，計畫中的資料中心將自設海水淡化設施，將海水轉化為淡水，同時回收用於冷卻伺服器設備的水資源，且有潛力進一步供給周邊社區使用。

  ## 業界團體要求合約保障

  菲律賓管理學會（MAP）對Pax Silica既未表態支持，也未明確反對，而是敦促政府將其視為一場戰略性談判。MAP表示，菲律賓「既不應以盲目樂觀的態度看待Pax Silica，也不應本能地加以抵制」。

  「關鍵問題在於，相關協議、治理架構與執行機制，能否以最大化菲律賓人民利益的方式加以設計，同時有效管控經濟、社會、環境與地緣政治風險，」MAP在週四發布的聲明中如此指出。

  MAP認為，這項倡議有助於菲律賓減少對勞動力輸出與消費驅動成長的依賴，並推動產業價值鏈升級，從原料或低度加工資源轉向半導體與先進製造。但MAP強調，這些成果「不能憑空假設，必須透過審慎談判、合約保障與制度性監管加以實現」。

  MAP呼籲，協議內容應鼓勵「礦產加工、零組件製造、技術開發、先進製造與研究活動的逐步附加價值提升」。

  ## 談判結果決定目標能否實現

  若克拉克樞紐與更廣泛的Pax Silica計畫能按預定規模落地，菲律賓將從服務業與低附加價值出口，轉型為以半導體與AI基礎設施為核心的產業結構。DICT將克拉克設施定位為2033年1.5吉瓦資料中心生態系目標的重要基石，並以300億美元總投資作為支撐。

  這些目標能否達成，取決於政府與民間能否在談判中建立治理框架，同時滿足DICT的連結雄心，以及MAP對可執行保護機制的要求——雙方均表示，相關程序目前正積極進行中。
