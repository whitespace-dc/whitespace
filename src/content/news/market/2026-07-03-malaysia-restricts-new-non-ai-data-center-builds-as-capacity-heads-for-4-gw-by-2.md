body_en: |
  Malaysia's data center capacity is projected to quadruple to as much as 4 gigawatts (GW) by 2029, driven by global demand for artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure, but the government restricted new non-AI data center investments in February 2026 as electricity and water demands from the sector's rapid expansion began to strain local resources, according to analysis published by the Asean+3 Macroeconomic Research Office (Amro).

  Total installed capacity stood at roughly 0.9 to 1 GW in 2025. The Amro analysis projects that to rise to between 3 and 4 GW by 2029 if current investment trends hold.

  The southern state of Johor now accounts for approximately 80% of the country's operational data center capacity, with Klang Valley — the historical center of the domestic sector — playing a secondary role. Johor's dominance is driven largely by hyperscale operators, the largest-scale cloud and AI infrastructure providers, that have concentrated their Malaysian investments there.

  ## Why Johor became the focal point

  Johor's rise traces directly to a policy decision in neighboring Singapore, which holds one of Asia's most extensive networks of undersea cables connecting operators to global digital infrastructure. Singapore imposed a temporary pause on approvals for large-scale data center developments between 2019 and 2022. Investors seeking to access Singapore's connectivity at lower operating cost found a natural alternative directly across the Causeway, the Amro analysis states.

  ## The resource factors driving investment

  Three structural features make Malaysia attractive to data center operators, which run facilities continuously and consume large quantities of power and water.

  Electricity costs in Malaysia remain competitive relative to many regional peers and advanced economies, the analysis notes, though it flags that global energy price pressures linked to Middle East conflict could push Malaysian tariffs higher. The country currently maintains a comfortable reserve margin of generation capacity.

  Water availability reinforces the attraction. Abundant rainfall and low pressure on water resources suit the water-intensive cooling systems used in modern, high-performance facilities. The scale of that water demand is substantial: a single 100-megawatt (MW) facility — large enough to support significant cloud or AI computing workloads — consumes approximately 4.2 million litres of water per day, the analysis states.

  Geography provides a third advantage. Malaysia sits outside the Pacific Ring of Fire, giving it lower exposure to earthquakes and other natural disasters that can disrupt continuous operations.

  Government policy has amplified these natural advantages. The Digital Ecosystem Acceleration (DESAC) scheme and the Malaysia Digital (MD) program offer tax incentives and investment allowances for digital infrastructure projects. Malaysia has also developed a regulatory framework covering site planning, sustainability requirements, renewable energy procurement, and strategic development priorities. The Amro analysis describes this framework as more systematic than those of regional peers and says it has strengthened investor confidence.

  ## Constraints are tightening

  The February 2026 restriction on new non-AI data center approvals marks a shift in Malaysia's posture toward the sector. While the country is expanding electricity generation capacity, including through new gas-powered projects, the Amro analysis warns that demand could outpace supply in some locations.

  Water presents a more immediate and concentrated constraint as facilities multiply. Pressure on local water infrastructure could intensify as data center clusters expand, the analysis argues, increasing the importance of resource-efficient cooling technologies and stronger sustainability standards for the sector's long-term viability.

  ## Economic spillovers remain narrow

  Beyond resource limits, the Amro analysis raises questions about whether the investment surge generates durable domestic economic benefit.

  During construction, most equipment — servers, cooling systems, networking hardware, and integrated racks — is imported. Domestic value captured during the construction phase is concentrated mainly in construction activity itself rather than in higher-value manufacturing or technology supply chains.

  Operational data centers offer more potential. Spending on utilities, facility leasing, maintenance, and support services generates higher domestic value added, and the sector can support growth in information and communications technology (ICT) services exports, including cloud computing, hosting, and data processing. Deeper integration into software development and enterprise services could significantly increase the sector's contribution to economic growth over time, the analysis argues.

  Employment follows the same uneven pattern. Construction generates substantial but temporary jobs. Operational facilities typically employ only 30 to 50 full-time staff, and the industry already faces shortages of specialized talent in IT infrastructure management and environmental controls. Competition for skilled workers with Singapore is intensifying, the analysis notes.

  ## The conditions for durable growth

  The Amro analysis, authored by Dr. Jade Vichyanond, a senior economist at Amro, frames the central challenge for Malaysia as converting digital infrastructure investment into lasting economic activity rather than merely adding generating capacity. The analysis identifies three requirements for that transition: deeper domestic supply chains, a larger pool of specialized domestic talent, and greater participation in higher-value digital services such as software development and enterprise platforms.

  Malaysia's total data center capacity is forecast to reach between 3 and 4 GW by 2029, up from about 1 GW in 2025, assuming current investment trajectories continue.

  ## 馬來西亞限制非AI資料中心新建案，總容量料於2029年前衝上4GW

  根據東協加三總體經濟研究辦公室（Amro）發布的分析報告，受全球人工智慧（AI）基礎建設需求帶動，馬來西亞資料中心容量預計到2029年將成長至多達4吉瓦（GW），約為現有規模的四倍。然而，隨著該產業快速擴張開始對當地電力與水資源造成壓力，馬國政府已於2026年2月起限制核准新的非AI資料中心投資案。

  馬來西亞2025年的總裝置容量約為0.9至1GW。Amro的分析預測，若目前的投資趨勢持續，這一數字到2029年將攀升至3至4GW之間。

  南部州屬柔佛如今佔全國資料中心運營容量的約80%，歷來是本土產業重心的巴生河流域則退居次要地位。柔佛的主導地位主要來自超大規模業者——即規模最大的雲端與AI基礎建設供應商——他們已將在馬來西亞的投資高度集中於該州。

  ## 柔佛何以成為投資焦點

  柔佛的崛起，直接源於鄰國新加坡的一項政策決定。新加坡擁有亞洲最綿密的海底電纜網路之一，是業者接入全球數位基礎建設的重要樞紐。2019年至2022年間，新加坡對大型資料中心開發案的核准申請實施暫停措施。Amro的分析指出，希望藉助新加坡網路連接能力、同時又尋求較低營運成本的投資者，自然而然地在一橋之隔的對岸找到了替代方案。

  ## 驅動投資的資源條件

  資料中心需要全天候不間斷運作，且須大量消耗電力與水資源，馬來西亞有三項結構性優勢對業者深具吸引力。

  首先是電費。馬來西亞的電力成本相較於許多區域同儕及已開發經濟體仍具競爭力，不過該分析也提醒，與中東衝突相關的全球能源價格壓力可能推升馬國電費。目前馬來西亞的發電備用容量仍屬充裕。

  水資源的充沛進一步強化了投資吸引力。豐沛的降雨量與相對寬鬆的水資源壓力，非常適合現代高效能設施所採用的耗水型冷卻系統。這類用水量相當可觀：Amro的分析指出，一座100百萬瓦（MW）的資料中心——足以支撐大規模雲端或AI運算工作負載——每日耗水量約達420萬公升。

  地理位置則是第三項優勢。馬來西亞位處太平洋火環帶之外，遭受地震及其他可能中斷連續運作的天然災害的風險相對較低。

  政府政策更進一步放大了這些先天優勢。「數位生態系統加速計畫」（DESAC）及「馬來西亞數位」（MD）計畫為數位基礎建設項目提供稅務優惠與投資抵減。馬來西亞也建立了涵蓋選址規劃、永續性要求、再生能源採購及策略發展重點的監管框架。Amro的分析將此框架評價為優於區域同儕，並指出它有效增強了投資者信心。

  ## 限制條件日趨緊繃

  2026年2月對新的非AI資料中心核准案實施限制，標誌著馬來西亞對該產業態度的轉變。儘管馬國正在擴充發電容量，包括推動新的天然氣發電項目，Amro的分析仍警告，部分地區的電力需求可能超過供給。

  隨著資料中心持續增建，水資源問題所帶來的限制更為迫切且集中。分析認為，隨著資料中心叢集擴大，當地水資源基礎設施的壓力將進一步加劇，這使得節能冷卻技術與更嚴格的永續標準，對該產業長期存續而言愈發重要。

  ## 帶動本土經濟的效益仍有限

  除資源限制外，Amro的分析也對這波投資熱潮能否為馬來西亞本土經濟帶來持久效益提出質疑。

  在建設階段，絕大多數設備——包括伺服器、冷卻系統、網路硬體及整合式機架——均仰賴進口，本土在建設階段所能掌握的附加價值，主要集中於營造工程本身，而非附加價值更高的製造業或技術供應鏈。

  進入運營階段後，帶動本土效益的潛力則相對較大。在公用事業、設施租賃、維護及支援服務上的支出，能創造較高的本土附加價值，且該產業有助於推動資訊及通訊技術（ICT）服務出口成長，涵蓋雲端運算、主機代管及資料處理等領域。分析認為，若能更深入整合軟體開發與企業服務，長期而言將可顯著提升該產業對經濟成長的貢獻。

  就業效益同樣呈現高低不均的態勢。建設階段可創造大量但屬於短期性質的工作機會，而運營中的資料中心通常僅聘用30至50名全職員工，且業界在IT基礎設施管理與環境控制方面的專業人才已普遍短缺。分析指出，與新加坡之間搶奪技術人才的競爭也在持續加劇。

  ## 實現持續成長的必要條件

  這份由Amro資深經濟學家Jade Vichyanond博士撰寫的分析報告，將馬來西亞面臨的核心挑戰定位為：如何將數位基礎建設投資轉化為持久的經濟活動，而不僅僅是擴充裝置容量。分析指出，實現這一轉型須具備三項要件：更深化的本土供應鏈、規模更大的本土專業人才庫，以及更積極投入軟體開發、企業平台等更高附加價值的數位服務。

  在現有投資軌跡持續的前提下，馬來西亞的資料中心總容量預計將從2025年的約1GW，成長至2029年的3至4GW之間。
