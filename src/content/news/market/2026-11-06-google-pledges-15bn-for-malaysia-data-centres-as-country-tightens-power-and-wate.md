body_en: |
  Google announced a $15 billion investment in Malaysia on 4 November 2026, covering new data centre campuses, more than 1 gigawatt (GW) of clean energy capacity added to the national grid, water security projects, and skills training for 800,000 Malaysians by 2031, as the country applies increasingly strict power and water requirements to data centre approvals.

  Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim attended the announcement in Kuala Lumpur. Ruth Porat, president and chief investment officer of Alphabet and Google, said the company would be adding "local power and water resources as our operations scale." The spending will be deployed across 2027 and 2028.

  Google said construction would add an average of $1.1 billion a year to Malaysia's gross domestic product and support more than 37,000 jobs annually. The planned campuses are designed to run at a power usage effectiveness (PUE) of 1.12 to 1.14. PUE measures how efficiently a facility uses electricity, where 1.0 represents perfect efficiency with no power lost to cooling or other overhead.

  The package builds on a $2 billion commitment Google made in 2024 for its first Malaysian data centre and cloud region at Elmina Business Park in Selangor, the state adjacent to Kuala Lumpur.

  ## New campuses outside Johor

  Google's new campuses will not be located in Johor, the southern state that has served as Malaysia's main data centre hub. Alongside Elmina in Selangor, Google bought 389 acres in Port Dickson, Negeri Sembilan, in 2025 for a second site.

  ## Tightening approvals

  The investment arrives during a period of increasing selectivity in Malaysian data centre policy. Prime Minister Anwar told parliament in February that proposals for data centres unrelated to artificial intelligence (AI) had not been approved for close to two years.

  On 28 October, Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof, who also serves as minister of energy transition and water transformation, said projects in areas with low water reserve margins would not be considered, that data centres must hold two days of water reserves, and that at least 30% of their energy must come from renewables.

  Johor has formalized that scrutiny through a special technical committee that assesses every proposal on water demand, electricity demand, utility infrastructure, and site suitability, The Edge reported. The state coordination committee rejected roughly 30% of data centre applications in 2024 for failing sustainability requirements, according to BMI, a unit of Fitch Solutions, which describes the shift as moving from broad investment promotion to a selective model.

  ## Power and water: the numbers

  The Ministry of Energy Transition and Water Transformation, known as Petra, told parliament in October that peak electricity demand from data centres in Peninsular Malaysia is projected to reach 8.39 GW by 2030, with Johor accounting for 4.42 GW and Selangor 1.48 GW. Google's pledge to add more than 1 GW of clean generation amounts to roughly 12% of that projected total demand.

  Water supply presents a sharper near-term constraint. Petra projects data centre water consumption in Johor and Selangor will reach 389.04 million litres a day (MLD) by 2030, against 63.90 MLD consumed today, a roughly six-fold increase. Capacity Media described a hyperscaler bringing its own power and water supply as "becoming the price of entry" for large-scale projects in Malaysia.

  ## Johor's pipeline and vacancy gap

  Despite the constraints, Johor holds the largest incoming data centre pipeline of any Asia-Pacific market tracked by Knight Frank's Data Centre Atlas 2026, at 8,542 MW. Its colocation vacancy rate, the share of available capacity that is unoccupied, stands at just 0.7%, compared with 4.9% in Singapore, 20.5% in Jakarta, and 23.3% in Bangkok. Live IT capacity in Johor stood at 1,110 MW at the start of 2026, roughly 13% of what is in the pipeline.

  Malaysia as a whole accounts for more than half of the data centre capacity under construction across Southeast Asia, according to DC Byte.

  ## Investment spreads beyond Johor

  As land, power, and water tighten in Johor and the Klang Valley, investor interest has begun spreading to other states, Knight Frank said in August. Sarawak's premier confirmed in October an AI data centre plan as part of a broader $50 billion investment package, though the company involved was not named.

  The geographic dispersal follows a pattern that began with Singapore's 2019 moratorium on new data centres, which pushed demand across the Strait of Johor to Malaysia's southern state. With Johor's own constraints now acting as a ceiling, investment is moving further, to Negeri Sembilan, Selangor, and Sarawak.

  ## Regional significance

  Malaysia's approval of the Google commitment reinforces the country's ambition to position itself as a technology and AI hub in Southeast Asia and across the ASEAN region. The conditions applied to the investment, including clean energy sourcing and water self-sufficiency at scale, reflect a shift in how the Malaysian government governs large infrastructure projects, from open-door promotion to requirements set by energy and water regulators, a pattern BMI describes as now embedded in state-level processes.

  Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah told parliament in July, as reported by AFP, that data centres are forecast to account for one third of Malaysia's total energy consumption by 2035.
body_zh: |
  Google於2026年11月4日宣布對馬來西亞投入150億美元，投資範疇涵蓋新設資料中心園區、為國家電網新增逾1 GW清潔能源容量、水資源安全專案，以及2031年前為80萬名馬來西亞人提供技術培訓——與此同時，馬來西亞正對新設資料中心的電力與用水申請實施日趨嚴格的審查。

  馬來西亞總理安華·易卜拉欣（Anwar Ibrahim）出席在吉隆坡舉行的發布儀式。Alphabet暨Google總裁兼首席投資長露絲·波拉特（Ruth Porat）表示，隨著公司營運規模擴大，將「持續投入在地電力與用水資源」。這筆資金將於2027至2028年間陸續到位。

  Google表示，相關工程建設每年平均將為馬來西亞國內生產毛額（GDP）貢獻11億美元，並每年支持逾3萬7,000個就業機會。規劃中的園區，設計電力使用效率（PUE）介於1.12至1.14之間。PUE是衡量設施用電效率的指標，數值1.0代表完美效率，意即無任何電力耗損於冷卻或其他附屬系統。

  此次投資計畫，是在Google於2024年承諾投入20億美元、在雪蘭莪州（Selangor）埃爾米納商業園（Elmina Business Park）建設首座馬來西亞資料中心與雲端區域的基礎上進一步擴大。雪蘭莪州緊鄰吉隆坡。

  ## 新園區選址避開柔佛

  Google的新園區將不設於柔佛州——這個馬來西亞南部的州屬向來是馬國資料中心的主要重鎮。除雪蘭莪的埃爾米納之外，Google已於2025年在森美蘭州（Negeri Sembilan）波德申（Port Dickson）購入389英畝土地，作為第二處選址。

  ## 審批趨嚴

  此次投資宣布之際，馬來西亞資料中心政策正處於日趨嚴選的時期。總理安華今年2月在國會表示，與人工智慧（AI）無關的資料中心提案，已近兩年未獲批准。

  10月28日，兼任能源轉型及水務部長的副總理法迪拉·尤索夫（Fadillah Yusof）宣布，位於水資源備用裕度偏低地區的專案將不予考慮；資料中心必須備有兩天的水資源儲備；且至少30%的能源須來自再生能源。

  《邊緣報》（The Edge）報導，柔佛州已透過一個特別技術委員會，將上述嚴格審查正式制度化，逐案評估用水需求、用電需求、公共設施基礎架構及選址適宜性。惠譽解決方案（Fitch Solutions）旗下研究單位BMI指出，柔佛州在2024年約有30%的資料中心申請因未達永續要求而遭否決，並將此一轉變定性為從廣泛招商引資轉向選擇性審批的模式。

  ## 電力與用水：數字面貌

  能源轉型及水務部（Petra）於10月向國會報告，半島馬來西亞資料中心的尖峰用電需求預計將於2030年達到8.39 GW，其中柔佛州占4.42 GW，雪蘭莪州占1.48 GW。Google承諾新增逾1 GW的清潔能源發電，約相當於上述預計總需求的12%。

  用水供應則構成更為迫切的近期限制。Petra預估，柔佛與雪蘭莪的資料中心至2030年日用水量將達3億8,904萬公升（MLD），而目前日用水量僅為6,390萬公升，增幅約達六倍。媒體Capacity Media將超大規模業者自備電力與水源，形容為「在馬來西亞推動大型專案的入場門票」。

  ## 柔佛的建設管線與空置率落差

  儘管限制重重，根據萊坊（Knight Frank）《2026年資料中心圖鑑》（Data Centre Atlas 2026）所追蹤的亞太市場，柔佛州坐擁全區最龐大的資料中心待建管線，規模達8,542 MW。其共置（colocation）空置率——即可用容量中尚未出租的比例——僅0.7%，相比之下新加坡為4.9%、雅加達為20.5%、曼谷為23.3%。2026年初，柔佛的在線IT容量為1,110 MW，約為待建管線規模的13%。

  DC Byte資料顯示，馬來西亞整體佔東南亞在建資料中心容量的逾一半。

  ## 投資版圖擴及柔佛以外

  隨著柔佛與巴生河流域（Klang Valley）的土地、電力及用水資源日益趨緊，萊坊於8月表示，投資方目光已開始轉向其他州屬。砂拉越（Sarawak）州首席部長於10月確認了一項AI資料中心計畫，作為逾500億美元投資方案的一部分，惟涉及的企業名稱未予透露。

  這種地理分散化的趨勢，延續了自新加坡2019年暫停新設資料中心以來的軌跡——彼時大量需求越過柔佛海峽，轉移至馬來西亞南部的柔佛州。如今柔佛本身的種種限制已形成一道上限，投資進一步向森美蘭、雪蘭莪乃至砂拉越延伸。

  ## 區域戰略意義

  馬來西亞批准Google此次投資承諾，強化了馬國將自身定位為東南亞及東協（ASEAN）科技與AI樞紐的雄心。此次投資附帶的條件——包括採用清潔能源及大規模自給用水——反映出馬來西亞政府治理大型基礎設施專案方式的轉變：從門戶大開的招商促進，轉向由能源與水務主管機關設定門檻的審批模式。BMI將此一模式描述為已深植於各州層級流程之中。

  副總理法迪拉今年7月告訴國會（據法新社報導），資料中心預計將於2035年占馬來西亞總能源消耗的三分之一。
