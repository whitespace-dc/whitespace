body_en: |
  Australian data centre operator NextDC shed 41.7% of its market value on 21 October 2026, with shares closing at A$5.35, their lowest level since 2018, after electricity distributor Jemena suspended the high-voltage network works required to increase power supply to its M3 data centre in Melbourne's West Footscray. Jemena does not expect to restart the works before February 2027, meaning the next increase in available power capacity will slip into fiscal year 2028.

  The selloff came when NextDC emerged from a trading halt and disclosed the disruption to the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX). Chief executive Craig Scroggie said Jemena had "suspended parts of the network works" required for the next power step-up to M3.

  ## Guidance reductions

  NextDC removed the affected M3 customer contracts, worth approximately A$140 million in both net revenue and underlying earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation (EBITDA), from its FY27 guidance. Underlying EBITDA guidance fell to A$245 million to A$265 million, from A$385 million to A$410 million, a reduction of roughly 36%. Net revenue guidance was cut to A$475 million to A$500 million, from A$615 million to A$640 million.

  Capital expenditure guidance was trimmed to A$4.5 billion to A$5 billion, from A$5.25 billion to A$5.75 billion. The revised range includes approximately A$230 million of capitalised interest that the prior guidance excluded. NextDC put all new development projects and land acquisitions on hold.

  ## What blocked Jemena's crews

  M3 has operated since 2022 and sits about 15 metres from residential homes. The data centre has a total planned capacity of 225 megawatts (MW) and construction of its data halls is continuing. The bottleneck is grid connection, not customer demand or construction progress.

  The grid upgrade requires replacing existing power poles in the inner-west suburbs of Yarraville and West Footscray with larger poles capable of carrying 66-kilovolt (66kV) transmission lines. According to NextDC, Jemena attributed the suspension to safety concerns, a lack of access to work sites caused by community and stakeholder opposition, and the local council, acting as road authority, withholding consent while further consultation takes place. In September, residents physically blocked Jemena crews by leaving cars and bins in the road.

  M3 has faced sustained local opposition over diesel generator use and noise pollution. Campaign group Citizens of Tottenham welcomed the company's setback. A Victorian government decision on a permit for the site's next stages, lodged in February 2026, is now not expected until 2027, after the state election on 28 November.

  ## Contract and financing exposure

  What distinguishes the NextDC case from typical planning disputes is where the risk landed. The affected M3 contracts include provisions for service-level credits where capacity is delivered late or not fully powered and, in some circumstances, give customers the right to terminate. NextDC said it is considering whether force majeure clauses or other contract provisions offer relief. Its revised guidance also excludes capacity that is already billing, because the existing grid supply cannot be relied on to support the full contracted power ramp.

  Reuters Breakingviews noted that data centre contracts can contain delay clauses allowing a customer to cancel entirely if project completion runs more than 12 months late, which means a local dispute over poles can become a question of contract enforceability for operators building against hyperscaler deadlines.

  NextDC also warned that a material change to expected customer billing could affect its ability to draw down its A$6.7 billion of undrawn senior debt while remaining within financial covenants. The company reported liquidity of A$8.68 billion at 30 September 2026 and said it was in compliance with all covenants.

  NextDC is not the first operator to invoke force majeure over power delays. Oracle has already issued a force majeure notice over power delays at its Project Jupiter campus in New Mexico, preserving the right to defer rent if the site misses its 2028 start date.

  ## Analyst reaction

  Analysts were divided on whether the selloff went too far. RBC Capital Markets analyst Tom Beadle said the delays were outside NextDC's control but showed that community concerns represent a real risk to the timing of contracted capacity for data centre owners.

  Macquarie retained its Outperform rating on the stock but cut its price target 35% to A$12.90 and reduced its EBITDA forecasts by 35% for FY27 and 23% for FY28. The bank argued NextDC does not need to raise new equity, citing interest cover of 1.67 times in its bear-case scenario, above the 1.5 times covenant threshold. Shares recovered 7.5% to A$5.75 on 22 October but remained well below their level before the announcement.

  ## A risk spreading beyond the United States

  Community opposition to data centres has largely been framed as a US phenomenon. The NextDC episode suggests the risk is spreading to markets previously considered lower-risk, and that the flashpoint is increasingly the grid connection rather than the data centre building itself.

  Europe is not insulated. Finland has ordered Google to halt construction at two data centres after more than 300 hectares of forest were cleared. At a Capacity Europe roundtable this month, 70% of infrastructure leaders said they expect grid connections to take more than 18 months.

  Canada presents another test. EverWind is proposing a data centre at Point Tupper, Nova Scotia, to be leased for 20 years to artificial intelligence company Anthropic, with a 95 MW first phase and plans for a 325 MW expansion by mid-2028. EverWind has described it as the largest private investment in the province's history, but a petition against the project gathered more than 18,000 signatures within a week.

  Tech critic Paris Marx has argued that opposition is forming quickly in Canada because communities have already observed how similar projects played out in the United States. In Ontario, the municipality of Oakville has imposed the province's first data centre moratorium, with the question of who pays for grid upgrades at the centre of the debate.

  EdgeCore's Bill Jabjiniak has argued that the scale of stalled projects indicates developers have been treating community engagement as a formality.

  The NextDC episode raises a practical question for the industry: whether social licence for grid infrastructure works now needs to be secured as early and as carefully as power capacity itself. For NextDC, the immediate test is whether the Victorian government's permitting decision, not now expected before 2027, arrives in time to prevent customers exercising termination rights under their M3 contracts.
body_zh: |
  澳洲資料中心業者NextDC於2026年10月21日市值蒸發41.7%，股價收盤報5.35澳元，創下2018年以來最低水位。起因是電力配售商Jemena暫停了為墨爾本西富茲克雷（West Footscray）M3資料中心提升供電容量所需的高壓電網擴建工程。Jemena預計最快要到2027年2月才能恢復施工，意味著下一階段的可用電力容量提升將延入2028財年。

  NextDC解除交易停牌、向澳洲證券交易所（ASX）披露此事後，股價旋即遭到市場拋售。執行長克雷格·斯克羅吉（Craig Scroggie）表示，Jemena已「暫停M3下一階段升電所需的部分電網工程」。

  ## 業績預測下修

  NextDC將受影響的M3客戶合約從2027財年業績預測中剔除。這批合約的淨營收與扣除利息、稅項、折舊及攤銷前盈利（EBITDA）均約達1.4億澳元。基礎EBITDA預測從原先的3.85億至4.10億澳元，大幅下調至2.45億至2.65億澳元，降幅約36%；淨營收預測則從6.15億至6.40億澳元，縮減至4.75億至5.00億澳元。

  資本支出預測從52.5億至57.5億澳元，調降至45億至50億澳元。修訂後的區間包含約2.3億澳元的資本化利息，而此前的預測並未計入這一部分。NextDC同步宣布，暫停所有新開發項目及土地收購計畫。

  ## 是什麼阻擋了Jemena的施工人員

  M3資料中心自2022年起運營，與住宅區僅距約15公尺，規劃總容量為225百萬瓦（MW），資料機房建設仍在持續推進。目前的瓶頸在於電網連接，而非客戶需求或工程進度。

  電網升級工程需將亞拉維爾（Yarraville）和西富茲克雷等內西郊區的現有電線桿，替換為能夠承載66千伏（66kV）輸電線路的大型電線桿。根據NextDC的說明，Jemena將暫停施工歸因於三項因素：安全顧慮、社區及利害關係人的抗議導致工地無法進場，以及地方議會以道路管理機關身分拒絕核發施工許可、要求展開進一步諮詢。今年9月，有居民以停放車輛和垃圾桶封堵路面的方式，實際阻擋Jemena施工人員進場。

  M3長期以來因柴油發電機的使用和噪音污染問題，持續面臨當地居民的強烈反對。倡議團體「托頓漢市民」（Citizens of Tottenham）對公司此次受挫表示歡迎。維多利亞州政府對該場址下一階段建設許可申請（申請於2026年2月提出）所作的裁決，目前預計要待2027年才能出爐，彼時已是11月28日州選舉之後。

  ## 合約與融資風險

  NextDC此案之所以有別於一般規劃糾紛，在於風險的落點。受影響的M3合約含有服務水準賠償條款，若容量延遲交付或未能達到完整供電功率，客戶可要求賠償；在特定情況下，客戶甚至享有終止合約的權利。NextDC表示，正在研究不可抗力條款或其他合約條款能否提供保障。修訂後的業績預測亦將現有計費容量排除在外，原因是既有電網供應無法支撐合約所規定的完整升電需求。

  路透社評論欄目Reuters Breakingviews指出，資料中心合約可能包含延遲條款，允許客戶在項目完成時間逾預定日期12個月以上時全面取消合約；這意味著一場電線桿引發的地方紛爭，可能演變成業者趕超超大規模雲端業者（hyperscaler）交期時的合約執行力問題。

  NextDC亦警告，若客戶計費出現重大變化，可能影響公司在遵守財務契約條件下，動用其67億澳元未提取優先債務的能力。公司披露截至2026年9月30日的流動性為86.8億澳元，並表示目前符合所有財務契約要求。

  NextDC並非首家因供電延遲而援引不可抗力的業者。甲骨文（Oracle）已就其新墨西哥州「木星計畫」（Project Jupiter）園區的供電延遲發出不可抗力通知，為該場址若錯過2028年啟用目標，保留延後支付租金的權利。

  ## 分析師反應

  分析師對此波跌幅是否反應過度，看法分歧。加皇資本市場（RBC Capital Markets）分析師湯姆·比德爾（Tom Beadle）表示，這次延誤並非NextDC所能掌控，但顯示社區反對已成為資料中心業者按時交付容量的真實風險。

  麥格理（Macquarie）維持對該股的「優於大盤」（Outperform）評級，但將目標價下調35%至12.90澳元，並分別下修2027財年及2028財年的EBITDA預測35%及23%。麥格理認為NextDC無須辦理新股增資，在悲觀情境下，利息覆蓋率仍達1.67倍，高於財務契約要求的1.5倍下限，可提供足夠緩衝。消息公告次日（22日），股價回升7.5%至5.75澳元，但仍遠低於公告前的水位。

  ## 風險蔓延至美國以外

  社區反對資料中心的現象，過去多被視為美國特有的問題。NextDC事件顯示，這一風險正向過去被認為較低風險的市場蔓延，且引爆點愈來愈集中在電網連接，而非資料中心建築本身。

  歐洲亦難置身事外。芬蘭已下令谷歌（Google）停止在兩座資料中心的建設，起因是逾300公頃的林地遭到砍伐。在本月舉行的Capacity Europe圓桌論壇上，70%的基礎設施主管預計電網連接所需時間將超過18個月。

  加拿大也面臨另一場考驗。EverWind計畫在新斯科細亞省（Nova Scotia）塔珀角（Point Tupper）興建資料中心，並與人工智慧公司Anthropic簽訂20年租約；首期規模為95百萬瓦（MW），並計畫於2028年中前擴建至325百萬瓦（MW）。EverWind將此計畫定位為該省歷史上最大規模的私人投資，然而一份反對請願書在短短一週內便蒐集到逾1萬8,000份署名。

  科技評論人巴黎·馬克斯（Paris Marx）指出，加拿大反對聲浪之所以迅速凝聚，是因為當地社區已親眼見證相似項目在美國的發展軌跡。在安大略省（Ontario），奧克維爾（Oakville）市已頒布全省首例資料中心開發暫停令，而電網升級費用由誰承擔，正是爭議的核心所在。

  EdgeCore的比爾·賈比尼亞克（Bill Jabjiniak）認為，滯期項目的規模足以說明，開發商長期以來將社區溝通視為例行公事，而非真正的優先要務。

  NextDC事件為整個產業帶來一個切實的問題：為電網基礎設施工程取得「社會認可」（social licence），是否需要與爭取電力容量本身一樣提早布局、審慎經營。對NextDC而言，眼前最迫切的考驗，是維多利亞州政府的許可裁決能否在預計2027年以後才能出爐的情況下，趕在客戶依M3合約行使終止權之前到位。
