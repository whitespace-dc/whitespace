body_en: |
  Indonesia's Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia announced Monday that the government will introduce regulations requiring data center investors to share economic activity with domestic and regional businesses, saying large-scale investment that bypasses local entrepreneurs is not acceptable.

  Bahlil made the remarks at The Sanur in Bali on October 19, 2026, on the sidelines of events that drew investors to the island. "We will make a regulatory decision that besides investor friends bringing technology to build data centers, they must share with local entrepreneurs," he said, according to Kompas.com. "Don't let everything be done by them alone — what kind of country is this? We need positive synergy; don't let everything be monopolized."

  ## The proposed local-content rule

  Bahlil said the planned regulation would be structured to attract foreign investment while mandating collaboration with both national and regional entrepreneurs in the specific area where a data center project is built. His stated goal is to ensure that local businesspeople become, in his words, "masters in their own land."

  He framed the policy as a "win-win" arrangement: investors are welcome to enter the market and bring the capital and technology required for large-scale facilities, but they must open the supply chain and economic activity to Indonesian partners rather than executing projects end-to-end through their own organizations.

  Data centers were singled out for this treatment partly because of their limited direct employment. Bahlil acknowledged that high-technology infrastructure absorbs relatively few workers relative to the scale of capital deployed. "This data center is high-tech investment, but employment absorption is small — not many — but the impact on the people is extraordinary," he said. Local business partnerships, in his framing, are the mechanism to broaden that community impact.

  ## Power supply: 20 GW of new demand projected

  Separately, Bahlil disclosed a significant power-supply challenge tied to the data center investment pipeline. He said that based on a conversation he had with the Director General of PLN — PT PLN (Persero) is Indonesia's state-owned electricity utility — the additional power required to support planned data center investment is estimated at 20 gigawatts (GW), or 20,000 megawatts (MW).

  To put that figure in context, Bahlil said 20 GW equals approximately 20 percent of Indonesia's total existing electricity capacity. Meeting that demand would require a substantial expansion of the national grid.

  Bahlil said the government needs to map the intended locations of new data center projects before it can plan energy supply to match. He stated he would request that the Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs (Komdigi) collect location data from investors planning to build in Indonesia, so that power infrastructure can be coordinated directly with those sites.

  "We will try to synchronize. Later I will request data from Komdigi on where those who will build data centers plan to do so, so that the energy supply can go directly there," he said.

  ## Significance for investors

  The combination of a forthcoming local-partnership mandate and the scale of new power demand will shape the operating environment for data center developers eyeing Indonesia as a growth market. Investors will need to factor in both regulatory requirements around domestic business inclusion and the government's capacity to deliver power at the volumes required.

  Bahlil emphasized that investment size alone will not be the government's measure of success. He said investment quality — defined by its multiplier effect across society — is the standard against which projects will be judged.

  The minister said Komdigi will be tasked with gathering investor location data as the next concrete step toward synchronizing energy planning with the data center pipeline.
body_zh: |
  印尼能源暨礦產資源部長巴赫里勒·拉哈達利亞（Bahlil Lahadalia）週一宣布，政府將制定法規，要求資料中心投資者與國內及地方企業共享經濟活動，並明確表示，繞過本地創業者的大規模投資絕不可接受。

  巴赫里勒於2026年10月19日在峇里島The Sanur出席吸引投資者齊聚的系列活動期間發表上述言論。據Kompas.com報導，他說：「我們將做出法規決定——投資者朋友除了引進技術來建設資料中心，還必須與本地創業者共享利益。不能讓一切都由他們自己包辦——這是什麼樣的國家？我們需要正向的協同效應，不能讓一切都被壟斷。」

  ## 擬議的本地內容規定

  巴赫里勒表示，計畫中的法規架構將兼顧吸引外資與強制合作的雙重目標，規定投資者必須與資料中心建設地點所在的全國性及地區性創業者攜手合作。他明確宣示，政策目標是確保本地商人成為——用他的話說——「自己土地上的主人」。

  他將這項政策定位為一種「雙贏」安排：歡迎投資者進入市場，引進大型設施所需的資金與技術，但必須向印尼合作夥伴開放供應鏈與經濟活動，而不是讓整個專案從頭到尾由自己的組織一手包辦。

  資料中心之所以被列為特別規範對象，部分原因在於其直接僱用人數相當有限。巴赫里勒坦承，高科技基礎建設相較於其龐大的資本投入，所能吸納的勞動力相對偏少。他說：「資料中心是高科技投資，但對就業的吸收很小——沒有很多——但對人民的影響卻是非凡的。」在他的論述框架中，與本地企業建立合作關係，正是擴大社區影響力的機制。

  ## 電力供應：預估新增需求達20 GW

  另外，巴赫里勒也揭露了與資料中心投資管線密切相關的一項重大電力挑戰。他表示，根據他與印尼國營電力公司PT PLN（Persero）總幹事的一次對談，支撐計畫中資料中心投資所需的額外電力估計達20吉瓦（GW），即20,000百萬瓦（MW）。

  為了讓這個數字更具體，巴赫里勒指出，20 GW約相當於印尼現有電力總裝置容量的20%。要滿足這樣的需求，國家電網必須進行大規模擴建。

  巴赫里勒表示，政府必須先掌握新資料中心專案的預定建設地點，才能據此規劃配套的能源供應。他說，他將要求通訊暨數位事務部（Komdigi）向計畫在印尼興建資料中心的投資者收集地點資料，以便將電力基礎建設與各建設地點直接協調整合。

  他說：「我們將設法進行同步協調。稍後我將向Komdigi索取資料，了解那些要建設資料中心的人打算在哪裡動工，好讓能源供應能夠直接送達當地。」

  ## 對投資者的意義

  即將出台的本地合作規定，加上新增電力需求的龐大規模，將共同形塑資料中心開發商布局印尼這個成長型市場時的整體營運環境。投資者必須同時將國內企業納入的法規要求，以及政府是否有能力以所需規模穩定供電，這兩項因素一併納入規劃考量。

  巴赫里勒強調，投資規模本身並不是政府衡量成功與否的標準。他表示，投資品質——以其對整體社會的乘數效應來定義——才是評斷專案的真正基準。

  這位部長表示，Komdigi將被賦予收集投資者地點資料的任務，作為推動能源規劃與資料中心投資管線同步協調的下一個具體步驟。
