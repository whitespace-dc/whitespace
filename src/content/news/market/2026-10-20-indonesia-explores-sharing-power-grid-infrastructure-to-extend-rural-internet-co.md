body_en: |
  Indonesia's Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs (Komdigi) and the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM) are exploring a co-sharing arrangement that would allow electrical infrastructure built under the government's village electrification program to also carry telecommunications equipment, Communications and Digital Affairs Minister Meutya Hafid said Monday.

  Meutya disclosed the talks at the opening of Bali Tech Week 2026 in a statement reported by Kompas.com. Working teams from both ministries are currently in discussions, though Meutya said the partnership remains at an exploratory stage and she declined to provide specific details of any agreement.

  "We are in talks with the Ministry of ESDM on the village electrification program, where teams are currently discussing co-sharing, so that electrical infrastructure can also be used for telecommunications infrastructure," Meutya said, as quoted by Kompas.com.

  ## What co-sharing would mean in practice

  Co-sharing infrastructure refers to arrangements in which multiple services — in this case electricity distribution and mobile or broadband connectivity — use the same physical assets such as poles, towers, or rights-of-way. Under the proposed framework, the Listrik Masuk Desa, or "electricity into villages," program would serve a dual purpose: extending the power grid to rural areas while simultaneously providing mounting points or conduits for telecommunications equipment.

  Meutya framed the arrangement as a connectivity measure with direct economic consequences. "All connectivity must be realized for growth," she said, according to Kompas.com.

  ## Digital economy backdrop

  The proposal comes as Indonesia projects its national digital economy will reach $99 billion in 2026, making it the largest digital economy in Southeast Asia, according to figures cited by Meutya at the event. That value is projected to grow to $360 billion by 2030, she added.

  Meutya also referenced the Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA), which Kompas.com describes as the first digital economy framework agreed upon by member states of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN). The minister cited broader regional cooperation on the digital economy as a factor that could accelerate Indonesia's growth trajectory.

  ## Significance

  If the co-sharing arrangement advances to a formal program, it would allow Indonesia to extract additional value from rural electrification spending by extending internet connectivity to villages at the same time power lines are installed, without requiring separate infrastructure deployments. The talks signal that Indonesian policymakers are treating connectivity expansion as an explicit economic development tool rather than a standalone infrastructure objective. No timeline or formal agreement has been announced.
body_zh: |
  印尼通訊暨數位事務部（Komdigi）與能源暨礦產資源部（ESDM）正在研議一項基礎設施共用安排，讓政府農村電氣化計畫所建置的電力基礎設施，同時承載電信設備。通訊暨數位事務部長梅緹亞·哈菲德（Meutya Hafid）於週一對外揭露了這項進展。

  梅緹亞在2026年巴里島科技週開幕式上宣布此一協商，相關報導由羅盤網（Kompas.com）刊出。兩部會的工作小組目前正在進行討論，不過梅緹亞表示，這項合作關係仍處於初探階段，她也婉拒透露任何具體協議細節。

  「我們正在與能源暨礦產資源部就農村電氣化計畫進行協商，工作小組目前正就共用議題展開討論，希望電力基礎設施也能同時用於電信基礎設施，」梅緹亞接受羅盤網引述時如此表示。

  ## 共用基礎設施的實際意義

  基礎設施共用是指多種服務——在本案中為電力配送與行動或寬頻連線——共同使用同一套實體資產，例如電桿、鐵塔或路權走廊的安排。在所提出的框架下，「電力入村」計畫（Listrik Masuk Desa）將兼具雙重用途：在將電力延伸至偏鄉地區的同時，也為電信設備提供架設點或管道。

  梅緹亞將此安排定性為具有直接經濟效益的連線措施。「所有連線建設都必須落實，以促進成長，」她在羅盤網的報導中表示。

  ## 數位經濟背景

  此一提案的背景，是印尼預估其全國數位經濟規模將於2026年達到990億美元，成為東南亞最大的數位經濟體——這是梅緹亞在活動上所引用的數據。她補充，這一規模預計將於2030年進一步成長至3,600億美元。

  梅緹亞也提及《數位經濟框架協議》（DEFA），羅盤網將其描述為東南亞國家協會（東協）成員國所達成的首個數位經濟框架協議。這位部長將更廣泛的區域數位經濟合作，列為可望加速印尼成長軌跡的因素之一。

  ## 重要性

  若基礎設施共用安排正式推進為具體計畫，印尼將得以從農村電氣化支出中獲取額外價值——在拉設電力線路的同時，同步將網路連線延伸至村落，毋須另行部署獨立的基礎設施。這些協商所釋出的訊號顯示，印尼政策制定者已將擴大連線覆蓋明確定位為推動經濟發展的工具，而非單純的基礎建設目標。目前尚無任何時程或正式協議的相關公告。
