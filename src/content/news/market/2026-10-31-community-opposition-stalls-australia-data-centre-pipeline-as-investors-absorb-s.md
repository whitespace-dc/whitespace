  ## Community resistance derails projects across Australia

  A surge in community opposition to data centre construction across Australia has cancelled projects, pulled an initial public offering, and triggered a sharp repricing of assets across the sector, with Goldman Sachs Group Inc estimating that only about one-third of the country's planned capacity will be delivered by 2030.

  The pipeline had expanded rapidly. Builders announced plans to add around 23 gigawatts of compute capacity — enough to power tens of millions of homes — up from just 6 gigawatts in February, according to the Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CBA). Australia had drawn developers with competitive economics: rental rates in Sydney run at roughly half those in Singapore, and major cities offer more available land than dense markets such as Hong Kong and Tokyo.

  But a series of high-profile setbacks have exposed how quickly that advantage can be neutralised by local opposition.

  ## A cascade of cancellations

  In late September, Goodman Group, described as Australia's biggest property developer, scrapped a plan to build a 90-megawatt facility — roughly equivalent to the output of a mid-sized power plant — in a Sydney suburb. In early October, Firmus Grid Ltd's 288-megawatt Bell Bay site in Tasmania was hit with appeals just weeks after receiving local council approval.

  The most consequential blow fell when data centre operator Nextdc Ltd cut its earnings forecast and placed development of a major Melbourne facility on hold. Electricity provider Jemena had suspended work to supply additional power to Nextdc's M3 facility — which already has 60 megawatts in operation — because of local opposition to the installation of 22-metre-high power poles. Nextdc said its best hope is that Jemena will resume work in February.

  Nextdc's separate M4 campus in Melbourne, a 150-megawatt facility approved by the state government at the start of the year, is facing fierce resident opposition. Goldman Sachs, which tracks 7.5 gigawatts of projects concentrated mainly in Sydney and Melbourne, put the M4's probability of completion at just 13%.

  Project withdrawals have occurred at all stages of development, including at advanced stages after data centre connection requests have been completed. The CBA, drawing on data from the Australian Energy Market Operator, calculated a median probability of only 28% that any given development will progress from its current status to completion.

  ## Capital markets reprice the sector

  Financial markets have responded sharply. Nextdc's A$1.1 billion (approximately US$773 million) convertible bond, issued in September at a coupon of 1.75%, was yielding around 12% in the wake of the company's earnings cut. Nextdc's shares have fallen more than 40% since the bond was issued.

  Firmus Grid, which had been considered a venture capital darling as recently as August, pulled a much-anticipated IPO in October. The company had sought a valuation of approximately US$30 billion before shelving the offering. UniSuper, one of Australia's largest pension funds, chose not to participate in the Firmus IPO and has indicated it is steering clear of direct investments in data centres.

  UniSuper's chief investment officer, John Pearce, said of the Firmus offering that bankers and vendors had all become "a bit greedy" and that a fair share price would have been "probably closer to US$1 than US$11."

  ## Why permits are no longer enough

  The pattern across these cases points to a structural shift in how data centre projects must be assessed. Having planning permits and even council approval does not, on its own, confer what analysts are calling a social licence to operate. Opposition from local communities has proven capable of blocking or significantly delaying projects even after formal regulatory milestones are reached.

  The CBA's analysis, using Australian Energy Market Operator data, puts the sector's overall delivery odds in stark terms: a median 28% probability of any project reaching completion from its current stage.

  ## What comes next

  Goldman Sachs's assessment, covering 7.5 gigawatts of projects mostly in Sydney and Melbourne, implies that analysts must now evaluate each development individually — weighing its specific location, timing, financing structure, and local political environment — rather than treating pipeline announcements as reliable indicators of future capacity. Nextdc's Melbourne M3 facility remains dependent on Jemena resuming grid connection work, with February the earliest expected restart date.

  ## 社區反對聲浪使澳洲各地計畫脫軌

  澳洲各地針對資料中心建設的社區反對聲浪急遽升溫，已造成多項計畫取消、一宗首次公開募股（IPO）撤件，以及整個產業資產價值的大幅重估。高盛集團估計，至2030年，澳洲的規劃產能中僅約三分之一得以付諸實現。

  澳洲的資料中心建設管線原本擴張迅速。根據澳洲聯邦銀行（CBA）統計，開發商宣布的新增運算容量計畫約達23吉瓦（GW）——足以供應數千萬戶家庭用電——相較於今年2月的6吉瓦大幅增加。澳洲以具競爭力的經濟條件吸引開發商：雪梨的租金約為新加坡的一半，且各大城市相較於香港、東京等高密度市場擁有更充裕的土地。

  然而，一連串備受矚目的挫折揭示了這項優勢在地方反對聲浪下可以多麼迅速地化為烏有。

  ## 接連而來的取消潮

  9月下旬，被譽為澳洲最大地產開發商的古德曼集團（Goodman Group）宣告放棄在雪梨郊區興建一座90百萬瓦（MW）設施的計畫，其規模約相當於一座中型發電廠的輸出。10月初，Firmus Grid有限公司位於塔斯馬尼亞州Bell Bay、容量288MW的園區，在獲得地方議會核准後僅數週便遭提出上訴。

  打擊最為深遠的一記重拳，是資料中心營運商Nextdc有限公司下調盈利預測，並將墨爾本一座重要設施的開發工程暫時擱置。電力供應商Jemena因地方居民反對安裝22公尺高的電線桿，已暫停為Nextdc的M3設施供電擴容工程——該設施目前已有60MW投入營運。Nextdc表示，最樂觀的情況是Jemena於明年2月恢復施工。

  Nextdc另一座位於墨爾本的M4園區，容量150MW，雖已獲州政府於年初批准，目前卻面臨居民的強烈反對。高盛追蹤了主要集中在雪梨與墨爾本、合計7.5吉瓦的專案，評估M4的完工機率僅13%。

  計畫撤案的情況遍及開發各個階段，包括資料中心電網連接申請已完成的後期階段。澳洲聯邦銀行引用澳洲能源市場營運商（AEMO）的數據，計算出任一開發計畫從現狀推進至完工的中位數機率僅為28%。

  ## 資本市場重新為該產業定價

  金融市場的反應十分劇烈。Nextdc發行的11億澳元（約7億7,300萬美元）可轉換債券，原本於9月以1.75%的票息發行，在公司下調盈利預測後，殖利率已攀升至約12%。自該債券發行以來，Nextdc的股價已累計下跌逾40%。

  Firmus Grid在今年8月仍被視為創投圈寵兒，卻於10月撤回了一宗備受矚目的IPO。該公司在擱置上市計畫前，原尋求約300億美元的估值。澳洲最大退休基金之一UniSuper選擇不參與Firmus的IPO，並表示將迴避對資料中心的直接投資。

  UniSuper首席投資長約翰·皮爾斯（John Pearce）對Firmus的上市案表示，銀行家和賣方都變得「有點貪婪」，並指合理的股價「大概比較接近1美元，而非11美元」。

  ## 為何取得許可已不再足夠

  上述案例所呈現的規律，指向資料中心計畫評估方式的結構性轉變。取得規劃許可乃至議會核准，本身並不足以賦予分析師所稱的「社會執照」（social licence to operate）。事實已一再證明，即便在正式監管里程碑達成之後，地方社區的反對仍足以阻礙或大幅延誤計畫的推進。

  澳洲聯邦銀行引用澳洲能源市場營運商的數據進行分析，以毫不修飾的數字呈現整個產業的交付機率：任一計畫從現狀推進至完工的中位數機率為28%。

  ## 後續展望

  高盛針對主要集中於雪梨與墨爾本的7.5吉瓦專案所作的評估顯示，分析師如今必須逐一評量每一個開發案——綜合考量其具體地點、時程、融資結構與當地政治環境——而非將管線公告視為未來產能的可靠指標。Nextdc的墨爾本M3設施仍有賴於Jemena恢復電網連接工程，最早預計於明年2月重新啟動。
