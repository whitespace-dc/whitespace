body_en: |
  Private equity firm Next Capital has launched Dataspace, a new Australian data centre operator built around five former Fujitsu facilities with a combined 14.5MW of stated information technology capacity and 17,803 square metres of technical space.

  The portfolio was acquired from Fujitsu in July 2026 for just under AU$200 million (approximately US$134.5 million), financed through Next Capital's AU$375 million (US$252.2 million) fifth fund, according to the Australian Financial Review. The five sites span Sydney, Melbourne, Perth and Brisbane, covering four of Australia's main data centre markets.

  ## Portfolio and leadership

  All five facilities are designed to Tier III standards — an industry classification meaning they are engineered for concurrent maintainability, allowing infrastructure components to be repaired or replaced without taking the facility offline. The largest site, Greystanes in western Sydney, accounts for 7MW of the portfolio's stated IT capacity. Sydney's two facilities together provide 9.8MW. The remaining three sites contribute 2MW in Melbourne, 1.7MW in Perth and 1MW in Brisbane.

  Dataspace has appointed Gavin Milton-White as chief executive. Milton-White previously served as head of transformation service delivery for Fujitsu Asia Pacific. "We are starting with a very strong platform including established facilities, experienced people, long-standing customers and infrastructure that has been operating reliably for decades," he said, adding that the company can support customers with existing available capacity while also expanding within current locations and pursuing longer-term development opportunities.

  ## Australia's data centre market

  The launch comes as Australia's data centre market is projected to grow from US$5.4 billion in 2026 to US$8.2 billion by 2034, according to IMARC Group analysis published in October 2026. Australia currently hosts 1.6 gigawatts of operational data centre capacity across 163 tracked facilities, according to Data Centres Australia's Australian Data Centre Forecast Issue 2, published the same month. Operational capacity is forecast to reach 3.9GW by 2030, supported by 97 additional facilities, as demand for cloud services and artificial intelligence compute expands.

  Hyperscale facilities — large campuses built primarily for a single cloud provider's own use — accounted for 41.6% of the Australian market in 2025, with colocation (multi-tenant facilities housing equipment owned by third-party customers) representing 33.4%, according to IMARC Group.

  ## Power and land constraints

  The pace of expansion faces structural limits. Analysis published in November 2025 by integrated law firm Ashurst Perkins Coie identifies limited infrastructure-ready sites, rising construction costs and grid constraints as the primary factors restricting new supply, particularly in Sydney and Melbourne. Dataspace's existing, operational facilities could offer a faster route to additional capacity than greenfield development, provided sufficient power is available to support expansion.

  The broader market has attracted large commitments. In June 2025, Amazon pledged AU$20 billion to expand, operate and maintain its data centre infrastructure in Australia between 2025 and 2029. Blackstone's AU$24 billion acquisition of AirTrunk in 2024 demonstrated investor appetite for established operators with existing grid connections and permits.

  ## Regulatory backdrop

  Dataspace's plans take shape under new government expectations introduced in March 2026. Australia published five national expectations for new and expanded data centre and AI infrastructure developments, covering national interest, energy, water, employment and local innovation. The expectations do not create new legal obligations, but alignment is intended to influence regulatory prioritisation.

  ## Competitive position and outlook

  At 14.5MW in aggregate, Dataspace operates at a scale well below the hyperscale and large-colocation operators that dominate the Australian market, including NEXTDC, AirTrunk and Equinix. Its stated strategy is to unlock further capacity within existing sites and pursue additional development opportunities in Sydney and Melbourne, where power and land constraints make existing permitted facilities particularly valuable.

  How much of that potential can be realised will depend on power availability, the pace of site upgrades to support higher-density cloud and AI workloads, and the operator's ability to compete for customers in a market where operational capacity is forecast to nearly triple to 3.9GW by 2030.
body_zh: |
  私募股權公司Next Capital正式宣布成立Dataspace，這家新興澳洲資料中心業者的核心資產，是五座前富士通設施，合計IT容量達14.5MW，技術空間共17,803平方公尺。

  整批資產於2026年7月以略低於2億澳幣（約1億3,450萬美元）的價格自富士通手中購入，資金來源為Next Capital規模3億7,500萬澳幣（約2億5,220萬美元）的第五號基金，《澳洲金融評論》對此有詳細報導。五處據點分布於雪梨、墨爾本、伯斯及布里斯本，涵蓋澳洲四大主要資料中心市場。

  ## 資產組合與領導團隊

  五座設施均達到Tier III標準——這是業界一項重要分級，意指設施具備「並行維護」能力，可在基礎設施元件進行維修或更換期間維持不斷線運作。位於雪梨西部格雷斯坦（Greystanes）的最大據點，單站IT容量即達7MW；雪梨兩處設施合計提供9.8MW。其餘三站分別貢獻墨爾本2MW、伯斯1.7MW及布里斯本1MW。

  Dataspace已任命加文．米爾頓—懷特（Gavin Milton-White）出任執行長。米爾頓—懷特此前擔任富士通亞太區轉型服務交付主管。他表示：「我們以一個非常堅實的平台起步，包括完善的設施、經驗豐富的員工、長期合作的客戶，以及數十年來穩定運行的基礎設施。」他補充說，公司可以利用現有的可用容量為客戶提供服務，同時在現有據點內擴充，並尋求更長遠的開發機會。

  ## 澳洲資料中心市場

  Dataspace的成立，恰逢澳洲資料中心市場高速擴張之際。根據IMARC集團於2026年10月發布的分析報告，澳洲資料中心市場規模預計將從2026年的54億美元成長至2034年的82億美元。澳洲資料中心協會（Data Centres Australia）同月出版的《澳洲資料中心預測第二期》顯示，澳洲目前在163座已追蹤設施中，共擁有1.6GW的運營容量。隨著雲端服務與人工智慧算力需求持續擴張，預計至2030年運營容量將在97座新增設施的支撐下達到3.9GW。

  就市場結構而言，IMARC集團的數據顯示，超大規模設施——即主要為單一雲端供應商自用而建造的大型園區——在2025年佔澳洲市場的41.6%；代管（供多個租戶託管自有設備的共用設施）則佔33.4%。

  ## 電力與土地限制

  市場擴張的步伐面臨結構性瓶頸。整合型律師事務所Ashurst Perkins Coie於2025年11月發布的分析指出，基礎設施就緒用地匱乏、建設成本攀升，以及電網容量不足，是限制新供給的主要因素，尤以雪梨和墨爾本為甚。Dataspace手中現有的、已運行的設施，只要具備足夠電力支撐擴容，或可提供比從零開發更為快捷的增容途徑。

  大型市場承諾持續湧現。2025年6月，亞馬遜宣布在2025年至2029年間投入200億澳幣，用於在澳洲擴建、運營及維護其資料中心基礎設施。黑石集團（Blackstone）於2024年以240億澳幣收購AirTrunk，充分展現出投資者對已持有電網接入許可與核准證照的成熟業者的強烈興趣。

  ## 法規背景

  Dataspace的發展計畫，是在澳洲政府於2026年3月推出新期望框架的背景下成形的。澳洲政府就新建及擴建資料中心與人工智慧基礎設施，公布了五項全國性期望，涵蓋國家利益、能源、用水、就業及本地創新等面向。這些期望並未創設新的法律義務，但遵循這些期望，預計將有助於在監管優先排序上取得優勢。

  ## 競爭地位與展望

  以14.5MW的合計規模，Dataspace的體量遠低於主導澳洲市場的超大規模與大型代管業者，包括NEXTDC、AirTrunk及Equinix。其既定策略是在現有據點內釋放更多容量，並在電力與土地資源最為緊俏的雪梨與墨爾本追求更多開發機會——在這兩個市場中，已取得許可的現有設施具備特別高的戰略價值。

  然而，這些潛力能否真正轉化為實際產出，將取決於電力供應情況、設施升級以支援更高密度雲端及AI工作負載的速度，以及在激烈市場中爭取客戶的能力。而此時澳洲運營容量預計將在2030年前近乎三倍增長至3.9GW，整個產業的競爭格局勢必日趨激烈。
