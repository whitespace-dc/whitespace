body_en: |
  Fitch Ratings does not see immediate oversupply risk in Malaysia's data center sector, with natural resource constraints acting as a brake on supply growth and the proximity of Johor to capacity-constrained Singapore serving as a hedge against weakening AI demand, the firm's head of Asia-Pacific infrastructure said.

  Sajal Kishore, Fitch's head of Asia-Pacific infrastructure and project finance, said the central question for investors is whether a given facility can serve cloud demand if AI-driven workloads slow. Cloud computing, not AI, is the primary driver of data center demand across Asia today, accounting for roughly 70% of current usage, with AI representing the remaining 30%, Kishore estimated. He predicted that ratio will eventually reverse.

  ## Location as the key risk variable

  Kishore said AI-related demand carries more uncertainty than cloud demand because the underlying business models and technology can evolve rapidly, potentially rendering a facility redundant. That makes location a central risk factor.

  Johor, Malaysia's primary data center hub, benefits from geographic proximity to Singapore, where land, power, and water constraints have restricted new development. Data centers in Johor can absorb cloud demand or AI inference workloads spilling over from Singapore, providing an alternative use case if AI demand weakens. Facilities built in remote locations solely for AI workloads are inherently riskier, Kishore said, because they have fewer fallback uses.

  ## Capacity scale and natural supply limits

  Malaysia could have roughly 2 gigawatts (GW) — enough to power millions of homes and representing large-scale industrial infrastructure — of operational and planned data center capacity by year-end, based on reports, Kishore estimated. That would exceed Singapore's current 1.4 GW to 1.5 GW.

  Power and water availability naturally limit how quickly new capacity can be built in Malaysia, Kishore said, reducing the risk that supply outpaces demand. As constraints emerge in Johor, new investment is likely to shift progressively to the Klang Valley and other parts of the country rather than creating an oversupply condition, he added.

  ## Policy reinforces consolidation

  Malaysian Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim announced in February that proposals for data centers in Malaysia unrelated to AI have been blocked since 2024. BMI, a research affiliate of the Fitch Group, said in a May 11 report that the policy validates a trend toward approving only larger, capital-intensive AI data centers. The effect, BMI said, is to raise barriers to entry and favor large, well-funded platforms over new entrants, particularly within Johor, as consolidation among established players continues.

  ## Financing growing more difficult

  Financing is an emerging challenge for the sector, Kishore said. Investor appetite remains strong and new data center announcements continue across the region, but the volume of capital required is large and lenders are becoming cautious about AI-related risks — particularly the risk around contract renewal once initial leases expire.

  Most Asian data center projects continue to rely on syndicated bank loans. Some Malaysian banks are willing to lend only against the initial customer contract period, Kishore noted, which forces developers to seek refinancing once those contracts mature. Over time, he expects the sector to rely more on capital markets, structured finance, and private credit, in line with funding models already common in the United States.

  Bloomberg reported, citing unnamed sources, that AirTrunk — the data center operator backed by private equity firm Blackstone — is close to filing confidentially for an initial public offering of a real estate investment trust (REIT) in Singapore. Bloomberg had previously reported in April that AirTrunk was seeking to raise approximately US$1.5 billion from the offering.

  ## Regional spillover extends beyond Johor

  The demand dynamics that drove investment into Malaysia are now spreading further across the region, Kishore said. Tokyo, Singapore, and Sydney — Asia's leading data center markets — are increasingly constrained by land, power, or water, pushing developers into secondary locations. Johor was an early beneficiary of that trend, and spillover is now extending to Batam in Indonesia and Thailand.

  Australian AI infrastructure firm Firmus Technologies said last week it would develop its first data center in Batam through a strategic partnership with Singapore-based DayOne and US chip company Nvidia. Firmus said it expects to secure up to US$30 billion in committed off-take agreements in its first six years.

  Kishore said data center expansion will naturally continue to spread to additional locations as existing markets face tightening constraints over time.
body_zh: |
  惠譽信評（Fitch Ratings）不認為馬來西亞資料中心業目前面臨立即的供過於求風險。該機構亞太基礎建設業務主管指出，天然資源的制約自然壓抑了供給成長的速度，而柔佛州緊鄰產能受限的新加坡，則可在AI需求走弱時提供對沖。

  惠譽亞太基礎建設及專案融資部門主管Sajal Kishore表示，投資人最核心的問題，在於當AI驅動的工作負載放緩時，特定設施是否仍能承接雲端需求。Kishore估計，目前在亞洲，雲端運算——而非AI——才是資料中心需求的主要驅動力，佔當前使用量約七成，AI則佔其餘三成。他預測這個比例最終將出現逆轉。

  ## 地點是關鍵風險變數

  Kishore表示，AI相關需求的不確定性高於雲端需求，原因在於背後的商業模式與技術可能快速演進，進而使某些設施淪為冗餘。這使得地點成為核心風險因素。

  馬來西亞最主要的資料中心樞紐——柔佛州，受惠於與新加坡的地理接近性。新加坡因土地、電力與水資源的制約，新建開發案受到嚴格限制。柔佛州的資料中心可承接從新加坡溢出的雲端需求或AI推論工作負載，在AI需求走弱時提供替代用途。Kishore指出，純粹為AI工作負載而建於偏遠地點的設施，本質上風險更高，因為這類設施可退而求其次的替代用途較少。

  ## 容量規模與天然供給限制

  Kishore根據相關報告估計，馬來西亞到今年底可能擁有約2吉瓦（GW）的在運營及規劃中資料中心容量——足以供應數百萬戶家庭用電，是大規模工業基礎設施的代表性指標——將超越新加坡目前的1.4至1.5 GW。

  Kishore表示，電力與水資源的供應自然限制了馬來西亞新增容量的建設速度，從而降低供給超越需求的風險。他補充，隨著柔佛州出現瓶頸，新投資可能逐步向巴生河流域（Klang Valley）及全國其他地區轉移，而非造成供過於求的局面。

  ## 政策強化整併趨勢

  馬來西亞首相拿督斯里安華·依布拉欣於今年二月宣布，自2024年起，與AI無關的資料中心提案已遭封鎖。惠譽集團旗下研究機構BMI在5月11日的報告中指出，這項政策印證了一項趨勢——當局傾向於只核准規模更大、資本密集型的AI資料中心。BMI表示，此舉的效果是提高進入門檻，在已建立的業者之間持續整併之際，有利於資金雄厚的大型平台，而非新進業者，這在柔佛州境內尤為明顯。

  ## 融資難度上升

  Kishore表示，融資是這個產業正在浮現的挑戰。投資人的胃口依然強勁，全區域的資料中心新案公告持續不斷，但所需資金規模龐大，且貸款機構對AI相關風險愈來愈審慎——尤其是初始租約到期後的合約續簽風險。

  亞洲大多數資料中心專案仍依賴銀行聯貸。Kishore指出，部分馬來西亞銀行只願意在初始客戶合約期間內提供融資，這迫使開發商在合約到期後必須尋求再融資。他預期，隨著時間推移，這個產業將更加仰賴資本市場、結構性融資與私募信貸，與美國已相當普遍的融資模式接軌。

  彭博社引述不具名人士報導，由私募股權公司黑石集團（Blackstone）支持的資料中心營運商AirTrunk，即將在新加坡秘密申請不動產投資信託（REIT）首次公開募股（IPO）。彭博社先前於四月報導，AirTrunk尋求透過此次募股籌集約15億美元。

  ## 區域溢出效應擴及柔佛州以外

  Kishore表示，推動投資湧入馬來西亞的需求動能，如今正進一步蔓延至整個區域。東京、新加坡與雪梨——亞洲最重要的資料中心市場——日益受到土地、電力或水資源的制約，促使開發商轉向次級市場。柔佛州是這波趨勢的早期受益者，而溢出效應如今已延伸至印尼的巴淡島（Batam）及泰國。

  澳洲AI基礎建設公司Firmus Technologies上週表示，將透過與新加坡DayOne及美國晶片公司輝達（Nvidia）的策略合作，在巴淡島開發其首座資料中心。Firmus表示，預計在前六年內取得最高達300億美元的承購協議承諾。

  Kishore表示，隨著現有市場長期面臨日益收緊的限制，資料中心的擴張自然將持續蔓延至更多地點。
