body_en: |
  Microsoft has decided not to renew its contract with Oracle to rent AI computing capacity and has been gradually unwinding its use of Oracle's resources in recent months, U.S. media reported November 6, citing people familiar with the matter.

  The termination of the Oracle arrangement comes as Microsoft is also increasing scrutiny of its broader set of partnerships with neocloud providers — specialist cloud companies that sell compute capacity to large-scale cloud operators — following data center delivery delays, engineering failures, and mounting regulatory pressure, according to a separate U.S. media report from November 5.

  Oracle's capacity can no longer satisfy Microsoft's current requirements, the reports said, implying the problem is that Oracle's hardware runs on older-generation Nvidia graphics processing units (GPUs).

  Oracle's spokesperson told Reuters that demand for OCI — Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, the company's public cloud platform — "remains strong, including demand for older GPUs."

  ## Neocloud Spending Under Review

  Microsoft has spent more than $80 billion competing for AI compute resources, forging partnerships with neocloud operators including Nscale, Nebius, CoreWeave, and IREN. Those partnerships, valued at tens of billions of dollars, are now under heightened scrutiny, the reports said.

  People familiar with the matter told the publication that Microsoft intends to reduce its neocloud partner list, concentrate on a small group of key suppliers, and expand its own data center capacity in parallel.

  ## OpenAI Financing Contracts

  The developments arrive as the broader AI sector confronts growing regulatory risk. The U.S. Congress has shifted, with the Democratic Party now holding both the Senate and the House of Representatives, raising the prospect of stricter AI industry oversight.

  OpenAI, which is working toward a public offering, has encountered fundraising difficulties in this environment. Reports from late October said OpenAI had cut the target for its current funding round by $10 billion. Media reported on November 6 that multiple major investors in that same round have significantly reduced their planned contributions.

  SoftBank, the Japanese telecommunications conglomerate and one of OpenAI's most prominent backers, plans to invest only hundreds of millions of dollars in the current round, far below previous commitments that ran to tens of billions of dollars. SoftBank has invested approximately $65 billion in OpenAI to date, including $30 billion in a funding round completed in March of this year.

  微軟已決定不再續約向甲骨文租用AI算力，並在近幾個月來逐步縮減對甲骨文資源的使用。美國媒體11月6日援引知情人士說法報導了這一消息。

  終止甲骨文合約一事，恰逢微軟也正加強審視其與新興雲端（neocloud）業者之間更廣泛的合作關係——新興雲端業者是指專門向大型雲端營運商銷售算力的雲端公司。美國媒體11月5日的另一篇報導指出，微軟此舉肇因於資料中心交付延遲、工程失誤，以及日益沉重的監管壓力。

  相關報導指出，甲骨文的算力已無法滿足微軟當前的需求，言下之意是甲骨文的硬體仍搭載較舊世代的輝達繪圖處理器（GPU）。

  甲骨文發言人向路透社表示，OCI（Oracle Cloud Infrastructure，即甲骨文公有雲平台）「需求依然強勁，包括對舊世代GPU的需求」。

  ## 新興雲端支出遭全面審查

  微軟已投入逾800億美元搶佔AI算力資源，並與Nscale、Nebius、CoreWeave、IREN等新興雲端業者建立合作關係。這些合計數百億美元的合作案，如今面臨更嚴格的審查。

  知情人士向該媒體透露，微軟打算縮減新興雲端夥伴名單，集中深耕少數核心供應商，同時也將同步擴建自有資料中心產能。

  ## OpenAI融資合約

  上述發展出現之際，整個AI產業正面臨日益上升的監管風險。美國國會政治版圖生變，民主黨如今同時掌控參議院與眾議院，外界預期AI產業將面臨更嚴格的監理。

  正積極籌備上市的OpenAI，在此環境下遭遇融資困境。10月底的報導指出，OpenAI已將本輪融資目標下調100億美元；11月6日又有媒體報導，同一輪融資中的多位大型投資人已大幅削減原定出資金額。

  日本電信集團軟銀身為OpenAI最具代表性的支持者之一，據報導計畫在本輪融資中僅投入數億美元，遠低於此前承諾的數百億美元規模。軟銀迄今已對OpenAI投入約650億美元，其中包括今年3月完成的一輪融資所投入的300億美元。
