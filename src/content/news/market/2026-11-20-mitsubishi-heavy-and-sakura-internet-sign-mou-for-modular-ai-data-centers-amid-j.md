body_en: |
  Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) and SAKURA internet signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) on 18 November 2026 to explore modular artificial intelligence (AI) data centres in Japan, with the stated aim of accelerating construction and improving energy efficiency as the country's data centre market strains against chronic electricity shortfalls.

  The partnership will combine MHI's expertise in power, cooling and security systems with SAKURA internet's cloud and data centre operations. The two companies plan to develop standardised infrastructure designs and modular construction methods, though SAKURA internet confirmed no decisions have been made on development locations or timelines.

  ## Japan's market and the electricity constraint

  Japan's data centre market generated $23.4 billion in revenue in 2024 and is forecast to reach $33.4 billion by 2030, a compound annual growth rate of 6.7% from 2025, according to JLL's February 2026 Japan Data Centre Market Opportunities report.

  Electricity access is the defining constraint on that growth. Securing power for data centre developments can take eight to 10 years in Greater Tokyo and three to five years in Greater Osaka, JLL estimates. MHI said infrastructure supply is already trailing workload demand, characterising energy — both power and cooling — as "the current scaling constraint" for AI data centres.

  ## How modular construction is intended to help

  Under the proposed approach, power and cooling modules would be manufactured and tested off-site before delivery, reducing on-site construction work and simplifying commissioning. Capacity could then be added incrementally as demand grows. MHI said the method delivers "predictable quality" and reduced timelines by integrating power, cooling and control systems from the earliest design stages rather than developing them separately.

  Modular construction would not, however, eliminate delays in obtaining grid connections. Electricity availability remains a critical variable in determining when any new facility could come online, MHI acknowledged.

  ## Geographic concentration and government pressure to diversify

  Around 90% of Japan's data centres are located in Greater Tokyo and Greater Osaka, according to the JLL analysis. The Japanese government is pushing for regional diversification through two programmes: the Digital Infrastructure Development Plan 2030 and the Watt-Bit Collaboration programme, which coordinates electricity and telecommunications network development to support new facilities in regions with greater electricity availability.

  A modular approach could support that goal by enabling capacity to be deployed closer to regions where infrastructure constraints are less acute, the companies said.

  Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) is also tightening energy efficiency standards. Under the Energy Conservation and Non-Fossil Energy Conversion Act, qualifying data centres will need to achieve a power usage effectiveness (PUE) of 1.3 or better within two years of operation starting from fiscal 2029. PUE measures total facility electricity consumption relative to the energy used by IT equipment.

  ## SAKURA internet's role in Japan's sovereign AI push

  The partnership is framed partly around Japan's interest in domestic AI and cloud sovereignty — keeping data and AI workloads under national control rather than routing them through foreign infrastructure.

  SAKURA internet is a central figure in that effort. In March 2026, it became the first domestic cloud provider formally approved for Japan's Government Cloud programme, which underpins cloud services for government systems. Under the Economic Security Promotion Act, METI authorised GPU cloud subsidies totalling ¥72.5 billion ($453.4 million) in 2024; SAKURA internet received approximately ¥50.1 billion ($313.3 million) of that total — about 69% — distributed among five beneficiaries.

  SAKURA internet said the MHI partnership is not designed to displace international hyperscalers but to strengthen Japan's domestic cloud and AI ecosystem by giving organisations greater control over their data and workloads.

  ## Implications for the Asia-Pacific region

  MHI said many Asia-Pacific markets face the same combination of pressures as Japan: rising AI computing demand, constrained power availability, compressed deployment timelines and growing interest in digital sovereignty. The company said this environment is driving increased regional interest in sovereign AI and cloud infrastructure.

  No specific sites, investment figures or project timelines have been announced under the MoU.
body_zh: |
  三菱重工業（MHI）與SAKURA Internet於2026年11月18日簽署合作備忘錄（MoU），探索在日本共同開發模組化人工智慧（AI）資料中心的可能性，目標是加速建設並提升能源效率，以因應日本資料中心市場長期面臨的電力嚴重短缺困境。

  此次合作將整合三菱重工在電力、冷卻與安全系統方面的專業技術，以及SAKURA Internet在雲端與資料中心營運方面的豐富經驗。兩家公司計畫共同研發標準化基礎設施設計與模組化建設方式，但SAKURA Internet確認，目前尚未就開發地點或時程做出任何決定。

  ## 日本市場與電力制約

  根據世邦魏理仕顧問公司（JLL）2026年2月發布的《日本資料中心市場機會》報告，日本資料中心市場2024年營收達234億美元，預計至2030年將成長至334億美元，2025年起的複合年均成長率為6.7%。

  電力取得是制約市場成長的關鍵因素。JLL估計，在大東京地區，資料中心開發取得電力供應可能需要長達八至十年；在大阪都會區則需三至五年。三菱重工表示，基礎設施供給已跟不上工作負載需求，並將能源——包括電力與冷卻——定性為當前AI資料中心擴張的「瓶頸所在」。

  ## 模組化建設如何發揮作用

  依照此次合作的構想，電力與冷卻模組將在廠外完成製造與測試後再運抵現場，藉此減少現場施工工作量並簡化試運轉程序，容量也可隨需求成長逐步擴充。三菱重工表示，透過在最早的設計階段便整合電力、冷卻與控制系統，而非分開研發，此方法可提供「可預期的品質」，並有效縮短建設時程。

  然而，模組化建設並無法消除取得電網連接所需的漫長等待。三菱重工坦承，電力供應仍是決定任何新設施何時能夠上線的關鍵變數。

  ## 地理集中與政府推動分散化壓力

  根據JLL的分析，日本約有90%的資料中心集中於大東京地區與大阪都會區。日本政府正透過兩項計畫積極推動區域多元化：「數位基礎設施發展計畫2030」，以及「瓦特位元協作計畫」（Watt-Bit Collaboration）——後者協調電力與電信網路的開發，以支援電力供給較為充裕地區的新設施建設。

  兩家公司表示，模組化方式有助於實現此目標，使容量得以部署在基礎設施限制較不嚴峻的地區。

  日本經濟產業省（METI）也在加強能源效率標準。根據《節約能源及非化石能源轉換法》，符合資格的資料中心須於2029財年起開始營運後兩年內，將電力使用效率（PUE）提升至1.3以下。PUE衡量設施整體耗電量相對於IT設備用電量的比率。

  ## SAKURA Internet在日本主權AI推進中的角色

  此次合作亦呼應日本對國內AI與雲端主權的高度重視——讓資料與AI工作負載留在國內掌控之下，而非透過境外基礎設施處理。

  SAKURA Internet在此一努力中扮演核心角色。2026年3月，它成為首家正式獲准加入日本「政府雲端」計畫的國內雲端業者，該計畫為政府系統的雲端服務提供基礎支撐。根據《經濟安全保障推進法》，經濟產業省於2024年核准的GPU雲端補助款合計達725億日圓（約4億5,340萬美元）；在五家受益業者中，SAKURA Internet獲得約501億日圓（約3億1,330萬美元），佔總額約69%。

  SAKURA Internet表示，與三菱重工的合作並非旨在取代國際超大規模雲端業者，而是透過讓各機構對其資料與工作負載擁有更大掌控權，進一步強化日本的本土雲端與AI生態系。

  ## 對亞太地區的影響

  三菱重工表示，亞太地區許多市場與日本面臨相同的多重壓力：AI運算需求攀升、電力供給受限、部署時程壓縮，以及對數位主權日益增長的重視。該公司指出，此一環境正推動亞太區域各地對主權AI與雲端基礎設施的興趣持續升溫。

  目前，兩家公司尚未就此備忘錄宣布任何具體地點、投資金額或專案時程。
