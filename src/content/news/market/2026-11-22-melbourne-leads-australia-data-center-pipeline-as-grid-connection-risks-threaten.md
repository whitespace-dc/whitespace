body_en: |
  Melbourne has overtaken Sydney as the leading market in Australia's data center development pipeline while internal government documents reveal that Victorian energy officials warned the state could face a "major structural challenge" to its electricity grid from the surge in proposed projects.

  Melbourne's tracked capacity — covering operational facilities and projects at all stages of development — rose by 1.7 gigawatts (GW) to 10.7 GW between April and October, according to a report by research firms DC Byte and Data Centres Australia. Sydney's tracked capacity fell by 3.6 GW to 7.8 GW over the same period. Nationally, total tracked capacity increased by 5.2 GW to 26.8 GW.

  Despite the headline pipeline figures, operational capacity — facilities actually available to customers — rose by just 0.1 GW to 1.6 GW across the country, underscoring the gap between project announcements and usable infrastructure. Early-stage proposals, where project sizes and schedules are most susceptible to change, account for 19.6 GW, or about 73% of the national total. Only 0.4 GW was under construction, defined as data halls undergoing mechanical and electrical fit-out.

  ## Grid warnings from inside government

  The scale of proposed investment prompted formal concern from officials before the pipeline reached its current size. Australian Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action officials briefed Victoria's energy minister in March on risks posed by plans to attract up to A$25 billion (S$21.4 billion) in data center investment to the state, according to documents obtained by The Age newspaper under freedom of information laws.

  The briefing warned the investment plan could cause a "step change" scenario — a term used to describe a sudden surge in energy demand — and that the government lacked oversight over how such projects would affect the grid. The documents also cited growing concern from other energy users about data center expansion.

  In response, the government approved measures requiring power distributors to alert the energy minister before making grid connections for any project exceeding 30 megawatts (MW), roughly the scale of a mid-sized data center campus. Victoria separately announced in September that new data centers would need to provide their own renewable energy supply.

  ## Grid connections as the binding constraint

  Analysts and researchers identify the same bottleneck as the principal risk to delivery. Dr. William Lee, senior research director for computing systems platforms and technologies at research firm IDC, said the problem is not whether Australia can generate enough electricity but whether individual sites can secure physical connections to the grid.

  "Australia's challenge before 2030 is connecting individual sites, not generating enough electricity," he told Data Center Knowledge.

  DC Byte forecasts 3.9 GW of operational capacity nationally by 2030, with the figure projected to rise from 1.8 GW at the end of 2026 to 2.3 GW in 2027, 2.7 GW in 2028, 3.4 GW in 2029, and 3.9 GW by year-end 2030. Sydney is projected to reach 2.0 GW by that date, ahead of Melbourne's 1.4 GW despite Melbourne's larger development pipeline. Sydney and Melbourne together are forecast to account for approximately 3.4 GW of the 3.9 GW national total.

  IDC's Lee said local connection works for large campuses in Western Sydney and Melbourne's west represent the largest single risk to that forecast. Matthew Lee, executive director and co-head of Australian data centers at property advisory firm JLL, also identified proposed terminal stations — high-voltage substations that connect large campuses to the transmission network — as a major delivery risk, warning that delays to those infrastructure projects could prevent facilities from opening even where customers are ready to occupy them.

  IDC's Lee said a credible energization date — the moment a facility first draws power — requires a signed connection agreement, confirmed schedules for transmission or terminal-station works, equipment delivery slots, and consent for network construction. DC Byte's "committed" capacity classification, which rose nationally from 3.1 GW to 5.2 GW, signals confidence in eventual delivery but does not establish when each phase will open.

  ## Sydney's pipeline falls while committed capacity rises

  Sydney's decline in total tracked capacity was driven by withdrawn or revised early-stage proposals, not by setbacks among projects closer to delivery. Early-stage capacity in Sydney fell from 8.6 GW to 4.2 GW, while committed capacity rose from 1.9 GW to 2.6 GW. Sydney retained more operational capacity than Melbourne, at 0.9 GW versus 0.5 GW.

  JLL's Lee said the shift in Sydney's pipeline reflects changes in timing rather than a permanent loss of megawatts. New South Wales introduced a planning framework in August that offers developers a commitment to assessment within 75 days for projects meeting defined standards covering environmental performance, energy and water supply, community infrastructure, and skills. That pathway can accelerate planning decisions but does not resolve the grid infrastructure needed to bring facilities online.

  Melbourne moved 1.2 GW into committed capacity during the reporting period, compared with 0.7 GW in Sydney. DC Byte analyst Teresa Tan said Victoria's Development Facilitation Pathway helped eligible projects advance. JLL's Lee cited artificial intelligence demand as an additional driver behind Melbourne's growth. Melbourne's early-stage capacity rose by a more modest 0.3 GW to 7.8 GW, leaving the majority of its pipeline subject to significant delivery uncertainty.

  DC Byte tracked 163 operational facilities and 97 proposed new projects nationwide, seven more than in April. Of those proposed projects, 52 have announced individual capacity above 100 MW. By comparison, only four operating facilities currently exceed that threshold.

  ## Community opposition compounds delivery risk

  Beyond grid infrastructure, community resistance has begun to affect project timelines in Victoria. NextDC, one of Australia's largest data center operators, cut its full-year earnings guidance by one-third and placed development plans on hold after protests delayed power supply to a major project in Melbourne. The company subsequently stated it would not develop sites near residential areas.

  Bloomberg has previously reported that more than three-quarters of data centers planned around Australia's biggest cities are at risk from stretched power grids and local opposition, a situation the report identified as threatening the country's ambition to become an artificial intelligence hub for the Asia-Pacific region.

  Simon Croft, chief executive of industry and policy at the Housing Industry Association, said on Nov. 21 that data centers would be a significant issue for the Victorian government in state elections scheduled for Nov. 28, particularly in inner-city suburbs earmarked for large facilities.

  DC Byte noted that new state policies and an apparent pause in approvals ahead of the election could affect future forecasts for Victoria, potentially adding further uncertainty to a pipeline in which most capacity remains early-stage.

  ## Regional expansion still faces infrastructure gaps

  Locations outside Sydney and Melbourne now account for 31% of the development pipeline, up from 19% two years ago. Regional New South Wales added 1.3 GW of early-stage capacity; regional Victoria added 0.7 GW. Early-stage projects are also active in Queensland, Tasmania, South Australia, and Western Australia, with growing developer interest around Morwell, east of Melbourne.

  JLL's Lee said large-scale power availability in metropolitan Sydney and Melbourne has some form of end date, making regional markets increasingly relevant over time. He cautioned, however, that connectivity, construction logistics, supply chains, and staffing remain less proven at large scale in those markets.

  IDC's Lee said credible regional sites require access to high-voltage infrastructure, renewable generation, a signed grid connection, and a water supply plan, and that few locations currently combine all of those requirements for large deployments.

  Customer commitments cover approximately 90% of operational and under-construction capacity, according to DC Byte's Tan — a measure that applies to the most advanced portion of the market, not to the full 26.8 GW national total. JLL's Lee said a high proportion of capacity arriving in 2027 and 2028 is expected to be pre-committed, though larger supply additions from 2029 onward could begin to shift that balance.
body_zh: |
  墨爾本已超越雪梨，躍居澳洲資料中心開發管線首位，同時內部政府文件揭露，維多利亞州能源官員曾警告，擬議中的投資浪潮恐對當地電網造成「重大結構性挑戰」。

  根據研究機構DC Byte與Data Centres Australia聯合發布的報告，墨爾本的追蹤容量——涵蓋已投入運營的設施及各開發階段的專案——在四月至十月間增加1.7 GW（十億瓦），達到10.7 GW。同期，雪梨的追蹤容量下滑3.6 GW至7.8 GW。全澳洲追蹤容量總計增加5.2 GW，達到26.8 GW。

  儘管管線數字亮眼，真正可供客戶使用的實際運營容量，全國僅增加0.1 GW至1.6 GW，凸顯出專案公告與可用基礎設施之間的巨大落差。早期規劃提案——專案規模與時程最易變動的階段——佔19.6 GW，約為全國總量的73%。至於已進入「施工中」狀態（即資料機房正在進行機電安裝）的容量，僅有0.4 GW。

  ## 政府內部的電網警告

  大規模投資提案早在管線規模達到目前水準之前，便已引發官方正式關切。根據《時代報》依資訊公開法取得的文件，澳洲能源、環境暨氣候行動部官員於三月就計畫吸引最多250億澳幣（約214億新加坡幣）資料中心投資至維州一事，向能源部長進行了簡報，說明相關風險。

  簡報警告，這項投資計畫可能引發「階梯式變化」（step change）情境——用於描述能源需求突然激增的術語——並指出政府對此類專案將如何影響電網缺乏監管機制。文件同時引述其他電力用戶對資料中心持續擴張的日益憂慮。

  為此，政府批准了相關措施，要求電力配電業者在為任何超過30 MW（百萬瓦）的專案——大約相當於中型資料中心園區的規模——進行電網接入前，必須事先通知能源部長。維多利亞州也於九月另行宣布，新設立的資料中心必須自行提供再生能源供應。

  ## 電網接入成為關鍵瓶頸

  分析師與研究人員一致指出，電網接入是影響如期交付的首要風險。IDC研究機構運算系統平台暨技術高級研究總監William Lee博士表示，澳洲面臨的問題不在於能否產生足夠的電力，而在於個別場址能否取得實體的電網接入。

  「澳洲在2030年前的挑戰，是如何為各個場址完成電網接入，而非發電量不足，」他向《Data Center Knowledge》表示。

  DC Byte預測，全澳洲的實際運營容量將在2030年達到3.9 GW。具體而言，預計從2026年底的1.8 GW出發，2027年達到2.3 GW，2028年達到2.7 GW，2029年達到3.4 GW，2030年底達到3.9 GW。在市場排名上，儘管墨爾本的開發管線規模較大，雪梨預計屆時仍以2.0 GW領先墨爾本的1.4 GW。雪梨與墨爾本合計，預計約佔全澳3.9 GW總量中的3.4 GW。

  IDC的William Lee指出，西雪梨及墨爾本西部大型園區的本地電網接入工程，是上述預測的最大單一風險所在。房地產顧問公司JLL澳洲資料中心業務執行董事暨聯席主管Matthew Lee，也點出擬議中的「終端站」（terminal station）——將大型園區連接至輸電網路的高壓變電所——是重大的交付風險，警告此類基礎設施一旦延誤，即便客戶已準備好進駐，設施仍可能無法如期開幕。

  IDC的William Lee表示，要確立一個可信的「通電日期」（即設施首次從電網取電的時間點），必須同時具備：已簽署的接入協議、確認輸電或終端站工程的施工時程、設備交付時間窗口，以及電網施工的相關許可。DC Byte的「已確認」容量分類——全國從3.1 GW增至5.2 GW——反映了市場對最終交付的信心，但並未確立各階段的具體開放時間。

  ## 雪梨管線萎縮，已確認容量同步攀升

  雪梨追蹤容量的下滑，係由早期階段提案撤回或調整所致，而非較接近交付的專案遭遇挫折。雪梨早期階段容量從8.6 GW降至4.2 GW，而已確認容量則從1.9 GW升至2.6 GW。雪梨的實際運營容量仍高於墨爾本，分別為0.9 GW與0.5 GW。

  JLL的Matthew Lee表示，雪梨管線的變動，反映的是時程調整，而非永久性的兆瓦流失。新南威爾斯州於八月推出了一套規劃框架，針對符合環境績效、能源與供水、社區基礎設施及技能培訓等特定標準的專案，承諾在75天內完成審查。此一快速通道可加速規劃決策，但無法解決讓設施上線所需的電網基礎設施問題。

  在同一報告期間，墨爾本有1.2 GW進入已確認容量，高於雪梨的0.7 GW。DC Byte分析師Teresa Tan表示，維多利亞州的「開發促進通道」（Development Facilitation Pathway）協助符合資格的專案加快推進。JLL的Matthew Lee另外引述人工智慧需求，作為推動墨爾本成長的額外動力。墨爾本的早期階段容量僅小幅增加0.3 GW至7.8 GW，意味著其管線大部分仍面臨相當程度的交付不確定性。

  DC Byte全澳共追蹤163個已運營設施及97個擬議新建專案，較四月增加7個。在這些擬議專案中，52個已公告的單體容量超過100 MW。相比之下，目前超越這一門檻的運營設施僅有4個。

  ## 社區反對加劇交付風險

  除電網基礎設施外，維多利亞州也開始出現社區抵制現象，影響專案時程。澳洲最大資料中心營運商之一NextDC，在抗議活動延誤其墨爾本重大專案的供電後，將全年獲利預測下調三分之一，並暫停相關開發計畫。該公司其後表示，未來將不在住宅區附近開發場址。

  彭博社此前報導，澳洲各大城市周邊計畫興建的資料中心，逾四分之三面臨電網容量吃緊及在地反對的雙重壓力，報告指此一情況正威脅澳洲成為亞太地區人工智慧樞紐的雄心。

  住宅建築業協會（Housing Industry Association）政策與產業執行長Simon Croft於11月21日表示，資料中心議題將成為維多利亞州政府在11月28日州選舉中的重要課題，尤其是在被劃定為大型設施預定地的市區內環郊區。

  DC Byte指出，新的州政府政策，以及選舉前明顯趨緩的審批步調，可能影響維多利亞州的未來預測，為本已多數停留在早期階段的管線帶來額外的不確定性。

  ## 區域市場擴張仍面臨基礎設施缺口

  雪梨與墨爾本以外的地點，目前佔開發管線的31%，較兩年前的19%顯著提升。新南威爾斯州區域市場新增1.3 GW的早期階段容量；維多利亞州區域市場增加0.7 GW。昆士蘭州、塔斯馬尼亞州、南澳洲及西澳洲均有早期階段專案積極推進，墨爾本東部的莫維爾（Morwell）一帶也引發越來越多開發商的關注。

  JLL的Matthew Lee指出，雪梨及墨爾本都會區的大規模電力供應，在某種程度上存在期限，使得區域市場的重要性隨時間推移日益突出。然而他也提醒，這些市場在網路連接、施工物流、供應鏈與人力配置等方面，大規模部署的可行性仍有待充分驗證。

  IDC的William Lee表示，要成為可信的區域場址，必須同時具備高壓輸電基礎設施、再生能源發電來源、已簽署的電網接入協議，以及供水計畫，目前能同時滿足以上所有條件、適合大規模部署的地點仍屬少數。

  根據DC Byte的Teresa Tan，客戶承諾涵蓋約90%的「已運營」與「施工中」容量——此一比例反映的是市場中進展最為成熟的部分，而非全澳26.8 GW的總量。JLL的Matthew Lee表示，2027年及2028年到位的容量中，預計將有高比例已事先預訂；但自2029年起，隨著供給規模持續擴大，這一平衡態勢可能開始出現轉變。
