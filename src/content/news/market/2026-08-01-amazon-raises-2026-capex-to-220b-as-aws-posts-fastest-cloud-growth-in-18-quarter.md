body_en: |
  Amazon raised its 2026 capital expenditure forecast to approximately $220 billion on Thursday and reported that its Amazon Web Services (AWS) cloud division grew 36.7% year over year in the second quarter to $42.2 billion in revenue, the unit's fastest growth in 18 quarters. Chief Executive Andy Jassy said the company expects AI infrastructure capacity to remain constrained through 2027, with committed demand already extending into 2028.

  "We will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026," Jassy said on the earnings call. "I believe this dynamic will also be true in 2027, too. In fact, the demand we already have for 2028 is striking."

  The updated capex projection is $20 billion above the roughly $200 billion Amazon had previously guided. Jassy attributed the increase primarily to rising costs for memory chips, hard drives, and solid-state drives rather than a broader acceleration of construction. "I think it's no secret right now to any company in the world that there are inflated prices right now on some of the components like memory, hard drives, and SSDs," he said. Amazon also flagged "resource and supply volatility, including for memory chips" among the risks facing its business.

  ## AWS financial results

  AWS posted $16.62 billion in operating income for the quarter, a 36.8% operating margin, up from $10.2 billion in the second quarter of 2025. The division now operates at a $169 billion annualized revenue run rate. AWS revenue beat the average analyst estimate of approximately $40.5 billion to $40.6 billion, according to data compiled by Bloomberg and the London Stock Exchange Group.

  Amazon as a whole reported $200.6 billion in total revenue for the quarter ended June 30, a 20% increase year over year and the first time the company has exceeded $200 billion in a single quarter. Amazon spent more than $53 billion on property and equipment during the period, including proceeds from some asset sales.

  Both Amazon's AI business and its chips business surpassed $25 billion annual revenue run rates, with both growing at triple-digit percentages year over year, the company said. AWS's contracted backlog reached $496 billion, also growing at a triple-digit rate year over year, giving the company unusual forward visibility into infrastructure demand.

  Free cash flow turned sharply negative. Amazon reported an outflow of $7.6 billion for the trailing 12 months at quarter's end, compared with positive $18.2 billion for the equivalent period a year earlier.

  Amazon shares rose more than 9% in after-hours trading Thursday after closing at $235.50 in New York. The stock has gained 2% in 2026 to date.

  ## Why spending is rising

  Jassy framed the capex increase as a cost-side phenomenon, not a demand-side one. Sid Nag, chief executive and chief research officer at Tekonyx, said AWS's commentary points to the same conclusion: "The increase wasn't driven by building more data centers, but by the rising cost of equipping them, particularly high-bandwidth memory, which has become one of the most constrained and expensive components in AI infrastructure."

  Nag added that the results confirm the AI industry remains in an infrastructure deployment phase rather than an optimization phase. "When AWS says demand already extends into 2028 while capacity remains constrained through 2027, it signals that compute, power, networking, and data center construction have become the primary bottlenecks, not customer interest," he said. "The next phase of AI investment will be shaped as much by silicon and memory economics as by concrete, steel, and power."

  Amazon said it is on pace to double its power capacity by the end of 2027 compared with 2025. Jassy said most of Amazon's planned 2027 capacity has already been reserved by customers.

  ## The economics of data center investment

  Jassy devoted a significant portion of the earnings call to explaining the expected return profile on Amazon's infrastructure spending. Data centers require capital roughly two years before they begin generating revenue but can remain productive for more than 30 years without requiring that start-up capital again, he said. Servers and networking equipment operate on a shorter cycle: they are typically purchased a few months before deployment, reach break-even in less than three years, and have a useful life of at least five to six years.

  Most of Amazon's AI capacity is contracted for terms of at least five years, Jassy said, meaning the company expects to generate substantial free cash flow from its server assets in the two to three years after those assets break even. "As we get a few years out and the revenue growth outpaces the incremental CapEx growth, the resulting revenue, free cash flow, and return on invested capital are very compelling," Jassy said.

  He added that AWS could "very possibly" become a trillion-dollar annual revenue business.

  Holger Mueller, vice president and principal analyst at Constellation Research, placed Amazon's spending in a broader competitive context. "We are in the gold rush era — you need to build it to sell," Mueller said. "All vendors are trying to assemble the investment needed for more capacity. Amazon is no exception."

  Mueller also noted that Amazon faces a constraint some rivals do not: it must continue funding the capital needs of its retail business while sustaining record AI infrastructure investment.

  ## Trainium adoption and chip ambitions

  Amazon highlighted growing adoption of Trainium, its custom AI accelerator chip. Jassy said Anthropic and OpenAI have made multi-year, multi-gigawatt commitments to Trainium, alongside an expanding list of AI startups and enterprise customers. He added that Amazon is actively exploring the possibility of selling Trainium chips for deployment in third-party data centers after receiving interest from customers wanting to use the processors outside AWS.

  "We're actively having those conversations and exploring, and I expect there's a real chance we'll do that in the future," Jassy said.

  Matt Kimball, vice president and principal analyst at Moor Insights & Strategy, called the Anthropic and OpenAI commitments one of the strongest endorsements yet for Amazon's AI accelerator. "Two of the frontier labs putting multi-year, multi-gigawatt commitments behind Trainium is about the strongest validation a piece of silicon can get," Kimball said. "This training scale is where accelerators are truly tested — they either hold up, or they don't."

  Kimball added that the commitments challenge the perception that Nvidia and AMD have effectively locked up the AI training market. He cautioned, however, that a move into merchant silicon — selling chips directly to outside customers rather than consuming them within AWS — would require go-to-market capabilities Amazon does not currently operate, including public roadmaps, field engineering, lifecycle support, system integration, and channel infrastructure.

  ## AI driving traditional cloud growth

  Amazon argued that AI workloads are increasing demand for conventional cloud infrastructure rather than displacing it. Jassy said workloads including reinforcement learning, post-training, agent orchestration, storage, and vector databases rely heavily on traditional compute and storage services, and that AWS's Graviton processors are benefiting as customers deploy more AI applications.

  "We're seeing strong growth across both AI and non-AI," Jassy said. "Growth in one is driving growth in the other."

  Chief Financial Officer Brian Olsavsky echoed that view, saying enterprises migrating traditional workloads are also expanding core cloud consumption alongside AI deployments. Mueller said that aligns with enterprise patterns in which AI handles intent and orchestration while conventional cloud infrastructure and databases carry out the underlying execution.

  AWS expanded its partner roster during the period, signing large cloud infrastructure and chip supply agreements with OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Pinterest, and Snowflake, the company said.

  ## Industry context and significance

  Amazon's results arrive as the broader technology industry faces scrutiny over whether outlays of this scale will produce commensurate returns. Industry-wide AI-related capital expenditure in 2026 is expected to exceed $700 billion, a level of investment that has pushed free cash flow negative at multiple large technology companies. Alphabet reported negative free cash flow for the first time since its initial public offering, while rival Microsoft reported the fastest cloud growth since 2022 and indicated it would hold the line on new capital spending this year.

  Sky Canaves, an analyst at Emarketer, said investors are unlikely to be deterred by Amazon's negative cash flow given the pace of AWS growth and cost controls elsewhere in the business.

  Dan Morgan, portfolio manager at Synovus Trust, said the results addressed a central concern among investors. "The market was worried about AWS losing market share, but now those worries have been dispelled," Morgan said. "Once again it's proven that AWS's leading position remains solid, and the AI wave is benefiting all major players simultaneously."

  Steven Dickens, chief executive and principal analyst at HyperFrame Research, said the numbers reflect a strategic refocus by Amazon. "With the recent announcements around focusing away from model development and a focus on model hosting, the company is back to what it does best, namely infrastructure, and that focus is showing in the results," Dickens said.

  ## Guidance

  For the third quarter, Amazon projected total revenue of $197 billion to $202 billion and operating income of $22.5 billion to $26.5 billion. Analysts on average had estimated revenue of $203.9 billion and operating income of $25.1 billion, according to Bloomberg. Jassy said most of the 2027 capacity AWS is building has already been reserved by customers, and that a significant share of 2028 capacity has also been committed.
body_zh: |
  亞馬遜週四將2026年資本支出預測上調至約2,200億美元，並公布旗下Amazon Web Services（AWS）雲端部門第二季營收達422億美元，年增36.7%，創下該部門18季以來最快成長速度。執行長安迪·賈西（Andy Jassy）表示，公司預計AI基礎設施容量將持續緊缺至2027年，且承諾的需求已延伸至2028年。

  「2026年我們仍將無法滿足所有需求，」賈西在法說會上表示。「我相信這個情況在2027年同樣成立。事實上，我們已收到的2028年需求規模令人震驚。」

  最新的資本支出預測比亞馬遜先前指引的約2,000億美元高出200億美元。賈西將此次上調主要歸因於記憶體晶片、硬碟與固態硬碟（SSD）成本攀升，而非更大規模的建設加速。「全球任何企業現在都很清楚，記憶體、硬碟、SSD等部分零組件的價格目前確實受到通膨壓力，」他說道。亞馬遜也在業務風險清單中列入「資源與供應波動，包括記憶體晶片」等項目。

  ## AWS財務表現

  AWS本季創下166.2億美元的營業利益，營業利益率達36.8%，高於2025年第二季的102億美元。該部門目前年化營收規模達1,690億美元。AWS營收超越彭博社與倫敦證券交易所集團匯整的分析師平均預估約405至406億美元。

  亞馬遜整體公布截至6月30日的季度總營收達2,006億美元，年增20%，是公司首度在單季突破2,000億美元門檻。亞馬遜本季在廠房與設備上的支出逾530億美元，其中包含部分資產出售所得。

  亞馬遜表示，其AI業務與晶片業務的年化營收均已突破250億美元，兩者年增率均達三位數百分比。AWS的合約積壓訂單達4,960億美元，年增率同樣達三位數，使公司對基礎設施需求擁有罕見的前瞻能見度。

  自由現金流則急轉直下。亞馬遜公布截至季末的過去12個月自由現金流流出76億美元，相比一年前同期正182億美元大幅惡化。

  亞馬遜股價週四收盤報235.50美元，盤後交易上漲逾9%。該股今年迄今漲幅為2%。

  ## 支出增加的原因

  賈西將資本支出增加定位為成本面現象，而非需求面驅動。Tekonyx執行長兼首席研究長席德·納格（Sid Nag）表示，AWS的說法指向相同結論：「增加並非源於興建更多資料中心，而是源於設備採購成本上升，尤其是高頻寬記憶體——這已成為AI基礎設施中最緊缺、最昂貴的零組件之一。」

  納格補充，此次財報確認AI產業仍處於基礎設施部署階段，而非優化階段。「當AWS表示需求已延伸至2028年、而產能持續緊缺至2027年，這意味著運算、電力、網路與資料中心建設已成為主要瓶頸，而非客戶意願不足，」他說道。「AI投資的下一階段，將同時受到矽晶片與記憶體的經濟學，以及混凝土、鋼材與電力的形塑。」

  亞馬遜表示，其電力容量預計在2027年底前較2025年翻倍。賈西表示，亞馬遜規劃中的大部分2027年產能已被客戶預訂。

  ## 資料中心投資的經濟學

  賈西在法說會上花費相當篇幅說明亞馬遜基礎設施支出的預期投資回報結構。他表示，資料中心需要在開始產生營收前約兩年投入資本，但此後可在無需再次投入同等啟動資金的情況下，維持逾30年的生產力。伺服器與網路設備則遵循較短的週期：通常在部署前數個月採購，不到三年即可回本，且使用壽命至少五至六年。

  賈西表示，亞馬遜大多數AI容量的合約期限至少五年，意味著公司預計在伺服器資產回本後的兩至三年內，將產生可觀的自由現金流。「隨著我們展望未來幾年，當營收成長超過增量資本支出成長時，由此帶來的營收、自由現金流與投入資本回報率將非常亮眼，」賈西說道。

  他補充，AWS「很有可能」成為年營收兆元規模的事業。

  Constellation Research副總裁暨首席分析師霍爾格·穆勒（Holger Mueller）將亞馬遜的支出置於更廣泛的競爭脈絡中。「我們正處於淘金熱時代——你必須蓋好才能賣，」穆勒說道。「所有廠商都在努力籌集擴大產能所需的投資，亞馬遜也不例外。」

  穆勒也指出，亞馬遜面臨部分競爭對手所沒有的制約：它必須在維持創紀錄AI基礎設施投資的同時，持續為其零售業務的資本需求提供資金。

  ## Trainium晶片的採用與雄心

  亞馬遜強調其自研AI加速晶片Trainium的採用持續擴大。賈西表示，Anthropic與OpenAI已對Trainium做出多年、多GW（吉瓦）規模的承諾，另有越來越多AI新創公司與企業客戶陸續加入。他還表示，在收到客戶希望在AWS以外環境使用Trainium處理器的需求後，亞馬遜正積極探索將Trainium晶片出售給第三方資料中心部署的可能性。

  「我們正在積極進行相關洽談與探索，我預期未來我們很有可能付諸實行，」賈西說道。

  Moor Insights & Strategy副總裁暨首席分析師麥特·金柏（Matt Kimball）稱這兩大前沿實驗室的承諾，是亞馬遜AI加速器迄今獲得的最強背書之一。「兩家前沿實驗室為Trainium做出多年、多GW的承諾，這幾乎是一款矽晶片所能獲得的最強認可，」金柏說道。「這種訓練規模才是加速器真正接受考驗的地方——要麼撐得住，要麼撐不住。」

  金柏補充，這些承諾挑戰了輝達與AMD已有效壟斷AI訓練市場的既有認知。然而他也警告，若要進軍商用晶片市場——即直接向外部客戶銷售晶片，而非在AWS內部消化——將需要亞馬遜目前尚不具備的市場推廣能力，包括公開的產品路線圖、現場工程支援、全生命週期服務、系統整合，以及通路基礎設施。

  ## AI帶動傳統雲端成長

  亞馬遜表示，AI工作負載正在推動對傳統雲端基礎設施的需求，而非取代它。賈西表示，強化學習、後訓練（post-training）、代理人協作（agent orchestration）、儲存與向量資料庫等工作負載，都高度仰賴傳統運算與儲存服務，且隨著客戶部署更多AI應用，AWS的Graviton處理器也因此受益。

  「我們在AI與非AI業務上都看到強勁成長，」賈西說道。「其中一方的成長正在帶動另一方的成長。」

  財務長布萊恩·歐薩夫斯基（Brian Olsavsky）也呼應這一看法，表示正在進行傳統工作負載遷移的企業，也同步在AI部署之外擴大核心雲端消費。穆勒表示，這與企業的應用模式相符——AI負責處理意圖與協作流程，傳統雲端基礎設施與資料庫則承擔底層執行任務。

  AWS本季擴大合作夥伴陣容，與OpenAI、Anthropic、Meta、Pinterest及Snowflake簽署了大型雲端基礎設施與晶片供應協議。

  ## 產業背景與意義

  亞馬遜此次財報發布之際，科技業整體正面臨外界對此規模龐大的支出能否帶來相應回報的質疑。2026年全產業AI相關資本支出預計將超過7,000億美元，這樣的投資規模已使多家大型科技公司的自由現金流轉為負值。Alphabet自首次公開募股以來首度出現自由現金流為負的情況，競爭對手微軟則公布2022年以來最快的雲端成長速度，並表示今年將控制新增資本支出規模。

  Emarketer分析師史凱·卡納夫斯（Sky Canaves）表示，鑑於AWS的成長速度以及公司其他業務的成本管控，投資人不太可能因亞馬遜的負自由現金流而卻步。

  Synovus Trust投資組合經理丹·摩根（Dan Morgan）表示，此次財報化解了投資人的核心疑慮。「市場擔心AWS市佔率流失，但這些疑慮現在已煙消雲散，」摩根說道。「再次證明，AWS的領導地位依然穩固，AI浪潮正同步嘉惠所有主要業者。」

  HyperFrame Research執行長暨首席分析師史蒂文·狄更斯（Steven Dickens）表示，這些數字反映亞馬遜的策略性重心調整。「隨著近期宣布將重心從模型開發轉向模型託管，公司回歸到自己最擅長的領域，也就是基礎設施，而這個聚焦也在財報結果中充分展現，」狄更斯說道。

  ## 財測展望

  亞馬遜預測第三季總營收介於1,970億至2,020億美元之間，營業利益介於225億至265億美元之間。彭博社統計的分析師平均預估為營收2,039億美元、營業利益251億美元。賈西表示，AWS正在興建的大部分2027年產能已被客戶預訂，且2028年產能中也有相當大比例已獲承諾。
