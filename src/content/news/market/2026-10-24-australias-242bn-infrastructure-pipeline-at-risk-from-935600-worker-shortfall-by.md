body_en: |
  ## Labour crisis threatens $242 billion pipeline

  Australia's A$242 billion public infrastructure pipeline faces a projected cumulative workforce shortfall of 935,600 workers by 2035 if federal and state governments fail to implement a coordinated labour strategy, Master Builders Australia (MBA) chief executive Denita Wawn has warned. Infrastructure Australia (IA), the government-sanctioned independent infrastructure adviser, named data centres alongside energy, defence and water as sectors where shortages of engineers, designers and experienced project directors are already constraining delivery.

  The construction industry alone faces an immediate shortage of 141,000 workers, according to the Australian Constructors Association, which warns the gap could reach 300,000 by mid-2027 without a national response. IA's 2025 Infrastructure Market Capacity Report, published in November 2025, identifies labour as the most critical delivery risk in the country; approximately 60 percent of firms surveyed by the agency agreed with that assessment.

  ## The workforce picture

  At the end of last year, 204,000 workers were engaged across Australia's infrastructure sector. Trades workers and labourers made up 62 percent of that total, project management professionals accounted for 12 percent, and engineers, architects and scientists comprised the remaining 26 percent.

  Wawn placed the cost of inaction in stark terms. "The analysis shows the most expensive option for governments is to do nothing," she said. "That would place $242 billion worth of infrastructure and housing construction activity at risk while allowing the cumulative workforce shortage to blow out to 935,600 workers by 2035."

  ## Where the $242 billion is allocated

  The pipeline is split across three major sectors. Transport commands $129 billion — more than half the total — earmarked for major rail links, regional road corridors and logistics hubs. Social housing and public building projects account for $77 billion. Utilities and energy receive $36 billion, of which $20 billion is expected to be absorbed by transmission network upgrades and grid decarbonisation.

  Public and private sector ambitions for renewable energy projects — transmission lines, solar, wind and pumped hydro — are estimated to total $163 billion over the next five years, IA chief executive Adam Copp said, with a significant share directed into regional areas.

  ## Regional exposure is acute

  Regional labour shortages — those outside major metropolitan centres — are forecast to quadruple between 2025 and 2027, driven primarily by energy projects that are being delayed at the approvals stage. Firms are holding off on workforce investment until they see construction begin, the agency said. Ten regions across New South Wales, Tasmania and Queensland are forecast to see public investment rise by at least 200 percent, intensifying competition for an already thin local labour pool.

  Queensland is seeking one million new residents over the next decade to address its share of the gap, the report noted, with international incentives and domestic programmes under consideration.

  ## Proposed remedies

  MBA's blueprint sets out 32 policy recommendations. These include reform of prior learning recognition, activation of the 18,400 construction-qualified workers already residing in Australia, and a construction training bond aimed at improving apprenticeship completion rates. Wawn said the attrition problem is as significant as the recruitment problem. "Attracting apprentices means little if we continue losing over 50 percent before they complete their training," she said.

  Immigration, the MBA said, remains an essential part of the solution but cannot substitute for rebuilding the domestic skills pipeline.

  IA's Copp called for governments to incentivise trialling of Modern Methods of Construction (MMC) — industrialised building techniques that can reduce on-site labour intensity — pointing to the United Kingdom, United States and Singapore as precedents. "We need to start investing in innovation rather than fixating on delivering at the lowest possible cost," he said. The agency is also recommending consistent nationwide training programmes to upskill workers in these methods.

  ## The AI and automation variable

  The prospect of artificial intelligence and robotics closing part of the gap is treated by analysts as a medium-term variable rather than a near-term solution, and one that itself depends on expanded data centre capacity and new transmission infrastructure — both of which face the same labour constraints.

  The Commissioner of Jobs and Skills Australia has predicted that the number of Australian workers displaced by automation and AI will exceed current forecasts of 600,000. Research from McKinsey and AlphaBeta, cited in the report, estimates that as many as 1.3 million Australian workers — approximately 9 percent of the workforce — face job losses, automation or radical role transitions because of AI. The federal Department of Employment and Workplace Relations has reported no immediate mass layoffs across the broader economy, but government bodies and economists expect structural disruption to scale significantly by 2030.

  ## What it means for infrastructure delivery

  The convergence of a fixed pipeline deadline, a structural labour deficit and an unproven technology offset creates a narrow window for policy action. IA has been tracking supply and demand across the infrastructure sector annually since 2020; its 2025 report is the first to rank labour, rather than materials or financing, as the single greatest delivery risk.

  For data centres and the energy transmission networks that serve them, the report's findings are directly material: both are listed as private-sector demand competing for the same constrained pool of engineers and project directors that public infrastructure programmes are drawing on. Regional labour shortages are forecast to quadruple between 2025 and 2027, driven by energy projects, with firms currently withholding workforce commitments pending regulatory approvals.

  ## 勞動力危機威脅2,420億元建設大計

  澳洲2,420億澳元公共基礎建設計畫若聯邦與各州政府未能推行整合性勞動力策略，累計人力缺口恐在2035年前達93萬5,600人。澳洲建築業主協會（Master Builders Australia，MBA）執行長丹妮塔．沃恩（Denita Wawn）提出上述警告。政府授權的獨立基礎建設顧問機構澳洲基礎建設局（Infrastructure Australia，IA）在報告中點名資料中心與能源、國防及水利產業，指出這些領域已因工程師、設計師與資深專案主任短缺而影響計畫推進。

  澳洲建設業者協會（Australian Constructors Association）指出，建築業目前面臨14萬1,000人的即時缺口，若無全國性因應措施，缺口可能在2027年中前擴大至30萬人。IA於2025年11月發布的《2025年基礎建設市場容量報告》將人力列為全澳最關鍵的交付風險；該機構調查的企業中，約60%認同這一評估。

  ## 人力現況全面檢視

  去年底，澳洲基礎建設部門共有20萬4,000名從業人員。其中，技術工人與勞工佔62%，專案管理專業人員佔12%，工程師、建築師與科學家合計佔其餘26%。

  沃恩以直白的語言點出不作為的代價。她說：「分析顯示，對政府而言最昂貴的選項就是什麼都不做。那將使價值2,420億元的基礎建設與住宅建設活動陷入風險，並讓累計人力缺口在2035年前膨脹至93萬5,600人。」

  ## 2,420億元資金的分配

  這筆資金分布於三大主要部門。交通建設佔1,290億元，超過總額半數，用於重大鐵路幹線、地區公路走廊與物流樞紐。社會住宅與公共建築計畫獲配770億元。公用事業與能源部門分得360億元，其中200億元預計用於輸電網路升級與電網去碳化。

  IA執行長亞當．科普（Adam Copp）指出，再生能源項目——涵蓋輸電線路、太陽能、風電與抽蓄水力發電——的公私部門合計投資在未來五年預估達1,630億元，其中相當比例將流向偏遠地區。

  ## 區域缺工問題更為嚴峻

  主要都會區以外地區的勞動力短缺預計將在2025年至2027年間擴大為原來的四倍，主要原因是能源計畫仍停滯在審批階段。IA指出，企業目前按兵不動，等待建設正式開工後才願意投入人力。新南威爾斯、塔斯馬尼亞與昆士蘭共十個地區預計公共投資將至少成長200%，使本就稀缺的在地人力競爭愈發激烈。

  報告指出，昆士蘭州正尋求在未來十年吸引100萬名新居民，以彌補其人力缺口，目前正研議國際招募誘因與國內配套方案。

  ## 解決方案的提議

  MBA的建議藍圖提出32項政策建議，包括改革先前學習認可制度、啟用目前已在澳洲境內且具備建築資格的1萬8,400名工人，以及設立建築訓練保證金以提升學徒結訓率。沃恩指出，人才流失問題與招募問題同樣嚴峻。她說：「若超過50%的學徒仍在完訓前離開，招募再多人也是徒勞。」

  MBA表示，移民仍是解方的重要一環，但無法取代重建國內技術人才培育管道的工作。

  IA的科普呼籲政府鼓勵試行「現代建築工法」（Modern Methods of Construction，MMC）——一種可降低現場施工人力密集度的工業化建築技術——並以英國、美國與新加坡為前例。他說：「我們需要開始投資創新，而不是一味追求最低成本交付。」該機構同時建議推動全國統一的培訓課程，讓工人精進這類工法的相關技能。

  ## AI與自動化的未知數

  分析人士認為，AI與機器人科技能否填補部分人力缺口，目前仍屬中期變數而非近期解方，且這本身也有賴於擴充資料中心容量與新建輸電基礎設施——而兩者同樣面臨相同的人力制約。

  澳洲就業與技能委員（Commissioner of Jobs and Skills Australia）預測，受自動化與AI衝擊而失業的澳洲勞工人數將超越現有60萬人的預估值。報告引用麥肯錫（McKinsey）與AlphaBeta的研究，估計多達130萬名澳洲勞工——約佔勞動力的9%——將因AI而面臨失業、被自動化取代或職能大幅轉型。聯邦就業及勞動關係部目前尚未通報整體經濟出現大規模即時裁員，但政府機構與經濟學家預期，結構性衝擊將在2030年前大幅加深。

  ## 對基礎建設交付的深遠影響

  既定的建設期程、結構性人力赤字以及尚未經過驗證的科技補位方案三者匯流，留給政策行動的窗口相當狹窄。IA自2020年起每年追蹤基礎建設部門的供需狀況；2025年報告是首次將人力——而非物料或融資——列為最重大單一交付風險的版本。

  對資料中心及其所仰賴的能源輸電網路而言，這份報告的發現具有直接影響：兩者均被列為私部門需求端，與公共基礎建設計畫爭奪同一批稀缺的工程師與專案主任。區域勞動力短缺預計將在2025年至2027年間擴大四倍，主要由能源計畫驅動，而企業目前仍在等候法規審批後才敢承諾投入人力。
