body_en: |
  Vantage Data Centers has abandoned its planned Trident campus in Aurora, Colorado after the city council passed a moratorium on new data center development and residents mounted sustained opposition to the project.

  The Trident project would have been the largest data center in Colorado: an 860,000-square-foot (roughly 80,000-square-meter) two-story facility drawing 500 megawatts of total power, with 334MW serving IT equipment directly. For context, 500MW is roughly the continuous output of a mid-sized natural gas peaking plant, and among the largest single-campus power commitments in the U.S. data center market. The project was designed to use closed-loop cooling and included investment in local water replenishment.

  "While we will no longer pursue the current site for our proposed campus, Vantage remains committed to being a responsible long-term partner in the state," the company said in a statement. Vantage added that it had voluntarily begun reviewing alternative sites before the moratorium passed.

  ## The moratorium

  The Aurora city council approved the moratorium on Monday, restricting new data center development on land west and south of the intersection of Interstate 70 and Powhaton Road. The restriction runs through September 2027, or until the council takes further action. An earlier attempt at a moratorium in October had failed.

  Vantage's project had already received approval, meaning it could potentially have continued under a grandfather provision. The company nonetheless chose to exit.

  ## How the opposition built

  The Trident project was approved through Aurora's Airport District zoning process — a pathway that residents say did not require them to be notified before approval was granted. Planning filings had been publicly available since August 2025, but no public notices were posted.

  Once community awareness grew, organized opposition followed quickly. Residents of the nearby Reserve at Green Valley Ranch, a 55-and-older community, dressed in black and locked arms in front of the council's offices in September. A subsequent city council meeting ran five hours after 40 residents spoke against the project.

  Aurora Mayor Mike Coffman, who faces reelection next year, publicly declared in October that the data center "must be stopped."

  ## Jobs and economic claims

  Vantage said the project would have generated more than 3,000 skilled construction jobs and between 30 and 50 permanent positions once operational, excluding indirect roles such as contracted electricians, maintenance workers, and security personnel.

  ## QTS project unaffected

  QTS, a competing data center operator, is also developing a facility in Aurora with a planned capacity of 160MW. With construction described as mostly finished, that project is not expected to be affected by the moratorium.

  In a statement to Data Center Dynamics, QTS said it pays for the energy costs of its facilities, that its data centers use no additional water once operational, and that the projects create local jobs. "Every community has the right and responsibility to thoughtfully evaluate growth and development," QTS said, adding that it remains "focused on being a good neighbor and building lasting partnerships in the communities we serve, including Aurora."

  ## What it signals

  The Trident withdrawal illustrates how community opposition, combined with municipal action, can halt a project even after it has cleared regulatory approval — a dynamic with implications for data center developers pursuing large-scale deployments near residential areas. The Aurora moratorium runs through September 2027, giving the city time to revise its zoning and notification framework before new proposals can advance.

  Vantage said its examination of alternative sites in Colorado is continuing.
body_zh: |
  Vantage Data Centers已宣布放棄其在科羅拉多州奧羅拉市規劃中的Trident園區，起因是該市市議會通過了新資料中心開發禁建令，加上當地居民長期強力抗議。

  Trident計畫原本將成為科羅拉多州規模最大的資料中心：一座約八十六萬平方英尺（約八萬平方公尺）的兩層樓建築，總用電量達500MW，其中334MW直接供應IT設備使用。就規模而言，500MW相當於一座中型天然氣調峰電廠的持續輸出功率，也是美國資料中心市場中單一園區電力承諾規模最大的案例之一。該計畫設計採用密閉循環冷卻系統，並納入了對當地水資源補注的投資。

  Vantage在聲明中表示：「雖然我們不再繼續推進目前預定地點的園區計畫，但Vantage仍致力於成為科羅拉多州負責任的長期合作夥伴。」Vantage並補充說明，早在禁建令通過之前，公司便已主動著手評估替代選址。

  ## 禁建令詳情

  奧羅拉市議會於週一正式通過這項禁建令，限制在70號州際公路與波哈頓路交叉口以西及以南的土地上新設資料中心。該限制將持續至2027年9月，或直至市議會採取進一步行動為止。先前在十月提出的禁建令草案已告失敗，此次方才獲得通過。

  Vantage的計畫在此之前已獲得核准，理論上或可依「既有豁免條款」繼續推進。然而，該公司仍選擇退出。

  ## 反對聲浪如何形成

  Trident計畫係透過奧羅拉機場區域分區程序獲批，而居民指出，這套程序在核准前並不要求向周邊居民發出通知。相關規劃文件自2025年8月起已可公開查閱，但始終未張貼任何公告通知。

  隨著社區居民逐漸意識到此事，組織化的反對行動迅速成形。鄰近的「綠谷牧場保護社區」（Reserve at Green Valley Ranch）是一個以55歲以上長者為主的社區，其居民身穿黑衣、手挽手地在市議會辦公室前展開抗議。此後召開的一場市議會會議中，共有40名居民站出來反對該計畫，會議也因此延長至五個小時才結束。

  明年面臨市長連任選戰的奧羅拉市長麥克・科夫曼（Mike Coffman）於十月間公開表態，直言這座資料中心「必須被阻止」。

  ## 就業與經濟效益宣稱

  Vantage表示，該計畫原本將創造逾3,000個技術性建築工作機會，以及30至50個長期職位，此外尚不包含外包電工、維護人員及保全等間接相關職位。

  ## QTS計畫不受影響

  競爭對手QTS也正在奧羅拉開發一座規劃容量為160MW的資料中心。由於施工已大致完成，該計畫預計不會受到此次禁建令的影響。

  QTS在回覆《Data Center Dynamics》的聲明中表示，公司自行承擔旗下設施的能源費用，其資料中心在正式營運後不額外耗用水資源，且這些計畫為當地創造了就業機會。「每個社區都有權利、也有責任審慎評估成長與發展，」QTS表示，並強調持續「專注於成為良好的鄰居，在我們服務的社區——包括奧羅拉——建立長久的夥伴關係。」

  ## 此案的指標意義

  Trident計畫的撤出揭示了一個值得業界關注的動態：社區反對力量結合市政府的行動，即便在計畫已通過法規審查之後，仍可能迫使開發商放棄計畫。這對在住宅區附近尋求大規模部署的資料中心開發商而言，具有深遠的影響。奧羅拉的禁建令將持續至2027年9月，為該市爭取到足夠的時間，在新提案得以推進之前修訂其分區法規與公告通知機制。

  Vantage表示，公司在科羅拉多州尋找替代選址的工作仍在持續進行中。
