body_en: |
  Two gigawatt-scale data centers are under construction in Visakhapatnam on India's southern coast, one by Google and the Adani Group as part of a $15 billion project and a second by Brookfield Asset Management and Reliance. Together they will more than double India's total data center capacity relative to the end of last year. The Indian government is providing sweeping subsidies to underpin both investments, while local residents, activists, and former senior officials have raised concerns about water use, environmental approvals, and the absence of public consultation.

  ## Scale of the Build-Out

  Google's project, which also includes laying new subsea cables, is anchored by a one-gigawatt (GW) data center being developed on land near the village of Tarluvada on the outskirts of Visakhapatnam. Excavators were clearing the site last month. The Brookfield Asset Management and Reliance joint venture is building an equally large facility in the same city. When both are operational, the additions will more than double India's total data center capacity compared with the end of 2025.

  ## India's Structural Gap in AI

  The projects target a structural imbalance: India produces roughly 20% of the world's data but holds only a fraction of global data center capacity. A Morgan Stanley report described India as a "laggard" in building AI capability, with billions of dollars in investment leaving the country over the past year for competitors including Taiwan and South Korea, which manufacture advanced AI components such as chips. The country is currently positioned as a deployer of AI technology for domestic and international markets rather than an early innovation hub, and officials see the data center build-out as a prerequisite for even that role.

  For Nara Lokesh, the information technology minister of Andhra Pradesh, the state surrounding Visakhapatnam, the investments extend beyond computing infrastructure. "For me, it's not merely about the data centre," Lokesh said. "So the power electronics, the air conditioning, the building materials guy, the whole nine yards. We are now bringing all of them to my state to do the manufacturing."

  ## Subsidies and the Adani Partnership

  India is offering Google and its local partner, the Adani Group, subsidies including discounted land, energy, and water, as well as tax breaks. Google's partner, billionaire Gautam Adani, is seen as a close ally of Prime Minister Narendra Modi, whose wealth has grown significantly during Modi's 12 years in office. Lokesh said the projects follow "all due process" and that the state is reducing bureaucratic barriers rather than weakening regulatory oversight.

  ## Job Projections Disputed

  Officials have said the Google project will create approximately 120,000 jobs during construction and around 60,000 permanent positions once operational. Critics have challenged those figures, arguing they do not align with employment outcomes at comparably sized data centers built elsewhere.

  Project documents submitted to support the environmental clearance — covering details such as parking capacity and daily water consumption for sanitation — indicated the permanent workforce would be a fraction of the 60,000 figure cited by officials. Data centers of this type are described as facilities that "guzzle energy and water" but are "not big job creators." The International Monetary Fund has warned that AI could bring about what it called a "tsunami hitting the labour market," with particular impact on India's technology support sector, a major component of the country's economy.

  ## Water, Environment, and Costs

  Visakhapatnam sits in a region described as water-stressed, with supply disruptions occurring in summer months. Critics say environmental clearance rules were relaxed to permit construction in ecologically sensitive areas, and that information on where water and electricity will be sourced — and who will bear the cost of subsidies — has not been disclosed publicly.

  Google executives and officials from the Adani subsidiary involved said the facilities will use cooling technology that minimizes water consumption and will not draw on the local potable water supply. Google is also investing in clean energy generation and will "ensure no costs are passed on to local households, local businesses, or the state government," a project official said.

  EAS Sarma, a former senior official in India's ministries of finance and energy who holds a doctorate in energy planning, disputed that assurance. He said the cost of building new electricity transmission lines would ultimately appear on residents' power bills. "Every subsidy is a cost for the society," Sarma said.

  ## Calls for Public Debate

  VS Krishna, a campaigner with the Human Rights Forum operating in the region, said the scope of resource commitments being made without public consultation is the central concern. "This is not a defence deal, this is a developmental project. Why should it be shrouded in this opacity?" Krishna said. "This whole region is being asked to absorb an enormous amount of infrastructure cost, environmental risks, without a public debate."

  Sarma also raised questions about the Adani Group's role in securing project approvals. "Google is very clever — it has joined hands with Adani, because Adani can clear anything here in the government of India," he said.

  ## What It Means for India's AI Standing

  The Visakhapatnam projects reflect a wider competition among India's southern states, which Lokesh described as more advanced and easier to do business in than their northern counterparts, for foreign technology investment. Data center projects of comparable scale in the United States have been blocked or delayed by community opposition centered on energy and water demands, and India's willingness to offer subsidies and streamlined approvals has become a differentiator for developers facing those constraints.

  Both facilities remain under construction. When operational, the two projects will bring India's total data center capacity to more than double what it was at the end of 2025.
body_zh: |
  兩座吉瓦級資料中心正在印度南部海岸城市維沙卡帕特南（Visakhapatnam）動工興建——其一由Google與阿達尼集團（Adani Group）合資推動，是一項規模達150億美元計畫的一環；另一座則由Brookfield資產管理（Brookfield Asset Management）與信實集團（Reliance）攜手打造。兩座設施建成後，將使印度的資料中心總容量較去年底倍增有餘。印度政府為兩項投資提供大規模補貼，包括折扣土地、電力與水資源，但當地居民、社運人士及前資深官員則對用水量、環境許可及欠缺公眾諮詢等問題提出強烈關切。

  ## 建設規模

  Google的計畫除鋪設新海底電纜外，核心是一座1 GW（吉瓦）的資料中心，建地位於維沙卡帕特南郊外Tarluvada村附近，上個月已有挖土機在當地展開整地作業。Brookfield資產管理與信實集團的合資企業，也在同城興建規模相當的設施。兩座資料中心全數投入運作後，印度資料中心總容量將相較2025年底超過翻倍。

  ## 印度的人工智慧結構性缺口

  這兩項計畫瞄準的是一個結構性失衡：印度產生全球約20%的資料，卻僅掌握全球資料中心容量的極小一部分。摩根士丹利（Morgan Stanley）一份報告將印度描述為建立人工智慧能力的「落後者」，過去一年間已有數十億美元投資出走，流向台灣、南韓等競爭對手——這些地方製造人工智慧晶片等先進元件。目前印度被定位為面向國內外市場的人工智慧技術應用端，而非早期創新重鎮，官員視資料中心大規模擴建為扮演這一角色的先決條件。

  對安得拉邦（Andhra Pradesh，維沙卡帕特南所在邦）資訊科技部長納拉．洛克什（Nara Lokesh）而言，這些投資的意義遠不止於運算基礎設施。「對我來說，這絕不只是資料中心，」洛克什說。「電力電子、空調、建材，整個產業鏈，全都要一起來。我們現在要把這些廠商通通引進我的邦來設廠製造。」

  ## 補貼與Adani合作關係

  印度為Google及其本地合作夥伴阿達尼集團提供的補貼，涵蓋折扣土地、電力與水資源，並附帶租稅優惠。Google的合作對象、億萬富翁高塔姆．阿達尼（Gautam Adani）被視為總理納倫德拉．莫迪（Narendra Modi）的親密政治盟友，其財富在莫迪執政十二年間大幅增長。洛克什表示，相關計畫「依循所有正當程序」，邦政府著力於降低官僚障礙，而非削弱監管力度。

  ## 就業預測遭質疑

  官方表示，Google計畫在施工期間預計創造約12萬個工作機會，設施投入營運後則提供約6萬個永久職缺。批評人士對這些數字提出強烈質疑，認為與同等規模資料中心在其他地方的實際就業成果明顯不符。

  為支持環境許可申請而提交的計畫文件——內容涵蓋停車容量、每日衛生用水量等細節——顯示永久員工人數將遠低於官方引用的6萬人。這類資料中心被形容為「大量吞噬能源與水資源」，卻「並非就業的大量創造者」。國際貨幣基金（IMF）曾警告，人工智慧可能引發其所稱的「勞動市場海嘯」，且對印度科技支援業的衝擊將尤為顯著——該產業是印度經濟的重要支柱。

  ## 用水、環境與成本

  維沙卡帕特南所在地區屬水資源緊張區域，夏季供水中斷時有所聞。批評人士指出，環境許可規定遭到放寬，允許在生態敏感地區施工，而水源與電力的取用方式及補貼成本的最終承擔方，迄今未有任何公開說明。

  Google高管及阿達尼旗下相關子公司的官員表示，設施將採用最小化耗水量的冷卻技術，不會動用當地飲用水源。Google也將投資潔淨能源發電，並將「確保不將任何成本轉嫁給當地居民、當地企業或邦政府」，一名計畫官員如此表示。

  曾任印度財政部與能源部資深官員、擁有能源規劃博士學位的EAS薩爾馬（EAS Sarma）對此一保證提出異議。他指出，興建新輸電線路的成本，最終將反映在居民的電費帳單上。「每一項補貼都是社會必須承擔的成本，」薩爾馬說。

  ## 呼籲公開辯論

  在當地活動的人權論壇（Human Rights Forum）倡議人VS克里希納（VS Krishna）表示，在欠缺公眾諮詢的情況下做出如此規模的資源承諾，才是問題的核心所在。「這不是國防合約，這是一項發展計畫。為什麼要被如此不透明所籠罩？」克里希納說。「整個地區被要求承擔龐大的基礎設施成本與環境風險，卻沒有任何公開辯論。」

  薩爾馬也對阿達尼集團在取得計畫審批方面所扮演的角色提出質疑。「Google非常聰明——它與阿達尼聯手，因為阿達尼能在印度政府裡打通任何關節，」他說。

  ## 對印度人工智慧地位的意義

  維沙卡帕特南的計畫，折射出印度南部各邦競逐外資科技投資的更廣泛態勢。洛克什形容南部諸邦比北部更為先進、營商環境更為友善。在美國，同等規模的資料中心計畫因社區對能耗與用水的反對，已遭阻擋或延宕；印度願意提供補貼、簡化審批流程，已成為吸引面臨此類困境之開發商的差異化籌碼。

  兩座設施目前仍在建設中。待全面投入運作後，這兩項計畫將使印度資料中心總容量超過2025年底的兩倍。
