body_en: |
  Australia's corporate watchdog said on Oct 29 that it is reviewing the collapse of the US$5 billion (S$6.4 billion) initial public offering of Firmus, an Nvidia-backed artificial intelligence data centre operator, and has already engaged the four banks that managed the failed deal.

  Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) chair Sarah Court told a hearing of Australian lawmakers that the regulator had not yet opened a formal investigation but was actively looking at the matter. "I think it's fair to say that we are having a look at the broad range of issues that have been reported in the media, including engaging with the joint lead managers," Court said. "This was a very significant event, we understand that and we need to look at it carefully. That's what we're doing."

  ## The IPO collapse

  Firmus had planned to raise US$5 billion in the listing, which would have valued the company at US$30.6 billion. The IPO fell apart on Oct 9 after failing to attract enough investor backing, according to The Straits Times citing Reuters. Had it succeeded, the offering would have been the second largest in Australian stock market history.

  The US$30.6 billion target valuation represented triple the value Firmus achieved during a private fundraising round in August, a sharp premium that may bear on ASIC's review of how the deal was presented to potential investors.

  ## Banks under scrutiny

  The four joint lead managers on the transaction were Bank of America, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, and Australian brokerage Morgans. Court confirmed ASIC had engaged with those firms as part of its preliminary review. Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan declined to comment on the ASIC inquiries; Bank of America and Morgans did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

  ## Significance

  The ASIC review signals that regulators are prepared to examine not only whether adequate disclosures were made to prospective investors but also the pricing and syndication practices that underpinned a deal valued at more than three times its most recent private round. The outcome of any formal investigation could shape how large IPOs are structured and marketed in Australia's capital markets going forward.

  ASIC has not yet indicated a timeline for deciding whether to escalate the matter to an official investigation.

  ## 澳洲ASIC展開調查：輝達背書的Firmus AI資料中心50億美元IPO宣告破局

  澳洲企業監管機構於10月29日表示，正在審查AI資料中心業者Firmus首次公開募股（IPO）破局一事。Firmus獲輝達（NVIDIA）投資支持，原計畫募資50億美元（約新台幣1,920億元），監管機關已與承辦此案的四家銀行展開接觸。

  澳洲證券與投資委員會（ASIC）主席莎拉．柯特（Sarah Court）在國會聽證會上表示，監管機關尚未正式立案調查，但已積極著手檢視相關事宜。「我可以明確地說，我們正在廣泛審視媒體所報導的各項議題，包括與各聯合主辦承銷商進行接觸，」柯特表示，「這是一件非常重大的事件，我們理解其嚴重性，必須審慎看待，這正是我們目前的做法。」

  ## IPO破局始末

  Firmus原計畫透過此次掛牌募集50億美元，以此計算公司估值為306億美元。據《海峽時報》引述路透社報導，該案於10月9日因未能吸引足夠的投資人認購而告吹。若此案順利完成，將成為澳洲股票市場史上規模第二大的公開發行案。

  306億美元的目標估值，相當於Firmus在今年8月完成的一輪私募融資估值的三倍。這一大幅溢價，或許正是ASIC審查此案向潛在投資人揭露資訊方式的重要關注點之一。

  ## 四大承銷銀行受到矚目

  此次交易的四家聯合主辦承銷商分別為美國銀行（Bank of America）、摩根大通（JPMorgan）、摩根士丹利（Morgan Stanley），以及澳洲本土券商Morgans。柯特確認，ASIC已於初步審查階段與上述機構接觸。摩根士丹利與摩根大通均拒絕就ASIC的查詢置評；美國銀行與Morgans則未立即回應置評請求。

  ## 事件意義

  ASIC此次審查釋出明確訊號：監管機關不僅準備檢視向潛在投資人所作的資訊揭露是否充分，也將深入檢驗一筆估值超過最近私募輪次三倍以上的交易，其背後的定價機制與承銷安排是否妥適。一旦正式調查有所結論，將可能對未來澳洲資本市場大型IPO的架構設計與推介方式產生深遠影響。

  ASIC目前尚未說明是否決定升級為正式調查的時間表。
