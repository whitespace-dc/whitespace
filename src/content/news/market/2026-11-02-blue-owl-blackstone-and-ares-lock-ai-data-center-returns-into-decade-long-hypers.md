body_en: |
  Major alternative asset managers including Blue Owl, Blackstone, and Ares Management are directing capital into AI data center infrastructure and securing returns through 12- to 20-year leases with investment-grade technology tenants, executives said this week, as shares of several firms rose and the sector drew fresh scrutiny over counterparty and financing risks.

  Blue Owl rose 6.5% on Thursday, while Apollo Global gained 3.7%, Blackstone and KKR each added 2.5%, and Ares Management gained 2%, according to the report. The moves came as executives at multiple firms made public the scale of their data center commitments and sought to frame their exposure as a credit bet on large technology companies rather than a speculative wager on AI adoption.

  "We are not making a bet on AI when we invest in data centers," Blue Owl Chief Financial Officer Alan Kirshenbaum said. "We are taking Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, credit risk."

  ## Blue Owl's portfolio and lease structure

  Blue Owl said it has more than 130 data centers operating or under construction, representing nearly 14 gigawatts of leased and owned capacity. A gigawatt of data center capacity is roughly equivalent to the continuous power draw of a large city and supports tens of thousands of high-density computing racks. The company reported an additional 21 gigawatts in its development pipeline.

  Blue Owl Co-Chief Executive Marc Lipschultz described the firm's model as owning physical infrastructure and leasing it under contracts spanning 15 to 20 years with annual rent escalators, which he said creates "a highly predictable and growing stream of rental income" for investors. Lipschultz said the firm focuses on limiting risks tied to short-term leases and token pricing — a reference to the variable revenue model used by cloud computing resellers whose income fluctuates with AI workload demand.

  ## Blackstone and Ares structures

  Blackstone said the vast majority of its data center portfolio carries initial lease terms of 15 to 20 years. The firm said 96% of its data center counterparties hold investment-grade credit ratings and carry an average market capitalisation of $2.5 trillion — a figure reflecting the scale of hyperscale cloud and technology companies that dominate data center demand.

  "So when we think about risk, that doesn't feel particularly risky to us," Blackstone President Jonathan Gray told analysts on a post-earnings call.

  Ares Management said its data center development strategy is centered on metropolitan markets including Tokyo, Osaka, London, and São Paulo, and that it pre-leases projects to investment-grade hyperscalers — large cloud providers such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud — under 12- to 15-year leases with no termination rights and inflation escalators.

  ## Capex scale and financing evolution

  Ares estimates AI-related capital expenditure demand at just over $5 trillion through 2030, of which roughly $2 trillion is attributed to physical data center buildings and adjacent power generation and transmission infrastructure.

  Ares Chief Executive Michael Arougheti addressed a recent AI-related market selloff by saying investors are becoming more discerning about counterparty risk and how to price it. He described a shift in how large-scale AI infrastructure projects are being financed.

  "As the capex numbers have continued to scale, they went from largely equity-funded to then high-grade funded and now you're starting to see some of that funding demand flow through into the leveraged finance and private credit market," Arougheti said. "So there's an evolution here."

  Arougheti also downplayed the effect of higher borrowing costs on AI capital expenditure financing, arguing that the alternative would be to fund projects with equity.

  ## Moody's flags concentration and circular-financing risks

  Ratings agency Moody's said this week that AI and digital infrastructure are creating large, long-duration financing needs that broaden the private credit market beyond traditional direct lending. The agency noted that many borrowers and counterparties in the sector are investment grade, but warned that "concentration, construction, residual-value and circular-financing risks are growing."

  Circular-financing risk refers to situations where the same pool of capital is recycled through related entities in a sector, potentially masking the true distribution of credit exposure across lenders and investors.

  The warnings follow broader concerns that debt-laden neocloud companies — cloud computing providers that lease data center capacity and resell it to AI developers, often on short-term contracts — could default if AI spending does not deliver adequate returns to their own customers. Alternative asset managers emphasized that their own exposure runs to the hyperscale tenants occupying their buildings, not to the neoclouds.

  ## Significance

  The positioning by Blue Owl, Blackstone, Ares, and their peers reflects a strategy of capturing AI infrastructure demand through real asset ownership and long-term contracted income rather than through equity stakes in AI companies or short-duration technology leases. By anchoring leases to investment-grade hyperscalers at terms of 12 to 20 years, the firms are structuring their data center exposure closer to infrastructure or real estate investment than to technology venture capital.

  The strategy does not eliminate risk: Moody's specifically identified construction risk and residual-value risk — the possibility that data centers lose value if technology shifts reduce demand for their specific design — as growing concerns across the sector.

  Ares has estimated that AI-related infrastructure capital expenditure will reach just over $5 trillion globally by 2030, with approximately $2 trillion of that directed at physical buildings and power infrastructure, suggesting the pipeline of potential development and financing activity these firms are positioning to capture remains, by their own projections, in early stages.
body_zh: |
  Blue Owl、黑石（Blackstone）及Ares Management等主要另類資產管理業者，正將資本導入AI資料中心基礎設施，並透過與投資等級科技租戶簽訂12至20年租約的方式鎖定報酬。多家業者高管本週公開表示，在旗下多支股票走揚的同時，市場也對交易對手風險與融資風險展開新一輪審視。

  Blue Owl周四股價上漲6.5%，Apollo Global上漲3.7%，黑石與KKR各漲2.5%，Ares Management則上漲2%。多家業者高管公開揭露其資料中心布局的規模，並積極將自身的曝險定性為對大型科技公司的信用投注，而非押注AI能否廣泛被市場採用的投機行為。

  「我們投資資料中心，並不是在押注AI，」Blue Owl財務長艾倫·柯申鮑姆（Alan Kirshenbaum）表示，「我們承擔的是微軟、亞馬遜、Meta的信用風險。」

  ## Blue Owl的投資組合與租約結構

  Blue Owl表示，旗下目前已有逾130座資料中心正在營運或建設中，合計擁有近14吉瓦（GW）的已租賃與自有產能。一吉瓦的資料中心產能，相當於一座大型城市持續運作所需的電力，可支撐數萬個高密度運算機架。該公司另有21吉瓦的產能正在開發管線中。

  Blue Owl聯席執行長馬克·利普舒爾茨（Marc Lipschultz）描述公司的商業模式是持有實體基礎設施，並以15至20年合約進行出租，合約中附有年度租金調漲條款。他表示，這為投資人創造「高度可預測且持續成長的租金收入」。利普舒爾茨指出，公司著重於規避短期租約及AI代幣計費的相關風險——後者指的是雲端運算轉售業者所採用的浮動計費模式，其收入會隨AI工作負載需求的波動而起伏。

  ## 黑石與Ares的結構

  黑石表示，旗下資料中心投資組合中，絕大多數的初始租期均為15至20年。該公司指出，96%的資料中心交易對手持有投資等級信用評等，平均市值高達2.5兆美元——這一數字充分反映出主導資料中心需求的超大規模雲端及科技公司的龐大規模。

  「因此，當我們思考風險時，這對我們而言並不特別令人憂慮，」黑石總裁喬納森·格雷（Jonathan Gray）在法說會後的分析師電話會議上表示。

  Ares Management表示，其資料中心開發策略以東京、大阪、倫敦及聖保羅等大都會市場為核心，並以12至15年租約將專案預先出租給投資等級的超大規模業者——亦即亞馬遜雲端服務（AWS）、微軟Azure及Google Cloud等大型雲端供應商——合約不設終止權，並附有通膨調漲條款。

  ## 資本支出規模與融資演變

  Ares估計，AI相關資本支出需求到2030年將略超5兆美元，其中約2兆美元將用於資料中心實體建築以及周邊的電力生產與輸配電基礎設施。

  Ares執行長麥可·阿魯蓋提（Michael Arougheti）針對近期AI相關市場拋售表示，投資人正變得愈來愈審慎，更在意交易對手風險及如何為其定價。他描述了大型AI基礎設施專案融資方式正在經歷的轉變。

  「隨著資本支出數字持續攀升，融資方式從以股權融資為主，演變為以高評等債券融資為主，如今你開始看到部分資金需求流向槓桿融資與私人信貸市場，」阿魯蓋提說，「這是一個演變的過程。」

  阿魯蓋提也淡化了借貸成本上升對AI資本支出融資的衝擊，主張若不採債務融資，替代方案就是以股權資金支應。

  ## 穆迪點名集中度與循環融資風險

  信用評等機構穆迪（Moody's）本週表示，AI與數位基礎設施正產生龐大且期限長的融資需求，使私人信貸市場的觸角延伸至傳統直接借貸以外的領域。穆迪指出，該領域的許多借款方與交易對手均持有投資等級評等，但警告「集中度風險、建設風險、殘值風險及循環融資風險正持續升高」。

  所謂循環融資風險，是指同一資金池在同一產業的關聯實體間反覆流轉，可能掩蓋信貸曝險在各貸款方與投資人之間的真實分布情況。

  上述警告，呼應外界對背負高額債務的新興雲端（neocloud）業者的廣泛憂慮。這類業者以短期合約租用資料中心產能，再轉售給AI開發商；一旦AI支出無法為其客戶帶來足夠回報，便可能面臨違約風險。另類資產管理業者強調，自身的曝險對象是進駐旗下資料中心的超大規模租戶，而非新興雲端業者。

  ## 意義

  Blue Owl、黑石、Ares及其同業的這一布局策略，反映出透過持有實體資產與長期合約收益來捕捉AI基礎設施需求的思路，而非透過持有AI公司股權或短期科技租賃來達成。透過將租約錨定於投資等級的超大規模業者、期限訂為12至20年，這些業者正將其資料中心曝險的性質，塑造得更接近基礎設施或不動產投資，而非科技創投。

  這一策略並不能消除所有風險：穆迪特別指出，建設風險以及殘值風險——即若技術轉型導致特定設計的資料中心需求萎縮、資產可能隨之貶值的可能性——正成為整個產業日益嚴峻的隱憂。

  Ares估計，全球AI相關基礎設施資本支出到2030年將略超5兆美元，其中約2兆美元將投入實體建築及電力基礎設施。以各業者自身的預測來看，這些業者積極爭取的開發與融資商機，仍處於相當早期的階段。
