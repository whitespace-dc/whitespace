body_en: |
  AirTrunk's planned Singapore Exchange listing has been postponed and DayOne Data Centers is weighing a delay or a reduced offering for its US initial public offering (IPO), as public market appetite for data center assets weakens amid rising borrowing costs, credit downgrades, and growing community opposition over resource use.

  Bloomberg reported on Oct. 13 that AirTrunk's Singapore real estate investment trust (S-REIT) IPO, scheduled for the Singapore Exchange (SGX) this year, had been "postponed." The Australian Financial Review had flagged the risk on Oct. 3, attributing it to stock market volatility, a sell-off in technology and AI stocks, and surging borrowing costs. AirTrunk, though officially headquartered in Australia, holds a Singapore government capacity award alongside joint-venture partner ByteDance, and its listing had been expected to be the largest IPO on the SGX this year. The company was acquired by private equity firm Blackstone and the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board in December 2024, and its planned offering was valued at A$4.4 billion (RM12.5 billion).

  Bloomberg separately reported that DayOne Data Centers — Singapore-headquartered and backed by Shanghai-listed GDS Holdings — is weighing whether to delay its US IPO or reduce its size to raise between US$2.5 billion (RM10.14 billion) and US$3 billion. DayOne has been waiting for a favorable window and is also considering pushing back the share sale until market conditions improve, Bloomberg said.

  Firmus Grid, an Australian AI and data center company, also postponed its Australian Securities Exchange (ASX) listing on Oct. 8.

  ## Credit stress across the sector

  The IPO retreats coincide with visible financial strain among data center operators. In the week of Oct. 12 to 16, bonds issued by CoreWeave — a US-based neocloud, a company that borrows heavily to rent out AI computing capacity — fell in value, and the cost of insuring its debt against default hit a record. Oracle, which has taken on heavy debt to build data centers, saw its debt rated one notch above junk by credit agencies.

  DayOne itself is loss-making. Its Form 1 registration document filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) shows a net loss of US$369 million attributable to ordinary shareholders for the financial year ended December 2025, and a further US$82 million net loss in the first half of financial year 2026. The company has nonetheless continued to raise debt. In August, Singapore's three largest local banks — DBS Bank, Oversea-Chinese Banking Corp (OCBC), and United Overseas Bank (UOB) — extended a four-year S$530 million (RM1.68 billion) green loan for DayOne's first Singapore data center. In December 2025, Brookfield and an unnamed global sovereign investor provided €500 million in mezzanine financing — a form of subordinated debt that sits between senior loans and equity in a company's capital structure — expandable to €1 billion, for DayOne data centers in Finland. In October 2026, DayOne drew a further syndicated loan for its Johor, Malaysia data centers.

  ## Singapore's data center capacity program

  DayOne's Singapore asset was allocated through a government licensing process that reopened the city-state's data center market. When Singapore's Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) lifted a moratorium on new data center construction in 2023, its first Data Centre Call for Application (DC-CFA) — a competitive round awarding limited power capacity to approved operators — allocated 80 megawatts (MW) to four entities: the GDS Holdings-backed entity that was later renamed DayOne, AirTrunk-ByteDance, Equinix, and Microsoft. IMDA's second DC-CFA allocated 50 MW each to Keppel, Equinix, Digital Realty, and ST Telemedia — all either publicly listed or backed by large corporations.

  DayOne's Singapore data center is scheduled for completion next year. When DayOne broke ground on the Singapore site in July 2025, the company signed a power purchase agreement with Sembcorp Power under which all operations will be covered by renewable energy, and a research and development partnership with the National University of Singapore (NUS) for the Sustainable Tropical Data Centre Testbed Phase 2.0 initiative.

  Beyond Singapore, DayOne has built campuses at Nusajaya Tech Park and Kempas Tech Park in Johor, and is developing two campuses on Indonesia's Batam island: Nongsa Digital Park and Kabil Industrial Tech Park. The company's SEC filing states that it has secured approximately 1.4 gigawatts (GW) of bookings from five global hyperscalers — large-scale cloud and technology platform operators.

  ## Water conflict in Batam

  Rapid data center expansion on Batam has generated public opposition. The BBC reported on Oct. 13 that around 300 residents had lost access to running water at home while data centers in the new Nongsa Digital Park appeared to have adequate water to cool their equipment — a conflict that illustrates the resource pressures data center construction can create for nearby communities.

  Mayank Srivastava, chief executive of BDx, a Singapore-headquartered data center developer and operator, said the industry had not adequately considered community impacts during its early expansion. "Specific to the case of data centres, probably there wasn't enough awareness about the impact on communities, and in the initial days, developers just went wherever there was surplus power, and then when the data centres kept expanding in those locations, the power supply had to be upgraded. Nobody was really thinking about aligning costs and incentives, and as it would happen, the costs were passed out of the community, which is not a fair thing to do in the first place, and that is why you saw a lot of community resentment," Srivastava said.

  Srivastava argued that the cost of infrastructure upgrades required by a data center's presence — such as expanded power grids — should be borne proportionally by the data center rather than spread across local communities. "If a factory is coming up which needs the power infrastructure to be upgraded, then the cost of that upgrade should be borne by that factory," he said.

  ## Oracle's force majeure in New Mexico

  Similar community and regulatory resistance has emerged in the United States. In September, Oracle issued a force majeure notice to a unit of Blue Owl Capital regarding Project Jupiter, a planned US$165 billion data center campus in New Mexico. Force majeure is a legal provision that may allow a party to defer contractual obligations due to circumstances outside its control. By invoking it, Oracle aims to defer full rent payments — potentially by up to three years — if the site fails to reach operational status by its 2028 target, shifting delay and financing risk back to Blue Owl and its lenders rather than walking away from its anchor tenant role.

  Project Jupiter has faced setbacks on multiple fronts. In July, the New Mexico State Land Office rejected Oracle's application for the "Green Chile Project," a 27.4-kilometer natural gas pipeline extension needed to supply fuel cells at the site. State and local officials have also objected to the project over projected water consumption, carbon dioxide emissions, and what they describe as insufficient direct economic benefit to New Mexico. On Oct. 14, Oracle proposed a ten-year Energy Service Agreement with El Paso Electric under which it would pay US$100 million to support affordable electricity rates for New Mexico customers.

  ## What the slowdown signals

  The simultaneous stalling of IPOs, rising debt costs, credit downgrades, and regulatory and community resistance across multiple jurisdictions point to a tightening of the conditions that have supported data center construction at scale. Operators have drawn on private equity, sovereign capital, and syndicated bank lending to build capacity ahead of equity returns, an approach that is increasingly exposed as borrowing costs rise.

  Underlying demand from hyperscalers has not disappeared. DayOne's claimed 1.4 GW in bookings suggests that occupancy commitments from large technology companies remain active. But the gap between that demand signal and the valuations sought in recent IPO filings appears, given the string of postponements, to exceed what public equity investors are prepared to accept at current rates.

  As of the time of publication, DayOne had not disclosed a revised timeline or final size for its US IPO, and AirTrunk had not announced a new date for its SGX listing.

  AirTrunk原定的新加坡交易所（SGX）掛牌計畫已宣告暫緩，DayOne資料中心正在評估是否延後或縮減美國首次公開募股（IPO）規模；借貸成本上升、信用評級遭調降，以及各地社區對資源耗用問題的反彈聲浪日益升高，導致公開市場對資料中心資產的胃納量明顯收縮。

  彭博新聞（Bloomberg）於10月13日報導，AirTrunk原計劃今年在新加坡交易所掛牌的不動產投資信託（S-REIT）IPO已「暫緩」。澳洲金融評論報（Australian Financial Review）早在10月3日便示警此一風險，將原因歸咎於股市劇烈波動、科技股與AI股遭到拋售，以及借貸成本急遽攀升。AirTrunk雖官方登記總部位於澳洲，但持有新加坡政府頒發的機房容量許可，並與合資夥伴字節跳動（ByteDance）共同持股；其掛牌案原被預期是今年新加坡交易所規模最大的IPO。該公司於2024年12月由私募股權公司黑石集團（Blackstone）及加拿大退休金計畫投資委員會（CPPIB）收購，計畫募資估值約達44億澳幣（約125億馬幣）。

  彭博另報導，總部位於新加坡、由在上海掛牌的GDS控股（GDS Holdings）支持的DayOne資料中心，正評估是否延後美國IPO時程，或縮減規模，目標募資25億美元（約101.4億馬幣）至30億美元。彭博指出，DayOne一直在等待有利的市場時機，同時也考慮將股票發售推遲至市況改善後再行進行。

  澳洲AI與資料中心公司Firmus Grid也於10月8日宣告延後在澳洲證券交易所（ASX）掛牌。

  ## 信貸壓力籠罩全產業

  IPO退場與整個產業可見的財務壓力同步浮現。在10月12日至16日當周，美國新世代雲端業者CoreWeave——一家大量舉債、專門出租AI算力的公司——所發行的債券價值下跌，其債務違約保險成本更創下歷史新高。大量舉債興建資料中心的甲骨文（Oracle），其債務評級則被信評機構列為僅高於垃圾級一個等級。

  DayOne本身亦處於虧損狀態。其向美國證券交易委員會（SEC）提交的Form 1申請文件顯示，截至2025年12月止財務年度，歸屬於普通股股東的淨虧損達3.69億美元；2026財務年度上半年則再虧損8,200萬美元。儘管如此，公司仍持續舉債。今年8月，新加坡三大本地銀行——星展銀行（DBS Bank）、華僑銀行（OCBC）及大華銀行（UOB）——共同為DayOne在新加坡的第一座資料中心提供一筆為期四年、金額達5.3億新加坡幣（約16.8億馬幣）的綠色貸款。2025年12月，布魯克菲爾德（Brookfield）與一家未具名的全球主權投資人，為DayOne在芬蘭的資料中心提供5億歐元的夾層融資——一種介於優先貸款與股權之間的次順位債務——並可擴大至10億歐元。2026年10月，DayOne再就其位於馬來西亞柔佛州的資料中心取得一筆聯合貸款。

  ## 新加坡資料中心容量計畫

  DayOne在新加坡的資產，是透過政府許可程序取得的，該程序重新開放了新加坡的資料中心市場。2023年，新加坡資訊通信媒體發展局（IMDA）解除新資料中心建設的禁令後，其第一輪「資料中心申請計畫」（DC-CFA，Data Centre Call for Application）——一項以競爭方式將有限電力容量配置給核准業者的程序——共向四個主體分配了80百萬瓦（MW）的容量：即後來更名為DayOne的GDS控股關聯實體、AirTrunk與ByteDance合資體、Equinix，以及微軟（Microsoft）。IMDA第二輪DC-CFA則各分配50 MW給吉寶（Keppel）、Equinix、Digital Realty，以及新電信媒體（ST Telemedia）——上述皆為上市公司或有大型企業背書。

  DayOne在新加坡的資料中心預計明年完工。2025年7月，DayOne在新加坡基地舉行動土典禮時，與勝科電力（Sembcorp Power）簽署購電協議，確保所有營運所需電力均來自再生能源，並與新加坡國立大學（NUS）建立研究開發夥伴關係，共同推進「永續熱帶資料中心試驗臺2.0」（Sustainable Tropical Data Centre Testbed Phase 2.0）計畫。

  在新加坡以外，DayOne已在柔佛州的努沙加雅科技園（Nusajaya Tech Park）及甘巴士科技園（Kempas Tech Park）建立園區，並正在印尼巴淡島（Batam）開發兩座園區：弄沙數位園區（Nongsa Digital Park）及卡比爾工業科技園（Kabil Industrial Tech Park）。公司向SEC提交的申請文件顯示，已從五家全球超大規模業者（hyperscaler）——即大型雲端與科技平台業者——取得約1.4吉瓦（GW）的預訂容量。

  ## 巴淡島的用水衝突

  巴淡島的資料中心快速擴張引發了民眾反彈。BBC於10月13日報導，約有300名居民家中自來水供應中斷，而弄沙數位園區內的資料中心卻似乎有充足水源供設備冷卻使用——這場衝突具體呈現了資料中心建設對周邊社區可能造成的資源壓力。

  新加坡資料中心開發商兼營運商BDx的執行長馬揚克·斯里瓦斯塔瓦（Mayank Srivastava）表示，業界在早期擴張階段未能充分考量對社區的衝擊。「就資料中心而言，業者對社區衝擊的認知或許不夠充分。在初期，開發商只是往有多餘電力的地方跑，等資料中心在那些地點持續擴張，電力供應就必須升級。沒有人真正在思考如何讓成本與誘因相匹配，而實際上成本被轉嫁到社區之外，這本身就不公平，這也是為何會出現強烈社區反彈，」斯里瓦斯塔瓦說。

  他主張，資料中心進駐所需的基礎設施升級費用——例如擴容電網——應由資料中心按比例承擔，而非分攤至當地社區。「如果有一座工廠進駐需要升級電力基礎設施，那麼升級費用就應該由那座工廠來承擔，」他說。

  ## 甲骨文在新墨西哥州的不可抗力爭議

  美國境內也出現了類似的社區與監管阻力。9月，甲骨文就「木星計畫」（Project Jupiter）——一個位於新墨西哥州、總額高達1,650億美元的資料中心園區——向藍貓頭鷹資本（Blue Owl Capital）旗下一家子公司發出不可抗力（force majeure）通知。不可抗力是一項法律條款，允許一方因超出可控範圍的情形而延遲履行合約義務。甲骨文援引此條款，意在爭取延後支付全額租金——最多可達三年——條件是若該基地未能在2028年目標期限前投入營運，則將延遲與融資風險轉嫁回藍貓頭鷹及其融資方，而非逕行退出主力租戶的角色。

  木星計畫在多個層面遭遇挫折。今年7月，新墨西哥州土地辦公室（New Mexico State Land Office）駁回了甲骨文的「綠辣椒計畫」（Green Chile Project）申請——這是一條長達27.4公里的天然氣管線延伸工程，用以為基地燃料電池供應燃料。州府及地方官員也就預估耗水量、二氧化碳排放量，以及他們認為對新墨西哥州直接經濟效益不足等問題，對該計畫提出異議。10月14日，甲骨文向埃爾帕索電力公司（El Paso Electric）提出一項為期十年的能源服務協議，表示願意支付1億美元以支持新墨西哥州民眾享有平價電費。

  ## 降溫釋出的訊號

  IPO同步喊停、債務成本上揚、信用評級遭調降，以及多個司法管轄區接連出現監管與社區阻力，共同指向支撐大規模資料中心建設的有利條件正在收緊。業者過去依賴私募股權、主權資本與銀行聯合貸款，搶在股票投資回報到位之前建設容量，但隨著借貸成本上升，這套模式所承受的壓力日益加重。

  來自超大規模業者的基本需求並未消失。DayOne宣稱取得1.4 GW的預訂容量，顯示大型科技公司的進駐承諾依然持續。然而，從一連串IPO延後的情況來看，這些需求訊號與近期IPO申請文件所尋求的估值之間的落差，在當前利率環境下，似乎已超過公開股市投資人所能接受的水準。

  截至本文發稿時，DayOne尚未公布美國IPO的修訂時程或最終規模，AirTrunk亦未宣布新加坡交易所掛牌的新日期。
