body_en: |
  China has built dozens of data centers in its rural southwestern province of Guizhou as part of a national strategy to support rapid AI expansion along the eastern seaboard using cheaper land, cooler temperatures, and abundant renewable energy — while the economic benefits to host communities remain contested.

  Huawei's largest data center, a town-like campus in the Guian New Area of Guizhou, anchors a cluster that also includes facilities operated by Tencent and Apple. China is on course to nearly double its total data center capacity within the next five years as the technology race with the United States intensifies and countries compete to secure computing resources for AI development.

  ## Eastern Data Western Computing

  The Chinese government formalized the build-out in 2021 under a policy called "Eastern Data Western Computing," which redirects data processing workloads from densely populated, energy-constrained coastal cities to less populated western regions. Guizhou's cooler climate, available land, and growing renewable energy base made it an early focal point of the program.

  China has mandated that data centers draw 80 percent of their power from renewable sources by the end of this decade. Regions such as Guizhou and Inner Mongolia, which have expanded solar and wind capacity significantly, are well positioned to help operators meet that requirement, according to Simeng Deng, a senior analyst at Rystad Energy.

  Deng also noted a secondary benefit: the large, continuous power draw of server farms can absorb surplus electricity from renewables, a problem known as curtailment, in which more power is generated than the grid can use at a given moment. Data centers "can be an engine to digest or absorb the surplus of renewable power generation," Deng said.

  ## Limited local economic gains

  Despite the scale of investment, researchers question how much the data center concentration has improved living standards in Guizhou.

  The province has averaged annual GDP growth of 7.4 percent over the past decade, but wage growth over the same period ranked second to last among all Chinese provinces, according to a report by the Taiwan-based Research Institute for Democracy, Society and Emerging Technology (DSET). DSET researchers Cartus Bo-Xiang You and Angela Glowacki concluded that while data centers drive heavy investment in land and equipment, "their impact on boosting local per capita income remains limited."

  Andrew Stokols, a researcher at Singapore Management University, said data centers do not necessarily create a large spillover effect on local employment. "The hope is that you can use data centres as an engine for broader digital development, bringing in industries that will come in around data centres," Stokols said, adding that "immediate benefits have been elusive" both in China and elsewhere.

  The infrastructure push has also contributed to a significant debt burden. Guizhou ranked 30th among Chinese provinces for its debt-to-revenue ratio in 2024 and 27th for its debt-to-GDP ratio, according to DSET data.

  Residents of Guian New Area interviewed in July expressed broadly positive views of the development. Local shopkeeper Shu Peihua said the area had transformed from "a barren mountain" as transportation improved and trade expanded. Resident Li Xixiu said employment opportunities increased after the centers were built, with villagers no longer needing to travel elsewhere for work. A street vendor cited the establishment of two universities in the area since the facilities opened.

  In contrast to community opposition that has accompanied data center projects in Europe and the United States — where residents have raised concerns about energy prices, water use, and noise — public dissent in China is tightly controlled, particularly around central government policy.

  ## National competitiveness driving continued construction

  The build-out is set to continue regardless of the debate over local economic impact. Stokols said Chinese policy documents were linking computing power to national power even before the AI boom triggered by tools such as ChatGPT. "It's essential for national competitiveness in the age of digital technology," he said. "The AI competition has accelerated that."

  China's trajectory toward nearly doubling data center capacity within five years reflects the scale of that national ambition.

  中國在西南省份貴州集中建設數十座資料中心，作為國家戰略的一環，藉由當地低廉地價、涼爽氣候與豐沛再生能源，為東部沿海的AI快速擴張提供支援。然而，研究人員指出，這波建設潮對當地社區帶來的經濟效益仍充滿爭議。

  華為在貴州貴安新區建設的最大資料中心，規模宛如一座城鎮，是整個資料中心聚落的核心，騰訊與蘋果的機房亦坐落其中。隨著美中技術競賽持續升溫，各國競相爭奪AI發展所需的算力資源，中國預計在未來五年內將資料中心總容量提升近一倍。

  ## 東數西算

  中國政府於2021年正式推動「東數西算」政策，將資料運算工作負載從人口稠密、能源受限的沿海城市，引導至人口較少的西部地區。貴州涼爽的氣候、可供開發的土地，以及日益壯大的再生能源基礎，使其成為這項計畫的早期重點區域。

  中國已規定，資料中心在本十年結束前須以再生能源供應80%的電力。根據睿諮得能源（Rystad Energy）高級分析師鄧思夢（Simeng Deng）的說法，貴州與內蒙古等地大幅擴充了太陽能與風能裝置容量，在協助業者達成這項要求方面具有明顯優勢。

  鄧思夢也點出另一層附帶效益：伺服器機房持續且大量的用電需求，有助於消納再生能源的剩餘電力。這種現象稱為「棄電」（curtailment），意指在某一時刻發電量超過電網所能消納的程度。她表示，資料中心「可以作為消化或吸收再生能源剩餘發電量的引擎」。

  ## 在地經濟效益有限

  儘管投資規模龐大，研究人員仍質疑，資料中心的集中建設究竟在多大程度上改善了貴州的民生水準。

  根據台灣民主、社會與新興科技研究院（DSET）發布的報告，貴州過去十年的年均GDP成長率達7.4%，但同期薪資成長幅度在全國各省中名列倒數第二。DSET研究員游博翔（Cartus Bo-Xiang You）與安琪拉．格拉瓦基（Angela Glowacki）結論指出，資料中心雖帶動土地與設備的大量投資，但「對提升當地人均所得的效果依然有限」。

  新加坡管理大學研究員安德魯．斯托科爾斯（Andrew Stokols）表示，資料中心不見得能為當地就業創造顯著的外溢效果。他說：「外界期待能以資料中心作為引擎，帶動更廣泛的數位發展，吸引相關產業進駐周邊。」他補充道，「但無論在中國還是其他地方，立即可見的效益始終難以企及。」

  基礎設施建設熱潮也造成貴州背負沉重的債務負擔。根據DSET資料，2024年貴州的債務收入比在全國各省中排名第30，債務GDP比則排名第27。

  今年7月，貴安新區的居民受訪時普遍表達正面看法。當地雜貨店老闆舒培花表示，隨著交通改善、商業往來擴大，這一帶已從「一片荒山」蛻變至今。居民李喜秀說，資料中心建成後就業機會增加，村民不再需要外出打工。一名攤販則提到，設施開幕以來，當地已新設了兩所大學。

  相較於歐美地區的資料中心開發案往往引發社區反對——居民常就電費上漲、用水問題與噪音提出抗議——中國境內的公眾異見受到嚴密管控，尤其涉及中央政府政策之時更是如此。

  ## 國家競爭力驅動持續建設

  無論在地經濟效益的爭議如何，這波建設潮都將持續下去。斯托科爾斯指出，早在ChatGPT等工具引爆AI熱潮之前，中國的政策文件就已將算力與國家力量緊密連結。「在數位科技時代，算力對國家競爭力至關重要，」他說，「AI競賽更進一步加速了這一進程。」

  中國預計在五年內將資料中心容量提升近一倍的發展軌跡，正清楚反映出這股源自國家層次的雄心壯志。
