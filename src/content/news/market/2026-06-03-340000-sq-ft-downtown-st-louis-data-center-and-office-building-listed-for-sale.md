  A 340,000-square-foot building combining data center and office space in downtown St. Louis, Missouri has been listed for sale. The property carries a history of enterprise data center operation by health insurance providers, making it one of the larger legacy infrastructure assets to come to market in the Midwest urban core.

  ## Property Profile

  At 340,000 sq ft, the asset is substantial for the St. Louis market. Its downtown location typically implies dense fiber infrastructure, proximity to enterprise tenants, and established utility relationships — attributes that matter to data center operators evaluating a retrofit. The mixed data center and office footprint gives prospective buyers flexibility: a colocation provider could focus capital on repurposing the data hall space, while an enterprise operator could use the combined footprint as an owned campus with attached administrative facilities.

  ## Healthcare IT Heritage

  The building's background in health insurance data center operations is a meaningful detail for compliance-sensitive buyers. Healthcare-sector data centers are generally built to high availability and physical security standards, reflecting HIPAA and related regulatory requirements. That heritage can shorten the due-diligence checklist for buyers who need to demonstrate a compliance-ready environment. However, prospective operators should conduct thorough assessment of installed power capacity, cooling plant, and mechanical systems — legacy facilities often carry deferred capital investment, and the gap between historical use and current colocation or hyperscale standards can be significant.

  ## Market Context

  The listing adds a rare large-footprint option to a secondary Midwest market that has attracted growing attention as operators look beyond saturated Tier 1 hubs. St. Louis offers central U.S. geography, multiple long-haul fiber routes, lower real estate costs than Chicago or Kansas City, and an established enterprise customer base anchored by healthcare, financial services, and logistics sectors. Purpose-built supply in the market remains limited, which means a 340,000-square-foot downtown building with a data center pedigree — even one requiring modernization investment — represents an uncommon opportunity for a buyer with a repositioning thesis.
