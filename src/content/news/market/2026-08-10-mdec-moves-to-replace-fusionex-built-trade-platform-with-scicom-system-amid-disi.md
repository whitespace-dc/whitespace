body_en: |
  Malaysia's Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC) is replacing the Digital Free Trade Zone (DFTZ) platform originally built by collapsed software firm Fusionex with a new system developed by Bursa Malaysia-listed Scicom MSC Bhd, as the agency moves to reclaim control of infrastructure that underpins the country's e-commerce import-clearance process.

  The transition, which Scicom's new platform — designated DFTZ2.0 — will execute, has been accompanied by a social-media disinformation campaign falsely accusing MDEC chief executive Anuar Fariz of improperly appointing Scicom, a company critics claim lacks experience in government projects. Anuar and Leo Ariyanayakam, the founder and chief executive of Scicom, have both rejected those claims as untrue, according to Digital News Asia (DNA), which reported the story.

  ## What the DFTZ is and why it matters

  The DFTZ is an electronic trade platform connecting merchants, logistics operators, and government agencies to Malaysia's Customs clearance systems. Its purpose is to digitise the processing of e-commerce shipments — specifically goods valued below RM500, classified under the De Minimis threshold — that were previously handled through manual paperwork submitted physically to the Royal Malaysian Customs Department.

  Before the DFTZ existed, no government agency had an accurate picture of De Minimis import volumes into Malaysia because all associated documentation was filed manually. MDEC identified the problem as early as 2016 and designed the DFTZ as the solution, with Chinese e-commerce giant Alibaba appointed as the first catalytic partner.

  The DFTZ operates by linking to the Sistem Maklumat Kastam (SMK), the backbone of Malaysia's Customs clearance process. SMK integrates with the National Single Window (NSW), the national single electronic trade gateway that replaced multiple separate submission channels for traders and government agencies. DagangNet Technologies Sdn Bhd, a wholly owned subsidiary of DNeX Bhd, built and has operated the NSW since 2009 and was, until 2017, the only entity with access to SMK. MDEC obtained access to SMK from the Ministry of Finance in 2017 as part of a broader government digitalisation push.

  ## How Fusionex won and what it was promised

  In 2017, MDEC invited vendors to build the DFTZ platform under a revenue-share model, explicitly declining to pay for the system itself. Fusionex, then a widely respected Malaysian software company led by founder and chief executive Ivan Teh, won the bid by offering to cover all platform costs in exchange for retaining the data generated and the intellectual property in the system. As part of the arrangement, Fusionex committed to paying MDEC a guaranteed RM2 million (approximately USD 450,000 at current rates) per year.

  The entity operating the platform on Fusionex's behalf was incorporated as FX DFTZ eServices Sdn Bhd, a detail confirmed to DNA by a person familiar with Fusionex's finances and indirectly affirmed by the country head of a global company that worked under the DFTZ structure.

  ## Fusionex's collapse and its lingering shadow

  Fusionex imploded in December 2023, falling from a position as one of Malaysia's most prominent homegrown technology exporters to facing allegations of massive corporate fraud. Ivan Teh has remained publicly silent on the allegations. Deloitte Malaysia was appointed as liquidator and has been conducting an investigation into Fusionex's books for more than two years, a process that remained ongoing at the time of DNA's reporting.

  A person familiar with the liquidation told DNA that "something is not right with their books." A former Fusionex manager told DNA that signs of irregularity appeared as early as 2019, specifically in relation to the DFTZ project, where accounts appeared to be transferred to a separate company. Those suspicions at the time were obscured by two structural features of Fusionex's operations, the former manager said.

  First, each major business division within Fusionex — covering e-commerce, commodities, casino entertainment systems, and software services — operated in complete silos, with division heads reporting individually and separately to Ivan and his brother Isaac rather than across functions. A senior Fusionex executive corroborated that account to DNA. Second, Ivan's management style generated strong personal loyalty; the former manager described him as "a great boss who always took care of his people," a dynamic that DNA notes has made it difficult for liquidators to persuade former Fusionex C-suite executives to cooperate against him even under offers of immunity.

  The irregularities being investigated coincided with the period in which Ivan was in the closing stages of negotiating the sale of Fusionex International Plc to Japan's Hitachi Ltd, a deal Hitachi announced on April 10, 2020.

  ## MDEC's call for a replacement platform

  Despite the DFTZ platform continuing to function, MDEC issued what it deliberately framed as a "call for partnership" — not a conventional government tender — in January 2025 to rebuild the system. The agency replicated the original revenue-share structure, seeking a private partner to fund and build the replacement without direct government expenditure. Scicom MSC Bhd was subsequently selected.

  MDEC has characterised the transition as a necessary step to reclaim a nationally significant platform from an entity — Fusionex and its successor vehicle FX DFTZ eServices — that the agency determined could no longer properly hold it, following due process.

  ## Fear and reluctance

  Despite the DFTZ not being a project running into hundreds of millions of ringgit, DNA reported an unusual degree of fear and unwillingness to speak on the record among parties involved. The Royal Malaysian Customs Department's public relations unit did not respond to questions. A retired senior Customs officer involved in the project from the outset declined to speak. Former MDEC chief executive Mahadhir Aziz and former chairman Syed Ibrahim, who held their posts when the dispute with Fusionex surfaced internally in 2024, both declined comment, referring questions to MDEC and citing their departure from the agency. An industry association was unable to persuade any member company to speak even off the record. A global company's country head declined to share information, citing non-disclosure agreements.

  ## Significance

  The transition matters beyond the immediate platform dispute. The DFTZ represents a single point of digital access to Malaysia's Customs clearance infrastructure for De Minimis e-commerce — a segment that has grown rapidly in volume and that no government agency could accurately quantify before the platform existed. Ownership of the platform, and of the data it generates, carries commercial and policy significance for Malaysia's digital trade governance.

  The disinformation campaign directed at MDEC's chief executive illustrates a broader risk for government-linked digital infrastructure projects: that transitions between private technology partners can be disrupted through targeted online attacks on decision-makers, regardless of the factual basis of the claims.

  Deloitte Malaysia's liquidation investigation into Fusionex's full financial history, now in its third year, remains ongoing.
body_zh: |
  馬來西亞數位經濟公司（MDEC）正以馬來西亞交易所（Bursa Malaysia）上市企業Scicom MSC Bhd所開發的新系統，取代原由已倒閉軟體公司Fusionex建置的數位自由貿易區（DFTZ）平台，藉此重新掌控支撐馬國電子商務進口通關流程的核心基礎設施。

  Scicom的新平台命名為DFTZ2.0，這項移交作業同時伴隨著一波社群媒體假訊息攻勢。不實言論指控MDEC執行長Anuar Fariz以不當方式授予Scicom合約，批評者稱該公司缺乏執行政府專案的經驗。根據率先報導此事的《Digital News Asia》（DNA）指出，Anuar與Scicom創辦人兼執行長Leo Ariyanayakam均否認上述說法，斥之為不實指控。

  ## 數位自由貿易區的定位與重要性

  DFTZ是一套電子化貿易平台，將商家、物流業者與政府機關連結至馬來西亞海關的通關系統。其核心功能在於將電子商務貨件的處理流程數位化——尤其是定價低於500令吉、依「最低應稅額」（De Minimis）門檻分類的商品，這些貨件在過去必須以紙本文件人工遞交至馬來西亞皇家海關。

  DFTZ建置之前，由於所有相關文件均以人工方式申報，政府機關完全無法掌握馬國最低應稅額進口商品的實際數量。MDEC早在2016年便識別出這項問題，並以DFTZ作為解決方案，同時邀請中國電商巨頭阿里巴巴擔任首個催化合作夥伴。

  DFTZ透過連結「Sistem Maklumat Kastam」（SMK）系統運作，SMK是馬來西亞海關通關流程的核心骨幹。SMK整合了國家單一窗口（National Single Window，NSW）——一個取代多個獨立申報管道的國家統一電子貿易閘道。DNeX Bhd的全資子公司DagangNet Technologies Sdn Bhd自2009年起建置並營運NSW，且直至2017年為止，是唯一可存取SMK的機構。MDEC於2017年從財政部取得SMK存取權，此舉是政府更廣泛數位化推進計畫的一環。

  ## Fusionex如何得標及其條件承諾

  2017年，MDEC以「收益分成」模式邀請廠商建置DFTZ平台，明確表示不直接支付系統建置費用。當時仍是馬來西亞最受敬重軟體公司之一的Fusionex，在創辦人兼執行長Ivan Teh的領導下勝出，提出由Fusionex自行承擔所有平台費用，換取保有系統所產生的資料及相關智慧財產權。依據合約安排，Fusionex承諾每年向MDEC支付保證金200萬令吉（按現行匯率約合45萬美元）。

  實際營運此平台的法人實體被登記為FX DFTZ eServices Sdn Bhd，這一細節由熟悉Fusionex財務狀況的人士向DNA確認，並獲得一名曾在DFTZ架構下合作的全球企業馬來西亞負責人間接印證。

  ## Fusionex的崩潰與遺留問題

  Fusionex於2023年12月急速殞落，從馬來西亞最具代表性的本土科技出口商之一，淪為面臨大規模企業詐欺指控的對象。Ivan Teh至今對此保持公開沉默。德勤馬來西亞（Deloitte Malaysia）獲任命為清算人，展開對Fusionex帳冊的調查，這項調查在DNA發稿時已進行逾兩年，仍在持續進行中。

  一名知悉清算進度的消息人士向DNA透露：「他們的帳目明顯有問題。」一名前Fusionex主管則表示，異常跡象早在2019年便已浮現，具體與DFTZ專案有關——帳目疑似遭轉移至另一家公司。這名前主管指出，當時的可疑跡象被Fusionex內部兩項結構性特徵所掩蓋。

  其一，Fusionex旗下各主要業務部門——涵蓋電子商務、大宗商品、博弈娛樂系統及軟體服務——各自在完全獨立的部門體制下運作，各部門主管分別直屬於Ivan及其弟Isaac，彼此之間並無橫向溝通。一名Fusionex前高階主管也向DNA證實了這一說法。其二，Ivan的管理風格在員工之間建立了強烈的個人忠誠；這名前主管形容他「是個非常照顧下屬的好老闆」，DNA指出，這種情感紐帶使得清算人在提供免責承諾的情況下，仍難以說服前Fusionex高管出面作證。

  上述被調查的帳目異常期間，恰與Ivan正處於談判將Fusionex International Plc出售給日本日立公司（Hitachi Ltd）最後階段的時間重疊——日立於2020年4月10日正式宣布了這項收購案。

  ## MDEC尋求替代平台

  儘管DFTZ平台持續正常運作，MDEC仍於2025年1月特意以「徵求合作夥伴」而非傳統政府標案的形式，公告啟動系統重建計畫。該機構複製了原有的收益分成架構，尋求由私部門夥伴自行出資建置替代系統，無需政府直接支出。Scicom MSC Bhd其後獲得選定。

  MDEC將這次移交定性為必要之舉——目的是依照正當程序，將這個具全國戰略意義的平台，從已確定無法妥善持有的Fusionex及其後繼法人FX DFTZ eServices手中重新收回。

  ## 普遍的恐懼與噤聲

  儘管DFTZ並非涉資數億令吉的龐大專案，DNA報導揭示了相關各方異乎尋常的恐懼與拒絕公開受訪的態度。馬來西亞皇家海關的公關部門對採訪問題未予置理。一名從計畫啟動之初便參與其中的退休資深海關官員拒絕受訪。前MDEC執行長Mahadhir Aziz與前主席Syed Ibrahim——兩人任職期間，恰好是2024年Fusionex爭議在內部浮現之時——均婉拒置評，僅將採訪問題轉交現任MDEC處理，並以已離職為由迴避。一個業界公會甚至無法說服任何會員企業在匿名條件下發言。一家全球企業的馬來西亞區負責人則以保密協議為由，拒絕提供任何資訊。

  ## 意義與影響

  這場移交作業的意義遠不止於眼前的平台之爭。DFTZ是馬來西亞海關通關基礎設施在最低應稅額電子商務領域的唯一數位存取入口——而這個市場區隔的交易量近年急速攀升，且在該平台問世之前，沒有任何政府機關能夠準確掌握其規模。平台的所有權，以及其所產生的資料，對馬來西亞數位貿易治理具有深遠的商業與政策意涵。

  針對MDEC執行長的假訊息攻勢，也揭示了政府關聯數位基礎設施專案所面臨的更廣泛風險：民間科技夥伴之間的移交過程，可能因針對決策者的網路輿論攻擊而遭到干擾，無論相關指控是否有事實根據。

  德勤馬來西亞對Fusionex完整財務歷史的清算調查，目前已進入第三年，仍在持續進行中。
