body_en: |
  Britain's government has staked its AI industrial strategy on tens of billions of pounds in commitments from American technology companies, but a series of setbacks — including Microsoft's October 2026 withdrawal from a flagship data center project and a grid power shortfall that could delay key sites until the early-to-mid 2030s — has exposed the limits of an approach built on attracting foreign-owned infrastructure rather than cultivating domestic capacity.

  The assessment, made by David Sherman of io.net writing for Data Center Dynamics, challenges Prime Minister Andy Burnham's framing of Britain as an "honest broker" in global AI governance, arguing that the country's diplomatic positioning rests on infrastructure controlled almost entirely by US companies.

  ## US companies anchor UK AI buildout

  Microsoft has pledged £22bn ($28.8bn) in UK investment over four years — its largest-ever commitment in the country — with roughly half directed at land, buildings, and equipment. Google has opened a data center at Waltham Cross as part of a £5bn ($6.4bn) package that also covers research and DeepMind operations.

  The concentration extends to cloud computing. AWS and Microsoft each account for 30 to 40 percent of UK cloud infrastructure revenues, with Google a distant third. UKCloud, a British provider built for public sector contracts, went into liquidation in 2022. AI investment is tracking the same pattern, Sherman writes.

  ## Legal sovereignty and the CLOUD Act

  Physical location does not determine legal jurisdiction. Under the US CLOUD Act — a 2018 US law allowing American authorities to compel US companies to hand over data they control regardless of where it is stored — the US government can access data held on a server in Britain by an American provider. When French senators asked Microsoft France's head of public and legal affairs whether he could guarantee French citizens' data would not go to US authorities without France's consent, he said he could not.

  Sherman notes the same exposure would exist if Britain's infrastructure were dominated by any single foreign country, not only the United States.

  ## Tech Prosperity Deal setbacks

  The government's headline response was the Tech Prosperity Deal, valued at £31bn ($40.6bn), with £22bn coming from Microsoft. The deal included Stargate UK — OpenAI's plan to run its technology on Nvidia chips at data center sites operated by Nscale — billed as a vehicle for developing sovereign AI capability. In April 2026, OpenAI put the project on hold, citing UK energy costs and regulation. That decision was OpenAI's, not the government's.

  In October 2026, Microsoft withdrew from the data center at Loughton in Essex it had been developing with Nscale. The Loughton site had been billed as the location for the UK's largest AI supercomputer, with Microsoft as the anchor customer. The site has reportedly been told it will not receive sufficient grid power until the early to mid-2030s. Microsoft said it remains committed to its broader $30bn UK investment plan.

  After OpenAI's departure from Nscale's sites in April, Google reportedly took capacity at a separate Nscale data center in West London.

  ## Grid constraints and AI Growth Zones

  The government has established AI Growth Zones intended to streamline planning and power connections for large compute developments. One of them, at Blyth in Northumberland, has £10bn ($13.1bn) in backing from Blackstone, the US investment firm. Microsoft said its ability to invest at scale in Britain owed "no small measure" to government work on planning reform and electricity capacity.

  The Loughton delay illustrates a constraint facing projects of all sizes: land, power, and a grid connection are the most difficult inputs to secure for a data center in Britain. A site at the center of the government's flagship deal facing a potential decade-long wait for sufficient power indicates the scale of the grid problem confronting the broader market.

  ## GPU access and idle capacity

  Large cloud providers typically secure next-generation GPU allocations well in advance, leaving British startups to compete for remaining capacity at higher prices or accept long-term contracts with Amazon, Microsoft, or Google. Sherman notes there is nonetheless significant idle compute sitting in company data centers, university facilities, and smaller providers, with additional capacity tied up in contracts that customers are not fully using.

  Sherman argues the government should back platforms that aggregate spare capacity across both centralized facilities and distributed networks, establish a publicly funded compute access program allowing UK teams to rent capacity by the hour without committing to a hyperscaler contract, and introduce open standards to reduce the cost of moving workloads between providers.

  ## EU moves toward ownership requirements

  The European Union has drawn parallel conclusions. More than 80 percent of the EU's key digital products, services, and infrastructure come from outside Europe, the Commission has stated. In June 2026, the Commission published a package of proposals — including the Cloud and AI Development Act — to reduce reliance on American and Chinese firms across chips, cloud, AI, and open-source software. Under the act, public sector cloud contracts above certain risk thresholds would require a European-owned and European-controlled provider, not merely one that stores data within Europe.

  Britain's current approach has focused primarily on getting buildings built on UK soil rather than on ownership or control criteria of that kind.

  ## Burnham's diplomatic positioning

  Burnham announced a US defense AI partnership at the United Nations in New York in September 2026, where he also told other countries that Britain could serve as an honest broker on AI governance because of its ties with the US, Europe, China, India, and Brazil. He has said AI will be at the heart of Britain's G20 presidency, with the summit scheduled for Manchester in 2027.

  Sherman's argument is that an honest-broker role requires leverage, and that leverage is difficult to maintain when the infrastructure underpinning a country's AI capacity is predominantly owned and legally governed by a single foreign partner.

  ## What it means for the industry

  For data center operators and planners, the near-term practical constraint remains grid capacity. The Loughton situation demonstrates that even projects with large anchor customers, government backing, and a prominent place in a national deal can face power waits extending into the next decade. For policymakers, the EU's move toward ownership-based sovereignty thresholds in public sector cloud contracts sets a marker that Britain has not yet matched.

  Burnham's G20 presidency will give Britain a platform to raise AI governance before leaders from countries facing comparable infrastructure dependencies when world leaders convene in Manchester in 2027.
body_zh: |
  英國政府以美國科技巨頭數百億英鎊的投資承諾作為AI產業戰略的基石，然而一連串挫折——包括微軟於2026年10月退出一項旗艦資料中心計畫，以及電網供電缺口可能使關鍵基地延至2030年代初至中期才能正式運作——已揭露出這套「引進外資基礎建設、輕忽本土能量培育」策略的侷限。

  上述評估出自io.net的大衛・謝爾曼（David Sherman），發表於《資料中心動態》（Data Center Dynamics）。文章質疑英國首相安迪・伯納姆（Andy Burnham）將英國定位為全球AI治理「誠信中間人」的論述，指出英國的外交姿態所依賴的基礎建設，幾乎全數由美國企業掌控。

  ## 美企主導英國AI建設

  微軟承諾四年內在英國投資220億英鎊（約288億美元），創下其在英最大規模的投資承諾，其中約半數資金流向土地、廠房與設備。谷歌（Google）在沃爾瑟姆克羅斯（Waltham Cross）開設一座資料中心，作為50億英鎊（約64億美元）投資套案的一部分，涵蓋研究業務與DeepMind的相關營運。

  這種集中態勢同樣延伸至雲端運算領域。AWS與微軟各佔英國雲端基礎建設營收的30%至40%，谷歌則遠遠落後位居第三。英國本土供應商UKCloud以承接公部門合約起家，已於2022年清算倒閉。謝爾曼指出，AI投資正循著相同軌跡發展。

  ## 法律主權與CLOUD法案

  實體位置並不決定法律管轄權。依據美國《雲端法》（CLOUD Act，2018年通過），美國當局可強制要求美國企業提交其所管控的資料，無論資料儲存於何處。當法國參議員詢問微軟法國公共暨法律事務主管，能否保證法國公民的資料不會在未經法國同意的情況下移交美國當局，對方坦言無法做出此項保證。

  謝爾曼指出，若英國的基礎建設由任何單一外國主導，而非僅限美國，同樣的風險照樣存在。

  ## 科技繁榮協議受挫

  政府最受矚目的回應是「科技繁榮協議」（Tech Prosperity Deal），總值310億英鎊（約406億美元），其中220億英鎊來自微軟。該協議包含「英國星際之門」（Stargate UK）計畫——OpenAI擬將其技術部署於輝達晶片上，由Nscale負責營運資料中心基地——被定位為發展主權AI能力的載體。2026年4月，OpenAI以英國的能源成本與監管環境為由，宣布暫緩該計畫。這是OpenAI的決定，而非政府的選擇。

  2026年10月，微軟退出其與Nscale共同開發、位於艾塞克斯郡洛頓（Loughton）的資料中心。洛頓基地原被定位為英國最大AI超級電腦的所在地，微軟是主要的錨定客戶。據報導，該基地被告知，要到2030年代初至中期才能獲得充足的電網供電。微軟表示，其在英國整體300億美元的投資計畫承諾不變。

  OpenAI於4月撤出Nscale各基地後，谷歌據報已承接西倫敦另一座Nscale資料中心的部分容量。

  ## 電網限制與AI成長區

  政府設立了「AI成長區」（AI Growth Zones），旨在簡化大型運算設施的規劃審批與電力接入程序。其中位於諾森伯蘭郡布萊斯（Blyth）的成長區，獲得美國投資公司百仕通（Blackstone）100億英鎊（約131億美元）的資金支持。微軟表示，政府在規劃改革與電力容量方面的努力，「在相當程度上」促成了其大規模投資的意願。

  洛頓案例揭示了所有規模計畫共同面臨的制約：土地、電力與電網接入，是在英國建設資料中心最難取得的三項關鍵要素。一個身處政府旗艦協議核心、卻可能面臨長達十年等待充足電力的基地，充分說明了整體市場所面對的電網困境有多嚴峻。

  ## GPU取得困難與閒置算力

  大型雲端供應商通常提前鎖定次世代GPU的配額，使英國新創企業只能以更高的價格競爭剩餘容量，或被迫接受與亞馬遜、微軟、谷歌簽訂長期合約。謝爾曼指出，儘管如此，企業資料中心、大學設施及中小型供應商內部仍有大量閒置算力，另有部分容量因客戶未充分使用的合約而遭到佔用。

  謝爾曼主張，政府應支持能整合集中式設施與分散式網路閒置容量的平台，建立由公共資金資助的算力取用計畫，讓英國團隊能以小時計費租用算力、無須綁定超大規模業者的合約，並推動開放標準以降低工作負載在不同供應商間遷移的成本。

  ## 歐盟朝所有權要求方向邁進

  歐盟已得出類似結論。歐盟委員會表示，超過80%的歐盟關鍵數位產品、服務與基礎建設來自歐洲以外。2026年6月，歐盟委員會發布一套政策提案——包括《雲端與AI發展法》（Cloud and AI Development Act）——旨在降低歐洲在晶片、雲端、AI與開源軟體領域對美國及中國企業的依賴。依據該法案，超過特定風險門檻的公部門雲端合約，將要求由歐洲所有、歐洲控制的供應商承接，而非僅要求資料儲存於歐洲境內。

  英國目前的做法，主要著眼於在英國土地上蓋起建築，尚未觸及此類所有權或控制權的標準。

  ## 伯納姆的外交定位

  伯納姆於2026年9月在紐約聯合國大會上宣布一項英美國防AI夥伴關係，同場亦向其他國家表示，英國因與美國、歐洲、中國、印度及巴西均有連結，可在AI治理上扮演誠信中間人的角色。他表示，AI將是英國擔任G20輪值主席國的核心議題，峰會預計於2027年在曼徹斯特舉行。

  謝爾曼的論點是：要扮演誠信中間人，需要有足夠的籌碼，而當一國AI能力所依賴的基礎建設，主要由單一外國夥伴所擁有並受其法律管轄時，這種籌碼便難以維持。

  ## 對產業的意義

  對資料中心營運商與規劃業者而言，近期最實際的制約仍是電網容量。洛頓案例說明，即便是擁有大型錨定客戶、政府背書、且在國家協議中佔有顯眼地位的計畫，也可能面臨供電等待期延伸至下一個十年的困境。對政策制定者而言，歐盟朝向在公部門雲端合約中設定所有權型主權門檻的舉措，樹立了一個英國至今尚未比肩的標竿。

  2027年世界領袖齊聚曼徹斯特之際，伯納姆的G20輪值主席任期將賦予英國一個平台，向同樣面臨基礎建設依賴問題的各國領袖提出AI治理議題。
