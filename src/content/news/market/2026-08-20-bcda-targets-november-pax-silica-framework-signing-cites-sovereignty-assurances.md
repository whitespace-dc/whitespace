body_en: |
  The Philippines' Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) said US-Philippine negotiations over a governing framework for the country's Pax Silica technology hub are continuing, with the agency targeting a November signing while acknowledging the timeline may extend.

  BCDA President and Chief Executive Officer Joshua M. Bingcang, speaking at the Kapihan sa Manila Bay forum on Wednesday, described the framework as a business contract covering commercial, technical, and legal aspects. "That is something that we want, but negotiations can always take longer," Bingcang said. "As long as the two parties come up with a mutually agreed arrangement, it should be a win-win."

  ## The hub

  The Philippines is one of 24 signatories to the US-led Pax Silica initiative, which seeks to build partnerships in chip manufacturing, artificial intelligence (AI) hardware, and data centers. Under the initiative, the BCDA is developing a 1,619-hectare AI hub in New Clark City, Tarlac, specializing in critical components for AI infrastructure.

  Bingcang said the hub is "purely commercial" with no defense-related activities, and that no hyperscale data centers — the largest-scale facilities in the industry — will be built as part of the project.

  ## Sovereignty assurances

  Bingcang cited US Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg as stating that "sovereignty will reside with the host country" for any Pax Silica-related development. "It's a business contract. Normally it is favorable to the host country," Bingcang said.

  Those statements follow scrutiny over reports — described by one legal commentator as either floated proposals or rumors — that Economic Security Zones envisioned under the initiative might carry diplomatic-style immunities or apply foreign legal standards on Philippine soil. Jemy Gatdula, dean of the UA&P Law School and a Philippine Judicial Academy lecturer for constitutional philosophy and jurisprudence, argued in an analysis published by BusinessWorld that such arrangements would conflict with constitutional provisions on sovereignty, the national economy, and natural resources.

  Gatdula cited the Regalian Doctrine — codified in Article XII, Section 2 of the Philippine Constitution — which reserves ownership and ultimate control of natural resources to the state. He cited the Supreme Court's ruling in La Bugal-B'laan Tribal Association v. Ramos as establishing that foreign participation in resource extraction is permissible only where the Philippine government retains ultimate control, supervision, and management. He also cited Nicolas v. Romulo as affirming that while the Executive may conclude international agreements, constitutional limits remain controlling and foreign entities operating within Philippine territory remain subject to Philippine jurisdiction.

  ## Power, water, and incentives

  The hub will operate on its own independent power and utilities supply and will not draw on the Luzon power system, Bingcang said. "That is our goal — to have an embedded, independent power supply," he said. For water, the BCDA is considering impounding tanks to collect rainwater for industrial use.

  Investment incentives will be governed by two existing laws: Republic Act 7227, the Bases Conversion and Development Act of 1992, which authorizes the BCDA's special economic zones, and Republic Act 12066, the CREATE MORE Act (Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises to Maximize Opportunities for Reinvigorating the Economy Act), which extends tax benefits to qualifying enterprises.

  ## Projected scale

  Government projections cited in Gatdula's analysis estimate the initiative could attract $40 billion to $70 billion in investments across semiconductor manufacturing, AI, cloud infrastructure, critical minerals processing, and advanced logistics. The New Clark City hub alone is projected to generate more than 130,000 jobs, while the broader Luzon Economic Corridor — a regional development zone associated with the initiative — could eventually support up to one million positions across manufacturing, logistics, research, construction, and supporting industries, Gatdula wrote. More than 50 multinational companies have reportedly expressed interest in participating.

  Gatdula argued the initiative represents the Philippines' best opportunity in a generation to move beyond low-value manufacturing into semiconductors, AI, and advanced electronics. He framed it partly as a response to what he described as China's use of trade, investment, tourism, and supply chains as economic pressure against countries resisting its geopolitical aims — a pattern he said has affected the Philippines, Australia, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan to varying degrees. He also noted that the Philippines is a founding member of the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), described as the world's largest free trade agreement, and argued that policymakers must balance that framework's liberalizing commitments with security-driven controls on sensitive technology sectors.

  ## Opposition

  The project has drawn public opposition over its anticipated energy and water demands, potential environmental degradation, and possible displacement of local communities. Bingcang said the BCDA will provide financial assistance and livelihood programs for those affected.

  ## Significance

  The November target, if met, would formally anchor the Philippines' role within the Pax Silica bloc and provide a legal basis for investment decisions by the multinational companies that have expressed interest. The negotiation outcome will also determine whether sovereignty assurances cited by both governments are reflected in binding contractual language — the question Philippine constitutional scholars and legal commentators have identified as the central test of the arrangement.

  Bingcang said the BCDA will continue to pursue a November signing while acknowledging the timeline is not fixed.
body_zh: |
  菲律賓基地轉型暨發展局（BCDA）表示，菲美雙方就「Pax Silica」科技園區管理框架協議的協商持續推進，該機構以11月為目標完成簽署，但同時坦言時程可能延後。

  BCDA局長兼執行長Joshua M. Bingcang週三出席馬尼拉灣「Kapihan sa Manila Bay」論壇時，將此框架協議定性為涵蓋商業、技術與法律層面的商業合約。他表示：「這是我們的目標，但談判往往需要更長的時間。只要雙方能達成互利的安排，應該就是雙贏的結果。」

  ## 園區概況

  菲律賓是美國主導之「Pax Silica」倡議的24個簽署國之一，該倡議致力於在晶片製造、人工智慧（AI）硬體及資料中心領域建立合作夥伴關係。在此架構下，BCDA正在塔拉克省新克拉克市開發一座占地1,619公頃的AI園區，專注於AI基礎設施的關鍵元件。

  Bingcang表示，該園區「純屬商業性質」，不涉及任何國防相關活動，也不會在計畫範圍內興建超大型資料中心——亦即業界規模最大的設施。

  ## 主權保障

  Bingcang引述美國國務院負責經濟事務的副國務卿Jacob Helberg的聲明指出，對於任何Pax Silica相關開發案，「主權將歸屬於地主國」。Bingcang說：「這是一份商業合約，通常對地主國較為有利。」

  上述聲明的背景是外界對相關報導的質疑。有法律評論人士將部分說法定性為浮上檯面的提案或謠言，指倡議構想中的「經濟安全區」可能享有類外交豁免待遇，或在菲律賓境內適用外國法律標準。UA&P法學院院長、菲律賓司法學院憲政哲學與法理學講師Jemy Gatdula在《BusinessWorld》發表的分析文章中論稱，此類安排將牴觸菲律賓憲法中有關主權、國民經濟與自然資源的相關條款。

  Gatdula引述「王室財產原則」（Regalian Doctrine）——明文規定於菲律賓憲法第十二條第二款——主張國家保有自然資源的所有權與最終控制權。他援引最高法院在「La Bugal-B'laan Tribal Association v. Ramos」案中的裁決，確立外國參與資源開採的前提是菲律賓政府保留最終控制、監督與管理權。他亦引述「Nicolas v. Romulo」案，重申行政部門雖得締結國際協定，但憲法限制仍具拘束力，在菲律賓境內運作的外國實體依法仍受菲律賓司法管轄。

  ## 電力、水源與投資優惠

  Bingcang表示，園區將自備獨立電力及公用事業供給系統，不會仰賴呂宋島電網。他說：「我們的目標是建立內嵌式的獨立電源。」在水源方面，BCDA正研議設置蓄水槽，收集雨水供工業用途使用。

  投資優惠將依循兩項現行法律：一是1992年《基地轉型暨發展法》（第7227號共和國法案），授權BCDA設立特別經濟區；二是《CREATE MORE法》（第12066號共和國法案，即《企業復甦與稅收獎勵措施以最大化振興經濟機會法》），對符合資格的企業提供租稅優惠。

  ## 預估規模

  Gatdula分析文章中所引用的政府預測顯示，此倡議可望在半導體製造、AI、雲端基礎設施、關鍵礦物加工及先進物流等領域吸引400億至700億美元（約新台幣1.3兆至2.3兆元）的投資。單是新克拉克市園區，預計就能創造逾13萬個就業機會；而與倡議相關的更廣泛「呂宋經濟走廊」——一個區域發展帶——最終可在製造、物流、研究、建設及支援產業等領域支撐多達100萬個職缺，Gatdula如此寫道。據報已有逾50家跨國企業表達參與意願。

  Gatdula認為，此倡議代表菲律賓數十年來難得一見的機遇，得以從低附加價值製造業躍升至半導體、AI及先進電子領域。他將此部分定性為對中國的回應——他描述中國慣於以貿易、投資、觀光及供應鏈作為施壓工具，對抗拒其地緣政治目標的國家，並指出此一模式已對菲律賓、澳洲、日本、南韓及台灣造成不同程度的影響。他亦指出，菲律賓是「區域全面經濟夥伴關係協定」（RCEP）的創始成員——該協定被稱為全球規模最大的自由貿易協定——並主張決策者必須在該框架的開放化承諾與對敏感科技領域的安全管制之間取得平衡。

  ## 反對聲浪

  該計畫已引發公眾反對，主要集中於預期中的能源與水源需求、潛在的環境破壞，以及可能造成當地社區居民被迫遷置等議題。Bingcang表示，BCDA將為受影響者提供財務援助及生計輔導計畫。

  ## 重要意義

  若11月目標得以實現，將正式確立菲律賓在Pax Silica陣營中的角色定位，並為已表達興趣的跨國企業投資決策提供法律依據。談判結果亦將決定兩國政府所援引的主權保障承諾，能否以具拘束力的契約文字落實——這正是菲律賓憲法學者與法律評論人士所點出的核心檢驗所在。

  Bingcang表示，BCDA將持續推動11月簽署目標，但同時坦承時程並非板上釘釘。
