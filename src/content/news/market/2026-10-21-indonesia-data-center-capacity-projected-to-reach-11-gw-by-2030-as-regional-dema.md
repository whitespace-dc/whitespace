body_en: |
  Indonesia's installed data center capacity reached 630-637 megawatts (MW) at mid-2026 and is on a trajectory to hit 11 gigawatts (GW) by 2030, driven by rising demand for artificial intelligence and cloud computing infrastructure, according to analysis published Tuesday in the Indonesian financial newspaper Kontan by Jusuf Irianto, a professor in the Department of Public Administration at Universitas Airlangga.

  To put the scale in perspective: one gigawatt is roughly the output of a large power plant, and the projected 2030 figure would represent more than seventeen times the current installed base.

  ## Regional competitive dynamics

  Irianto attributed Indonesia's emerging position as a data center destination in Southeast Asia in part to capacity restrictions in Singapore and energy supply constraints in Malaysia — the two countries that have historically dominated regional hyperscale development. Those limitations, he wrote, are redirecting investment flows toward Indonesia.

  The analysis cited corporate activity organized in coordination with Danantara — a state-linked investment entity — alongside other companies as a driver of continued capacity expansion toward hyperscale scale. Hyperscale data centers are facilities large enough to support major cloud and AI workloads, typically measured in hundreds of megawatts of IT load.

  ## Renewable energy investment context

  The article, headlined "Investasi EBT untuk Data Center" (Renewable Energy Investment for Data Centers), framed the capacity build-out within the context of new and renewable energy (EBT, from the Indonesian *Energi Baru Terbarukan*) investment requirements, though the detailed renewable energy arguments appeared in the paywalled portion of the piece.

  ## Significance

  If the 11 GW projection is realized, Indonesia would rank among the largest data center markets globally by installed capacity, a shift that would have material consequences for power infrastructure planning, land use, and water resources across the archipelago. The projection, as stated by Irianto, assumes continued AI adoption and cloud computing growth sustaining demand through the end of the decade.

  ## 印尼資料中心容量預計2030年達11 GW，區域需求版圖重塑

  印尼已安裝的資料中心容量於2026年中達到630至637百萬瓦（MW），並預計在2030年前達到11吉瓦（GW），成長動力來自人工智慧及雲端運算基礎設施的需求持續攀升。以上數據出自印尼財經報紙《Kontan》本週二刊出的一篇分析文章，作者為艾爾朗嘎大學（Universitas Airlangga）公共行政學系教授尤素夫．伊里安托（Jusuf Irianto）。

  為使數字更具體易懂：1吉瓦約相當於一座大型發電廠的輸出功率，而2030年的預測數字將是目前裝置容量的逾17倍。

  ## 區域競爭態勢

  伊里安托指出，印尼之所以逐漸成為東南亞資料中心的投資目的地，部分原因在於新加坡的容量限制以及馬來西亞的能源供應約束——這兩個國家歷來主導區域超大規模（hyperscale）資料中心的發展。他在文中寫道，上述限制正將投資資金導向印尼。

  分析文章中亦提及，與國家連結投資機構Danantara協調組織的企業活動，以及其他公司的積極參與，是推動容量持續朝超大規模擴張的重要驅動力。超大規模資料中心係指足以支撐大型雲端與人工智慧工作負載的設施，其IT負載通常以數百百萬瓦計。

  ## 再生能源投資背景

  該文標題為「Investasi EBT untuk Data Center」（資料中心的再生能源投資），將此波容量建設置於新能源與再生能源（EBT，源自印尼語Energi Baru Terbarukan）投資需求的脈絡下加以論述，惟詳細的再生能源論證刊登於付費牆之後，外界無從得見全貌。

  ## 重要意義

  若11吉瓦的預測得以實現，印尼以裝置容量計算將躋身全球最大資料中心市場之列，此一轉變將對這個群島國家的電力基礎設施規劃、土地利用及水資源帶來實質影響。伊里安托表示，上述預測以人工智慧持續普及、雲端運算成長得以持續推升需求直至本十年末為前提假設。
