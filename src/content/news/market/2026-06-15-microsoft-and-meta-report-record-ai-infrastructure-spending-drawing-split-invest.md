body_en: |
  Microsoft and Meta delivered earnings results this week that placed the AI data center buildout in concrete financial terms, with Microsoft reporting $37.5 billion in quarterly capital spending and Meta guiding 2026 capital expenditures of $115 billion to $135 billion. Investor reactions to the two companies split sharply along a single axis: whether visible returns already justify the cost.

  ## Microsoft's cloud revenue and the cost of capacity

  Microsoft's fiscal second-quarter 2026 total revenue reached $81.3 billion. Microsoft Cloud revenue rose to $51.5 billion, up 26% year-over-year in reported terms and 24% on a constant currency basis. Intelligent Cloud revenue, which includes Azure, hit $32.9 billion, up 29%. Azure and other cloud services revenue grew 39%.

  Capital spending totaled $37.5 billion in the quarter, up nearly 66% year-over-year, Reuters reported, with roughly two-thirds of that sum going toward computing chips. That allocation reflects the current phase of AI infrastructure deployment: GPU availability, memory pricing, and the pace at which systems can be racked, powered, and cooled are binding constraints alongside physical construction.

  Microsoft's commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO), a measure of contracted future revenue not yet recognized, rose to $625 billion, up 110%. Reuters noted that a large portion of that cloud backlog is tied to OpenAI, the AI developer in which Microsoft is a major investor, a detail Microsoft's own leadership acknowledged as central to its AI positioning.

  Investor sentiment was cautious. Financial Times coverage reported concern that cost growth is running ahead of revenue growth, even as Microsoft leadership described the capital build as deliberate investment into persistent AI adoption.

  ## Meta's 2026 capex guidance

  Meta reported fourth-quarter 2025 capital expenditures of $22.14 billion, including finance lease principal, and full-year 2025 capex of $72.22 billion. For 2026, the company guided capex of $115 billion to $135 billion. Chief Financial Officer Susan Li attributed the spending to "investments in data centers, servers, and network infrastructure" in support of what the company described as "Meta Superintelligence Labs efforts and core business."

  Meta described four strategic mechanisms it is using to scale infrastructure while preserving financial flexibility: changing how it develops data center sites, establishing strategic partnerships, contracting cloud capacity from third parties, and establishing new ownership structures for some of its large data center sites. The reference to new ownership structures signals that capital structure and risk-sharing arrangements are being deployed alongside conventional site development as the company seeks to manage balance sheet exposure at this spending level.

  Meta said near-term expense growth will be driven primarily by infrastructure costs, including third-party cloud spend, depreciation, and infrastructure operating expenses, as well as continued hiring of technical talent.

  Investors responded positively. Coverage emphasized that Meta's AI infrastructure spending is being interpreted as directly supporting near-term revenue performance, particularly in advertising.

  ## Divergent market verdicts on the same cycle

  The split in investor reactions reflects a standard applied consistently across 2026 earnings: capital expenditure at scale is acceptable when the return mechanism is already visible in the income statement, and less so when payoff is described in forward-looking terms. Meta paired its capex guidance with evidence of AI-driven performance gains in core business revenue. Microsoft's Azure backlog is substantial, but the connection to near-term recognized revenue is less direct, and Reuters' reporting on the OpenAI dependency underscored how much of Microsoft's contracted demand flows through a single external relationship.

  ## Supply constraints as the defining condition

  Both companies described an infrastructure build operating under the same physical limits: power availability, construction timelines, supply chain capacity, silicon allocation, and cooling. Microsoft's chip-heavy capital allocation, with roughly two-thirds of quarterly capex going toward computing hardware, illustrates the sequence the broader data center industry has absorbed: silicon procurement drives the build timeline, and everything downstream must keep pace.

  Meta's multi-path site strategy, combining owned capacity, partnerships, contracted cloud capacity, and potentially new ownership models for large sites, represents an explicit acknowledgment that flexibility in how capacity is structured and financed is as strategically important as securing megawatts and interconnect.

  ## Industry implications

  Meta's 2026 guidance of up to $135 billion in capital expenditures, if executed, would represent a single-company annual infrastructure commitment at a scale the data center industry has not previously encountered. For developers, equipment suppliers, electrical contractors, and co-location operators, the combined signal from both calls is that demand-side growth remains in aggressive expansion and that the operational challenge has shifted to supply-side discipline: how constrained capacity is allocated, financed, and brought online at speed.

  Meta said it expects 2026 expense growth to be driven by depreciation, third-party cloud costs, and infrastructure operating expenses as new capacity comes online.
body_zh: |
  微軟與Meta本週相繼公布財報，將AI資料中心的建設規模化為具體財務數字——微軟單季資本支出達375億美元，Meta則預告2026年資本支出將落在1,150億至1,350億美元之間。投資人對兩家公司的反應，卻沿著同一條軸線截然分歧：現有獲利是否已足以說明如此龐大的支出是值得的。

  ## 微軟雲端營收與建設成本

  微軟2026財年第二季總營收達813億美元。微軟雲端營收攀升至515億美元，以報告幣別計算年增26%，以固定匯率基礎計算則成長24%。涵蓋Azure在內的智慧雲端營收達329億美元，年增29%；Azure及其他雲端服務營收則成長39%。

  本季資本支出合計375億美元，年增近66%（據路透社報導），其中約三分之二用於採購運算晶片。這樣的資源配置，反映出AI基礎建設部署目前所處的階段：GPU供給、記憶體定價，以及機架架設、供電與散熱系統的建置速度，和實體建設工程一樣，都是當前的關鍵制約。

  微軟的商業剩餘履約義務（RPO）——即已簽約但尚未認列的未來營收指標——攀升至6,250億美元，年增110%。路透社指出，這批雲端積壓訂單有相當大比例來自OpenAI，而微軟正是這家AI開發商的主要投資方；微軟高階主管亦坦承，這一關係是公司AI布局的核心定位。

  投資人態度審慎。《金融時報》的報導指出，市場憂慮成本增速已超越營收增速，儘管微軟管理層將此波資本建設定性為因應AI持續普及而進行的長期投資。

  ## Meta的2026年資本支出展望

  Meta公布2025年第四季資本支出（含融資租賃本金）為221.4億美元，2025年全年資本支出則達722.2億美元。展望2026年，該公司預計資本支出將介於1,150億至1,350億美元之間。財務長Susan Li表示，這些支出用於「資料中心、伺服器與網路基礎建設的投資」，以支持公司所稱的「Meta超級智慧實驗室（Meta Superintelligence Labs）工作及核心業務」。

  Meta說明，該公司正透過四項策略機制，在擴大基礎建設規模的同時保留財務彈性：調整資料中心場址的開發方式、建立策略合作夥伴關係、向第三方雲端服務商租用容量，以及為部分大型資料中心場址建立新的所有權結構。關於「新所有權結構」的說法，意味著Meta正在將資本結構安排與風險共擔機制，與傳統場址開發並行推進，以便在如此龐大的支出規模下管控資產負債表的風險敞口。

  Meta表示，短期內費用增長的主要驅動力將是基礎建設成本，涵蓋第三方雲端支出、折舊費用與基礎建設營運費用，以及持續招募技術人才。

  投資人反應正面。相關報導強調，市場將Meta在AI基礎建設上的投入，解讀為直接支撐近期核心營收表現——尤其是廣告業務——的具體行動。

  ## 同一週期，市場評價大相徑庭

  投資人反應之所以分歧，反映出2026年整個財報季一以貫之的標準：大規模資本支出，唯有在損益表上已清楚呈現回報機制時，市場才會接受；若回報仍以前瞻性語言描述，往往引發疑慮。Meta在公布資本支出展望的同時，也提供了AI帶動核心業務營收提升的具體佐證。微軟的Azure積壓訂單雖然規模龐大，但與近期已認列營收之間的連結較不直接；路透社對OpenAI依賴度的報導，更凸顯微軟大量合約需求流經單一外部關係的現實。

  ## 供應瓶頸是核心制約條件

  兩家公司都描述了這場基礎建設競賽所面臨的相同實體限制：電力供給、建設時程、供應鏈產能、晶片配額以及散熱系統。微軟以晶片採購為大宗的資本配置——單季資本支出約三分之二用於運算硬體——清楚呈現出整個資料中心產業已普遍面對的順序邏輯：矽晶採購決定建設時程，所有下游環節都必須設法跟上。

  Meta採取多路並進的場址策略——結合自建產能、策略合作夥伴、外租雲端容量，以及可能針對大型場址引入新所有權模式——明確表明：容量的結構化方式與融資彈性，在策略重要性上絲毫不亞於確保電力供給與互連能力。

  ## 產業影響

  Meta預計2026年資本支出最高可達1,350億美元，若如期執行，將是資料中心產業迄今未曾遇見的單一企業年度基礎建設承諾規模。對開發商、設備供應商、電氣承包商與主機代管業者而言，這兩場法說會傳遞的綜合訊號是：需求端仍處於積極擴張的軌道，而營運層面的挑戰已轉向供應端的紀律管理——也就是如何在受限的產能條件下進行配置、融資，並以最快速度讓設施上線。

  Meta表示，隨著新產能陸續上線，預計2026年費用增長將主要由折舊、第三方雲端成本及基礎建設營運費用所驅動。
