body_en: |
  Meta announced July 8 that it will build a 1-gigawatt (GW) data center campus in Sturgeon County, Alberta — its first in Canada and its 33rd globally — representing a C$13 billion (approximately US$9.17 billion) investment the company said was shaped by energy agreements reached years before the project was publicly disclosed.

  The campus, designed for AI workloads, will be built with capacity to scale to 1.8 GW and will consume electricity equivalent to roughly 800,000 homes. At peak construction, the site is expected to employ approximately 3,000 workers; once operational, it will support more than 300 permanent jobs. Gary Demasi, Meta's vice president for data center development, made the announcement in Calgary alongside Alberta Premier Danielle Smith and provincial officials.

  ## Power locked in before announcement

  Meta said it worked with Greenlight Limited Partnership, AltaLink, Capital Power, and the Alberta Electric System Operator (AESO, the body that manages the provincial grid) "to plan for and meet our energy needs years in advance of this data center coming online."

  That sequence — energy secured ahead of public disclosure — reflects what analysts describe as a structural shift in how hyperscalers (large companies that own and operate massive data center campuses) compete for AI infrastructure sites. Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, said competitive advantage has moved from land acquisition to control of power supply. "For a 1 GW AI campus, land and GPUs are not enough," Osnato told Data Center Knowledge. "The advantage belongs to operators that can prove generation, transmission, interconnection, regulatory approvals, and community durability years before the project comes online."

  Balaji Tammabattula, CEO of BaRupOn, said the timeline for those negotiations has lengthened substantially. "Grid interconnection and transmission upgrades are measured in years, and in some jurisdictions the queue itself is the bottleneck before a single permit is filed," Tammabattula said. "We've seen that engagement move from what used to be a six- to 12-month conversation right before construction, to something that now starts three to five years out, sometimes earlier for gigawatt-scale projects."

  ## Pembina and Capital Power supply the site

  Meta has signed a long-term tolling agreement with Pembina Pipeline for the Greenlight Electricity Centre, a new natural gas-fired power generation facility in Sturgeon County. Pembina announced last week it will proceed with the project, which is expected to be in service in late 2030. According to Pembina, the Greenlight facility will require approximately 150 million cubic feet per day of natural gas.

  Until Greenlight comes online, and for the following decade, Capital Power will supply 250 megawatts (MW) of electricity — enough to power roughly 200,000 average homes — from its existing natural gas-fired generation fleet.

  ## Ratepayer costs and clean energy matching

  Meta addressed directly the question of whether large data center loads shift infrastructure costs onto other utility customers, a widely debated topic as AI campuses scale toward gigawatt levels. The company said it pays "the full costs of our data centers' energy use so other consumers aren't negatively impacted" and is "fully funding new generation and grid infrastructure in Alberta to support our data center," stating those investments will improve reliability across the Alberta grid.

  Meta also said it will match 100% of the campus's annual electricity use with clean and renewable energy investments, consistent with its approach at other facilities.

  ## Alberta's grid and environmental opposition

  Alberta's electricity grid is 60% powered by natural gas. The province's emissions intensity is almost five times the national average, according to Reuters. Canada's federal government published an AI strategy last month that framed new data center investment as benefiting from the country's national electricity grid, which is largely powered by renewables and low-emission sources — a characterization that does not apply to Alberta specifically.

  Environmental groups were swift to condemn the announcement. Keith Stewart of Greenpeace Canada called for broader restrictions on such projects. "We need a moratorium on mega-data centers until we have legislated environmental and human rights protections on AI," Stewart said.

  Alberta's provincial government has given new large-load proponents the option to build their own power sources to avoid constraints on grid capacity, a policy that supports the structure of Meta's agreement with Pembina.

  ## Water and cooling

  The campus will use a closed-loop liquid cooling system with dry cooling, eliminating operational water use from the cooling system under normal conditions. Meta said water use at the site will be limited to construction, building operations, fire protection, and equipment maintenance. Demasi said the facility's total water consumption will be less than that of a typical golf course. Meta has pledged to achieve water-positive status globally by 2030, meaning it will restore more water than it consumes across its owned operations.

  ## Infrastructure investment and community commitments

  Meta plans to invest approximately $42.3 million in local road and water infrastructure, according to Data Center Knowledge. Capacity Media reported a CAD$60 million commitment to broader local infrastructure improvements. The company also announced it will launch its Data Center Community Action Grants program in the region to provide direct funding to local nonprofits.

  ## What comes next for Alberta

  Alberta Technology Minister Nate Glubish said at the announcement that several other gigawatt-scale data center proposals are currently in various stages of development in the province. "This is the first of its kind, the first of its size, the first of its scale, but it won't be the last," Glubish said.

  The province's competitive electricity market, availability of natural gas at a significant discount to the US benchmark, and cold climate — which lowers cooling costs — have drawn hyperscalers seeking sites outside more capacity-constrained US markets.

  The Meta announcement illustrates how the largest AI infrastructure projects are now structured: while the public announcement is the first visible milestone, the underlying work to secure generation, transmission, and regulatory alignment is completed years earlier. Tammabattula said that sequencing will increasingly determine which developers can execute at scale. "The companies willing to build or secure their own generation, and do the unglamorous work of transmission planning and regulatory coordination years ahead of need, are the ones who will be able to scale when everyone else is stuck waiting for interconnection," he said.

  Meta said the Pembina Greenlight Electricity Centre is expected to be in service in late 2030, establishing the earliest point at which the campus's primary dedicated generation supply will come online.
body_zh: |
  Meta於7月8日宣布，將在加拿大亞伯達省（Alberta）斯特金縣（Sturgeon County）興建一座1吉瓦（GW）資料中心園區——這是Meta在加拿大的第一座、全球第33座資料中心，總投資額達130億加幣（約91.7億美元）。Meta表示，整個計畫的架構奠基於公司在項目公開披露之前數年便已談妥的能源協議。

  這座園區專為AI工作負載設計，建成後可擴展至1.8 GW容量，耗電量相當於約80萬戶家庭。施工高峰期間，預計將僱用約3,000名工人；正式營運後，將支持逾300個永久職位。Meta資料中心開發部門副總裁蓋瑞・德馬西（Gary Demasi）偕同亞伯達省長丹妮葉・史密斯（Danielle Smith）及省級官員，在卡加利（Calgary）公開宣布此計畫。

  ## 宣布前已確保電力供應

  Meta表示，公司已與Greenlight有限合夥公司（Greenlight Limited Partnership）、AltaLink、Capital Power及亞伯達電力系統調度員（AESO，即負責管理省級電網的機構）合作，「提前數年規劃並滿足我們的能源需求，早於資料中心上線之前」。

  這種「先確保能源、再公開項目」的模式，反映了分析師所描述的一種結構性轉變——超大規模業者（即擁有並運營大型資料中心園區的企業）在競逐AI基礎設施選址時的新邏輯。Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾・奧斯納托（Neil Osnato）指出，競爭優勢已從土地取得，轉移至電力供應的掌控。「對於一座1 GW的AI園區而言，土地和GPU還不夠，」奧斯納托告訴《資料中心知識》（Data Center Knowledge）。「優勢屬於能在項目上線之前數年，便能證明自己握有發電、輸電、併網、法規批准及社區永續支持能力的業者。」

  BaRupOn執行長巴拉吉・塔馬巴圖拉（Balaji Tammabattula）表示，這類談判的時程已大幅拉長。「電網併網與輸電升級的周期以年計，在某些地區，光是排隊等候本身就是瓶頸，連第一張許可證都還沒申請，」他說。「我們已看到，這類接洽從過去施工前六到十二個月啟動，演變為如今提前三至五年，吉瓦規模的項目有時甚至更早開始。」

  ## Pembina與Capital Power供應電力

  Meta已與Pembina管道公司（Pembina Pipeline）就Greenlight電力中心（Greenlight Electricity Centre）簽訂長期電力服務協議（tolling agreement）——這是一座位於斯特金縣、全新興建的天然氣發電廠。Pembina上週宣布將推進該項目，預計於2030年底投入服務。根據Pembina公告，Greenlight電力中心每日約需1.5億立方英尺天然氣。

  在Greenlight電力中心上線之前及其後十年，Capital Power將從其現有天然氣發電機組中供應250百萬瓦（MW）電力——足以供應約20萬戶普通家庭。

  ## 電費負擔與潔淨能源配對

  外界普遍關注，大型資料中心是否會將基礎設施成本轉嫁至其他用電戶——這一議題隨著AI園區規模逼近吉瓦級而備受討論。Meta對此直接表態：公司支付「資料中心能源使用的全部費用，不讓其他消費者受到負面衝擊」，並表示「在亞伯達省全額出資興建新的發電與電網基礎設施以支持資料中心」，且這些投資將提升亞伯達電網整體的供電可靠度。

  Meta亦表示，將以潔淨能源與再生能源投資，100%抵銷園區全年電力用量，做法與其在其他設施的一貫方式相同。

  ## 亞伯達電網與環保團體反對聲浪

  亞伯達的電力供應有60%來自天然氣。據路透社報導，該省的碳排放強度幾乎是全國平均水準的五倍。加拿大聯邦政府上個月發布AI策略，將新資料中心投資定位為受益於全國電網——該電網主要依賴再生能源及低排放來源供電——但這一描述並不適用於亞伯達省的具體情況。

  環保團體對此次宣布迅速提出批評。綠色和平加拿大（Greenpeace Canada）的基思・史都華（Keith Stewart）呼籲對此類項目實施更廣泛的限制。「我們需要對超大型資料中心暫停核准，直到我們有以立法形式保障的AI環境與人權保護措施，」史都華說。

  亞伯達省政府已給予新增大型用電申請者自建電源的選項，以避開電網容量限制——這項政策為Meta與Pembina協議的架構提供了支撐。

  ## 用水與冷卻系統

  園區將採用閉迴路液冷系統搭配乾式冷卻，在正常運作條件下，冷卻系統不消耗運營用水。Meta表示，園區的用水僅限於施工、建築物運營、消防保護及設備維護。德馬西表示，設施的總用水量將低於一座典型高爾夫球場。Meta已承諾，在2030年前於全球實現「水源正值」（water positive）目標，意即在其自營業務中所補充的水量將超過消耗量。

  ## 基礎設施投資與社區承諾

  據《資料中心知識》報導，Meta計劃投資約4,230萬美元於當地道路與水利基礎設施。Capacity Media則報導，Meta對更廣泛的地方基礎設施改善承諾投入6,000萬加幣。此外，Meta宣布將在該地區啟動「資料中心社區行動補助計畫」（Data Center Community Action Grants），直接提供資金支持當地非營利組織。

  ## 亞伯達的下一步

  亞伯達省科技部長內特・格魯比什（Nate Glubish）在發布會上表示，目前有數個吉瓦級資料中心提案正在省內不同開發階段推進。「這是第一座同類型的、第一座這種規模的、第一座這個量級的，但絕不會是最後一座，」格魯比什說。

  亞伯達省的競爭性電力市場、較美國基準價格大幅折讓的天然氣供應，以及有助降低冷卻成本的寒冷氣候，已吸引超大規模業者在美國容量日益緊縮的市場之外另覓選址。

  Meta此次宣布揭示了當今最大規模AI基礎設施項目的運作模式：公開宣布不過是第一個對外可見的里程碑，而確保發電、輸電及法規協調的底層工作，早在數年前便已完成。塔馬巴圖拉表示，這種時序安排將愈來愈決定哪些開發商能夠規模化執行。「願意自建或確保自有發電能力、並在需求到來數年前就做好輸電規劃與法規協調這類枯燥工作的企業，才是在其他人苦等併網之際仍能持續擴張的贏家，」他說。

  Meta表示，Pembina Greenlight電力中心預計於2030年底投入服務，屆時將是園區主要專屬發電供應正式上線的最早時間點。
