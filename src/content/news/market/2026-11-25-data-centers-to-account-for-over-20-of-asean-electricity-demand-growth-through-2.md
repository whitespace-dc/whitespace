body_en: |
  Data centers will be responsible for more than one in five units of new electricity demand across Southeast Asia between 2025 and 2030, according to the 9th ASEAN Energy Outlook, a regional energy planning report that flags concentrated infrastructure buildout as an emerging pressure point for power grids.

  Under the report's most ambitious pathway — the ASEAN Coordinated Transition Scenario, or ACTS, which models a regionally coordinated shift toward efficiency, electrification, renewables, and cross-border cooperation — total ASEAN electricity demand is projected to grow by 28 million tons of oil equivalent (Mtoe) over the five-year period. Data centers account for 20.7% of that increase, the report states.

  ## Grid strain, not generation shortfall

  The report draws a distinction between regional generation capacity and local grid adequacy. While overall power generation capacity across the region is projected to remain sufficient, the rapid and geographically concentrated nature of data center load growth creates a different class of problem: stress on transmission and distribution networks at specific nodes.

  "Growth in data center load is rapid over the next five years and remains concentrated in certain locations," the report states, warning that this concentration could pressure both grid capacity and the reliability of power supply at those sites. The finding implies that expanding generation alone will not be enough to accommodate data center demand — network infrastructure at specific locations will also require attention.

  ## Contribution declines after 2030 as other sectors accelerate

  The data center share of incremental electricity demand is projected to fall sharply in the following decade. Between 2030 and 2040, the report estimates data centers will contribute 8.1% of ASEAN's electricity demand increase, as industrial electrification and electric transport scale up substantially.

  "Industrial and transport electrification grows much faster from the 2030s," the report notes, explaining the relative decline in data centers' contribution to demand growth. The figure reflects not a slowdown in data center consumption in absolute terms, but rather the faster acceleration of other sectors.

  By 2060, data centers' share of total ASEAN electricity consumption varies significantly by scenario. Under the Baseline Scenario — which assumes current policies and limited additional transition effort — data centers account for 15.2% of total regional electricity demand. Under the AMS Target Scenario, which reflects member states' existing national energy targets, the share reaches 17.5%. Under ACTS, with its heavier emphasis on efficiency and electrification of other sectors, that share falls to 11.4%.

  ## Flexibility as a mitigation tool

  The report identifies demand-side flexibility in data center operations as a potential tool for managing the grid impact of large-scale compute infrastructure. Because a portion of computing workloads are not time-sensitive, operators can in principle schedule those tasks to periods when renewable energy generation is higher, reducing pressure on the grid during peak or low-renewable periods.

  "Some non-urgent computing workloads can be scheduled when renewable energy availability is higher," the report states. It also points to cooling system optimization, compute load management, and on-site battery storage as mechanisms that could help align data center consumption with the variable output profile of renewable generation, while also easing stress on the broader electricity system.

  The projections underscore that electricity demand growth across ASEAN is no longer driven solely by population growth, economic expansion, and household consumption. Digital services, cloud computing, and technology infrastructure — including the facilities that underpin artificial intelligence workloads — now constitute a material and fast-growing component of the region's energy equation, one the report treats as structurally distinct from the industrial and transport electrification wave expected to dominate demand growth in the 2030s.
body_zh: |
  根據第九版《東協能源展望》，2025至2030年間，東南亞每新增五度電力需求，就有超過一度來自資料中心。這份區域能源規劃報告點名高度集中的基礎設施建設，是對供電網路形成新興壓力的潛在風險點。

  在報告最積極的路徑——「東協協調轉型情境」（ASEAN Coordinated Transition Scenario，ACTS）下，模型假設東協各國在效率提升、電氣化、再生能源及跨境合作方面協調推進，五年期間東協電力需求總計將增加2,800萬噸油當量（Mtoe）。報告指出，其中20.7%的增量來自資料中心。

  ## 電網承壓，而非發電量不足

  報告特別區分了區域發電容量與地方電網充裕程度的差異。儘管整個東協的整體發電容量預計仍然充足，但資料中心負載成長迅速且地理位置高度集中，衍生出截然不同的問題：特定節點的輸配電網路將承受龐大壓力。

  報告指出：「未來五年資料中心負載成長迅速，且集中於特定地點。」並警告，這種集中現象可能對電網容量及相關地點的供電可靠度形成壓力。此一發現意味著，單靠擴充發電量，並不足以因應資料中心的用電需求——特定地點的電網基礎設施同樣需要配套投資。

  ## 2030年後貢獻比重隨其他部門提速而下滑

  資料中心在電力需求增量中所占的比重，預計在接下來的十年將大幅下降。2030至2040年間，報告估計資料中心將貢獻東協電力需求增量的8.1%，主因是工業電氣化與電動運輸在此階段將大幅加速成長。

  報告說明：「工業與交通電氣化自2030年代起成長速度大幅加快。」這解釋了資料中心在需求增量中相對比重下滑的原因。這個數字反映的並非資料中心用電量的絕對衰退，而是其他部門加速成長所帶來的相對稀釋效應。

  到2060年，資料中心在東協電力總消費中所占的比重，因情境不同而有顯著差異。在「基準情境」（Baseline Scenario）下——假設維持現行政策、能源轉型力度有限——資料中心占東協電力總需求的15.2%。在「東協成員國目標情境」（AMS Target Scenario）下，反映各成員國現行國家能源目標，比重升至17.5%。而在更強調效率提升與其他部門電氣化的ACTS情境下，這一比重則降至11.4%。

  ## 需量彈性作為緩解工具

  報告點出資料中心營運的需量側彈性，是管理大規模運算基礎設施對電網衝擊的潛在工具。由於部分運算工作負載並不具時效性，業者原則上可將這些任務排程至再生能源發電量較高的時段，藉此降低電網在尖峰或低再生能源時段所承受的壓力。

  報告表示：「部分非緊急運算工作負載，可排程於再生能源供應較充裕的時段執行。」報告同時指出，冷卻系統最佳化、運算負載管理及場址內電池儲能，皆是有助於使資料中心用電模式配合再生能源發電變動特性的機制，並可同時舒緩整體電力系統的壓力。

  上述預測凸顯，東協電力需求成長的驅動力，已不再僅限於人口增長、經濟擴張與家庭消費。數位服務、雲端運算及科技基礎設施——包括支撐人工智慧工作負載的各類設施——如今已成為該地區能源版圖中不可忽視且快速成長的組成部分。報告將其視為結構上獨立的類別，有別於預計在2030年代主導需求成長的工業與交通電氣化浪潮。
