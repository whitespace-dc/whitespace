body_en: |
  Data center developers expanding to meet surging AI demand are encountering a new constraint: community and regulatory resistance rooted in concerns over land use, biodiversity loss, and local impact, according to an industry analysis published by Data Center Knowledge.

  The piece argues that land, long treated as a transactional input in data center development, is becoming the focal point of sustainability debates and a determinant of whether projects receive approval at all.

  ## Communities as gatekeepers

  Local authorities, responding to pressure from residents, are demanding more than regulatory compliance, the analysis states. They want, it says, "clear, demonstrable evidence that developers will act as responsible custodians of the land, before, during, and after construction." Environmental impact assessments, once standard currency for project approval, are described as insufficient on their own.

  Communities themselves are described as "powerful gatekeepers" that may ultimately decide where data centers can or cannot be built. Developers that fail to engage local stakeholders, the analysis warns, risk delays, opposition, or outright project rejection, as well as erosion of the "social license" needed to scale.

  ## The reputational gap

  The analysis identifies a perception problem working against the industry. Unlike steelworks or automotive plants, which are typically associated with visible job creation and regional economic identity, data centers are often characterized as low-employment, high-energy facilities that deliver limited local benefit. The piece contends this characterization is inaccurate, pointing to data centers' roles in cloud services, AI, financial systems, healthcare, logistics, and public services, as well as their contributions to renewable energy integration and technical employment.

  ## ESG pressure from corporate customers

  The scrutiny is not confined to planning authorities. Corporate customers with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) commitments are also paying closer attention to how and where their digital infrastructure is built, the analysis states. Operators able to demonstrate responsible development practices and community engagement are described as better positioned to win and retain enterprise partnerships.

  ## A new development model takes shape

  The analysis describes an emerging approach in which land use considerations are embedded from the earliest stages of site selection. Under this model, ecological sensitivity, restoration potential, and community impact sit alongside conventional criteria such as power availability and connectivity.

  Brownfield sites, meaning previously developed or degraded land, are cited as one avenue for turning environmental liabilities into assets. Beyond site selection, the analysis points to biodiversity initiatives including rewilding, native habitat creation, and green corridors as measures that signal long-term environmental commitment rather than cosmetic mitigation.

  Creating tangible community value, through local employment, skills development, or shared infrastructure, is described as shifting the narrative around data centers from imposition to regional contributor.

  ## Why land scarcity raises the stakes

  The analysis frames responsible land stewardship not only as an ethical requirement but as a competitive differentiator. As AI infrastructure demand intensifies, suitable land will become scarcer and more valuable, it argues. Developers with a track record of responsible operations will be better placed to secure future approvals and partnerships.

  The alternative, the piece states, is a widening gap between the industry and the communities it operates within. If data centers come to be perceived as extractive or disruptive, the analysis warns, opposition could create bottlenecks in deploying critical digital infrastructure, with downstream consequences for innovation and economic growth.

  The analysis concludes that operators' license to expand can no longer be assumed and must be earned on a site-by-site, community-by-community basis.
body_zh: |
  根據《Data Center Knowledge》發布的一份產業分析報告，為滿足AI算力需求持續攀升而積極對外擴張的資料中心開發商，正面臨一股新的阻力：來自社區與主管機關的抵制，根源在於各界對土地使用、生物多樣性喪失及在地衝擊的憂慮。

  該分析指出，土地過去長期被視為資料中心開發過程中可交易的生產要素，如今已成為永續發展辯論的核心焦點，更是決定專案能否取得審批的關鍵變數。

  ## 社區化身守門人

  分析報告指出，地方主管機關在居民壓力下，已不再滿足於單純的法規遵循，而是要求業者提出「明確、可驗證的證據，證明開發商在施工前、施工中及施工後，都將善盡土地管理責任」。過去作為專案審批標準文件的環境影響評估，如今單靠這份文件已不足以取得核准。

  報告中將社區本身形容為「強大的守門人」，最終可能左右資料中心能否落腳特定地點。報告警告，未能主動與在地利害關係人溝通的開發商，恐面臨延宕、反對，乃至專案遭全面否決的風險，同時也可能侵蝕推動規模擴張所需的「社會許可」。

  ## 聲譽落差

  報告點出一項對產業不利的認知問題。不同於鋼鐵廠或汽車製造廠通常與顯著的就業創造及區域經濟認同相連結，資料中心往往被貼上「用電量高、在地就業少、對當地貢獻有限」的標籤。報告認為這種刻板印象並不準確，並以資料中心在雲端服務、AI、金融系統、醫療、物流及公共服務中扮演的角色為例加以佐證，同時指出業者在促進再生能源整合及創造技術人才就業方面的貢獻。

  ## 企業客戶的ESG壓力

  外界的審視並不僅限於規劃主管機關。分析報告指出，肩負環境、社會與公司治理（ESG）承諾的企業客戶，也愈來愈關注其數位基礎設施的建置方式與地點選擇。能夠展現負責任開發實踐與社區參與的業者，在爭取和鞏固企業合作關係方面將更具競爭優勢。

  ## 全新開發模式成形

  分析報告描述了一種正在成形的新型開發方法——將土地使用考量納入選址評估的最早期階段。在這套模式下，生態敏感性、生態復育潛力及社區衝擊，與電力供應、網路連線等傳統評估標準並列為同等重要的選址依據。

  報告以棕地（即先前已開發或遭受破壞的土地）為例，指出這類土地是將環境負擔轉化為資產的途徑之一。在選址之外，報告還提及多項生物多樣性措施，包括野化還林、原生棲地營造及綠色廊道建置，認為這些舉措所傳遞的是長期環境承諾，而非流於表面的補救措施。

  透過在地就業、技能培育或共享基礎設施等方式為社區創造實質效益，被報告形容為可扭轉外界對資料中心「強加於地方」的負面觀感，進而建立「區域貢獻者」的正面形象。

  ## 土地稀缺性提高賭注

  報告將負責任的土地管理定位為不僅是道德責任，更是差異化的競爭優勢。報告主張，隨著AI基礎設施需求持續升溫，適合建設的土地將愈來愈稀缺、愈來愈珍貴。具備負責任營運記錄的開發商，將在未來的審批與合作關係爭取上占得先機。

  報告警告，另一條路是讓產業與所在社區之間的裂痕持續擴大。一旦資料中心被視為具掠奪性或破壞性的存在，外界反對聲浪可能在關鍵數位基礎設施的部署上造成瓶頸，進而對創新發展與經濟成長帶來連鎖負面影響。

  分析報告的結論是：業者擴張的許可已不再是理所當然，而必須在每一塊用地、每一個社區逐一爭取。
