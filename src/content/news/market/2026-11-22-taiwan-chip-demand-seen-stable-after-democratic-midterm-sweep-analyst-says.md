  ## Democratic sweep sparks Taiwan market selloff

  A Democratic sweep of both chambers of the US Congress has triggered a sharp selloff in Taiwan technology stocks and currency, but demand for Taiwan's AI chips and servers has not materially changed, according to an analysis published November 18 by Lian Xian-ming, president of the Chung-Hua Institution for Economic Research, in the Commercial Times.

  The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, a benchmark for chip stocks, fell more than 12 percent in a single session after the midterm results were announced. Executive-level changes at Nvidia added further uncertainty. Taiwan's stock market dropped 4,172 index points between November 2 and November 16, putting it roughly 20 percent below its 2026 high. Foreign investors sold a net approximately 1 trillion New Taiwan dollars (NTD) in October, followed by a further net NTD 600 billion in the first ten trading days of November. The New Taiwan dollar weakened to approach 33 to the US dollar.

  ## State governors, not Congress, are the real data center risk

  Lian argues that the US Congress has limited authority to directly restrict data center construction, and that any legislation Democrats might pass would still be subject to a presidential veto from Donald Trump. The more consequential shift, he writes, is at the state level.

  Democrats flipped gubernatorial seats in Texas, Ohio, and Georgia — all three among the heaviest data center states in the US. Each of the incoming governors campaigned on pausing or tightening regulation of new data center projects, specifically targeting power consumption, water use, land allocation, and tax incentives that have been granted to operators. Lian identifies this as the primary variable affecting data center site selection, not federal legislation.

  ## Three reasons demand for Taiwan chips is unlikely to collapse

  Lian offers three reasons why he considers a sharp fall in Taiwan chip demand unlikely despite the state-level shift.

  First, the near-term construction pipeline is already deep. The US currently has approximately 80 gigawatts (GW) — a measure of total electrical power capacity — of approved and under-construction data center capacity. Large campuses scheduled for delivery in 2027 are more than halfway complete, and the pipeline is backed by nearly one trillion US dollars in long-term lease contracts, limiting the risk of abrupt cancellation.

  Second, the incoming governors are seeking to regulate terms of construction — specifically to prevent rising electricity and water bills for local residents and to require local job creation — rather than imposing outright prohibitions, Lian writes.

  Third, global demand outside the United States is growing from a low base. The US currently accounts for approximately 75 percent of worldwide AI data center capacity, compared with roughly 45 percent of non-AI data center capacity. Many countries are at an early stage of AI data center buildout, and their demand is sufficient to sustain growth in AI chip and server volumes even if the US pace slows.

  ## Consolidation likely to benefit larger operators

  Lian identifies three structural consequences of the political shift for the data center industry.

  Smaller operators face the greatest exposure. High interest rates already make financing difficult for smaller players; reduced tax incentives and tighter power allocation would further disadvantage them relative to large hyperscale operators. Market share is likely to consolidate toward larger companies.

  Existing data centers that have already secured power connections will become scarcer and more valuable as the threshold for new construction rises.

  The consolidation may also improve supply chain visibility. Lian notes that in past cycles, multiple operators have submitted overlapping construction applications — inflating chipmaker demand estimates — without resolving power or funding constraints. Removing weaker entrants would yield more accurate capacity planning signals for the semiconductor supply chain.

  ## Analyst and institutional outlook

  Research firm Gartner has forecast continued expansion in AI computing demand, while IDC has assessed that the political changes will have limited impact on AI infrastructure demand over coming quarters, according to Lian. UBS and Morgan Stanley consider AI infrastructure capital expenditure cycles to remain healthy, he writes.

  Lian points to TSMC's continued capacity expansion as the most direct indicator of end-market chip demand. Because TSMC has visibility into actual customer order flows, its investment pace constitutes what he describes as the most reliable available demand signal.

  ## Significance for Taiwan's AI supply chain

  Taiwan's role in the AI supply chain, Lian writes, is analogous to a supplier of mining tools: regardless of which operators ultimately build and run data centers, they require Taiwan-made chips and servers. The market disruption changes the rules under which data centers are built, not the aggregate volume of AI compute that buyers intend to deploy.

  The near-term market turbulence reflects investor uncertainty about construction timelines rather than a deterioration in underlying chip demand fundamentals, Lian concludes. The question for future competitiveness, he writes, is which suppliers can deliver most efficiently under the new regulatory constraints at the US state level.

  ## 民主黨橫掃國會，台灣科技股遭受重挫

  美國國會期中選舉由民主黨全面拿下參、眾兩院，引發台灣科技股與新台幣匯率劇烈震盪，但台灣AI晶片與伺服器的需求並未出現實質變化。這是中華經濟研究院院長連賢明於11月18日在《工商時報》發表的分析所得出的結論。

  費城半導體指數在期中選舉結果公布後單日暴跌逾12%，輝達高層異動更進一步加深市場不確定性。台灣股市從11月2日至11月16日間累計下跌4,172點，相較2026年高點已回落約20%。外資10月份淨賣超約新台幣1兆元，11月前十個交易日又再淨賣超約新台幣6,000億元。新台幣匯率一度趨近33元兌1美元的關卡。

  ## 州長換人，才是資料中心的真正風險

  連賢明認為，美國國會直接限制資料中心興建的權限有限，民主黨即便通過相關立法，仍須面對總統唐納·川普的否決權。他指出，更具關鍵影響力的變化發生在州層級。

  民主黨在德州、俄亥俄州與喬治亞州翻轉了州長席位——這三州均是全美資料中心最密集的州份之一。新任州長均以暫緩或收緊新資料中心建設法規為競選主軸，具體鎖定電力消耗、用水量、土地分配，以及過去給予業者的租稅優惠。連賢明認為，影響資料中心選址的主要變數正是在此，而非聯邦立法。

  ## 台灣晶片需求難以崩跌的三大理由

  儘管州層級出現政策轉向，連賢明提出三項理由，說明他為何認為台灣晶片需求大幅下滑的可能性不高。

  首先，近期建設管線已相當深厚。美國目前已獲核准或正在興建的資料中心容量約達80吉瓦（GW，即總電力容量的衡量單位），預計2027年完工的大型園區已有超過一半完成進度，整個管線背後更有近1兆美元的長期租賃合約為後盾，大幅降低倉促取消的風險。

  其次，新任州長的訴求是規範建設條件——具體而言，是為了防止在地居民電費與水費上漲、並要求創造在地就業機會——而非全面禁止新建案，連賢明如此寫道。

  第三，美國以外的全球需求正從低基期持續成長。美國目前佔全球AI資料中心容量約75%，而在非AI資料中心容量中則約佔45%。許多國家的AI資料中心建設仍處於早期階段，這些國家的需求即便美國腳步放緩，也足以支撐AI晶片與伺服器出貨量的持續成長。

  ## 整合趨勢可望讓大型業者受益

  連賢明分析了此次政治轉向對資料中心產業帶來的三項結構性影響。

  中小型業者所面臨的衝擊最大。高利率環境本已使中小型業者的融資難度偏高；租稅優惠縮減與電力配置趨嚴，將進一步拉大其與大型超大規模（hyperscale）業者之間的競爭劣勢。市占率可望向大型業者集中。

  已取得電力連接的現有資料中心，將因新建門檻提高而變得更加稀缺、更具價值。

  此次整合也可能提升供應鏈能見度。連賢明指出，在過去的週期中，多家業者曾同時提交重疊的建設申請——在電力或資金問題尚未解決的情況下，人為拉高了晶片製造商的需求預估。淘汰體質較弱的業者，將為半導體供應鏈帶來更準確的產能規劃訊號。

  ## 分析師與機構展望

  據連賢明的引述，研究機構顧能（Gartner）預測AI運算需求將持續擴張，IDC則評估此次政治變化對未來幾季AI基礎設施需求的衝擊有限。瑞銀（UBS）與摩根士丹利（Morgan Stanley）均認為AI基礎設施資本支出週期依然健康，他如此寫道。

  連賢明以台積電持續擴大產能，作為終端市場晶片需求最直接的指標。由於台積電對客戶實際訂單流量具有高度能見度，其投資步調正是他所描述的「目前最可靠的需求訊號」。

  ## 對台灣AI供應鏈的意義

  台灣在AI供應鏈中的角色，連賢明寫道，就如同採礦工具的供應商：無論最終是哪些業者興建並營運資料中心，都需要台灣製造的晶片與伺服器。此次市場動盪改變的是資料中心的建設規則，而非買家預計部署的AI運算總量。

  連賢明最後結論指出，近期的市場震盪反映的是投資人對建設時程的不確定性，而非晶片需求基本面的惡化。他寫道，未來競爭力的關鍵問題，在於哪些供應商能在美國州層級新法規的約束下，最有效率地交付產品。
