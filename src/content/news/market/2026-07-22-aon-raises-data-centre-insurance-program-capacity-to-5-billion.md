body_en: |
  Aon has raised the capacity of its Data Centre Lifecycle Insurance Program (DCLP) to USD $5 billion, the broker announced, expanding available cover for digital infrastructure projects across construction, operations, cyber, cargo, and terrorism risks.

  The increase marks a step up from a prior enhancement that had lifted the program's ceiling to USD $3.5 billion. Aon said the latest revision reflects the growing scale and complexity of individual data centre projects driven by demand for artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and hyperscale facilities.

  ## What the program covers

  Under the updated structure, clients can access:

  - Up to **USD $5 billion** in Construction All Risks, Delay in Start-Up, Property Damage, and Business Interruption cover
  - Up to **USD $1 billion** in terrorism cover through existing Aon facilities
  - Up to **USD $500 million** in project cargo cover
  - Up to **USD $400 million** in Cyber and Technology Errors and Omissions cover
  - Up to **USD $200 million** in third-party liability cover outside the United States
  - Up to **USD $100 million** in third-party liability cover within the United States

  The program is backed by a panel of A-rated insurers from Lloyd's of London and company markets, alongside other insurance facilities. It also draws on services from Aon Global Risk Consulting, which provides climate risk advisory, environmental risk solutions, security risk consulting, risk engineering, and operational resilience support.

  ## A lifecycle approach to coverage

  The DCLP is structured to bring insurance capacity, engineering input, and risk analysis into data centre projects earlier in the development process. Aon said the approach is intended to address transition risks between the construction phase and ongoing operations, and to improve the insurability of assets over time.

  The program combines cover for assets from initial development through long-term operation within a single framework, rather than requiring separate single-line placements for each risk category. Owners, developers, lenders, and operators can use the program to address construction delays, physical damage, cyber incidents, liability exposures, and resilience planning together.

  Aon said this structure matters because a data centre failure can generate losses across multiple categories simultaneously, including equipment damage, delayed opening, business interruption, and contractual claims.

  ## Market context

  Data centres have attracted significant institutional and private capital as demand for computing and storage capacity rises, according to Aon. That growth has increased pressure on project sponsors and operators to secure insurance that covers both construction risk and ongoing operational exposure across large portfolios.

  Joe Peiser, Chief Executive Officer of Risk Capital at Aon, attributed the expansion directly to shifts in the underlying market. "Digital infrastructure has become one of the most important and capital-intensive asset classes in the global economy," Peiser said. "As clients build larger and more complex data center portfolios, they need access to greater insurance capacity alongside solutions that strengthen resilience throughout the asset lifecycle. Expanding DCLP to $5 billion demonstrates our ability to help clients access capital, manage risk and scale with confidence."

  ## Industry significance

  The DCLP expansion reflects a broader trend in which insurance products for digital infrastructure are becoming more specialised and moving toward multi-line packages rather than standalone placements. As data centre assets grow in value and become more central to cloud and AI workloads, the financial consequences of disruption rise correspondingly, placing greater demands on available insurance capacity.

  Aon operates in more than 120 countries. The updated program's USD $5 billion ceiling for core property and construction risks, combined with attached limits for terrorism and cyber exposures, represents the highest capacity threshold the DCLP has reached since its inception.
body_zh: |
  怡安（Aon）宣布，旗下「資料中心全生命週期保險計畫」（Data Centre Lifecycle Insurance Program，DCLP）的承保額度已提升至50億美元，進一步擴大數位基礎設施專案在建造、營運、網路、貨運及恐怖主義等各類風險方面的可用保障。

  此次增額是繼先前計畫上限調升至35億美元後的再次擴充。怡安表示，最新修訂反映了在人工智慧、雲端運算及超大規模設施的需求帶動下，單一資料中心專案的規模與複雜程度持續攀升的市場現實。

  ## 承保範圍

  更新後的架構下，客戶可取得下列保障：

  - 最高 **50億美元** 的建造一切險、開業延誤險、財產損失及營業中斷險
  - 最高 **10億美元** 的恐怖主義保險（透過怡安現有保險設施提供）
  - 最高 **5億美元** 的專案貨運保險
  - 最高 **4億美元** 的網路及科技錯誤與疏失保險
  - 最高 **2億美元** 的美國境外第三方責任保險
  - 最高 **1億美元** 的美國境內第三方責任保險

  該計畫由倫敦勞合社（Lloyd's of London）及公司市場中A級評等保險公司所組成的核保集團承保，並搭配其他保險設施。計畫同時整合了怡安全球風險諮詢（Aon Global Risk Consulting）的服務，涵蓋氣候風險顧問、環境風險解決方案、安全風險諮詢、風險工程及營運韌性支援。

  ## 全生命週期的保障理念

  DCLP的架構設計旨在讓保險承保能量、工程投入與風險分析，能在資料中心開發流程更早期的階段介入。怡安表示，此一做法的目的在於銜接建造階段與後續營運之間的轉換風險，並隨時間推移提升資產的可保性。

  該計畫將資產從初始開發到長期營運的各類保障，整合於單一框架之內，而非針對每類風險分別安排獨立的險種配置。資產所有人、開發商、貸款方及營運商均可透過此計畫，一次性地處理建造延誤、財產損失、網路事故、責任風險及韌性規劃等課題。

  怡安指出，這樣的架構設計至關重要，因為資料中心一旦發生事故，往往會同時引發多個類別的損失，包括設備損壞、開業延誤、營業中斷及合約索賠。

  ## 市場背景

  怡安表示，隨著運算與儲存容量的需求持續攀升，資料中心已吸引大量機構投資人及私人資本進駐。這波成長趨勢使得專案發起人與營運商在尋求保險保障時面臨更大壓力，既要涵蓋建造風險，也須在大型資產投資組合中持續維持營運曝險的保障。

  怡安風險資本執行長喬·派薩（Joe Peiser）將此次擴充直接歸因於底層市場的結構性轉變。他表示：「數位基礎設施已成為全球經濟中最重要、也最資本密集的資產類別之一。隨著客戶建立規模更大、更複雜的資料中心投資組合，他們需要取得更高的保險承保能量，同時搭配能在資產全生命週期中強化韌性的解決方案。將DCLP擴大至50億美元，充分展現我們協助客戶取得資本、管理風險並以信心擴展規模的能力。」

  ## 產業意義

  DCLP的擴充，反映了一個更廣泛的產業趨勢——數位基礎設施的保險產品正日益走向專業化，並從單一險種配置轉向多險種整合套裝方案。隨著資料中心資產價值不斷提升，在雲端與人工智慧工作負載中的核心地位愈發鞏固，業務中斷所帶來的財務衝擊也相應加重，對保險承保能量的需求隨之水漲船高。

  怡安在全球逾120個國家設有業務據點。此次更新計畫在核心財產與建造風險方面設定50億美元的承保上限，並附加恐怖主義及網路風險的獨立限額，是DCLP自成立以來所達到的最高承保額度。
