body_en: |
  Nvidia is in talks to provide roughly $250 billion in financing guarantees for OpenAI as part of a massive data center project in southern Ohio, the Wall Street Journal reported on July 26, citing people familiar with the matter.

  The guarantee would cover OpenAI's lease of a 10-gigawatt (GW) facility being developed by SB Energy, SoftBank's energy subsidiary, and associated construction debt, but would not extend to the Nvidia chips installed inside the center, the Journal said. Nvidia is also in separate discussions to finance OpenAI's chip purchases worth up to $350 billion. The project's total cost, including chips, is expected to exceed $500 billion.

  ## Strategic stakes for OpenAI and Nvidia

  For OpenAI, a deal would mark its first step toward controlling its own computing infrastructure rather than renting capacity from Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle, the WSJ reported. For Nvidia, backing the financing would guarantee demand for its chips for years to come.

  OpenAI, the company behind the ChatGPT service, is valued at $852 billion, making it one of the most valuable private companies in the world, but it remains unprofitable, raising questions about its ability to honor multi-billion-dollar infrastructure commitments, the report said. Nvidia had previously invested $30 billion in OpenAI, the Commercial Times reported, citing the same WSJ reporting.

  Nvidia's $250 billion backstop would support financing vehicles designed to reassure lenders about the project's funding, the Journal said.

  ## Project scale, location, and timeline

  The facility, described as among the largest of its kind, is to be built on federal and private land in southern Ohio, according to The Information, as cited by the Straits Times. The first phase, delivering approximately 800 megawatts (MW) of power, is expected to reach completion in 2028, the Journal said. A 10-GW campus at full build-out would represent roughly 12.5 times that initial phase.

  The power supply for the facility is controlled by the U.S. government and funded separately by Japan under a recent bilateral trade agreement, tied to Tokyo's pledge to invest $33 billion in a natural gas plant. U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is involved in deciding which companies gain access to the power allocation, the WSJ reported.

  ## Multiple companies competing for access

  OpenAI has been in advanced lease negotiations with SoftBank for several weeks and is among the companies showing the strongest interest in the project, the Journal said. Anthropic, Microsoft, and Google have also held separate discussions with Lutnick about the project in recent weeks.

  ## Circular-financing concerns

  The arrangement has drawn scrutiny over what the Commercial Times described as concerns about "closed-loop investment" and an industry bubble. The structure creates a circular dependency in which Nvidia finances the infrastructure that in turn purchases Nvidia chips; a disruption at one point in the chain could cascade across the broader AI supply chain, the newspaper reported.

  ## Implications for AI infrastructure and the supply chain

  The prospective deal reflects a wider shift in which technology companies are tapping debt and equity markets to fund AI infrastructure at scale. Industry spending on AI infrastructure is on track to exceed $700 billion in 2026, Reuters reported.

  In Taiwan, Foxconn, which manufactures AI servers, is expected to receive AI hardware orders connected to the Ohio project, the Economic Daily News reported. Teco Electric and Machinery, which holds an existing strategic alliance with Foxconn centered on modular data center solutions, is expected to pursue power infrastructure orders including transformers, switchboards, uninterruptible power supply (UPS) systems, and generators. Teco said its North American AI data center projects with Foxconn are expected to enter a revenue phase in 2027.

  Reuters said it could not immediately verify the WSJ report. Nvidia, OpenAI, and the U.S. Commerce Department did not respond to requests for comment.
body_zh: |
  輝達（Nvidia）正洽談為OpenAI提供約2,500億美元的融資擔保，協助其承租一座位於俄亥俄州南部、規模達10吉瓦（GW）的超大型資料中心。《華爾街日報》7月26日援引知情人士消息報導此事，整項計畫含晶片在內的總費用預計將超過5,000億美元。

  該擔保將涵蓋OpenAI向軟銀旗下能源子公司SB Energy所承租的10GW設施租約，以及相關的建設債務，但不包含資料中心內部所安裝的輝達晶片。《華爾街日報》指出，輝達另行洽談為OpenAI晶片採購提供最高3,500億美元的融資。整項計畫（含晶片）的總成本預計超過5,000億美元。

  ## OpenAI與輝達的戰略布局

  對OpenAI而言，若交易成立，這將是其首度邁向掌控自有運算基礎設施的一步，不再仰賴向微軟（Microsoft）、亞馬遜（Amazon）及甲骨文（Oracle）租用算力。《華爾街日報》指出，對輝達而言，出面擔保融資可確保旗下晶片未來數年的穩定需求。

  ChatGPT服務背後的母公司OpenAI目前估值達8,520億美元，是全球市值最高的非上市企業之一，但公司仍處於虧損狀態，外界因此質疑其是否有能力履行數千億美元規模的基礎設施承諾。《工商時報》援引同一篇《華爾街日報》報導指出，輝達此前已對OpenAI投資達300億美元。

  輝達的2,500億美元擔保，將用於支持專項融資工具，旨在讓貸款方對計畫的資金來源更具信心。

  ## 計畫規模、地點與時程

  據《海峽時報》引述《The Information》的報導，這座被形容為同類中規模最大的設施，將建於俄亥俄州南部的聯邦土地及私人土地上。《華爾街日報》報導，第一期工程將提供約800百萬瓦（MW）的電力，預計於2028年竣工。若整座園區全面建成，10GW的總容量將是首期規模的約12.5倍。

  該設施的電力供應由美國政府掌控，並在近期一項美日雙邊貿易協定框架下由日本另行出資，與東京方面承諾投資330億美元興建一座天然氣發電廠一事掛鉤。美國商務部長霍華德．路特尼克（Howard Lutnick）負責參與決定哪些企業得以獲得電力配額。

  ## 多家企業競逐入場資格

  《華爾街日報》指出，OpenAI已與軟銀就租約進行了數週的深入談判，是目前對該計畫表態最積極的企業之一。Anthropic、微軟及谷歌（Google）近幾週也分別就此計畫與路特尼克進行過洽商。

  ## 循環融資疑慮

  此安排已引發外界審視。《工商時報》指出，各界對「封閉式循環投資」及產業泡沫化抱有疑慮——此結構形成一種循環依賴：輝達為基礎設施提供擔保，而這些設施反過來採購輝達晶片；一旦鏈條中的某一環節出現問題，衝擊便可能蔓延至整個AI供應鏈。

  ## 對AI基礎設施與供應鏈的影響

  此潛在交易反映出一個更廣泛的趨勢——科技企業正大規模動用債務及股權市場，為AI基礎設施建設籌措資金。據路透社（Reuters）報導，業界2026年在AI基礎設施上的支出有望突破7,000億美元。

  在台灣，製造AI伺服器的鴻海預計將承接與俄亥俄州計畫相關的AI硬體訂單。《經濟日報》報導，與鴻海已建立現行戰略聯盟、共同聚焦模組化資料中心解決方案的東元電機，預計將爭取包含變壓器、配電盤、不斷電系統（UPS）及發電機在內的電力基礎設施訂單。東元表示，與鴻海合作的北美AI資料中心專案，預計將於2027年進入貢獻營收的階段。

  路透社表示，目前無法即時證實《華爾街日報》的報導內容。輝達、OpenAI及美國商務部均未回應媒體的置評請求。
