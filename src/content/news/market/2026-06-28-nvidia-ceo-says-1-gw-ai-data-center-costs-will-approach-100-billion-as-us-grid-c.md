body_en: |
  Jensen Huang, chief executive of NVIDIA, said the cost of building a single gigawatt-scale artificial intelligence data center has risen to $50-60 billion and will climb to $80-100 billion in the near future, up from roughly $20-30 billion when such projects first appeared. Huang made the remarks during the keynote address at the NVIDIA GTC Taipei 2026 developer event on June 1, 2026.

  The disclosures place the capital demands of frontier AI infrastructure on par with the largest industrial megaprojects, with a single campus now requiring investment that can exceed the cost of a major semiconductor fabrication plant.

  ## Cost trajectory and NVIDIA's share of the bill

  Huang said at the Taipei event that 1 GW data center investment "started at around $20-30 billion, has now reached $50-60 billion, and will soon be $80-100 billion," without specifying a timeline or detailing the drivers of the projected increase. He had previously cited the approximately $50 billion figure at NVIDIA's earnings presentation for the May-July 2025 quarter, announced August 27, 2025, at which time he also disclosed that roughly $35 billion of a $50 billion project — approximately 70 percent — flows to NVIDIA products.

  NVIDIA's contribution to a campus spans GPUs (graphics processing units, the primary chips used to train and run AI models), GPU-based servers, and networking equipment. The remaining 30 percent of project cost covers CPUs, memory chips, solid-state storage drives, racks, cooling systems, the data center building itself, power-receiving infrastructure, and backup power such as diesel generators and battery storage, according to the Nikkei xTECH article.

  ## Power demand at nuclear-reactor scale

  One gigawatt equals one million kilowatts of continuous electricity draw. For context, the Nikkei xTECH article notes that each of the five smaller reactors at Japan's Kashiwazaki-Kariwa Nuclear Power Plant produces 1.1 million kilowatts (1.1 GW), while its two larger units each produce 1.356 GW — meaning a 1 GW data center requires roughly the output of one nuclear reactor running continuously.

  U.S. electric utilities no longer have sufficient spare grid capacity to supply power at that scale to new large customers, the article states. That constraint has pushed data center developers into an unfamiliar posture: funding and building dedicated power generation assets co-located with or adjacent to the computing facilities themselves.

  ## The Kilby Project: Chevron and Microsoft in West Texas

  Chevron announced on June 22, 2026, that it would develop the "Kilby Project," a co-located power plant with 2.67 GW of generating capacity, to serve a Microsoft AI data center under construction in West Texas. Chevron did not disclose the plant's investment figure, according to the article.

  The article's section on co-located generation is headlined with the assertion that adding such a power plant requires approximately ¥500 billion (roughly $3 billion at the exchange-rate equivalencies cited elsewhere in the article, where $50 billion is rendered as approximately ¥8 trillion). The detailed cost breakdown supporting that figure falls behind the article's subscription paywall.

  ## What it means for the industry

  The cost escalation Huang described reflects two compounding pressures. The first is rising prices for semiconductors and components that dominate the capital budget — a category NVIDIA itself supplies. The second is the emergence of power-plant construction as a new line item in data center project budgets, a cost that did not exist when utilities could accommodate large loads from the existing grid.

  Together, these forces are pushing single-facility commitments toward levels once associated only with nuclear plants, large offshore energy projects, or national infrastructure programs. The combination of a $50-100 billion compute campus and a multi-billion-dollar co-located power plant extends total project cost further still, though neither Chevron nor Microsoft disclosed an all-in figure for the West Texas project.

  Huang said at GTC Taipei 2026 that per-campus costs will reach $80-100 billion in the near term.
body_zh: |
  輝達（NVIDIA）執行長黃仁勳表示，興建單座千兆瓦（1 GW）規模人工智慧資料中心的成本，已從此類計畫剛起步時的約200至300億美元，上漲至目前的500至600億美元，並將在不久的將來進一步攀升至800至1000億美元。黃仁勳是在2026年6月1日輝達GTC台北2026開發者大會的主題演講中發表上述言論。

  此番揭露，使前沿AI基礎設施的資本需求躍升至與最大型工業巨型計畫相當的水準——一座單一園區所需的投資規模，如今已可超越興建一座大型半導體晶圓廠的造價。

  ## 成本走勢與輝達在其中的佔比

  黃仁勳在台北活動中指出，1 GW資料中心的投資「從最初的約200至300億美元，如今已達500至600億美元，且即將來到800至1000億美元」，但未說明時程，也未詳述預計漲幅背後的驅動因素。他此前曾在2025年8月27日公布的輝達2025財年第二季（5月至7月）財報說明會上提及約500億美元的數字；當時他也透露，在一個500億美元的計畫中，約350億美元——即約70%——流向輝達的產品。

  輝達對一座園區的貢獻，涵蓋GPU（繪圖處理器，即用於訓練及執行AI模型的主要晶片）、GPU伺服器，以及網路設備。計畫成本的其餘30%，則涵蓋CPU、記憶體晶片、固態儲存裝置、機架、冷卻系統、資料中心建築本體、受電基礎設施，以及備用電源（如柴油發電機與電池儲能系統）等項目，據《日經xTECH》報導。

  ## 堪比核電廠規模的電力需求

  1 GW等同於100萬千瓦（kW）的持續用電量。《日經xTECH》指出，以日本柏崎刈羽核電廠為例，其五座較小型機組各可發電110萬千瓦（1.1 GW），兩座較大型機組則各可發電135.6萬千瓦（1.356 GW）——這意味著一座1 GW的資料中心，所需電力約等於一座核電機組持續滿載運轉的輸出量。

  美國的電力公共事業目前已無足夠的備用電網容量，可在此規模下為新的大型用電客戶穩定供電，報導如此指出。這一限制已迫使資料中心開發商採取一種過去並不熟悉的姿態：自行出資興建專屬發電設施，並將其與運算設施共址或毗鄰設置。

  ## 「基爾比計畫」：雪佛龍與微軟攜手進軍德克薩斯州西部

  雪佛龍於2026年6月22日宣布，將開發「基爾比計畫」（Kilby Project）——一座發電容量達267萬千瓦（2.67 GW）的共址電廠，為微軟正在德克薩斯州西部興建的AI資料中心提供電力。雪佛龍並未披露該電廠的投資金額，據報導。

  報導中關於共址發電設施的段落標題，斷言興建此類電廠需要約5000億日圓（依文中其他處所援引的匯率換算，約合30億美元；文中將500億美元換算為約8兆日圓）。支撐該數字的詳細成本明細，則位於該報導的訂閱付費牆之後。

  ## 對產業的意義

  黃仁勳所描述的成本攀升，反映了兩股相互疊加的壓力。其一，是主導資本預算的半導體與零組件價格持續上漲——而這正是輝達本身所供應的類別。其二，是電廠興建作為資料中心計畫預算中一個全新支出項目的浮現——這項成本在電力公共事業尚能從現有電網消化大量負載之際，根本不存在。

  兩股力量合流，正將單一設施的投資承諾推向過去僅與核電廠、大型離岸能源計畫或國家基礎設施工程相提並論的規模。500至1000億美元的運算園區，加上數十億美元的共址電廠，使整個計畫的總成本更上一層樓——儘管雪佛龍與微軟均未披露德克薩斯州西部計畫的總體金額。

  黃仁勳在GTC台北2026上表示，每座園區的成本近期將達到800至1000億美元。
