body_en: |
  The Philippines' Pax Silica artificial intelligence and semiconductor hub carries no allocation in the Bases Conversion and Development Authority's 2027 budget proposal, Senator Paolo Benigno A. Aquino IV confirmed Wednesday during Senate plenary debate, while business leaders warned that unresolved infrastructure deficiencies could prevent the complex from attracting the investment it is designed to draw.

  The BCDA, the government body that converts former military reservations into industrial hubs, is the primary developer and host for Pax Silica at New Clark City in Tarlac province. Senator Aquino said no bilateral agreement for the project has been signed and official funding details remain unconfirmed. Human Rights Watch has stated that private investors will finance the infrastructure and facilities of the flagship complex, which is planned for a 1,620-hectare site at New Clark City.

  Senator Aquino called for transparency before any government money is committed. "The press releases keep changing, the statements of our leaders on the project also keep changing and we call for transparency and a clear plan before we move on toward funding this," he said. The Senate plenary subsequently moved to submit the BCDA's P1.15-billion (Philippine peso) budget for approval.

  ## Displacement estimates in dispute

  The BCDA has set aside P322 million in corporate funds to assist families displaced by its projects, including Pax Silica. The agency told Senator Aquino that 198 families will be affected — a figure lower than the 400 families the BCDA cited during an earlier Senate hearing and far below recent reports that placed the count at around 1,000.

  Senator Aquino and Senator Maria Imelda Josefa Remedios R. Marcos-Manotoc flagged the shifting estimates. Senator Aquino said independent reviews by the Department of Social Welfare and Development or the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples are needed to validate the figures.

  Allegations about land rights for the indigenous Ayta community in Capas, Tarlac have drawn additional scrutiny. An Al Jazeera reporter on Friday posted an alleged quitclaim agreement, a document waiving land rights, bearing the thumbprint of an Ayta farmer who cannot read or write. Senator Marcos-Manotoc said her information does not support the allegations that Ayta residents were asked to sign such agreements. The BCDA has reaffirmed its commitment to ensuring no resident is displaced until appropriate funds are secured and all human rights are acknowledged and respected, according to Senator Aquino.

  ## Infrastructure as a prerequisite for investment

  Business leaders speaking at the BusinessWorld Forecast 2027 panel discussion warned that the Philippines must resolve infrastructure deficiencies before Pax Silica can attract the technology investment it targets.

  Stratbase Group founder and chief executive Victor Andres Manhit said the country would need reliable electricity, adequate supplies of pure water and efficient port connectivity to draw semiconductor manufacturers, artificial intelligence developers, advanced manufacturers and mineral processors to the hub. "How can a semiconductor company invest — why would they invest five, six, seven billion dollars in the Philippines when there's no power or pure water to support it?" he said.

  Mr. Manhit warned that the Philippines risked repeating a missed opportunity, recalling how the country lost manufacturing investments when production shifted from China to Southeast Asia and Vietnam attracted a larger share. He said Pax Silica could help the Philippines catch up with regional competitors, but only with a coordinated government strategy.

  He proposed creating a single government commission to oversee infrastructure development and allowing private companies to build and manage industrial hubs under the initiative. He cited the development of Fort Bonifacio through a partnership involving the BCDA as a model for public-private collaboration on large-scale industrial and infrastructure projects. "I think at least part of the industrial hub of Pax Silica must be privatized," he said, arguing that private-sector participation could improve the project's long-term viability and insulate it from shifting government policies.

  ## Sovereignty concerns and public opposition

  Philippine Star economist and political analyst Andrew James Masigan said the Philippines must also address geopolitical concerns surrounding the initiative, including questions about Philippine sovereignty and the country's relationship with the United States. He said the Philippines should weigh Pax Silica's potential economic benefits and job creation against the strategic interests of its foreign partners, while remaining alert to foreign interference and malign influence.

  Public opposition to the initiative is substantial. A national survey by WR Numero Research found that 77% of respondents initially opposed Pax Silica, compared with 7% who supported it and 16% who were undecided. After respondents were informed that the initiative was intended to strengthen the country's technology and semiconductor industries, contribute to economic growth and deepen ties with the United States, opposition held at 71%. Support rose to 13% and 16% remained undecided.

  "The findings indicate that the initiative faces both an awareness challenge and a predominantly negative initial response among respondents answering the support question," WR Numero said.

  The project has drawn broader criticism from groups concerned about land rights, displacement of communities, livelihoods, environmental impacts and the protection of local communities and natural resources.

  ## What the gaps mean for the project

  With no bilateral agreement signed and no state funding requested for 2027, the initiative currently relies on private investment to advance, according to Human Rights Watch. Senator Aquino called for independent government reviews of displacement figures by the Department of Social Welfare and Development or the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples before any communities are relocated. WR Numero Research found that 16% of survey respondents remained undecided on the initiative even after being briefed on its stated economic goals.

  ## 菲律賓Pax Silica半導體園區2027年預算掛零，商界示警基建缺口恐嚇退投資

  菲律賓人工智慧與半導體樞紐Pax Silica，在基地轉型暨發展局（BCDA）2027年預算案中毫無列款。參議員保羅·貝尼諾·阿基諾四世（Paolo Benigno A. Aquino IV）週三在參議院院會辯論中證實此事，商界領袖亦同步示警，指基礎建設問題懸而未決，恐令這座園區難以吸引其預期中的投資。

  BCDA是負責將舊軍事基地轉型為產業園區的政府機構，也是位於塔拉克省新克拉克市（New Clark City）Pax Silica計畫的主要開發單位與地主。阿基諾參議員表示，該計畫迄今尚未簽署任何雙邊協議，官方的資金細節仍不明朗。人權觀察（Human Rights Watch）指出，私人投資者將負責這座旗艦園區基礎建設與設施的融資；該園區規劃占地1,620公頃，落址於新克拉克市。

  阿基諾參議員呼籲，在政府投入任何公款之前，必須先落實資訊透明。他說：「新聞稿一改再改，政府領導人對計畫的說法也一變再變，我們要求在推進任何資金挹注之前，先做到資訊透明、拿出清楚的計畫。」參議院院會隨後決議將BCDA共計115億披索的預算送交批准。

  ## 遷置戶數估算爭議不斷

  BCDA已撥出3億2,200萬披索的自籌資金，用於協助因其各項計畫（包括Pax Silica）而須遷離的家庭。該機構告知阿基諾參議員，受影響家庭共198戶——此數字低於BCDA在稍早一場參院聽證會上援引的400戶，更遠低於近期報導所揭露的約1,000戶。

  阿基諾參議員與伊美黛·荷西法·瑪可仕-馬諾托克參議員（Maria Imelda Josefa Remedios R. Marcos-Manotoc）均對這些數字的前後矛盾提出質疑。阿基諾參議員表示，應由社會福利暨發展部或全國原住民委員會進行獨立審查，以核實相關數據。

  此外，塔拉克省卡帕斯（Capas）原住民艾塔（Ayta）族的土地權問題也引發更多關注。一名半島電視台（Al Jazeera）記者上週五公開一份疑似土地權利放棄聲明（quitclaim agreement）——即一份放棄土地權利的文件——上面印有一名不識字的艾塔農民的指印。瑪可仕-馬諾托克參議員表示，據她所掌握的資訊，並不支持艾塔居民遭要求簽署此類協議的說法。阿基諾參議員則表示，BCDA重申承諾，在取得足夠資金、充分尊重並保障所有人權之前，不會讓任何居民被迫遷離。

  ## 基礎建設是吸引投資的先決條件

  在「BusinessWorld 2027年展望」論壇的座談環節中，商界領袖警告，菲律賓必須先解決基礎建設缺口，Pax Silica才有能力吸引其所鎖定的科技投資。

  Stratbase集團創辦人暨執行長維克托·安德烈斯·曼希特（Victor Andres Manhit）指出，菲律賓必須具備穩定的電力、充足的純水供應，以及高效的港口連結，才能吸引半導體製造商、人工智慧開發商、先進製造業者與礦產加工業者進駐園區。他說：「如果菲律賓連電力和純水都無法保障，半導體公司為什麼要在這裡投資五、六、七十億美元？」

  曼希特警告，菲律賓面臨重蹈錯失良機覆轍的風險。他回顧，當年製造業從中國外移至東南亞時，越南吸走了更大份額的投資，菲律賓就此錯過一班車。他認為Pax Silica可望協助菲律賓迎頭趕上區域競爭對手，但前提是政府必須有統一協調的戰略。

  他建議成立單一政府委員會，統籌監管基礎建設開發，並允許私人企業在該計畫下自行興建及營運產業園區。他以BCDA參與博尼法西奧堡（Fort Bonifacio）開發的公私協力模式，作為大型產業與基礎建設計畫的成功參考案例。他說：「我認為Pax Silica的產業園區，至少應有部分走向民營化。」他主張，引入私部門參與，可提升計畫的長期可行性，並降低其對政府政策更迭的依賴。

  ## 主權疑慮與民間反對聲浪

  《菲律賓星報》（Philippine Star）經濟學家暨政治分析師安德魯·詹姆斯·馬西根（Andrew James Masigan）表示，菲律賓還必須正視這項計畫所引發的地緣政治疑慮，包括菲律賓主權問題，以及菲律賓與美國關係的討論。他說，菲律賓應在衡量Pax Silica潛在經濟效益與就業機會的同時，也審慎考量外國夥伴的戰略利益，並對外國干預及惡意影響保持警惕。

  民間對這項計畫的反對情緒相當強烈。WR Numero Research的全國民調顯示，77%的受訪者一開始表示反對Pax Silica，僅7%支持，16%未表態。在受訪者被告知該計畫旨在強化菲律賓科技與半導體產業、促進經濟成長、深化與美國關係之後，反對比例仍維持在71%；支持比例上升至13%，16%仍未表態。

  WR Numero表示：「調查結果顯示，這項計畫面臨認知不足的挑戰，而受訪者對支持與否的初始反應，也以負面居多。」

  此外，各界對土地權利、社區遷置、民眾生計、環境衝擊，以及本地社區與自然資源的保護等議題，也對該計畫提出了更廣泛的批評。

  ## 缺口對計畫意味著什麼

  雙邊協議尚未簽署、2027年亦無任何國家預算列款，依據人權觀察的說法，Pax Silica目前完全仰賴私人投資推進。阿基諾參議員呼籲，在任何社區遷置行動啟動之前，應先由社會福利暨發展部或全國原住民委員會，對遷置戶數進行獨立的政府審查。WR Numero Research的調查也發現，即便受訪者已被告知計畫的既定經濟目標，仍有16%的人對此計畫持保留態度、尚未決定立場。
