body_en: |
  Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. called for domestic investment in artificial intelligence and participation in shaping global AI governance, saying the Philippines risks falling behind in the global economy if it remains a passive consumer of the technology.

  Marcos made the remarks at a media briefing in Shenzhen, China, following last week's Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) meetings. He said Southeast Asian leaders had reached a shared view that their countries should not function as AI customers alone.

  "We should be part of the decisions that are being made as to where to take AI," Marcos said, as reported by BusinessWorld. "You cannot hide from it. What you do is to understand what it is, understand what the industry is doing and how that can benefit us."

  Marcos also addressed Pax Silica, a US-led initiative he described as an effort to widen the sources of high-technology commodities with AI at its center. The Philippines has not yet signed the initiative and domestic protests have been raised against it. Marcos said his administration intends to participate. "It is our best opportunity to be part of the global discussion on AI and Pax Silica. And we should take full advantage of it and we intend to," he said.

  He said South Korea, Japan, and Latin American countries had expressed similar positions, emphasizing the need to shape AI's development rather than accept outcomes determined by others.

  ## IMF call for urgency

  International Monetary Fund (IMF) Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva, speaking at the APEC meetings in Shenzhen, urged leaders to rapidly build digital infrastructure, prepare workforces for AI, and adopt safeguards against risks to financial stability and cybersecurity. "To put it very simply, you snooze, you lose," she said.

  ## Philippine capacity and cost concerns

  Former Budget Secretary Romulo Neri struck a cautious note, telling BusinessWorld the Philippines should adopt AI selectively based on practical uses. "AI use is costly in terms of data center investments, power and water for cooling," he said. He added that the country is suited only to small data centers, with a maximum capacity he placed at 50 megawatts (MW).

  Neri also questioned the financial sustainability of the US AI business model, pointing to gaps between revenue and costs, operating and financial losses, and growing reliance on debt among major providers. He cited competition from Chinese firms such as DeepSeek, which offer AI processing at a fraction of US providers' prices, as additional pressure on that model.

  Jose Enrique Africa, executive director of research organization IBON Foundation, said the country's challenge extends beyond attracting capital. "AI adoption can be improved to the satisfaction of foreign investors without the domestic economy becoming more productive and industrialized," he told BusinessWorld. "The challenge isn't simply to attract foreign investment but to develop genuinely Filipino technological capabilities and productive industries."

  ## Governance framework and regional declaration

  On November 12, Marcos signed Executive Order No. 131, adopting a national AI governance framework and establishing the National Artificial Intelligence Council. The council is charged with coordinating government AI policies, regulating responsible innovation, and overseeing AI development and infrastructure.

  At the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit in Manila, a Philippines-led declaration on AI for micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) was adopted by member states. The declaration targets lower costs and broader access to AI tools for small businesses and advances regional AI governance standards and safeguards against AI-enabled online abuse.

  Manila's concurrent actions, including a domestic governance council, a regional MSMEs declaration, and a presidential signal of intent on Pax Silica, mark an effort to position the Philippines as a participant rather than a recipient in global AI policy. The practical gap between that ambition and the infrastructure constraints Neri outlined, including limited power capacity and high data center costs, now falls to the National Artificial Intelligence Council to manage.

  菲律賓總統小費迪南·馬科斯呼籲國內積極投資人工智慧，並主動參與全球AI治理規則的制定。他警告，若菲律賓持續扮演技術被動消費者的角色，將在全球經濟競爭中陷入落後。

  馬科斯是在中國深圳召開的媒體簡報會上發表上述談話，時間緊接上週舉行的亞太經濟合作組織（APEC）峰會。他表示，東南亞各國領袖已達成共識：各國不應僅以AI客戶的身份自居。

  「我們應該參與那些決定AI走向的討論，」馬科斯說，此言論由《商業世界》（BusinessWorld）報導。「你無法迴避它。你能做的，是去了解它是什麼、了解這個產業在做什麼，以及這一切如何能讓我們受益。」

  馬科斯也談及「帕克斯矽卡」（Pax Silica）倡議——他將其描述為一項以AI為核心、由美國主導、旨在拓寬高科技商品供應來源的計畫。菲律賓目前尚未簽署該倡議，國內亦出現抗議聲浪。馬科斯表示，其政府有意加入。「這是我們參與AI與帕克斯矽卡全球討論的最佳機會，我們應該充分把握，我們也確實打算這麼做，」他說。

  他指出，南韓、日本及拉丁美洲各國也表達了相似立場，強調應主動形塑AI的發展方向，而非被動接受由他人所決定的結果。

  ## IMF呼籲各國把握時機

  國際貨幣基金組織（IMF）總裁克里斯塔利娜·格奧爾基耶娃在深圳APEC峰會上發言，敦促各國領袖盡速建設數位基礎設施、為勞動力迎接AI時代做好準備，並建立因應金融穩定與網路安全風險的防護機制。她以一句話直白道出：「說得簡單一點，你若鬆懈，就會落後。」

  ## 菲律賓的容量與成本隱憂

  前預算部長羅慕洛·內里（Romulo Neri）則持謹慎態度，他告訴《商業世界》，菲律賓應依據實際應用需求，有選擇性地導入AI。「AI的使用成本不低，涵蓋資料中心投資、電力，以及冷卻用水，」他說。他補充，菲律賓只適合建設小型資料中心，他估計容量上限為50百萬瓦（MW）。

  內里也對美國AI商業模式的財務永續性提出質疑，指出主要業者存在營收與成本之間的落差、營業與財務均持續虧損，對舉債的依賴亦日益加深。他並以中國企業DeepSeek（深度求索）為例，指出其AI運算服務報價僅為美國業者的一小部分，對上述商業模式構成額外壓力。

  IBON基金會執行長荷西·恩里克·阿非利加（Jose Enrique Africa）表示，菲律賓面臨的挑戰不僅止於吸引資本。「AI的採用或許能讓外國投資者滿意，但國內經濟未必因此變得更有生產力、更工業化，」他告訴《商業世界》。「挑戰不只是吸引外資，而是要培育真正屬於菲律賓的技術能力與生產性產業。」

  ## 治理框架與區域宣言

  11月12日，馬科斯簽署第131號行政命令，採納國家AI治理框架，並成立「國家人工智慧委員會」。該委員會負責統籌政府AI政策、規範負責任的創新，並監督AI的發展與基礎設施建設。

  在馬尼拉召開的東南亞國家協會（東協）峰會上，一份由菲律賓主導、以AI支援微型及中小企業（MSMEs）為主題的宣言獲各成員國採納。該宣言以降低小型企業使用AI的成本、擴大工具取得管道為目標，同時推動區域AI治理標準，並建立防範AI被用於網路濫用行為的保護機制。

  馬尼拉此番同步出擊——在國內成立AI治理委員會、在區域層面推動中小企業AI宣言、在總統層級釋出加入帕克斯矽卡的意向——標誌著菲律賓有意在全球AI政策中扮演參與者而非接受者的角色。然而，這一抱負與內里所揭示的基礎設施現實之間的落差——包括有限的電力容量與高昂的資料中心成本——如今有待國家人工智慧委員會設法弭平。
