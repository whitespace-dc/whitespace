  ## Megaspeed profile

  Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud computing firm and Nvidia reseller, faces scrutiny from US and Singaporean authorities over suspected diversion of advanced AI chips whose export to China is restricted under US law. The company's former managing director, Huang Le — known in English as Alice Huang — is at the centre of a separate US inquiry into her alleged affair with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang and alleged links to Chinese intelligence.

  Megaspeed was incorporated in Singapore in 2015 under the name 7Road International and renamed Megaspeed International in March 2023, according to the company's business filings. It is wholly owned by Swiftdata, a Singapore-registered entity incorporated in October 2023 that lists "providing hosting services by non-data centres" as its principal activity.

  Two Singapore citizens appear as officers across both entities. Tan Yong Pong was appointed director of Swiftdata on October 31, 2023, and became a director of Megaspeed less than two weeks later. Ng Wei Shi Khloe is listed as company secretary of both firms. Mr. Tan and Ms. Ng are the only officers named in the business filings of either company. Reuters said it could not immediately contact Swiftdata, and Megaspeed did not respond to a request for comment.

  ## Alice Huang's tenure

  Huang Le ended an eight-month tenure as managing director of Megaspeed in October 2023, the same month Swiftdata was incorporated and Mr. Tan joined both companies as director. Singapore's home affairs ministry said last month that it is separately investigating her alleged links to Chinese intelligence, responding to a Wall Street Journal report that Singapore had expressed concern about her ties to Jensen Huang.

  The ministry said, however, that neither the Singapore government nor its officials had made the statements attributed to them in the Journal report, and that "no such investigations" had been conducted specifically regarding Alice Huang's connection to Jensen Huang. It confirmed that investigations into Megaspeed itself are continuing.

  ## Financial profile

  Megaspeed's financial disclosures show a sharp expansion in scale. Its assets grew from $33 million in 2023 to $3 billion in 2024, according to its business filings. Revenue rose from zero to $294 million over the same period. The company reported an after-tax loss of $20 million in 2024, compared with a loss of $21,283 the prior year. Its cash flow statement showed $844,555 directed to investing activities in 2024; in 2023, financing activities had brought in $10 million.

  On its website, Megaspeed describes itself as an Nvidia Cloud Partner for the Asia-Pacific region, offering customers access to Nvidia's GB200 NVL72 and HGX H100 computing systems — both high-performance platforms central to AI workloads and subject to US export restrictions when destined for China.

  ## Regulatory and law enforcement actions

  Singapore police said in October 2025 that they were investigating Megaspeed over "suspected breaches of our domestic laws," following a request by a US senator to the Commerce Department to examine whether the company had diverted Nvidia chips to restricted buyers.

  Singapore's government has stated that it does not enforce "unilateral export controls imposed by other countries," but that it acts "firmly against companies and individuals who violate our laws and are engaged in fraudulent and dishonest practices." Singapore is separately investigating four other individuals charged with chip fraud in an unrelated case.

  ## Singapore's position as a transit hub

  Megaspeed's case is one of several that have drawn attention to Singapore's emergence as a base for Chinese firms seeking to reduce exposure to US-China trade tensions — a practice described in trade circles as "Singapore-washing," referring to the domiciling of companies in the trade-focused city-state to distance them from Chinese ownership or control.

  The Megaspeed investigations, which span both US export-control channels and Singapore's domestic legal framework, reflect increasing pressure on intermediary jurisdictions as the US tightens enforcement of AI chip restrictions. Singapore's home affairs ministry said investigations into Megaspeed are ongoing.

  ## Megaspeed公司概況

  新加坡雲端運算公司Megaspeed同時也是輝達（Nvidia）授權經銷商，目前正遭美國與新加坡當局調查，懷疑其將依美國法律禁止出口至中國的先進AI晶片，轉售給受限買家。該公司前常務董事黃樂（英文名Alice Huang）是另一項美國調查的核心人物，調查範圍涵蓋她與輝達執行長黃仁勳（Jensen Huang）之間據稱的緋聞，以及疑似與中國情報機構的關聯。

  Megaspeed於2015年以「7Road International」之名在新加坡設立，依據商業登記文件，於2023年3月更名為Megaspeed International。該公司由新加坡登記實體Swiftdata全資持有，Swiftdata於2023年10月設立，登記主要業務為「提供非資料中心的託管服務」。

  兩名新加坡公民分別在上述兩家公司擔任要職。陳勇邦（Tan Yong Pong）於2023年10月31日獲任命為Swiftdata董事，不到兩週後亦成為Megaspeed董事。黃慧詩（Ng Wei Shi Khloe）則同時擔任兩家公司的公司秘書。陳先生與黃女士是兩家公司商業登記文件中唯一具名的管理人員。路透社表示，目前無法立即聯繫到Swiftdata；Megaspeed亦未回應採訪請求。

  ## 黃樂的任職經歷

  黃樂於2023年10月結束了擔任Megaspeed常務董事共八個月的任期，同月Swiftdata旋即設立，陳勇邦亦於同期加入兩家公司擔任董事。新加坡內政部上月表示，正就她疑似與中國情報機構的關聯展開獨立調查——此舉係回應《華爾街日報》一篇指稱新加坡曾對她與黃仁勳關係表示關切的報導。

  然而，內政部同時澄清，《華爾街日報》報導中所引述的相關言論，並非出自新加坡政府或其官員，且「並未針對」黃樂與黃仁勳的關係進行任何調查。該部確認，針對Megaspeed公司本身的調查仍在持續進行中。

  ## 財務概況

  Megaspeed的財務揭露顯示，該公司規模急遽擴張。依據商業登記文件，其資產從2023年的3,300萬美元，在2024年一舉躍升至30億美元。同期營收則從零成長至2.94億美元。公司2024年稅後虧損達2,000萬美元，對比前一年僅虧損21,283美元。現金流量表顯示，2024年流向投資活動的資金為844,555美元；2023年則透過融資活動取得1,000萬美元資金入賬。

  Megaspeed在官網上自稱是輝達亞太地區的雲端合作夥伴，為客戶提供存取輝達GB200 NVL72與HGX H100運算系統的服務——這兩款高效能平台均是AI工作負載的核心設備，依美國出口管制法規，出口至中國均受到嚴格限制。

  ## 監管與執法行動

  新加坡警方於2025年10月表示，正就「疑似違反本地法律」的情況調查Megaspeed，此前一名美國參議員曾致函美國商務部，要求審查該公司是否曾將輝達晶片轉售給受限買家。

  新加坡政府表示，不執行「其他國家單方面實施的出口管制」，但會「對違反本地法律、從事欺詐與不誠實行為的公司及個人採取堅定立場」。另外，新加坡目前亦就一宗獨立案件，另行調查四名被控晶片詐欺的當事人。

  ## 新加坡的中轉樞紐地位

  Megaspeed案是多起引發外界關注的案例之一，凸顯了新加坡近年逐漸成為中國企業尋求降低美中貿易緊張局勢曝險落腳地的現象。業界將此稱為「Singapore-washing」，意指企業將公司設籍於這個以貿易著稱的城市國家，藉此淡化其中國所有權或掌控關係。

  Megaspeed調查案橫跨美國出口管制途徑與新加坡國內法律框架，反映出隨著美國加緊執行AI晶片出口限制，中間過境司法管轄地所承受的壓力與日俱增。新加坡內政部表示，針對Megaspeed的調查至今仍在進行中。
