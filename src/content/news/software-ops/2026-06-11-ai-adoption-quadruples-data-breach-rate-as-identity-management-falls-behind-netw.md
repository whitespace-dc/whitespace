body_en: |
  Companies that widely deploy AI tools reported data breaches at nearly four times the rate of organizations that do not over the past 12 months, identity security firm Netwrix said in a report published Wednesday, as three-quarters of organizations admitted they are not fully overseeing the activity of AI agent accounts operating inside their systems.

  The breach rate among heavy AI users stood at 43% over the past 12 months, compared with 11% among organizations that do not widely use AI, according to the report. Netwrix attributed the gap to a structural security problem: AI tools require their own user accounts, and each account is a potential entry point for attackers. The firm calls the resulting exposure an expanded "identity footprint."

  ## AI agents acting on sensitive data without full oversight

  Forty-one percent of organizations are already allowing AI agents to access sensitive data and perform what Netwrix described as "vital tasks." Yet three-quarters of those same organizations are not fully tracking what those AI accounts are doing inside their systems, the report found.

  Identities, in security terms, include both human user accounts and non-human accounts created for software tools and automated processes. Non-human identities — machine accounts, service accounts, and agent credentials that act on behalf of users or systems — are the category AI tools expand most rapidly.

  "AI agents are now acting on behalf of humans against sensitive data," Netwrix researchers wrote. "Non-human identities need the same operational rigor long applied to privileged human access."

  ## Visibility and control gaps are widespread

  The report documents failures across the basic mechanics of identity governance. Three-quarters of organizations lack a single, unified view of which identities have access to sensitive data. More than half do not maintain an up-to-date inventory of that sensitive data. Seventy-one percent cannot quickly determine which identities can access which data, and 70% have no security strategy that connects data protection with identity governance.

  Control over existing accounts is equally limited. Seventy-six percent of organizations cannot immediately revoke a dormant or inactive account's access to data. Seventy-two percent say their accounts carry excessive permissions, or they do not know what permissions those accounts hold. Roughly two-thirds believe at least some accounts in their environment have unnecessary access to critical data. Only one in four companies said they were fully confident in their ability to identify potentially dangerous account permission configurations.

  ## Identity compromises drive three-quarters of breaches

  Identity-related weaknesses are already the dominant mechanism attackers use to reach sensitive data. Three-quarters of incidents in which hackers access sensitive data involve either a compromised identity or a misconfigured account permission, according to Netwrix. The firm found that most organizations are aware of this pattern but are not acting on it.

  ## Heavy AI users show stronger identity governance

  Netwrix identified one countervailing finding: the organizations deploying AI most extensively also tend to take identity management most seriously. The report does not quantify this correlation in detail but indicates that some high-volume AI adopters recognize the risk and have invested in governance accordingly.

  ## Report scope

  The findings are based on a worldwide survey of 2,317 security professionals across 1,889 organizations in 60 industries. The report also includes data on organizations' readiness to govern AI systems and on the frequency of unauthorized-identity-access incidents segmented by organization size.

  The core finding — that AI adoption is expanding organizations' identity footprints faster than security teams can track or control them — is a direct concern for security, procurement, and compliance functions. As the share of organizations granting AI agents access to sensitive systems grows, each new agent account added without full oversight widens the available attack surface. Netwrix's data show that only one in four organizations said they were fully confident in their ability to detect dangerous account permission configurations, a baseline that will be tested further as AI agent deployments expand.
body_zh: |
  廣泛導入AI工具的企業，過去十二個月的資料外洩率幾乎是未使用AI組織的四倍——這是身分安全廠商Netwrix週三（2026年6月10日）發布的報告所揭露的數字。報告同時指出，有四分之三的組織承認，對於AI代理帳號在其系統內部的行為，他們並未能完整掌握。

  根據報告，大量使用AI的組織在過去十二個月的資料外洩率高達43%，相較之下，未廣泛使用AI的組織僅有11%。Netwrix將這一差距歸因於一個結構性的資安問題：AI工具需要建立自己的使用者帳號，而每個帳號都是攻擊者的潛在入侵點。該公司將這種因此產生的暴露面稱為擴大的「身分足跡」（identity footprint）。

  ## AI代理存取敏感資料卻缺乏完整監控

  已有41%的組織允許AI代理存取敏感資料，並執行Netwrix所描述的「關鍵任務」。然而，報告發現，在這些組織中，有四分之三並未完整追蹤這些AI帳號在系統內部的活動情形。

  從資安角度來看，「身分」涵蓋人類使用者帳號，以及為軟體工具和自動化流程所建立的非人類帳號。非人類身分——包括機器帳號、服務帳號，以及代表使用者或系統運作的代理憑證——正是AI工具擴張最為迅速的類別。

  Netwrix研究人員在報告中寫道：「AI代理現在正代表人類對敏感資料採取行動。非人類身分需要獲得與特權人類存取長期以來相同程度的嚴格管理。」

  ## 可見度與控管落差普遍存在

  報告記錄了身分治理基本機制上的諸多缺失。四分之三的組織缺乏統一的視圖，無法一覽哪些身分可存取敏感資料。超過一半的組織未能維護最新的敏感資料清單。71%的組織無法快速判斷哪些身分可以存取哪些資料，70%的組織沒有將資料保護與身分治理相互串聯的資安策略。

  對現有帳號的控管能力同樣有限。76%的組織無法立即撤銷休眠或閒置帳號的資料存取權限。72%的組織表示，其帳號擁有過高的權限，或者根本不清楚這些帳號持有哪些權限。大約三分之二的組織認為，其環境中至少有部分帳號對關鍵資料擁有不必要的存取權。僅有四分之一的企業表示，對於識別潛在危險的帳號權限設定，他們具有完全的把握。

  ## 身分遭入侵佔資料外洩事件四分之三

  身分相關的弱點已成為攻擊者觸及敏感資料的主要手段。根據Netwrix的統計，四分之三涉及駭客存取敏感資料的事件，都牽涉到身分遭入侵或帳號權限設定錯誤。該公司發現，大多數組織雖然意識到這種模式，卻並未積極付諸行動。

  ## 大量導入AI的組織反而更重視身分治理

  Netwrix也發現了一個相對正面的現象：最積極部署AI的組織，往往也最認真看待身分管理。報告雖未詳細量化這種相關性，但指出部分高度採用AI的組織已認識到相關風險，並相應地投入治理機制。

  ## 報告範疇

  本報告的調查結果來自全球60個產業、1,889個組織中共2,317名資安專業人員的問卷調查。報告亦涵蓋各組織對AI系統進行治理的整備程度，以及依組織規模分類的非授權身分存取事件發生頻率。

  核心發現指向一個共同警訊：AI導入正以超越資安團隊追蹤與控管能力的速度，持續擴大組織的身分足跡。這對資安、採購及法規遵循等部門而言都是直接的隱憂。隨著允許AI代理存取敏感系統的組織比例持續提升，每新增一個未受完整監控的代理帳號，可供攻擊者利用的攻擊面就隨之擴大。Netwrix的資料顯示，僅有四分之一的組織表示對於偵測危險帳號權限設定具有完全信心——而隨著AI代理的部署規模持續擴張，這條基準線將面臨更為嚴峻的考驗。
