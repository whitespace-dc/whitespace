body_en: |
  Cryptocurrency-mining malware was deployed on South Africa's Lengau supercomputer between May and June 2026 after attackers gained unauthorized access to the system, Science, Technology and Innovation Minister Blade Nzimande told Parliament last week. The breach targeted the Centre for High Performance Computing (CHPC), which operates at the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) campus, and affected only certain components of the system rather than the broader national cyberinfrastructure, Nzimande said.

  ## What the attack exploited

  Nzimande attributed the breach to vulnerabilities inherent in the aging hardware. Lengau was installed in 2016, making it roughly a decade old. "Due to the legacy environment, some components are ageing, technically constrained or no longer fully supported by original vendors," Nzimande said. The minister did not disclose the specific vulnerability or attack vector used to gain access.

  The CSIR's Defence and Security cluster has engaged its cybersecurity specialists to conduct forensic analysis and remediation. Several monitoring measures have been introduced or reinforced to detect abnormal workloads, suspicious authentication activity, and other indicators of compromise, according to Nzimande. The investigation is ongoing.

  ## Oversight gaps acknowledged

  Nzimande signaled that existing incident-reporting processes had fallen short. "Going forward, the existing escalation, reporting and cyber-risk oversight arrangements will be strengthened to ensure faster response and executive visibility of cybersecurity incidents affecting strategic research infrastructure," he said. The statement implies that the breach did not reach executive attention as quickly as it should have under current protocols.

  ## Lengau's role and limitations

  Lengau, when delivered to the CHPC in 2016, was the fastest computer on the African continent, rated at one petaflop — one quadrillion floating-point operations per second — and replaced an earlier CHPC system called Tsessebe. The system has since supported climate modelling, medical research, and astronomy workloads. Compute demand from researchers has grown beyond what Lengau can efficiently serve, according to the CSIR and its National Integrated Cyberinfrastructure System (NICIS), the body that oversees national research computing infrastructure.

  ## Replacement system and funding

  NICS has already taken delivery of a replacement system rated at four petaflops — four times Lengau's original capacity. The new system will be brought online in phases to avoid disrupting research that currently runs on Lengau. The first phase is expected to be complete by the end of November 2026, with full deployment targeted for March 2027.

  NICS has been allocated ZAR 292 million (approximately US$18.2 million) for the 2026/27 financial year. That budget covers human resources, governance and management, operations, research and human capacity development, and technology, equipment, and built infrastructure, in addition to the new supercomputer. The portion of that allocation spent specifically on the new machine was not disclosed.

  "The department recognises that the renewal of ageing core infrastructure, including the Lengau high-performance computing system, is necessary to maintain South Africa's scientific competitiveness and to respond to increasing demand from the research and innovation community," Nzimande said.

  ## Significance for research infrastructure security

  The Lengau incident illustrates the security exposure that accumulates when high-performance computing systems age past vendor support cycles. Systems used for sensitive scientific research — climate modelling, medical data, astronomy — become targets for opportunistic attacks such as cryptomining, which consumes computational resources without necessarily exfiltrating data but can degrade research throughput and signal deeper access than operators initially recognize. The breach adds pressure on institutions managing legacy scientific infrastructure to accelerate refresh cycles and tighten incident-escalation chains.

  The CSIR said it will continue to monitor Lengau through the transition period. Full migration to the new four-petaflop system is projected to be complete by March 2027.

  ## 南非Lengau超級電腦遭加密貨幣挖礦入侵，老舊硬體漏洞成破口

  南非科學、技術與創新部長布萊德·恩齊曼德（Blade Nzimande）上週向國會表示，2026年5至6月間，加密貨幣挖礦惡意程式遭部署至南非超級電腦Lengau，攻擊者在此期間取得了對該系統的未授權存取權限。此次攻擊目標為高效能運算中心（CHPC），該中心位於科學與工業研究委員會（CSIR）園區；恩齊曼德表示，事件僅影響系統的特定元件，並未波及更廣泛的全國網路基礎設施。

  ## 攻擊如何得逞

  恩齊曼德將此次入侵歸因於老舊硬體所固有的安全漏洞。Lengau於2016年安裝，至今已近十年。「由於系統環境老舊，部分元件已日趨老化，在技術上受到限制，或已不再獲得原廠完整支援，」恩齊曼德說。部長並未披露攻擊者所利用的具體漏洞或入侵途徑。

  CSIR的國防與安全部門已調派網路安全專家進行鑑識分析與補救作業。據恩齊曼德表示，目前已導入或強化多項監控措施，以偵測異常工作負載、可疑的身分驗證活動及其他入侵指標。調查工作仍在持續進行中。

  ## 通報機制存在缺口

  恩齊曼德也坦承現有的事件通報流程有所不足。「未來，現行的升報、通報與網路風險監督機制將予以強化，以確保影響策略性研究基礎設施的網路安全事件能獲得更迅速的回應，並讓高層主管即時掌握情況，」他說。此聲明隱含著，依現行規範，此次入侵未能如預期般及時呈報至高層。

  ## Lengau的定位與限制

  Lengau於2016年交付CHPC時，是非洲大陸運算速度最快的電腦，效能達一petaflop——即每秒可執行一千兆次浮點運算——並取代了CHPC早先的系統Tsessebe。此後，該系統持續支援氣候模型建立、醫學研究及天文學等工作負載。根據CSIR及其旗下負責監管全國研究運算基礎設施的國家整合網路基礎設施系統（NICIS），研究人員的運算需求已超出Lengau所能有效承擔的範疇。

  ## 替換系統與資金規劃

  NICIS已接收一套效能達四petaflop的替換系統，運算能力為Lengau原始規格的四倍。新系統將分階段上線，以避免影響目前仍在Lengau上執行的研究工作。第一階段預計於2026年11月底前完成，全面部署目標則訂於2027年3月。

  NICIS在2026/27財政年度獲撥款2億9,200萬南非蘭特（約合1,820萬美元）。此預算涵蓋人力資源、治理與管理、營運、研究與人才培育，以及科技、設備與建設基礎設施，新超級電腦亦包含在內。然而，其中專門用於採購新機器的確切金額並未對外披露。

  「本部認識到，更新老舊的核心基礎設施——包括Lengau高效能運算系統——是維持南非科學競爭力、回應研究與創新社群日益增長需求的必要之舉，」恩齊曼德說。

  ## 對研究基礎設施安全的警示意義

  Lengau事件揭示了高效能運算系統在超過原廠支援週期後所累積的安全風險。用於敏感科學研究的系統——涵蓋氣候模型建立、醫療資料及天文學——往往成為加密貨幣挖礦等機會性攻擊的目標。此類攻擊雖不一定會竊取資料，卻會大量消耗運算資源、降低研究效能，並可能顯示攻擊者擁有比管理者最初所認知更深層的存取權限。此次入侵事件為管理老舊科學基礎設施的機構帶來壓力，促使其加速設備汰換週期，並強化事件升報機制。

  CSIR表示，將在過渡期間持續監控Lengau。預計至2027年3月，全面遷移至新的四petaflop系統的工作將告完成。
