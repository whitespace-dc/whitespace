  ## Framework demands management-level accountability for cyber resilience

  Japan's SCS evaluation system — a supply-chain cybersecurity assessment framework whose requirements are set by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) — requires companies to place incident-response oversight at the executive level, not solely within security departments, according to an analysis published by ITR analyst Yu Nakamura in ITmedia Enterprise.

  The framework uses NIST's definition of cyber resilience: the ability of systems that use or are supported by cyber resources to anticipate, withstand, recover from, and adapt to stress, attacks, and breaches. Although the word "resilience" does not appear in every incident-response section of the SCS criteria, Nakamura argues the framework is designed throughout to raise organizational resilience through improved incident-response and recovery capability.

  ## Requirement 7-1-1 and the recovery category

  The analysis highlights requirement No. 7-1-1, which falls under the broad classification "Recovery from Incidents," as the item most directly aimed at building corporate resilience. Multiple evaluation criteria within the framework embed incident-response obligations — including response to incidents and analysis of harmful events — that together signal an expectation of sustained improvement in organizational response capacity.

  ## Executive involvement made explicit at the ★4 level

  The framework's most explicit governance requirement appears at its ★4 rating tier, in evaluation criterion No. 1-4-1-1. That criterion requires a company's security department to conduct at least one annual cycle of reporting, approval, and internal sharing with the officer responsible for security oversight — covering multiple evaluation criteria, including those related to incident response.

  Nakamura reads this as a deliberate design choice: the framework positions incident-response resilience not as a standalone technical function but as something that must be linked to executive decision-making. A star-rating system is used to grade compliance levels, with ★4 representing a higher tier of maturity.

  ## IPA identifies three communication barriers

  Japan's IPA (Information Processing Promotion Agency, the government body responsible for IT promotion and security guidance) has published a document titled "Communications for Cyber Resilience" that addresses how security teams should engage management and other departments in both normal operations and during crises.

  Drawing on that document, the ITmedia analysis identifies three structural barriers that most often impede communication between security staff and executives:

  - **Knowledge gaps:** Differences in familiarity with security terminology and risk sensitivity between security specialists and business executives.
  - **Language differences:** Divergent definitions and connotations attached to shared words — the term "recovery," for example, may carry different implications for a security team than for a business unit.
  - **Values differences:** Competing priorities between information security and business continuity.

  ## Bridging the gap in normal operations

  The IPA document recommends two practices for building the executive relationships needed to satisfy the framework's reporting requirements before an incident occurs.

  The first is regular activity reporting: sharing SIRT (Security Incident Response Team) activities, industry attack trends, and case studies from other organizations with executives on a continuing basis, even when no incident is active. The document states that this ongoing information-sharing builds the trust that enables faster decision-making when a crisis does occur.

  The second is executive profiling: because executives have different concerns depending on their business backgrounds, security teams should learn each executive's area of focus through routine reporting, then tailor communications accordingly to support rapid decisions when needed.

  ## Significance for supply-chain security compliance

  The SCS framework's design — requiring annual executive review of incident-response criteria and signaling that technical controls alone are insufficient — places organizational communication structure on equal footing with technical safeguards as a compliance requirement. For companies seeking higher SCS ratings, satisfying criterion No. 1-4-1-1 will require building durable reporting relationships with security-responsible officers, not only maintaining technical controls.

  Nakamura concludes that the practical starting point for organizations is recognizing knowledge, language, and values differences as baseline assumptions, then institutionalizing regular activity reports and executive profiling as ongoing practices rather than one-off exercises ahead of an assessment cycle.

  ## 框架要求管理層對資安韌性承擔責任

  日本供應鏈資安評估制度SCS的要求由經濟產業省（METI）制定，根據IT研究機構ITR分析師中村勇於《ITmedia Enterprise》發表的分析報告，該框架明確要求企業將資安事件應變監督責任置於高層主管層級，而非僅由資安部門自行負責。

  SCS框架採用NIST對於資安韌性的定義：依賴或受資訊資源支撐的系統，能夠預測、承受、從壓力、攻擊及資安事件中復原，並加以調適的能力。儘管「韌性」一詞並未出現在SCS評估標準的每個事件應變章節中，中村認為，整套框架的設計宗旨在於透過強化事件應變與復原能力，全面提升組織韌性。

  ## 要求項目7-1-1與復原類別

  該分析特別點出編號7-1-1的要求項目，其所屬的大分類為「從事件中復原」，是整套框架中最直接以建立企業韌性為目標的條目。框架內多項評估標準均涵蓋事件應變義務，包括對事件的應處與對有害事件的分析，合計傳達出組織應持續提升應變能力的明確期待。

  ## ★4等級明確規範高層參與

  框架中對治理要求最明確的規定出現在★4評級，具體條目為評估基準編號1-4-1-1。該條目要求企業資安部門每年至少完成一次完整循環，包括向負責資安監督的高層主管進行報告、取得核准，並在內部進行資訊分享，涵蓋多項評估基準，其中包含與事件應變相關的條目。

  中村將此解讀為一項刻意的設計選擇：框架有意將事件應變韌性定位為必須與高層決策機制相連結的組織能力，而非獨立的技術職能。框架以星等系統評定合規程度，★4代表較高的成熟度等級。

  ## IPA點出三大溝通障礙

  日本資訊處理推進機構（IPA，為負責IT推廣與資安指引的政府機關）發布了一份題為「資安韌性溝通」的文件，探討資安團隊在日常營運及危機發生時，應如何與管理階層及其他部門進行有效溝通。

  《ITmedia》的分析引用該文件，歸納出三項最常阻礙資安人員與高層主管溝通的結構性障礙：

  - **知識落差：** 資安專業人員與業務主管之間，對資安術語的熟悉程度及風險敏感度存在明顯差異。
  - **語言差異：** 雙方對相同詞彙的定義與涵義解讀不同——例如「復原」一詞對資安團隊與業務單位可能意味著截然不同的事情。
  - **價值觀差異：** 資訊安全與業務持續運作之間存在優先順序上的競爭關係。

  ## 在日常營運中建立溝通橋梁

  IPA文件建議採取兩項做法，以便在事件發生之前，預先建立滿足框架報告要求所需的高層關係。

  第一項是定期活動報告：即便目前沒有任何資安事件，資安事件應變團隊（SIRT）仍應持續與高層分享團隊活動、產業攻擊趨勢及其他組織的案例研究。文件指出，這種持續的資訊分享能夠建立信任基礎，在危機真正發生時得以加快決策速度。

  第二項是高層輪廓建立：由於各位高層主管因業務背景不同而有各自的關注重點，資安團隊應透過例行報告了解每位主管的業務關切所在，進而在需要快速決策時，能夠針對個人提供量身定製的溝通內容。

  ## 對供應鏈資安合規的重要意義

  SCS框架的設計——要求每年進行一次高層主管對事件應變標準的審查，並明確傳達技術控制措施本身並不足夠——將組織溝通結構與技術防護措施並列為同等重要的合規要求。對於追求更高SCS評級的企業而言，要滿足評估基準1-4-1-1的要求，不能只靠維持技術控制措施，更須與負責資安監督的高層主管建立穩固的報告關係。

  中村在分析結尾指出，組織實務上的第一步，是將知識、語言與價值觀差異視為基本前提加以正視，再將定期活動報告與高層輪廓建立制度化，使其成為持續性的日常實踐，而非僅在評估週期前夕臨時進行的一次性作業。
