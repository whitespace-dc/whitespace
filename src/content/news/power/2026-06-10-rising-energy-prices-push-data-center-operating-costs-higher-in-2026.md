  ## Rising Energy Prices Push Data Center Operating Costs Higher in 2026

  Energy prices have surged by double digits since March amid Middle East turmoil, and electricity costs — the primary operating expense for data centers — are rising in tandem, according to Data Center Knowledge. U.S. electricity prices are projected to increase by more than 5% in 2026 even before accounting for any prolonged disruption to oil and gas shipments from the Middle East, with the actual figure potentially higher as global energy markets evolve through the rest of the year.

  The vast majority of electricity is still generated by burning fossil fuels, which means higher fuel prices feed directly into higher electricity bills for data center operators large and small.

  ## Four Pressure Points for Operators

  Data Center Knowledge identifies four areas where elevated fuel prices translate into concrete costs for data centers.

  The most immediate is operating expenditure. A 5%-plus increase in electricity prices represents a significant line-item growth for facilities that run power-intensive workloads around the clock. Sites running artificial intelligence and high-performance computing workloads, which carry outsized power densities, face greater absolute and percentage cost impact than smaller, less intensive deployments.

  Backup and resilience costs are also rising. Most data centers operate on-site backup generators — typically diesel-fueled, increasingly natural gas — to maintain uptime during grid outages. As fuel prices climb, so do the costs of fueling test runs, replenishing tanks, and securing longer-duration resilience. Facilities with frequent utility interruptions or stringent uptime commitments face the greatest exposure here.

  A third pressure point is grid interconnection. While higher fuel prices do not directly slow the process of connecting new builds to the grid, the same supply constraints that push prices higher can lead utilities to pace or re-sequence large new loads. In markets where available generation and transmission capacity are already stretched, that can translate into longer interconnection queues or phased power delivery for new data centers.

  Finally, more expensive electricity heightens public scrutiny. Skepticism about data centers in the United States is already broad-based, according to Data Center Knowledge, and steeper utility bills risk reinforcing perceptions that energy-hungry facilities are driving price increases for residential and commercial customers alike. That dynamic may produce more contentious community engagement and permitting processes in some locations.

  ## Why Impact Varies by Site

  Not all data centers are equally exposed. Facilities already online are insulated from interconnection delays that affect new projects. Operators with on-site renewable generation — solar, wind, or geothermal — or power purchase agreements are less exposed than those relying fully on spot electricity prices. Geography also plays a significant role: power prices vary markedly by region based on the local fuel mix, available generation capacity, and demand. Markets where capacity consistently exceeds load tend to see milder price increases than capacity-constrained markets, and areas less dependent on fossil fuels for electricity generation are less sensitive to swings in oil and gas prices.

  ## No Sign of Slowdown Yet

  Despite the cost pressures, there is little indication so far that higher fuel costs are slowing the data center industry's expansion. The pace of new data center development has remained consistent with the trajectory before the disruption of Middle East shipments tied to a war that began in late February 2026, and no major operator has publicly cited fuel costs as a reason to cut capacity or cancel expansions.

  Energy price shocks often play out with a lag, however. Many operators appear to be betting that fuel prices will ease if geopolitical tensions stabilize and power grids continue to add non-fossil generation capacity. If those assumptions prove wrong, Data Center Knowledge reports, strategic shifts — including changes to site selection, energy contracting, efficiency retrofits, and on-site generation — may follow months or even years down the road.

  ## 能源價格飆升，2026年資料中心營運成本大幅攀高

  據《Data Center Knowledge》報導，中東局勢動盪導致能源價格自三月以來出現雙位數漲幅，作為資料中心最主要營運支出的電費也隨之水漲船高。即便不計入中東油氣運輸遭受長期干擾的情境，美國電價預計在2026年仍將上漲逾5%；隨著全球能源市場在年內持續演變，實際漲幅有可能更高。

  目前絕大多數電力仍依賴化石燃料燃燒生產，這意味著燃料價格上漲將直接轉嫁為資料中心業者的電費負擔，無論規模大小皆難以倖免。

  ## 四大壓力來源

  《Data Center Knowledge》點出四個面向，說明燃料價格高漲如何化為資料中心的具體成本。

  最立即的衝擊在於**營運支出**。電價上漲逾5%，對於全天候運行高耗電工作負載的機房而言，是一筆不容忽視的成本增項。執行人工智慧（AI）與高效能運算（HPC）工作負載的站點，由於功率密度遠高於一般部署，所承受的絕對金額與百分比衝擊都更為顯著。

  **備援與韌性成本**同樣隨之攀升。大多數資料中心配備現地備用發電機——傳統上以柴油驅動，近年天然氣機組也日益普及——以維持電網中斷時的持續運作。隨著燃料價格上揚，定期測試運轉的燃料費、補充油箱的費用，以及爭取更長時效韌性保障的代價，也一併水漲船高。電網供電中斷頻繁、或對可用率要求嚴格的機房，在這一面向所承受的風險最大。

  第三個壓力點來自**電網併網**。儘管燃料價格上漲本身不會直接延緩新建案的電網連接流程，但造成價格飆升的同一批供應限制，可能促使電業公司放緩審查速度，或重新排序大型新增負載的接入順序。在現有發電與輸電容量已相當吃緊的市場，這可能意味著新建資料中心面臨更長的併網排隊等待，或分階段供電的安排。

  最後，電費上漲也加劇了**公眾的監督壓力**。《Data Center Knowledge》指出，美國社會對資料中心的疑慮原本就相當普遍，而電費持續攀升，恐將強化外界認為這些高耗能設施正在推高一般住宅與商業用戶電費的觀感。這種氛圍，可能使部分地區的社區溝通與申請許可程序變得更加棘手。

  ## 衝擊程度因站點而異

  並非所有資料中心都面臨相同程度的風險。已上線運營的機房不受新建案所遭遇的併網延誤影響。擁有現地再生能源發電設施——包括太陽能、風力或地熱——或已簽訂購電協議（PPA）的業者，比起完全仰賴即期電力市場採購的業者，所受衝擊相對較小。地理位置同樣舉足輕重：各地電價因當地燃料組合、可用發電容量與用電需求而有顯著差異。供電容量長期充裕的市場，漲幅往往較容量緊張的市場溫和；化石燃料在發電結構中佔比較低的地區，對油氣價格波動的敏感度也相對較低。

  ## 擴張步伐尚未出現放緩跡象

  儘管成本壓力持續累積，目前幾乎看不出燃料漲價正在拖慢資料中心產業擴張的跡象。新建資料中心的開發速度，與2026年二月下旬中東戰事爆發、進而波及油氣船運之前的軌跡相比，仍維持一致；也尚未有任何大型業者公開表示要以燃料成本為由，削減既有容量或取消擴建計畫。

  然而，能源價格衝擊往往存在時間遞延效應。許多業者看來正在押注：一旦地緣政治緊張態勢趨於穩定，加上電網持續增加非化石燃料發電容量，燃料價格終將回落。《Data Center Knowledge》報導指出，若上述假設最終證明有誤，包括選址策略調整、能源採購合約重組、效能改造提升，以及現地發電布建在內的策略轉向，或許將在數個月乃至數年後陸續出現。
