body_en: |
  Indonesia's government launched a three-year program in August 2026 to build 100 gigawatts-peak (GWp) of solar power capacity, a target Energy Minister Bahlil Lahadalia says requires roughly $73 billion in investment and that researchers warn faces structural barriers rooted in fossil-fuel lock-in and grid unreadiness.

  GWp, or gigawatts-peak, measures the maximum output of solar panels under optimal conditions, not the sustained supply a plant can deliver to the grid continuously.

  The program was formally launched on August 25, 2026, initially covering 14 projects with a combined announced capacity of approximately 5.3 GWp. That figure represents announced plans rather than projects already in commercial operation, according to the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM).

  ESDM projects the full 100 GWp build-out would reduce annual diesel consumption by up to 6 million kiloliters and cut carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions by approximately 140.16 million tons per year. The government says the program is intended to strengthen national energy security and reduce dependence on fuel oil, particularly diesel.

  ## Grid and investment challenges

  ESDM identified four structural challenges to the program: continued dependence on fossil-fuel power plants, transmission grid readiness, the volume of investment required, and project governance.

  ## Fossil-fuel lock-in

  A joint report published in July 2026 by the Institute for Economic and Social Research of the Faculty of Economics and Business at the University of Indonesia (LPEM FEB UI) and Greenpeace described what it termed fossil-fuel lock-in as a core constraint on Indonesia's power system. The report, titled "Fossil Fuel Lock-in and the 100 GW Solar Imperative: Structural Constraints and Reform Pathways for Indonesia's Energy Transition," attributed the condition to existing infrastructure, energy-pricing policy, and long-term power-plant contracts.

  Between 2015 and 2024, renewable energy capacity connected to the grid of PLN, Indonesia's state electricity company, grew by 4.4 GW. Over the same period, fossil-fuel generation capacity on the PLN system expanded by 25.9 GW. Those figures cover the PLN system only, not Indonesia's full national generation fleet.

  ## Planning documents

  The imbalance carries into Indonesia's current electricity-sector roadmap. LPEM FEB UI researcher Roes Regi Lutfi said the 100 GWp solar program risks running into obstacles because carbon lock-in remains reflected in electricity-sector planning documents. The National Electricity Supply Business Plan (RUPTL) for 2025-2034, the government's ten-year electricity plan, still envisions adding 10.3 GW of gas-fired and 6.3 GW of coal-fired generation capacity alongside the solar buildout.
body_zh: |
  印尼政府於2026年8月啟動一項為期三年的計畫，目標建置100吉瓦峰值（GWp）太陽能發電裝置容量。能源部長巴赫利爾·拉哈達利亞（Bahlil Lahadalia）表示，此計畫約需730億美元的投資，但研究人員警告，化石燃料鎖定與電網整備不足等結構性障礙，恐將阻礙整體建設進程。

  GWp即吉瓦峰值，衡量的是太陽能板在最佳條件下的最大輸出功率，而非發電廠能持續穩定供應電網的電力量。

  該計畫正式於2026年8月25日啟動，初期涵蓋14個專案，合計公告裝置容量約為5.3 GWp。印尼能源暨礦產資源部（ESDM）說明，上述數字代表的是已公告的計畫，而非已進入商業運轉的專案。

  ESDM預估，全面完成100 GWp建置後，每年最多可減少600萬公秉的柴油消耗，並削減約1億4,016萬公噸的二氧化碳（CO₂）年排放量。政府表示，此計畫旨在強化國家能源安全，降低對燃油——尤其是柴油——的依賴。

  ## 電網與投資挑戰

  ESDM指出該計畫面臨四大結構性挑戰：對化石燃料電廠的持續依賴、輸電電網的整備程度、所需的龐大投資規模，以及專案治理問題。

  ## 化石燃料鎖定

  2026年7月，印尼大學經濟商業學院經濟社會研究所（LPEM FEB UI）與綠色和平共同發布一份報告，將「化石燃料鎖定」定義為印尼電力系統的核心制約因素。該報告題為《化石燃料鎖定與100吉瓦太陽能的當務之急：印尼能源轉型的結構性制約與改革路徑》，並將此一困境歸因於既有基礎設施、能源定價政策及長期電廠合約。

  2015年至2024年間，印尼國家電力公司（PLN）電網中的再生能源裝置容量增加了4.4吉瓦（GW）；然而同一時期，PLN系統內的化石燃料發電裝置容量卻擴增了25.9 GW。上述數字僅涵蓋PLN系統，並未涵括印尼全國的完整發電機組。

  ## 規劃文件

  這樣的失衡也延伸至印尼現行的電力部門路線圖。LPEM FEB UI研究員羅斯·雷吉·盧特菲（Roes Regi Lutfi）指出，100 GWp太陽能計畫恐將遭遇阻礙，原因在於碳鎖定問題仍反映在電力部門的規劃文件之中。政府的十年電力計畫——《2025至2034年國家電力供應業務計畫》（RUPTL）——在推動太陽能建設的同時，仍規劃新增10.3 GW的燃氣發電裝置容量及6.3 GW的燃煤發電裝置容量。
