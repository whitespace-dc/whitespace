body_en: |
  ## Equipment failing ahead of schedule

  Rapid swings in power demand at AI data centers are cracking gas turbines, burning out batteries within weeks, and wearing down cooling systems far sooner than engineers designed for, according to interviews with more than three dozen power experts in the United States and Europe conducted by Bloomberg. The pattern is global, appearing in data centers across the Middle East, Africa, Europe, and North America, and is already reducing uptime and delaying project timelines.

  At xAI's Colossus computing facility in Memphis, Tennessee, gas-fired turbines developed cracks, according to one person familiar with the situation. Batteries were subsequently installed at the site to help smooth out power swings and reduce mechanical strain on the spinning turbines. SpaceX, the parent company of xAI, did not respond to requests for comment.

  Turbines have also cracked at smaller data centers in the United Kingdom, said Andrew Cunningham, chief executive of GeoPura Ltd, a company that supplies hydrogen for fuel cells that stabilize power flows at some of those sites.

  Batteries installed specifically to buffer power fluctuations have in some cases needed replacement within months or even weeks, according to the Uptime Institute, a standards and reliability advisory body, and other people working with operators.

  ## What drives the volatility

  AI computing facilities differ fundamentally from conventional data centers in the size and speed of their power swings. When training new machine-learning models, a process that mobilizes all available graphics processing units (GPUs) simultaneously, hundreds of thousands of processors can power up and down on a millisecond timescale.

  The result is demand shifts equivalent to the electricity consumption of factories, towns, or cities appearing and disappearing within seconds. Drew Baglino, a former Tesla executive who founded Heron Power Electronics Co., said AI facilities at times see power usage spike as much as 50 percent above their design capacity. "A 1 gigawatt facility may use 1.5 gigawatts for a split second," he said. Heron Power Electronics is developing equipment to manage power fluctuations for Nvidia's next-generation servers, which are due in 2027.

  A one-gigawatt data center draws roughly as much power as a city the size of Boston, according to Shannon Miller, founder and president of Mainspring Energy Inc., which works on micro-grid projects for industrial and data-center customers. Some AI campuses planned in Texas and the Midwest are more than five times that size, consuming nearly as much power on average as New York City, Miller said.

  "AI does create very unusual power demand," said Amber Villegas-Williamson, principal consultant at the Uptime Institute. "It's like over-revving your car wears out the engine faster than keeping a constant speed."

  Jon Parrella, chief executive of energy-storage developer Terraflow Energy, compared the stress to driving a performance car and shifting directly from sixth gear to first. "You can't swing that fast," he said.

  Most equipment was not engineered for swings of this magnitude or speed. A suite of technologies including batteries, capacitors, transformers, and flywheels can stabilize power flows, but in the race to bring AI computing capacity online quickly, not enough of these systems are being deployed at new data centers, multiple experts said.

  ## Reliability and revenue consequences

  Equipment degradation is translating directly into lost uptime. Data centers are built on the assumption they will operate continuously around the clock, 365 days a year. In practice, some AI facilities are achieving uptime closer to 80 percent, according to a person involved in financing such facilities. Unless the problem is addressed, this shortfall could begin affecting investors in certain projects within the next 12 to 24 months, that person said.

  The financial exposure is concentrated not in replacement hardware costs but in lost revenue from idle compute capacity. "The financial consequence is not primarily replacing a pump or a breaker or some power component — it's the value of that expensive compute capacity not generating revenue because it's offline," said Jason Hoffman, chief strategy officer at data-center builder and operator Switch. Industry estimates for the cost of unplanned downtime range from thousands to hundreds of thousands of dollars per minute depending on facility type and workload.

  Cracks or accelerated wear in power equipment can also trigger electrical arc flashes, where current jumps between conductors, potentially damaging the AI chips the facilities exist to run, said Jennifer Scanlon, chief executive of UL Solutions Inc., which tests and certifies new technology.

  To achieve the 99.999 percent reliability required by Microsoft Corp at a planned 2.67-gigawatt AI campus in West Texas — a facility large enough that its power draw would rank among the largest single loads on any regional grid — developer Joulent Inc and energy partner Chevron Corp built additional engineering time into the project schedule. Power delivery at that site will now begin in 2028 rather than 2027, said Chris James, chief executive of Joulent.

  ## Grid stability concerns

  The volatility extends beyond individual facilities. AI data centers' ability to add or remove demand equivalent to entire cities within seconds places new strain on power grids that were calibrated for much slower fluctuations.

  Of particular concern to regulators and utilities is the risk of sub-synchronous oscillations — a technical phenomenon in which destabilizing electrical waves propagate through the high-voltage transmission network, potentially damaging equipment connected to entirely different parts of the grid. "These loads are extremely dynamic or fluctuating, which causes grid instability and can lead to, if not corrected, potential blackouts or power outages," said Sreemant Roy, a power-quality expert and global offer manager at Schneider Electric in Nashville. "That has made utility companies globally very worried," Roy added.

  Within the past two years, the North American Electric Reliability Corp (NERC), the top U.S. regulatory body for grid reliability standards, has repeatedly warned that data centers represent one of the greatest risks to grid stability. NERC evaluated more than 33 gigawatts of operational data centers in the United States as part of that assessment.

  ## Investment backdrop

  The equipment reliability problems emerge alongside existing investor concern about the financial durability of the AI infrastructure build-out. Questions have already circulated about the depreciation rate of GPU racks — the computing hardware at the heart of AI data centers — and whether the industry can deliver the returns it has projected against hundreds of billions of dollars in planned spending by major technology companies.

  Premature degradation of power and cooling infrastructure adds a further variable to those calculations. Heron Power Electronics is targeting Nvidia's next-generation server platform, due in 2027, with equipment specifically designed to absorb the power fluctuations that current infrastructure was not built to handle.

  ## 設備提前損壞

  根據彭博社對美國及歐洲逾三十名電力專家的訪談，AI資料中心的電力需求急速震盪，正造成燃氣渦輪機龜裂、電池在數週內燒毀，並使冷卻系統的損耗速度遠早於工程師的設計預期。這個現象已遍及全球，在中東、非洲、歐洲及北美的資料中心均有出現，且已開始拖低運行率、延誤工程時程。

  xAI位於田納西州曼菲斯市的Colossus運算設施，其燃氣渦輪機已出現龜裂，據一名熟悉情況的消息人士透露。此後，該廠址陸續安裝了電池，以協助平滑電力震盪、降低旋轉渦輪機所承受的機械應力。xAI的母公司SpaceX未就媒體的置評請求作出回應。

  GeoPura有限公司執行長安德魯‧康寧漢（Andrew Cunningham）表示，英國規模較小的資料中心也出現了渦輪機龜裂的情況。GeoPura是一家供應氫氣給燃料電池的公司，其燃料電池被部署於部分廠址，用以穩定電力流動。

  根據標準與可靠性顧問機構Uptime Institute及其他與業者合作的相關人士表示，部分專門為緩衝電力波動而安裝的電池，在某些情況下數個月甚至數週內便需要更換。

  ## 震盪的成因

  AI運算設施在電力震盪的幅度與速度上，與傳統資料中心有著根本性的差異。在訓練新的機器學習模型時，這個過程會同時動員所有可用的繪圖處理器（GPU），數十萬顆處理器可在毫秒的時間尺度內快速啟停。

  其結果是，相當於工廠、城鎮或整座城市用電量的需求，在數秒內驟然浮現又消失。曾任特斯拉（Tesla）高階主管、後來創辦Heron Power Electronics公司的德魯‧巴格里諾（Drew Baglino）表示，AI設施的用電量有時會飆升至設計容量的50%以上。「一座1吉瓦的設施，可能在瞬間使用1.5吉瓦，」他說。Heron Power Electronics目前正在為輝達（NVIDIA）預計於2027年推出的新一代伺服器，開發管理電力波動的設備。

  據Mainspring Energy公司創辦人暨總裁夏農‧米勒（Shannon Miller）表示，一座1吉瓦的資料中心所消耗的電力，大約相當於波士頓這樣規模的城市。Mainspring Energy專門為工業及資料中心客戶承接微電網專案。米勒指出，德州及美國中西部規劃中的部分AI園區，規模超過前者的五倍，平均用電量幾乎與紐約市相當。

  「AI確實製造出非常不尋常的電力需求，」Uptime Institute首席顧問安柏‧維萊加斯-威廉森（Amber Villegas-Williamson）說。「就像把車子的引擎轉速拉得過高，比保持定速行駛更快磨損引擎。」

  儲能開發商Terraflow Energy的執行長強‧帕雷拉（Jon Parrella）將這種應力比喻為駕駛跑車時直接從六檔換入一檔。「你不可能換得那麼快，」他說。

  大多數設備在設計時，並未考量如此幅度或速度的震盪。電池、電容器、變壓器及飛輪等一系列技術可以穩定電力流動，但多名專家表示，在搶先將AI運算產能上線的競賽中，新建資料中心部署上述系統的數量遠遠不足。

  ## 可靠性與營收衝擊

  設備損耗正直接轉化為運行率的損失。資料中心在建設時，是以一年365天、全天候不間斷運行為前提。然而，據一名參與此類設施融資的人士表示，部分AI設施實際達到的運行率僅約80%。該人士指出，若問題無法解決，某些專案的投資人在未來12至24個月內便可能開始受到衝擊。

  財務風險的核心不在於更換硬體的費用，而在於閒置運算產能所造成的收入損失。「財務後果主要不是更換一個幫浦、一個斷路器或某個電力元件，而是那些昂貴的運算產能因為下線而無法創造收入的價值，」資料中心建設及營運商Switch的首席策略長傑森‧霍夫曼（Jason Hoffman）說。業界估計，視設施類型及工作負載而定，非計畫性停機的代價從每分鐘數千美元到數十萬美元不等。

  UL Solutions公司執行長珍妮佛‧史坎倫（Jennifer Scanlon）表示，電力設備的龜裂或加速磨損，也可能引發電弧閃絡——即電流在導體之間跳躍的現象——進而損壞資料中心賴以運作的AI晶片。UL Solutions專門從事新技術的測試與認證。

  為了達到微軟（Microsoft）在德州西部規劃中的2.67吉瓦AI園區所要求的99.999%可靠性——這座設施的用電量之大，足以躋身任何區域電網中單一最大負載之列——開發商Joulent公司與能源合作夥伴雪佛龍（Chevron）在工程排程中加入了額外的時間緩衝。Joulent執行長克里斯‧詹姆斯（Chris James）表示，該廠址的供電時程已從原定的2027年延後至2028年啟動。

  ## 電網穩定性疑慮

  這種波動性已超出個別設施的範疇。AI資料中心能在數秒內增減相當於整座城市的用電需求，這對原本只針對緩慢波動而校準的電網施加了全新的壓力。

  監管機構和電力公司尤其擔憂的是次同步振盪的風險——這是一種技術性現象，不穩定的電磁波在高壓輸電網路中傳播，可能損壞連接至電網完全不同位置的設備。「這些負載極度動態或震盪，導致電網不穩定，若不加以修正，可能引發停電或斷電，」施耐德電氣（Schneider Electric）駐納許維爾的電能品質專家暨全球業務總監斯里曼特‧羅伊（Sreemant Roy）說。「這讓全球各地的電力公司都非常憂慮，」羅伊補充道。

  過去兩年來，美國電網可靠性標準的最高監管機構——北美電力可靠性公司（NERC）——已多次警告，資料中心是電網穩定性面臨的最大風險之一。NERC在其評估中，對美國境內逾33吉瓦的運營資料中心進行了審查。

  ## 投資背景

  設備可靠性問題的浮現，伴隨著投資人對AI基礎設施建設財務持久性的既有疑慮。外界早已對GPU機架——AI資料中心核心運算硬體——的折舊速度，以及該產業能否在各大科技公司數千億美元計畫支出的背景下兌現所預期的回報，提出了諸多質疑。

  電力及冷卻基礎設施的提前損耗，為這些計算增添了更多變數。Heron Power Electronics正鎖定輝達（NVIDIA）預計於2027年推出的新一代伺服器平台，開發專門設計用於吸收現有基礎設施無力應對之電力波動的設備。
