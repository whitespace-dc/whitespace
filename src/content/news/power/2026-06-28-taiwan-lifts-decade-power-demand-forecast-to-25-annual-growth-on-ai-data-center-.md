body_en: |
  Taiwan has raised its official 10-year electricity demand growth forecast to 2.5% annually for 2026 through 2035, up from a prior estimate of 1.7%, as AI data center expansion adds a structural layer to the island's power planning that the government says has no precedent in past forecasts.

  The revised projection appears in Taiwan's newly published National Electricity Resources Supply and Demand Report, which for the first time explicitly incorporates AI data center power requirements into its demand modelling, according to Liu Ming-huan, Director of the Emerging Markets Research Center at the Taiwan Institute of Economic Research (TIER). The 0.8 percentage point increase in the projected growth rate is "almost entirely driven by AI-related demand," Liu said. The revised rate exceeds projected demand growth in both Japan and South Korea.

  Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs estimates that by 2030 incremental electricity demand will exceed 5 gigawatts (GW), approximately one-eighth of the island's current national peak consumption, making AI a variable affecting energy policy, grid investment, and capital market valuations far beyond the technology sector alone.

  ## From compute chain to power chain

  Liu said investors and planners who have tracked the AI buildout through chips, servers, cooling, and high-speed data transmission are being forced to broaden that view. "When AI data centers accelerate their deployment, investors viewing the AI supply chain will expand their focus from the 'compute chain' to the 'power chain,'" he said. The AI race, he argued, is shifting from GPUs (graphics processing units used to run AI workloads) to megawatts (MW), a measure of electricity generation and delivery capacity.

  AI data centers differ from the earlier generation of facilities focused on data storage and retrieval, Liu said. Current AI facilities concentrate on compute and inference workloads, placing higher and more continuous demands on power. What AI infrastructure requires, Liu said, is not only computing capacity but a full energy system encompassing generation, transmission, distribution, and storage.

  Liu pointed to Google's longstanding data center presence at Taiwan's Changbin site as an example of the island's prior large-scale data center experience, but said the current AI wave is categorically different in power intensity and character.

  ## Grid resilience over generation volume

  The government and industry challenge has shifted from simply expanding total generation capacity to strengthening the stability and resilience of the transmission and distribution network, according to Liu.

  Power planning at this scale is inherently long-lead: building a power plant takes six to eight years, Liu said, which means all planning must consistently run ahead of actual demand. Taipower (Taiwan Power Company) and similar entities conducting demand forecasting coordinate not only with macroeconomic models but also directly with large manufacturers to track their expansion and development plans over the coming years, he said.

  ## Two demand drivers

  The National Electricity Resources Supply and Demand Report identifies two distinct forces that will drive future electricity growth. The first is the construction and operation of data centers themselves. The second is the inference and compute demand generated as end applications expand into broader use, including agentic AI (AI systems capable of autonomous, multi-step tasks), edge AI, humanoid robots, and other smart devices.

  ## Macro and market implications

  Liu said AI's power infrastructure requirements have pushed the technology beyond a sector-specific investment story into a macroeconomic variable. New U.S. Federal Reserve Chair Walsh, who maintained rates unchanged at the June policy meeting, has historically viewed AI as a significant productivity driver while opposing prolonged quantitative easing on the grounds that rising asset prices can generate inflationary pressure, Liu noted. If data centers drive electricity prices higher and raise grid and equipment costs, they could create new cost pressures in the short to medium term, Liu said, meaning AI has moved from a technology topic to a productivity and inflation question the Fed must monitor.

  Taiwan's equity market fell 3.64% on June 26, with large-cap electronics stocks leading the decline on market concerns that high costs are beginning to weigh on AI hardware demand.

  With incremental demand projected to exceed 5 GW by 2030 and annual growth through 2035 that the government says will outpace Japan and South Korea, ensuring that energy and data center construction do not become bottlenecks is a prerequisite for AI's productivity gains to materialize, Liu said.

  ## AI資料中心浪潮推升電力需求　台灣上調十年成長預測至年增2.5%

  台灣已將2026至2035年官方十年電力需求年成長率預測從原先的1.7%上調至2.5%，原因是AI資料中心的快速擴張為台灣的電力規劃帶來前所未有的結構性新變數。

  此次修訂預測出現在台灣最新公布的《全國電力資源供需報告》中。台灣經濟研究院新興市場研究中心主任劉明環表示，這份報告首度將AI資料中心的用電需求明確納入需求模型。他指出，預測成長率上調的0.8個百分點「幾乎完全由AI相關需求所驅動」。修訂後的成長率更已超越日本與南韓的需求成長預測。

  台灣經濟部估計，至2030年新增電力需求將超過5GW（百萬瓩），約相當於目前全國尖峰用電量的八分之一，使AI成為影響能源政策、電網投資乃至資本市場估值的重要變數，其衝擊範圍遠超科技產業本身。

  ## 從算力鏈到電力鏈

  劉明環表示，長期追蹤AI建設浪潮的投資人與規劃者，過去關注的焦點集中在晶片、伺服器、散熱及高速資料傳輸，如今必須擴大視野。他說：「當AI資料中心加速建置，觀察AI供應鏈的投資人將把目光從『算力鏈』延伸至『電力鏈』。」他進一步指出，AI競賽的核心正從GPU（用於執行AI工作負載的繪圖處理器）轉移至MW（百萬瓦，電力發電與輸送容量的計量單位）。

  劉明環說，AI資料中心與早期以資料儲存與檢索為主的設施截然不同。現今的AI設施聚焦於運算與推論工作負載，對電力的需求更高、更持續。他表示，AI基礎設施所需的不僅是運算容量，而是涵蓋發電、輸電、配電與儲能的完整能源系統。

  劉明環以Google長期在台灣彰濱設置資料中心為例，說明台灣過去已有大型資料中心的建設經驗，但他強調，當前的AI浪潮在用電強度與特性上都屬於截然不同的等級。

  ## 電網韌性優先於裝置容量

  劉明環指出，政府與產業面臨的挑戰，已從單純擴增總發電容量，轉向強化輸配電網路的穩定性與韌性。

  這種規模的電力規劃本身就是長前置期的工程：劉明環說，興建一座電廠需要六至八年，這意味著所有規劃都必須持續超前於實際需求。台電（台灣電力公司）等負責需求預測的機構，不僅須配合總體經濟模型，也會直接與大型製造商協調，掌握其未來數年的擴產與發展計畫。

  ## 兩大需求驅動力

  《全國電力資源供需報告》點名兩股截然不同的力量將推動未來電力成長。第一是資料中心本身的建設與營運需求；第二是隨著終端應用日趨普及所衍生的推論與運算需求，涵蓋自主AI（能夠執行多步驟自主任務的AI系統）、邊緣AI、人形機器人及其他智慧裝置等領域。

  ## 總體經濟與市場影響

  劉明環表示，AI的電力基礎設施需求，已使這項技術從單一產業的投資議題升格為總體經濟變數。他提及，美國聯準會新任主席Walsh在6月決策會議上維持利率不變，Walsh過去一貫將AI視為重要的生產力驅動力，同時反對長期量化寬鬆，理由是資產價格上漲可能產生通膨壓力。劉明環說，若資料中心推升電價並墊高電網與設備成本，短中期內可能形成新的成本壓力，這意味著AI已從科技議題演變為聯準會必須關注的生產力與通膨課題。

  台灣股市於6月26日下跌3.64%，大型電子股領跌，市場擔憂高成本正開始壓抑AI硬體需求。

  劉明環強調，在新增需求預計於2030年前突破5GW、2035年前年成長率預計超越日本與南韓的背景下，確保能源供應與資料中心建設不成為瓶頸，是AI生產力紅利得以實現的先決條件。
