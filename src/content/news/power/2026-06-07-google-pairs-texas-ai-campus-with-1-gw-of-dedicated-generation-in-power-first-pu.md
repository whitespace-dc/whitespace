body_en: |
  Google is building its Meitner artificial intelligence campus in Texas alongside more than 1 gigawatt (GW) — roughly the output of a large nuclear reactor — of dedicated wind, solar, and battery storage generation, testing a design philosophy the company and energy developer Intersect Power call "power first," which prioritizes developing generation capacity in parallel with computing demand rather than connecting large new loads to existing grids and securing power afterward.

  The strategy, reported by Data Center Knowledge on June 5, 2026, reflects intensifying competition among hyperscalers — large-scale cloud and AI infrastructure operators — for access to electricity as grid constraints increasingly determine where new data center capacity can be built.

  ## What the power-first model involves

  Under the power-first approach, Google and Intersect co-locate generation assets directly with a data center facility. At Meitner, that means more than 1 GW of wind, solar, and battery storage systems built alongside the AI campus. Most of the site's electricity is expected to come from those clean energy resources, the companies said, with on-site gas generation providing backup to support reliability.

  The model is designed to reduce the data center's dependence on additional supply drawn from the local transmission grid. Utilities and grid operators across the United States are struggling to meet the unprecedented electricity demand driven by AI infrastructure, according to the article. Transmission upgrades typically take years to complete, and new generation projects face significant permitting and interconnection challenges. In many regions, power availability has become a decisive factor in where and when hyperscalers can expand their operations.

  ## Haskell County sets the precedent

  Meitner is not Google's first power-first project. Google and Intersect previously announced a joint development in Haskell County, Texas, where a Google data center is under construction alongside Intersect's Quantum Clean Energy Project: a 640-megawatt (MW) solar facility paired with 1.3 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of battery storage — enough stored energy to supply a medium-sized city for several hours. Quantum is scheduled to begin operations this month, with the associated Google data center already underway.

  Both Texas projects follow Google's finalization of its acquisition of Intersect in December 2025. Intersect specializes in pairing large industrial energy loads with dedicated renewable and gas generation assets. The Haskell County development came first, followed by the acquisition, and Meitner represents the third step in what the article describes as a shared ambition to transform how hyperscalers secure and deploy power for next-generation computing infrastructure.

  ## Grid operator responds with new framework

  The Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), which operates the state's main power grid, declined to comment specifically on the Meitner project. The grid operator did highlight a series of large-load interconnection reforms and planning initiatives aimed at addressing the growing demand from projects that combine major electricity loads with dedicated generation resources.

  Earlier this month, the ERCOT board approved its Batch Zero framework, a centralized mechanism to streamline transmission capacity allocation for large-load projects. Even facilities built with dedicated generation remain connected to broader transmission networks and regional reliability systems, meaning ERCOT's interconnection rules continue to apply to power-first sites.

  ## Industry appeal and open questions

  Rob Gramlich, president of Grid Strategies, told Data Center Knowledge that site constraints limit where the model can work but also drive its appeal to those who can access suitable locations. "It is hard to find places where renewables can provide a lot of the co-located power, so this type of project could be really attractive to others," Gramlich said.

  Whether the power-first model achieves widespread adoption remains an open question, according to the article. The Quantum Clean Energy Project in Haskell County is scheduled to come online in June 2026, with the Meitner site representing the next phase of a strategy Google began formalizing through its Intersect acquisition.

  ## Google德州AI園區搭配逾1GW專屬電力，力推「電力優先」策略

  Google正在德州興建Meitner人工智慧園區，並在旁邊同步建置逾1吉瓦（GW）的風電、太陽能與電池儲能發電設施——其規模相當於一座大型核能電廠的輸出功率。Google與能源開發商Intersect Power將這套設計理念稱為「電力優先」（power first），核心概念是與算力需求並行開發發電容量，而非先將大規模新負載接入既有電網、事後再設法取得電力授權。

  此一策略由《Data Center Knowledge》於2026年6月5日率先報導，折射出各大超大規模業者（hyperscaler，即大型雲端與AI基礎設施營運商）在搶奪電力資源方面的競爭日趨白熱化，電網限制也愈來愈成為決定新建資料中心能否落地的關鍵因素。

  ## 「電力優先」模式的運作方式

  在電力優先模式下，Google與Intersect將發電資產直接設於資料中心設施旁。在Meitner園區，這意味著逾1GW的風電、太陽能與電池儲能系統將與AI園區比鄰而建。兩家公司表示，園區大部分用電預計將來自這些潔淨能源，並輔以現場天然氣發電機組作為備援，以確保供電可靠性。

  此模式旨在降低資料中心對地方輸電網路額外供電的依賴。報導指出，美國各地的電力公司與電網營運商正疲於應對AI基礎設施帶動的空前電力需求。輸電線路升級往往需要數年才能竣工，新建發電專案又面臨繁瑣的許可審查與併網挑戰。在許多地區，電力供應能否到位，已成為超大規模業者決定擴張時機與地點的決定性因素。

  ## 哈斯克爾縣率先樹立先例

  Meitner並非Google首個電力優先專案。Google與Intersect此前已宣布在德州哈斯克爾縣（Haskell County）共同開發一處設施，Google資料中心正在當地興建，旁邊則是Intersect的Quantum潔淨能源專案——一座640百萬瓦（MW）的太陽能發電廠，搭配1.3吉瓦時（GWh）的電池儲能系統，儲電量足以供應一座中型城市數小時所需。Quantum預計本月正式投入運營，與之配套的Google資料中心已動工在即。

  上述兩個德州專案，均發生在Google於2025年12月完成收購Intersect之後。Intersect專精於將大型工業用電負載與專屬再生能源及天然氣發電資產配對整合。哈斯克爾縣的開發案先行，隨後完成收購，Meitner則代表第三步——報導將其描述為雙方共同的遠大抱負：從根本上改變超大規模業者為下一代運算基礎設施取得和部署電力的方式。

  ## 電網營運商推出新框架因應

  負責營運德州主要電網的「德州電力可靠度委員會」（ERCOT）婉拒就Meitner專案發表具體評論，但強調已推出一系列大型負載併網改革措施與規劃方案，以因應結合主要用電負載與專屬發電資源的專案日益增加的需求。

  本月稍早，ERCOT董事會批准了「第零批次」（Batch Zero）框架——一套集中化機制，旨在為大型負載專案簡化輸電容量的分配流程。即便是建有專屬發電設施的園區，仍與更廣泛的輸電網路及區域可靠度系統相連，因此ERCOT的併網規範依舊適用於採行電力優先策略的場址。

  ## 業界關注與尚待解答的問題

  Grid Strategies總裁羅布．格拉姆利奇（Rob Gramlich）向《Data Center Knowledge》表示，場址條件的限制雖然縮小了此模式的適用範圍，但也正因如此，對於能取得合適地點的業者而言，吸引力反而更強。「能讓再生能源提供大量共址電力的地點並不好找，因此這類專案對其他業者來說可能極具吸引力，」他說。

  電力優先模式能否獲得廣泛採用，目前仍是未解之題。哈斯克爾縣的Quantum潔淨能源專案預計於2026年6月上線，Meitner園區則代表Google透過收購Intersect逐步成形的這套策略的下一個發展階段。
