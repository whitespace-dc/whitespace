body_en: |
  Southeast Asian nations signed an enhanced ASEAN Power Grid (APG) agreement on Nov. 16 that expands a nearly two-decade-old framework to support cross-border electricity trading across the 10-member bloc, as the Philippines concluded its year as ASEAN chair.

  The new agreement updates the original APG Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) signed in 2007, extending its scope to cover multilateral electricity trading and regional market integration. The APG aims to physically connect the national electricity networks of all 10 ASEAN member states, with the program targeted for completion by 2045.

  The World Bank Group estimates that achieving full interconnection over the next 20 years will require approximately $800 billion in generation and transmission investments.

  ## What the agreement covers

  The enhanced MoU provides for cooperation across four areas: infrastructure planning, institutional arrangements, policy and regulatory development, and access to financing. That includes funding mechanisms for overland transmission lines and subsea power cables linking national grids.

  The agreement encourages pooling of resources among governments, private sector participants, and financial institutions, and is designed to facilitate funding for studies, construction, operation, and further development of the grid.

  Participation in the regional electricity market integration component will remain voluntary. Each country's involvement will be subject to its own national policies, laws, and regulations, according to the agreement's terms.

  ## Potential benefits cited

  The Philippine Department of Energy said the APG can help diversify power resources, improve power system resilience, and support the integration of renewable energy into national grids.

  ASEAN Secretary-General Kao Kim Hourn linked the agreement to the bloc's longer-term planning framework. "As we advance towards ASEAN Community Vision 2045, we welcome our partners and investors to translate this commitment into projects that deliver reliable, affordable and sustainable electricity for our peoples," he said.

  ## Philippine chairmanship closes

  The APG was identified as a key energy priority of the Philippine ASEAN chairmanship in 2026. Philippine Energy Secretary Sharon S. Garin said the country looks forward to advancing cross-border electricity trade and attracting investment in the grid under continued cooperation with member states and partners.

  The Philippines will formally transfer the ASEAN chairmanship to Singapore on Nov. 17. Singapore will lead the regional bloc through 2027.
body_zh: |
  東南亞十國於11月16日簽署強化版東協電網（APG）協議，就在菲律賓完成東協輪值主席任期之際。此一協議更新了2007年簽訂的原版合作備忘錄，將其範疇擴大至涵蓋多邊電力交易與區域市場整合，目標是將東協十個成員國的國家電網實現實體互聯，計畫於2045年前完成。

  世界銀行集團估計，在未來二十年實現全面電網互聯，將需要約8,000億美元的發電與輸電投資。

  ## 協議涵蓋範疇

  強化版備忘錄在四大領域提供合作框架：基礎設施規劃、制度安排、政策與法規制定，以及融資管道。這包括連結各國電網的陸上輸電線路及海底電力電纜的融資機制。

  協議鼓勵各國政府、民間企業及金融機構共同集資，旨在促進電網研究、建設、營運及進一步發展的資金籌措。

  區域電力市場整合部分的參與將維持自願原則。根據協議條款，各國是否參與，將取決於其本身的國家政策、法律與法規。

  ## 潛在效益

  菲律賓能源部表示，東協電網有助於分散電力來源、提升電力系統韌性，並支持各國電網整合再生能源。

  東協秘書長高金宏（Kao Kim Hourn）將此協議與東協的長期規劃框架相連結。他表示：「在邁向『東協共同體願景2045』的道路上，我們歡迎合作夥伴與投資者將這份承諾化為具體計畫，為各國人民提供可靠、負擔得起且永續的電力。」

  ## 菲律賓輪值主席任期結束

  東協電網被列為菲律賓2026年東協輪值主席任期的重點能源優先議題。菲律賓能源部長莎朗·嘉林（Sharon S. Garin）表示，菲律賓期待在持續與成員國及合作夥伴協作的基礎上，推動跨境電力交易並吸引電網投資。

  菲律賓將於11月17日正式將東協輪值主席職位移交給新加坡。新加坡將領導這個區域組織至2027年。
