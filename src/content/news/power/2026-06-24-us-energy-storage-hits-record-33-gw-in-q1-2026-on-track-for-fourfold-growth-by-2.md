body_en: |
  The United States installed a record 3.3 gigawatts (GW) / 8.4 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of energy storage in the first quarter of 2026, with utility-scale, residential, and commercial/community/industrial segments all posting their best-ever first-quarter results, according to the Q1 2026 U.S. Energy Storage Monitor published by Wood Mackenzie and the American Clean Power Association (ACP).

  Wood Mackenzie and ACP project cumulative U.S. installed energy storage capacity will reach 200 GW / 655 GWh by 2031, a fourfold increase from the current installed base. That medium-term outlook aligns with a separate projection from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), which in its June 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook expects U.S. energy storage capacity to double by the end of 2027.

  ## Demand backdrop

  The EIA expects U.S. electricity consumption to rise by 76 billion kilowatt-hours (kWh) in 2026 and by 126 billion kWh in 2027, driven by increased sales to commercial, industrial, and transportation customers. Above-average summer temperatures across much of the country will further lift demand in 2026, and the EIA expects renewables to "almost entirely" meet that increase. Solar generation could rise 19% this year and wind generation 10%, the agency said.

  Solar and storage are increasingly deployed at the same site. According to a June 10 report from the U.S. Solar Energy Industries Association (SEIA), solar and storage together accounted for 91% of nameplate generating capacity — the maximum rated output — added in the first quarter of 2026. Nearly half of new residential solar systems installed in the same period were paired with battery storage.

  ## Tax credit preservation as a foundation

  A central driver of the boom, according to Wood Mackenzie and ACP, is the preservation of the federal investment tax credit (ITC) for qualifying energy storage systems. The ITC, first authorized by the Inflation Reduction Act in 2022, can offset 30% or more of deployment costs. Last year's One Big Beautiful Bill Act retained the energy storage ITC even as it accelerated the expiration of the corresponding investment and production tax credits for wind and solar systems.

  ## Segment outlook

  Wood Mackenzie and ACP project utility-scale storage will account for 85% of capacity additions through 2031, driven by large-load customers signing colocation and capacity contracts with storage providers.

  The commercial, community, and industrial segment is forecast to grow 26% through 2031, supported by strong behind-the-meter demand — storage installed on the customer's side of the utility meter — in California and at least 215 megawatts (MW) of community-scale storage projects under development nationally.

  The residential segment faces a shallow contraction in 2026 following a surge of installations ahead of the expiration of the Section 25D residential clean energy tax credit at the end of 2025. Wood Mackenzie and ACP expect residential storage to then expand at a 12% average annual rate over the following four years.

  ## Domestic manufacturing

  Tax-code changes that favor energy storage systems with more U.S.-sourced content are accelerating domestic battery manufacturing. The Energy Storage Coalition, an industry group, said in March that U.S. factories now have enough capacity to supply 100% of domestic demand. Some of that capacity is coming from manufacturers that previously planned to produce electric vehicle batteries in the United States; Ford and General Motors both announced significant energy storage manufacturing investments in 2026, according to Wood Mackenzie and ACP.

  ## Significance

  The Q1 installation record, combined with the near- and medium-term forecasts from Wood Mackenzie, ACP, and the EIA, signals that the U.S. energy storage market is entering a period of durable structural growth backed by a preserved federal tax credit, rising large-load demand, growing domestic manufacturing, and a paired solar-and-storage deployment model that now represents the dominant share of new U.S. generating capacity additions.

  Wood Mackenzie and ACP expect installation volumes to continue rising over the next several years as U.S. battery manufacturing capacity scales further to meet that demand.

  ## 美國儲能Q1裝置量創新高　2031年前料成長四倍

  根據Wood Mackenzie與美國清潔能源協會（ACP）發布的《2026年第一季美國儲能監測報告》，美國在2026年第一季共裝設了3.3吉瓦（GW）／8.4吉瓦時（GWh）的儲能系統，創下歷史新高，公用事業規模、住宅及商業／社區／工業三大市場區塊均同步寫下各自的第一季最佳紀錄。

  Wood Mackenzie與ACP預測，美國累計儲能裝置容量將於2031年達到200GW／655GWh，較現有裝置規模成長約四倍。這項中期展望與美國能源資訊署（EIA）的預測方向一致——EIA於2026年6月發布的《短期能源展望》中預估，美國儲能容量將於2027年底前翻倍。

  ## 需求面背景

  EIA預測，受商業、工業及運輸客戶用電需求增加帶動，美國2026年電力消費將成長760億度（kWh），2027年將再增加1,260億度。2026年全美大部分地區夏季氣溫高於歷史均值，將進一步推升用電需求，EIA預期屆時再生能源將「幾乎完全」承擔此一增量。EIA指出，太陽能發電量今年可望成長19%，風力發電則預計成長10%。

  太陽能與儲能系統並設於同一場址的案例日益普遍。根據美國太陽能產業協會（SEIA）於6月10日發布的報告，2026年第一季新增的銘牌發電容量（即額定最大輸出功率）中，太陽能與儲能合計佔比高達91%。同期間新裝設的住宅太陽能系統中，近半數同步配置了電池儲能設備。

  ## 投資稅抵減保留奠定發展基礎

  Wood Mackenzie與ACP指出，這波儲能裝設熱潮的核心驅動力，在於聯邦投資稅抵減（ITC）對符合資格的儲能系統得以延續。ITC最初由2022年《降低通膨法案》授權實施，可抵減30%以上的部署成本。去年通過的《一個美好法案》雖加速了風能與太陽能系統對應投資及生產稅抵減的落日時程，但仍保留了儲能系統的ITC資格。

  ## 各區塊展望

  Wood Mackenzie與ACP預測，2031年前的容量新增量中，公用事業規模儲能將佔85%，主要動能來自大用電量客戶與儲能供應商簽訂共址配置及容量合約。

  商業、社區與工業區塊預計至2031年將成長26%，主要受加州強勁的表後端儲能需求——亦即裝設於客戶側電錶後方的儲能設備——以及全美至少215百萬瓦（MW）的社區規模儲能開發案支撐。

  住宅區塊方面，由於2025年底第25D條款住宅清潔能源稅抵減到期前曾出現搶裝潮，預計2026年將出現小幅回落。Wood Mackenzie與ACP預期，住宅儲能此後將在隨後四年以平均每年12%的速度穩定擴張。

  ## 國內製造

  稅制調整朝有利於採用更高比例美國本土製造零組件的儲能系統方向改革，正在加速國內電池製造業的發展。產業團體儲能聯盟（Energy Storage Coalition）於3月表示，美國工廠目前的產能已足以供應國內100%的需求。部分產能來自原本規劃在美國生產電動車電池、後來轉型佈局儲能製造的業者；根據Wood Mackenzie與ACP的資料，福特（Ford）與通用汽車（General Motors）均於2026年宣布了重大的儲能製造投資計畫。

  ## 市場意義

  第一季裝置量創下歷史紀錄，加上Wood Mackenzie、ACP與EIA的近中期預測，共同釋出一個明確訊號：美國儲能市場正步入一個具有結構性支撐的持續成長階段，其基礎包括獲得保留的聯邦投資稅抵減、大用電量客戶需求持續攀升、國內製造規模不斷擴大，以及太陽能與儲能並設模式已成為美國新增發電容量的主流。

  Wood Mackenzie與ACP預期，隨著美國電池製造產能持續擴充以因應市場需求，未來數年的儲能裝設量將持續上揚。
