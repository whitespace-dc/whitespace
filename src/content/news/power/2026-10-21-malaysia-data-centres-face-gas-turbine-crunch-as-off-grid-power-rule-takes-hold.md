body_en: |
  ## Malaysia forces data centres off the national grid

  Malaysia's newly approved data centres must secure their own electricity supply rather than drawing from the national grid, a condition written into letters of award for recently greenlit projects, raising immediate questions about whether enough gas and generation equipment exists to meet demand.

  Royce Tan Seng Hooi, a senior analyst at MBSB Research, said the move was "inevitable" given the speed at which Malaysia's data centre sector has expanded and the volume of power those facilities consume. "It was bound to happen," he told StarBiz, citing the pace of development and associated energy load.

  The off-grid condition most likely means developers will need to build their own power plants or contract directly with energy suppliers, bypassing Tenaga Nasional Bhd, the national utility. An industry insider quoted by StarBiz confirmed that a direct grid connection from Tenaga is no longer an option under the new framework.

  ## Global turbine shortage squeezes timelines

  For developers planning gas-fired generation, the most immediate obstacle is supply. A severe global shortage of large frame gas turbines — the kind typically used in utility-scale combined cycle plants capable of gigawatt-scale output — means manufacturers cannot deliver most units until 2030 or 2031 at the earliest, the industry insider said. The shortage has been driven by surging data centre construction in the United States and bulk procurement by buyers in the Middle East.

  The timing gap is significant: a developer that secured approval today and tried to commission a large gas turbine plant would likely face a multi-year wait before generation assets arrived.

  ## Smaller, modular turbines as a near-term workaround

  The industry insider pointed to smaller-scale alternatives as a way around the shortage. Reciprocating gas engines and aeroderivative gas turbines — units that range from single-digit to double-digit megawatts (MW) in output, compared with the hundreds or thousands of MW from large combined cycle plants — can be delivered faster and installed in phases as a data centre's load grows.

  "A DC may ask for 700MW, but it would only take them a few years until they reach anywhere near that," the insider said, noting that the modular approach aligns with how data centre capacity is actually built out over time. Although per-unit efficiency is lower than large combined cycle plants, the ability to stage deployment makes the smaller units commercially practical.

  ## IPP partnerships seen as the practical route

  Tan outlined what he described as a more practical structural approach: allowing data centre operators to secure dedicated generation capacity through partnerships with independent power producers (IPPs), such as YTL Power International Bhd and Malakoff Corp Bhd. He said arrangements could take the form of on-site generation, collocated power plants, or off-site setups where some grid access is preserved.

  Such deals would give IPPs a new growth avenue, leveraging their existing technical capabilities in operating large generation assets, Tan said.

  ## Renewable energy requirement adds complexity

  On top of the off-grid condition, RHB Research noted that newly approved data centres must also meet a 30% renewable energy mix requirement, adding another layer of procurement complexity. The research house said the requirement is expected to drive more off-takers toward agreements under the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme, or Cress — a framework that allows large consumers to contract directly for green electricity.

  A recent Cress Acceleration Package set the system access charge (SAC) — the fee paid to use the grid to deliver renewable energy — at 14 sen per kilowatt-hour (kWh), down from 20 sen, though a four-sen surcharge on green consumers brings the effective rate to 18 sen per kWh. RHB Research said it expects off-takers to proceed with Cress agreements to lock in long-term green tariffs and reduce exposure to future grid price increases.

  Tan said the 30% renewable requirement reinforces demand for Cress regardless of the SAC level. "No matter what the SAC is, DCs will still have to procure renewable energy," he said, adding that solar project developers he had spoken to remained commercially optimistic even at the 18-sen rate.

  ## Solar and storage cannot carry a 24/7 load

  Renewable energy alone, however, cannot meet the continuous power demands of a data centre. Solar generation is available for at most four to five hours per day in Malaysia's climate, Tan said, and pairing it with battery storage extends that to roughly eight hours — well short of the round-the-clock supply a data centre requires.

  The industry insider reinforced that point, describing solar-plus-storage as a supplemental option only: "It definitely cannot carry a 24/7 load in our climate without a firm backup." With a direct Tenaga grid line no longer available, he said developers are looking at diesel as a potential firm backup source.

  ## What the shift means for Malaysia's power sector

  The combined effect of the off-grid mandate, the 30% renewables requirement, and the gas turbine shortage reshapes how data centre power infrastructure will be financed, contracted, and built in Malaysia. Developers face a procurement environment in which the easiest supply options — large turbines and direct grid access — are either unavailable or foreclosed by policy.

  The conditions are likely to accelerate IPP partnership deals and Cress contracting activity, while pushing developers toward modular generation assets that can be staged over time. RHB Research said the lower SAC under the Cress Acceleration Package is expected to spur new Cress agreements in the coming months.

  ## 馬來西亞強制資料中心脫離國家電網

  馬來西亞新核准的資料中心必須自行確保電力供應，不得接入國家電網，此條件已寫入近期獲批專案的頒獎函中，即刻引發外界質疑：市場上是否有足夠的天然氣及發電設備可滿足需求。

  MBSB研究部門資深分析師陳成輝（Royce Tan Seng Hooi）表示，考量到馬來西亞資料中心產業的擴張速度及相關設施的龐大用電量，此舉「勢在必行」。他在接受《星洲財經》（StarBiz）採訪時指出，產業發展的步調與伴隨而來的電力負載，使這一結果「注定發生」。

  離網條件最有可能意味著開發商須自建電廠，或直接與能源供應商簽約，繞過國家電力公用事業機構馬來西亞國家能源公司（Tenaga Nasional Bhd，簡稱TNB）。一名不具名的業內人士在《星洲財經》的報導中確認，在新框架下，直接接入TNB電網已不再是選項。

  ## 全球渦輪機短缺壓縮建設時程

  對於計畫採用燃氣發電的開發商而言，最迫切的障礙在於供應鏈問題。上述業內人士表示，大型框架式燃氣渦輪機——即通常用於公用規模聯合循環電廠、可達吉瓦（GW）級輸出的機組——目前全球嚴重短缺，多數製造商的交貨時間已推遲至2030年或2031年以後。這場短缺危機的根源在於美國資料中心建設熱潮所引發的需求激增，以及中東買家的大批量採購。

  時程上的落差相當可觀：一個今日剛取得核准的開發商，若試圖籌建大型燃氣渦輪電廠，在發電設備到位之前，極可能面臨長達數年的等待。

  ## 小型模組化渦輪機成近期替代方案

  上述業內人士指出，小型替代方案可作為解決短缺問題的變通之道。往復式燃氣引擎（reciprocating gas engines）與航改型燃氣渦輪機（aeroderivative gas turbines）——輸出功率從個位數到雙位數百萬瓦（MW）不等，相較於大型聯合循環電廠動輒數百乃至數千MW的規模——交貨較快，且可隨資料中心負載的成長分階段安裝。

  「一座資料中心或許申請了700MW，但實際上要花好幾年才能接近那個用電量，」該業內人士說，並指出模組化方式與資料中心容量實際擴建的節奏相符。儘管單機效率不如大型聯合循環電廠，但可分段部署的特性使小型機組在商業上具有實際可行性。

  ## 與獨立發電商合作被視為可行路徑

  陳成輝提出了他認為在結構上更為可行的做法：允許資料中心營運商透過與獨立發電商（IPP）的合作取得專屬發電容量，合作對象如YTL電力國際公司（YTL Power International Bhd）及Malakoff公司（Malakoff Corp Bhd）。他表示，合作形式可為現場發電、毗鄰電廠，或保留部分電網接取的場外配置。

  陳成輝說，此類協議將為獨立發電商開創新的成長空間，使其得以善用在運營大型發電資產方面的既有技術能力。

  ## 再生能源要求增添採購複雜度

  除離網條件外，RHB研究部門指出，新核准的資料中心還須符合30%再生能源配比要求，為採購工作增添另一層複雜度。RHB研究部門表示，此要求預計將促使更多購電方轉向「企業再生能源供應計畫」（Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme，簡稱Cress）——該框架允許大型用電戶直接簽訂綠電合約。

  日前出爐的「Cress加速方案」將系統接取費（SAC）——即透過電網傳輸再生能源所需支付的費用——從每千瓦小時（kWh）20仙調降至14仙，不過綠電用戶須另加4仙附加費，實際費率因此為每kWh 18仙。RHB研究部門預期，購電方將積極簽訂Cress協議，以鎖定長期綠電費率，並降低未來電網電價上漲的風險。

  陳成輝表示，30%再生能源要求將強化對Cress的需求，不論SAC費率為何。「不管SAC是多少，資料中心都還是必須採購再生能源，」他說，並補充道，他接觸的太陽能專案開發商即使在18仙的費率下，商業展望依然樂觀。

  ## 太陽能與儲能無法支撐全天候負載

  然而，單靠再生能源無法滿足資料中心持續不斷的用電需求。陳成輝表示，在馬來西亞的氣候條件下，太陽能每天最多僅有四至五小時的發電時間，即便搭配電池儲能也只能延長至約八小時，遠不及資料中心全天候不間斷的供電需求。

  上述業內人士也強調了這一點，將太陽能加儲能定位為輔助選項：「在我們的氣候環境下，若沒有穩定的備援電源，太陽能加儲能絕對無法支撐24小時全天候的負載。」由於直接接入TNB電網的選項已不復存在，他表示開發商正考慮將柴油發電作為潛在的穩定備援電源。

  ## 此轉變對馬來西亞電力部門的意義

  離網強制規定、30%再生能源要求與燃氣渦輪機短缺三重因素疊加，正從根本上重塑馬來西亞資料中心電力基礎設施的融資、簽約與建設方式。開發商面臨的採購環境中，最容易取得的選項——大型渦輪機與直接電網接取——不是無從取得，便是遭政策封堵。

  上述情況可能加速獨立發電商合作協議的達成及Cress合約的簽訂，同時推動開發商轉向可分階段部署的模組化發電資產。RHB研究部門表示，Cress加速方案調低的SAC費率，預計將在未來數月內促成更多新的Cress協議。
