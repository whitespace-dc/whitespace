body_en: |
  American Electric Power (AEP) has committed approximately $4 billion to build a 702-megawatt solid oxide fuel-cell generating facility near Cheyenne, Wyoming, under a 20-year power purchase agreement (PPA) with Google, the companies disclosed in October 2026. The project would be one of the largest fuel-cell deployments announced for the data center industry.

  The installation will use solid oxide fuel cells, devices that convert fuel into electricity through an electrochemical process rather than combustion, supplied by Bloom Energy. Construction is expected to begin before the end of 2026, with full operations targeted for late 2028.

  The facility will be developed through Cheyenne Fuel Cell LLC, a venture approximately 92% owned and controlled by AEP, with funds advised by Basalt Infrastructure Partners holding the remaining interest. The structure places development and ownership responsibilities with AEP while providing Google long-term access to the facility's electricity output, rather than requiring the technology company to own or operate generating equipment directly.

  At roughly $4 billion for 702 MW, the project represents approximately $5.7 million per megawatt of planned generating capacity. Neither party has disclosed the electricity price Google will pay under the agreement, including any energy charges, capacity payments or other commercial terms.

  ## Deal timeline and structure

  Although Google's participation was publicly identified in October 2026, the procurement strategy underlying the Cheyenne project dates to November 2024, when Bloom Energy announced an agreement with AEP covering up to 1 gigawatt, or 1,000 megawatts, of solid oxide fuel-cell equipment. That arrangement included an initial commitment to purchase 100 MW, with options for an additional 900 MW, and was intended to help AEP serve large commercial and industrial customers facing growing electricity requirements without waiting for conventional grid infrastructure upgrades.

  The relationship advanced in January 2026, when AEP disclosed in a regulatory filing that an unregulated subsidiary had committed approximately $2.65 billion to purchase a substantial portion of the fuel-cell capacity covered by its earlier option. The company also reported a 20-year agreement to sell the planned facility's entire electricity output to an unidentified investment-grade customer, confirmed in October 2026 as Google. The $2.65 billion equipment purchase formed part of the overall project history and is not a separate investment on top of the $4 billion figure announced in October.

  For AEP, the project represents an opportunity to deploy capital in support of large-load customers beyond its traditional regulated utility territory. AEP chairman, president and chief executive officer Bill Fehrman was cited in announcing the agreement.

  ## Why solid oxide fuel cells at this scale

  Solid oxide fuel cells are an established technology, but their proposed deployment at 702 MW near Cheyenne represents a significant evolution in how data centers obtain electricity. The systems can operate on natural gas, biogas or hydrogen, depending on configuration. When running on natural gas, the fuel is reformed into a hydrogen-rich mixture that reacts with oxygen ions passing through a solid ceramic electrolyte, producing electricity, heat, water vapor and carbon dioxide. The process avoids conventional combustion but does not eliminate carbon emissions when natural gas is the primary fuel.

  One principal advantage for data center development is modularity. Standardized generating assemblies can be combined into larger installations, allowing capacity to be added incrementally rather than constructing a single large generating unit. That flexibility lets developers coordinate additional generating capacity with successive phases of data hall construction, electrical commissioning and computing equipment deployment.

  Multiple independent fuel-cell modules can also reduce dependence on any single generating unit, potentially allowing individual modules to be serviced or replaced without shutting down the entire installation. Modularity alone does not guarantee the availability required by hyperscale operations, however; installations must still be engineered as part of a complete power delivery system incorporating electrical redundancy, energy storage, backup capacity, and protection and control systems.

  A further consideration involves integration with emerging high-voltage direct-current data center architectures. In September 2026, Bloom promoted an 800-volt DC-native power architecture designed to reduce conversion equipment, electrical losses and infrastructure costs. Because solid oxide fuel cells generate direct-current electricity, Bloom contends the technology could simplify the power delivery chain for next-generation artificial intelligence systems. AEP and Google have not disclosed whether the Wyoming installation will use a DC-native distribution architecture or rely on conventional power conversion and interconnection equipment, a distinction that will affect whether the potential efficiency gains of direct-current generation can be realized.

  ## Carbon capture: a design objective, not yet an operating result

  AEP says the Cheyenne project is being designed to incorporate carbon-capture technology, with the objective of delivering near-zero-emissions electricity from a natural gas-fueled installation. The company has not publicly detailed the capture system's design or performance targets.

  According to Bloom Energy, its solid oxide fuel-cell technology produces a relatively concentrated carbon dioxide stream, which the manufacturer says could simplify capture compared with conventional gas-fired generating equipment, where carbon dioxide is diluted in much larger volumes of exhaust gas. Bloom contends this characteristic could reduce some of the equipment requirements, energy consumption and costs associated with carbon capture.

  ## Significance

  The Cheyenne commitment is among the largest announced fuel-cell power agreements for a data center campus and illustrates a growing pattern in which utilities and hyperscalers are entering multi-billion-dollar, decade-spanning power contracts outside conventional utility frameworks. For AEP, it extends capital deployment into unregulated generation serving a hyperscale customer at a scale that goes well beyond its established regulated utility operations. Construction is scheduled to begin before the end of 2026, with full operations targeted for late 2028.

  美國電力公司（American Electric Power，AEP）承諾投入約40億美元，在懷俄明州夏延市附近興建一座702百萬瓦（MW）固態氧化物燃料電池發電設施，並與 Google 簽署長達20年的購電協議（PPA）。兩家公司於2026年10月公開披露此一消息，這將成為資料中心產業迄今宣布的最大規模燃料電池建置案之一。

  該設施將採用由 Bloom Energy 供應的固態氧化物燃料電池——這種裝置透過電化學反應將燃料直接轉換為電力，而非依賴傳統燃燒方式。工程預計於2026年底前動工，目標在2028年底前全面投入運作。

  此專案將透過「夏延燃料電池有限責任公司」（Cheyenne Fuel Cell LLC）進行開發，AEP 持有約92%的股權並掌握控制權，其餘股份由 Basalt Infrastructure Partners 旗下基金持有。此一架構讓 AEP 承擔開發與所有權責任，同時讓 Google 享有長期採購設施電力的管道，無須直接擁有或操作發電設備。

  以702 MW、約40億美元計算，每百萬瓦的建置成本約為570萬美元。雙方均未披露 Google 依協議所須支付的電價，包括電能費用、容量費用或其他商業條款。

  ## 交易時間軸與架構

  雖然 Google 的參與身分於2026年10月才公諸於眾，但夏延專案的採購策略最早可追溯至2024年11月——當時 Bloom Energy 宣布與 AEP 簽訂一項協議，涵蓋最多1吉瓦（即1,000 MW）的固態氧化物燃料電池設備。該協議初期承諾採購100 MW，並保留額外900 MW 的選擇權，旨在協助 AEP 服務電力需求快速成長的大型商業與工業客戶，同時免去等待傳統電網基礎設施升級的漫長過程。

  合作關係於2026年1月進一步推進。AEP 在一份監管文件中揭露，其旗下一家不受管制的子公司已承諾斥資約26.5億美元，採購前述選擇權涵蓋之大部分燃料電池產能，並報告已簽訂一份為期20年的協議，將計畫設施的全部電力輸出售予一名未披露的投資級客戶——該客戶於2026年10月確認為 Google。26.5億美元的設備採購款屬於整體專案歷程的一部分，並非疊加於2026年10月公告之40億美元總額之外的額外投資。

  對 AEP 而言，此專案代表在其傳統受管制公用事業服務範圍以外，為大型用電客戶部署資本的機會。AEP 董事長兼總裁暨執行長 Bill Fehrman 在宣布協議時受到引述。

  ## 為何在此規模採用固態氧化物燃料電池

  固態氧化物燃料電池是一項成熟的技術，但在夏延市附近以702 MW 的規模部署，代表資料中心取得電力方式的重大演進。這類系統可依配置不同，使用天然氣、沼氣或氫氣作為燃料。以天然氣為燃料時，氣體先被重組為富含氫氣的混合物，再與透過固態陶瓷電解質傳導的氧離子發生反應，產生電力、熱能、水蒸氣與二氧化碳。此過程避免了傳統燃燒，但以天然氣為主要燃料時，並無法完全消除碳排放。

  對資料中心開發而言，模組化是此技術的一大優勢。標準化的發電組件可相互組合成更大規模的裝置，讓產能得以分批增加，無須一次性建造單一大型發電機組。此種彈性讓開發商得以配合資料機房各期施工、電氣調試與運算設備部署的進度，逐步擴充發電容量。

  多個獨立的燃料電池模組也能降低對單一發電單元的依賴，理論上允許個別模組在不停止整體設施運作的情況下進行維修或更換。然而，模組化本身並不足以保證超大規模資料中心所需的供電可靠性——設施仍須作為完整電力輸送系統的一部分加以設計，整合電氣備援、儲能、備用容量，以及保護與控制系統。

  另一項值得關注的面向，涉及與新興高壓直流資料中心架構的整合問題。2026年9月，Bloom 推廣了一種以800伏特直流電為原生架構的電力系統設計，旨在減少轉換設備、降低電力損耗並節省基礎設施成本。由於固態氧化物燃料電池本身產生直流電，Bloom 主張此技術可簡化下一代人工智慧系統的電力輸送鏈。AEP 與 Google 尚未披露懷俄明州設施是否將採用直流原生配電架構，或仍仰賴傳統電力轉換與併網設備——這一差異將決定直流發電的潛在效率優勢能否真正實現。

  ## 碳捕集：設計目標，尚非運作成果

  AEP 表示，夏延專案在設計上將整合碳捕集技術，目標是在以天然氣為燃料的設施中實現近零排放供電。該公司尚未公開說明捕集系統的設計細節或效能目標。

  根據 Bloom Energy 的說法，其固態氧化物燃料電池技術會產生濃度相對較高的二氧化碳氣流。製造商表示，與傳統燃氣發電設備相比，此一特性可能簡化碳捕集作業——傳統設備排放的廢氣體積龐大，二氧化碳濃度相對較低。Bloom 主張，這一特性有助於降低碳捕集所需的設備需求、能源消耗與相關成本。

  ## 重要意義

  夏延承諾案是迄今針對資料中心園區宣布的最大規模燃料電池供電協議之一，同時也呈現出一種日益普遍的趨勢——公用事業公司與超大規模資料中心業者，正在傳統公用事業框架之外，簽訂動輒數十億美元、橫跨十年的長期電力合約。對 AEP 而言，此案將資本部署延伸至不受管制的發電領域，服務超大規模客戶的規模遠超其既有的受管制公用事業業務。建設工程預計於2026年底前啟動，目標在2028年底前全面投入運作。
