  ## Europe lacks grid capacity to match US and China on AI data centers

  The European Union does not have enough grid capacity or physical space to invest in large-scale artificial intelligence data centers on a par with the United States, China, or Middle Eastern countries, the co-executive director of Belgian think tank Itinera warned in an interview with Taiwan's Central News Agency.

  Marc De Vos, who leads Itinera, said the EU is currently consumed by protecting existing industries rather than building new ones — a posture he attributed directly to the energy crisis that has struck Europe through the Russia-Ukraine war and ongoing Middle East instability. Both conflicts have driven sharp cost increases that have diverted political attention and public funding toward industrial preservation.

  "Europe's challenge is not China, America, or Russia — it is Europe itself," De Vos said.

  ## Belgium's grid already operating at twice planned capacity

  The gap between demand and infrastructure is already measurable on the ground. Bart Steukers, chief executive of Agoria, the Belgian technology industry federation, told the Central News Agency that applications to connect data centers to Belgium's electricity grid have risen ninefold since 2022. He said that if reserved power capacity scheduled for 2034 is included, current operational load has already reached twice the level anticipated in the national grid development plan.

  Steukers described energy as the decisive factor in competitiveness for Belgian technology companies, and said Europe is already showing signs of industrial decline partly because of its energy disadvantage relative to other regions.

  ## EU's Green Deal insufficient to close the gap

  De Vos acknowledged that the EU's Green Deal — the bloc's framework for decarbonization and infrastructure investment — theoretically directs continued spending into electricity grids, resources that could eventually support AI infrastructure. In practice, he said, Europe has fallen clearly behind in innovation and is fighting an uphill battle.

  He said the current situation does not allow Europe to predict an upper bound on the electricity demand that AI will eventually require, but it is clear that present grid capacity falls short of what would be needed to host data center investment at the scale seen in the United States, China, or the Middle East.

  ## Political fragmentation identified as structural obstacle

  Beyond energy, De Vos identified political fragmentation as the EU's other core obstacle to competing in AI. No individual EU member state is large enough to position itself as a significant AI actor on its own, he said, meaning the bloc can only compete collectively through EU-level institutions. Yet, he said, the fundamental problem is a lack of EU-level resources and the political will to drive major projects forward.

  De Vos said the era in which governments could step back and leave AI development entirely to markets has ended. The competition is now strategic and geopolitical, he said, which means decisions must be made. Without EU-level decision-making, the result will be individual member governments acting in isolation, he warned.

  ## Nuclear, renewables, and photonics seen as potential responses

  Steukers said an energy mix built on nuclear power and renewables, combined with more advanced grid modernization, could give Belgium and Europe a more stable and predictable operating environment — a precondition for attracting and retaining data center investment. He said AI data center energy consumption has already become one of the significant social questions facing the sector.

  On the technology side, Steukers pointed to photonics — a field that uses light rather than electrical signals to transmit data within chips — as one of the emerging approaches being tracked by industry that could substantially reduce the energy consumption of AI computing hardware, potentially reshaping the energy equation.

  He added that Belgian companies have already established positions in the offshore wind supply chain, and that a number of firms have demonstrated strong performance in smart grid technology and energy storage. Those capabilities represent a competitive opportunity, he said, provided governments establish stable and predictable policy frameworks that allow companies to plan over the long term.

  ## What it means for the industry

  The assessments from Itinera and Agoria signal that Europe's disadvantage in the AI infrastructure race is not primarily technological but structural: fragmented governance, an energy crisis rooted in geopolitical conflict, and grid infrastructure that was not dimensioned for the scale of compute demand that AI is now generating. Without EU-level coordination on both energy policy and AI investment, experts quoted by the Central News Agency indicate the bloc risks further marginalization as the United States and China continue to expand their data center capacity.

  Steukers said the prerequisite for unlocking Belgium's industrial potential in this environment is a government-provided framework that delivers long-term operational stability.

  ## 歐洲電網容量不足，難以在AI資料中心領域追上美中

  比利時智庫Itinera聯合執行主任在接受台灣中央社專訪時警告，歐盟目前既沒有足夠的電網容量，也沒有足夠的實體空間，能夠在大規模人工智慧資料中心的投資上與美國、中國或中東國家相提並論。

  Itinera智庫主任馬克．德沃斯（Marc De Vos）表示，歐盟眼前的心力全放在保護既有產業，而非建立新興產業，他直指這種態勢源於俄烏戰爭及中東局勢持續動盪所引發的能源危機。這兩場衝突推高了能源成本，使政治關注與公共資金大量流向護盤既有工業，而非開拓未來。

  德沃斯說：「歐洲面對的挑戰不是中國、美國或俄羅斯——而是歐洲自己。」

  ## 比利時電網負載已達規劃容量的兩倍

  需求與基礎建設之間的缺口，在現實中早已清晰可見。比利時科技產業聯合會Agoria執行長巴特．斯托克斯（Bart Steukers）向中央社表示，自2022年以來，資料中心申請接入比利時電網的案件已暴增九倍。他指出，若將預定於2034年釋出的保留電力容量一併計入，目前實際運轉的電力負載已是國家電網發展計畫原先預估水準的兩倍。

  斯托克斯將能源形容為比利時科技業競爭力的決定性因素，並表示歐洲在能源條件上相對其他地區處於劣勢，這已部分造成工業衰退的徵兆。

  ## 歐盟綠色協議不足以彌補差距

  德沃斯承認，歐盟的「綠色協議」——這套涵蓋去碳化與基礎建設投資的整體框架——在理論上確實持續挹注資金於電網，這些資源或許終能支撐AI基礎設施的發展。然而他說，實際上歐洲在創新層面已明顯落後，正在打一場逆風仗。

  他表示，現況並不允許歐洲預測AI最終所需電力的上限，但可以確定的是，目前的電網容量根本不足以承載美國、中國或中東規模的資料中心投資。

  ## 政治碎片化被點名為結構性障礙

  能源之外，德沃斯指出政治碎片化是歐盟在AI競爭中的另一核心障礙。他說，沒有任何一個歐盟成員國的體量足以單獨躋身重要AI角色，這意味著歐盟唯有透過歐盟層級的機構集體行動，才有競爭的可能。然而他說，根本問題在於歐盟層級的資源匱乏，以及推動重大計畫的政治意志不足。

  德沃斯表示，政府可以袖手旁觀、將AI發展全部交給市場的時代已經結束。他說，當前的競爭已是戰略性與地緣政治性的，這意味著必須做出抉擇。若欠缺歐盟層級的決策機制，結果將是各個成員國各自為政，他對此提出警告。

  ## 核能、再生能源與光子技術被視為潛在因應之道

  斯托克斯表示，若能建立一套以核能與再生能源為基礎的能源組合，搭配更先進的電網現代化，將可為比利時和歐洲提供更穩定、更可預期的營運環境——而這正是吸引並留住資料中心投資的前提條件。他說，AI資料中心的電力消耗已成為這個產業面臨的重大社會議題之一。

  在技術層面，斯托克斯特別提到光子技術——這是一種以光而非電信號在晶片內部傳輸資料的領域——認為這是業界正在追蹤的新興技術方向之一，有潛力大幅降低AI運算硬體的能源消耗，進而從根本上改變能源方程式。

  他補充指出，比利時企業已在離岸風電供應鏈中建立起穩固的地位，也有多家業者在智慧電網技術與儲能領域展現出強勁表現。他說，這些能力代表著競爭優勢的契機，前提是政府必須建立穩定可預期的政策框架，讓企業得以進行長期規劃。

  ## 對產業的啟示

  Itinera與Agoria的評估共同傳遞出一個訊號：歐洲在AI基礎設施競賽中的劣勢，根本原因並非技術落差，而是結構性問題——治理體系的碎片化、源自地緣政治衝突的能源危機，以及根本未針對當前AI所產生的龐大運算需求進行規劃的電網基礎設施。接受中央社採訪的專家指出，若欠缺歐盟層級在能源政策與AI投資上的協調整合，歐盟恐將在美國和中國持續擴張資料中心容量之際，面臨進一步被邊緣化的風險。

  斯托克斯表示，在這樣的環境下，要釋放比利時的產業潛力，先決條件是政府提供一套能確保長期營運穩定性的政策框架。
