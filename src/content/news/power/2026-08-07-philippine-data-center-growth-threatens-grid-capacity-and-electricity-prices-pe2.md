body_en: |
  The Philippine Energy Efficiency Alliance, Inc. (PE2), an industry group, warned Thursday that the rapid expansion of data centers in the Philippines risks straining the country's electricity supply and driving up power costs for consumers, as the government works to establish a regulatory framework for the sector.

  ## Grid pressure and cost concerns

  PE2 President Alexander D. Ablaza said the concentration of data centers in clusters poses a direct threat to electricity affordability. "The clustering of data centers poses a major threat to the affordability of electricity prices as they quickly demand new grid capacity upgrades," Ablaza said in a statement. He projected that data centers could eventually account for as much as 30% of local grid capacity.

  The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) expects national data center capacity to reach 1.5 gigawatts (GW) by 2028, as more operators establish facilities in the country. The Philippines is emerging as a strategic location for data centers, driven by its growing digital economy, improving connectivity, and large consumer base.

  ## Scale of power demand

  PE2's warning followed the International Energy Agency (IEA) releasing an analysis examining the relationship between artificial intelligence and energy. The IEA put the electricity requirements of the sector in concrete terms: a typical hyperscale data center — one of the large-scale facilities designed to serve millions of cloud users simultaneously — consumes roughly as much electricity as 100,000 households. The biggest facilities currently under development could use as much power as two million homes, the agency said.

  Globally, data centers are on track to consume nearly 1,000 terawatt-hours (TWh) of electricity by 2030, or roughly 3% of the world's total electricity demand, IEA energy analyst Vincent Jacamon said in a video published August 4. IEA analyst Siddharth Singh added that the rapid expansion of data centers requires additional power generation capacity and supporting infrastructure, underscoring the need for forward planning.

  ## Captive power and efficiency trade-offs

  To secure reliable supply independent of the public grid, some data center clusters in the Philippines are exploring captive power arrangements using natural gas, nuclear energy, or battery energy storage. Ablaza cautioned that a broad shift toward self-generation could reduce overall energy efficiency across the power sector.

  He called on the Department of Energy (DoE) to require clustered data centers to establish standalone generation and storage infrastructure, arguing that early policy action could contain the impact on the broader grid. "Government policy, if done early enough, can mitigate the impacts of data center market growth on the grid," Ablaza said.

  ## Government response

  The DoE is drafting a circular that would outline options for supplying electricity to data centers. Energy Secretary Sharon S. Garin said the government's priority is ensuring that any new power arrangements for the sector do not put ordinary consumers at a disadvantage. "We just need to make sure that anything we do to power a data center or hub will not put the rest of the country at a disadvantage," she said. "Consumers have protections in place."

  ## Significance for the sector

  The warnings from PE2 and the IEA reflect the scale of planning pressure that fast-growing data center markets place on national grids. Singh said proactive coordination between governments and technology companies is key to preventing reliability and affordability problems. "With proactive planning, better transparency from technology companies and smarter management of data center electricity consumption, there's no reason why reliable electricity and affordability cannot go hand in hand," he said.

  The DoE circular setting out electricity supply options for data centers remains in draft, with no release date announced.
body_zh: |
  菲律賓產業團體菲律賓能源效率聯盟（Philippine Energy Efficiency Alliance，簡稱PE2）週四示警，菲律賓資料中心快速擴張，恐對全國電力供應造成壓力、推升消費者電費負擔，而政府正積極為此一產業建立監管框架。

  ## 電網壓力與電費隱憂

  PE2理事長Alexander D. Ablaza表示，資料中心的群聚現象對電價的可負擔性構成直接威脅。他在聲明中指出：「資料中心的群聚對電價的可負擔性造成重大威脅，因為它們快速地要求提升新的電網容量。」Ablaza預估，資料中心最終可能占據當地電網容量高達三成。

  菲律賓資訊通訊技術部（DICT）預估，隨著更多業者在當地設立機房，全國資料中心容量將於2028年達到1.5吉瓦（GW）。受惠於蓬勃發展的數位經濟、持續改善的網路連線品質，以及龐大的消費人口，菲律賓正逐步崛起為資料中心的策略性設點地點。

  ## 用電需求的規模

  PE2發出警示之前，國際能源總署（IEA）甫發布一份探討人工智慧與能源關係的分析報告。IEA以具體數字說明此一產業的用電需求：一座典型的超大規模資料中心——即那類專為同時服務數百萬名雲端用戶而建的大型設施——其耗電量約相當於十萬戶家庭的用電總和。IEA並指出，目前正在興建中的最大型設施，用電量可能高達兩百萬戶家庭。

  IEA能源分析師Vincent Jacamon在8月4日發布的一支影片中表示，全球資料中心的用電量預計在2030年前接近1,000兆瓦時（TWh），約占全球總用電需求的3%。IEA分析師Siddharth Singh補充指出，資料中心的快速擴張需要額外的發電容量與配套基礎設施，凸顯超前部署規劃的必要性。

  ## 自備電源與效率的取捨

  為確保不依賴公共電網也能取得穩定電力，菲律賓部分資料中心聚落正評估採用天然氣、核能或電池儲能等自備電源方案。Ablaza警告，若大規模轉向自發電，恐將降低整體電力產業的能源效率。

  他呼籲能源部（DoE）要求群聚的資料中心建立獨立的發電與儲能基礎設施，主張盡早採取政策行動，可以遏制資料中心市場成長對整體電網造成的衝擊。「只要政府政策出手夠早，就能減輕資料中心市場成長對電網的影響，」Ablaza說道。

  ## 政府的回應

  能源部目前正草擬一份行政命令，內容將概述資料中心的供電選項。能源部長Sharon S. Garin表示，政府的首要之務是確保針對此一產業所採取的任何新供電安排，不會讓一般消費者處於不利地位。「我們只需確保，無論我們為資料中心或樞紐所做的任何安排，都不會讓其餘國民處於劣勢，」她說。「消費者受到完善的保護。」

  ## 對產業的深遠意義

  PE2與IEA的警示，反映出快速成長的資料中心市場對各國電網所帶來的龐大規劃壓力。Singh表示，政府與科技業者積極協調合作，是防止電力穩定性與可負擔性問題的關鍵。「只要超前規劃、科技公司提高透明度、更聰明地管理資料中心的用電，供電穩定與電費合理兩者並行，沒有任何理由辦不到，」他說。

  能源部規範資料中心供電選項的行政命令仍在草擬階段，尚未宣布發布日期。
