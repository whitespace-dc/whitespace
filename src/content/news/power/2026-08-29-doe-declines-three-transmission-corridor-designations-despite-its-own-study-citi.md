body_en: |
  The U.S. Department of Energy has declined to designate three proposed National Interest Electric Transmission Corridors (NIETCs) — geographic designations that can unlock certain federal transmission tools and, in limited circumstances, trigger federal permitting authority — even as its own draft 2026 National Transmission Needs Study identified accelerating electricity demand from data centers, domestic manufacturing, large industrial loads, and electrification as major drivers requiring additional transmission capacity.

  The three corridors that advanced to Phase 3, the final stage of federal review, before DOE concluded they lacked a sufficient designation basis are the Tribal Energy Access Corridor, the Southwestern Grid Connector Corridor, and the Lake Erie-Canada Corridor. DOE cited the 2023 National Transmission Needs Study, public and governmental input, and other information gathered during the designation process.

  ## What a NIETC designation does and does not do

  A NIETC is a geographic designation, not approval for a specific transmission project. Designation can activate certain federal transmission tools and, under defined circumstances, give the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) authority to issue permits for eligible transmission facilities within the corridor. DOE's decision removes that potential federal backstop for the three corridors in question.

  Former DOE Secretary Jennifer Granholm publicly challenged the decision. In a LinkedIn post, Granholm pointed to the contradiction between the agency's own transmission study and the corridor decisions. "You can't declare an energy emergency, demand that America pump out more electricity, and then purposefully make it harder to deliver that power where it's needed," she wrote.

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, offered a more qualified reading. "The Needs Study identifies a national transmission problem," Osnato said. "The NIETC process asks a different question: whether specific geographic corridors satisfy the statutory and evidentiary basis for federal designation." The two conclusions, he said, do not necessarily conflict.

  Osnato characterized the episode as illustrating a deeper structural gap: the United States has tools to identify transmission needs but fewer mechanisms to convert those findings into timely, coordinated interstate projects.

  ## The timeline mismatch driving the pressure

  Transmission planning, permitting, and construction can take five to ten years or longer, while large-load projects can seek interconnection within months or a few years, according to a July 2026 report from the Energy Systems Integration Group (ESIG) Large Loads Task Force prepared by Brattle principal Johannes Pfeifenberger, Warren Lasher, and ESIG's James Okullo. That asymmetry means grid planners face a recurring choice: build transmission ahead of confirmed demand and risk stranded infrastructure, or wait for firm commitments and risk making the grid the bottleneck.

  The ESIG report also notes that today's large loads differ from historical demand growth in ways that compound the problem: individual facilities can reach gigawatt scale, projects can cluster geographically, and development can move faster than major transmission projects can be built.

  ## ERCOT's queue illustrates the scale

  The dimension of the challenge is visible in Texas. As of June 2026, the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), which operates the grid serving most of the state, was tracking approximately 474.7 gigawatts (GW) of large-load interconnection requests. Of that total, roughly 420.8 GW — about 90.2 percent — were associated with data centers. Interconnection requests represent developer interest across a range of development stages, not committed or energized demand.

  The scale of the queue prompted state action. On June 10, Governor Greg Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) and ERCOT to review data center projects and address development risks and costs that could otherwise fall on Texas customers.

  ## Texas CREZ as a planning model

  The ESIG report points to Texas's Competitive Renewable Energy Zones (CREZ) program as an example of transmission planned around expected development rather than built project-by-project. CREZ was designed to move electricity from resource-rich West Texas to major population centers, with transmission built ahead of the full generation buildout it was meant to serve. The ESIG report also cites the Illinois Renewable Energy Access Plan (REAP) as a comparable development-zone approach.

  Nearly two decades after CREZ was constructed, data center developers are locating in areas where the resulting transmission and power infrastructure provides access to substantial capacity. Galaxy Digital's Helios campus in Dickens County — being developed as a multi-gigawatt data center platform — is one example cited by Data Center Knowledge of a project situated in an area where CREZ-enabled infrastructure exists.

  The ESIG report recommends moving beyond project-by-project transmission upgrades toward proactive, scenario-based, and multi-value planning, citing CREZ as a template.

  Osnato said the CREZ logic applies to clustered data center growth but with an important qualification. "CREZ showed the value of identifying development zones, planning transmission at scale, and avoiding a sequence of one-off upgrades that become slower and more expensive over time," he said. "That logic absolutely applies to clustered data center growth." However, he noted that CREZ was built around geographically concentrated generation — a different evidentiary profile than future data center load, which remains conditional on customer contracts, power delivery, financing, permitting, site readiness, and technology decisions.

  "I would borrow the CREZ concept of proactive zones and scale planning," Osnato said, "but pair it with a much stronger requirement to prove which loads have actually earned the right to drive that investment."

  ## Building ahead of evidence versus ahead of firm commitments

  Osnato favors a staged approach that separates early-stage corridor preservation from full capital commitment. He described a process in which planners develop broad demand scenarios and preserve transmission corridors and development options early, then release increasingly irreversible capital as underlying load passes defined evidence gates. A utility might secure rights-of-way or design a scalable transmission solution before every megawatt is contracted, while deferring full capital investment until the evidence of durable load is stronger.

  "In some places, yes," Osnato said when asked whether the current pace of data center development justifies building transmission ahead of firm commitments. "But 'ahead of firm commitments' should not mean 'ahead of evidence.'" He suggested framing transmission decisions around three questions: what load is represented; what is independently supported today; and what change would invalidate that conclusion.

  ## What comes next

  The DOE's decision not to designate the three corridors removes a potential federal tool for accelerating transmission in those areas, leaving transmission development to proceed through existing state and utility planning processes. The draft 2026 National Transmission Needs Study, which identified the pressing need for additional capacity, has not been finalized, and its conclusions about demand growth from data centers and other large loads remain in the policy record as grid planners and regulators in states including Texas weigh how to allocate planning investment across a queue dominated by conditional, not committed, demand.
body_zh: |
  美國能源部（DOE）決定不對三條已推進至聯邦審查最終階段的「國家利益電力輸電走廊」（NIETC）進行指定——這類地理指定可解鎖特定聯邦輸電工具，並在有限情況下觸發聯邦許可授權——儘管該部門自身起草的《2026年全國輸電需求研究》草案已明確指出，資料中心、國內製造業、大型工業負載及電氣化浪潮所帶來的用電需求正急遽攀升，迫切需要擴充輸電容量。

  三條已推進至第三階段（聯邦審查最終階段）的走廊，在DOE認定缺乏充分指定依據後，均宣告中止，分別為：部落能源通道（Tribal Energy Access Corridor）、西南電網連接走廊（Southwestern Grid Connector Corridor），以及伊利湖—加拿大走廊（Lake Erie-Canada Corridor）。DOE在決策中援引了2023年《全國輸電需求研究》、公眾與政府意見，以及指定程序中蒐集到的其他資料。

  ## NIETC 指定的意義與限制

  NIETC 是一種地理指定，而非針對特定輸電專案的核准。指定一旦生效，可啟動特定聯邦輸電工具，並在符合條件的情況下，授予聯邦能源管制委員會（FERC）在走廊範圍內對合格輸電設施發放許可的權力。DOE此次的決定，等同於撤除了三條走廊原本可能享有的聯邦後盾機制。

  前能源部長珍妮佛·葛蘭霍姆（Jennifer Granholm）對這一決定提出公開質疑。她在領英（LinkedIn）上發文，直指機構自身的輸電研究結論與走廊決策之間存在矛盾。「你不能一邊宣布能源緊急狀態、要求美國大量增加電力供應，一邊又刻意讓電力難以送達需要的地方，」她寫道。

  Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾·奧斯納托（Neil Osnato）則提出較為保留的解讀。「需求研究點出的是全國性的輸電問題，」奧斯納托說，「NIETC程序問的是另一個問題：特定地理走廊是否符合聯邦指定的法定與舉證基礎。」他表示，兩項結論未必相互矛盾。

  奧斯納托將這一事件定性為凸顯了美國更深層的結構性缺口：美國擁有辨識輸電需求的工具，卻缺乏足夠機制，將這些發現及時轉化為跨州協調推進的具體專案。

  ## 時間錯位：供需失衡的根源

  根據能源系統整合組（ESIG）大型負載工作小組於2026年7月發布的報告——由Brattle首席顧問約翰尼斯·費芬貝格爾（Johannes Pfeifenberger）、沃倫·拉舍爾（Warren Lasher）及ESIG的詹姆斯·奧庫洛（James Okullo）共同撰寫——輸電規劃、許可及施工往往需要五至十年甚至更長時間，而大型負載專案從申請電網併網到實際完成，有時僅需數月至數年。這種時間上的不對稱，讓電網規劃人員長期面臨一道艱難抉擇：在需求尚未確定之前超前布建輸電設施，冒著基礎建設閒置的風險；還是等到業者做出明確承諾後再行跟進，屆時電網卻可能反而成為瓶頸。

  ESIG報告同時指出，當代大型負載在性質上有別於過往的需求成長，進一步加劇了這一問題：單一設施的用電規模可達吉瓦（GW）等級，專案可能在地理上高度集中，開發速度也可能遠超主要輸電專案的施工時程。

  ## ERCOT 候電隊列揭示規模之大

  這一挑戰的規模，在德州一覽無遺。截至2026年6月，負責管理德州大部分電網的「德州電力可靠度委員會」（ERCOT）正在追蹤約474.7 GW的大型負載併網申請。其中，約420.8 GW——約佔90.2%——與資料中心相關。併網申請代表的是開發商在各開發階段的意願，並非已承諾或已通電的實際需求。

  如此龐大的候電隊列促使州政府採取行動。6月10日，德州州長葛雷格·艾伯特（Greg Abbott）指示德州公用事業委員會（PUCT）及ERCOT，對資料中心專案展開審查，並設法因應可能轉嫁給德州消費者的開發風險與成本。

  ## 德州 CREZ 作為規劃範本

  ESIG報告以德州「競爭性再生能源區」（CREZ）計畫為例，說明圍繞預期開發超前規劃輸電的做法，而非逐案推進。CREZ的設計初衷是將電力從資源豐富的西德州輸送至主要人口中心，在整體發電建設尚未完成之前，便先行鋪設服務該計畫所需的輸電線路。ESIG報告還援引伊利諾州的「再生能源通道計畫」（Illinois REAP）作為類似的開發區規劃案例。

  CREZ建成近二十年後，資料中心開發商正陸續進駐受惠於該計畫之輸電與電力基礎建設的地區。Galaxy Digital在迪肯斯郡（Dickens County）開發的Helios園區——定位為多吉瓦等級的資料中心平台——正是《Data Center Knowledge》援引的一個案例，坐落於CREZ所促成的輸電基礎建設所及之地。

  ESIG報告建議，應從逐案推進的輸電升級模式，轉型為主動式、情境化、多重效益的整合規劃，並以CREZ為範本。

  奧斯納托認為，CREZ的邏輯適用於集中式資料中心成長，但必須附加一項重要前提。「CREZ展示了辨識開發區、大規模規劃輸電，以及避免逐一推進、愈來愈緩慢且昂貴的零散升級所帶來的價值，」他說，「這套邏輯完全適用於集中式資料中心的成長。」然而，他也指出，CREZ當初是圍繞地理上高度集中的發電資源而建，其舉證背景與未來資料中心負載截然不同——後者仍取決於客戶合約、電力供應、融資、許可、場地就緒程度，以及技術決策等多重條件。

  「我會借鑑CREZ中主動設定開發區與大規模規劃的概念，」奧斯納托說，「但同時搭配更嚴格的要求：哪些負載確實已具備驅動該項投資的充分條件。」

  ## 在確鑿依據出現前超前布建，而非盲目超前

  奧斯納托傾向採取分階段推進的方式，將走廊的早期保留與全面資本承諾明確區分開來。他描述了一種程序：規劃人員先行制定廣泛的需求情境，盡早保留輸電走廊與開發選項，再隨著基礎負載通過各項舉證門檻，逐步釋出愈來愈難以逆轉的資本投入。一家電力公司或許會在所有電量尚未簽約前，便先行取得土地使用權或設計可擴充的輸電方案；但在持久性負載的證據更為充分之前，則推遲全額資本投入。

  「在某些地方，確實如此，」奧斯納托在被問及當前資料中心開發速度是否足以合理化超前布建輸電設施（先於明確承諾）時回答，「但『超前於明確承諾』不應等同於『超前於任何依據』。」他建議圍繞三個問題來框架輸電決策：這些負載代表什麼需求；哪些部分目前已獲得獨立支持；以及什麼樣的變化會推翻這項結論。

  ## 後續發展

  DOE決定不對三條走廊進行指定，等同於撤除了聯邦政府加速推動這些地區輸電建設的潛在工具，輸電開發將繼續透過現行州政府及電力公司的規劃程序推進。已辨識出迫切增容需求的《2026年全國輸電需求研究》草案尚未定案，其關於資料中心及其他大型負載需求成長的結論，仍留存於政策紀錄之中，供包括德州在內的各州電網規劃人員及主管機關參考，衡量如何在一個以條件性需求（而非承諾性需求）為主的龐大候電隊列中，合理分配規劃投資。
