body_en: |
  PLN, Indonesia's state electricity utility, implemented rotating power cuts across Medan and four surrounding districts starting June 6, 2026, after heavy rain and strong winds toppled 12 transmission towers in North Sumatra two days earlier, severing capacity on two major supply lines. The Institute for Essential Services Reform (IESR), an energy policy group, called the collapses a warning signal for national grid resilience and demanded a full government investigation.

  ## Transmission damage

  The failures struck two corridors. On the 275 kV extra-high-voltage overhead line (SUTET) between Galang and Simangkuk, three towers (T18, T19, T20) collapsed outright while two others (T17, T21) suffered structural deformation. On the 150 kV high-voltage overhead line (SUTT) between Tebing Tinggi and Sei Rotan, six consecutive towers, T77 through T82, fell; tower T76 bent.

  The Galang-Simangkuk 275 kV corridor forms part of the Sumatra Power Toll Road (Tol Listrik Sumatra), a strategic national transmission project that entered service in June 2019, making it a relatively recent asset, according to IESR.

  ## Load-shedding schedule and affected areas

  PLN's North Sumatra regional unit (PLN UID Sumut) said it was applying measured, rotating load management to contain the spread of disruption while repairs proceed. Affected areas include parts of Medan, Binjai, Deli Serdang, Serdang Bedagai, and Langkat. Customers in those areas face up to two interruptions per day, each lasting approximately three hours per session, PLN UID Sumut Communications Manager Darma Saputra said on June 6.

  Senior Manager for Finance, Communications, and General Affairs Efron Lumban Gaol said PLN is monitoring the system in real time and working to minimize customer impact. The utility is constructing emergency towers to accelerate restoration of supply to the North Sumatra grid. PLN apologized to affected customers.

  ## Calls for investigation

  IESR Chief Executive Fabby Tumiwa said on June 8 that transmission towers are designed to withstand heavy rain, lightning, and strong winds, all standard weather conditions in Indonesia, and that international design standards apply to such infrastructure. The collapses occurred just weeks after a separate Sumatra-wide blackout incident, he noted, and must not be attributed solely to adverse weather.

  "The primary cause of this incident must not be immediately concluded as adverse weather alone. What must be answered is why relatively new infrastructure could suffer sequential damage when facing such weather conditions," Tumiwa said in a written statement.

  IESR said the failure of a new asset on a strategic national project shows that grid resilience and design standards require deep re-evaluation. The group called on the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources, specifically its Directorate General of Electricity (DJK) as sector regulator, to conduct a full technical audit. It also called for an independent investigation to determine transparently whether the failures stem from design flaws, degraded materials, or execution weaknesses in the strategic transmission project.

  ## Benchmark for inquiry

  IESR cited Spain's 2025 blackout as a reference point, noting that Spanish grid operators, regulators, and the government jointly examined what allowed a local incident to escalate into a national crisis, identified aggravating factors, and addressed underlying systemic weaknesses. IESR said a comparable investigation is needed in Indonesia: one that looks not only for the immediate field cause but for why the system failed to respond to conditions that should have been accounted for at the design stage.

  The recurrence of major outages in Sumatra confirms serious gaps in national infrastructure planning that require transparent investigation and updated design and maintenance standards, IESR said. PLN said all personnel are working at full capacity to restore normal supply and that the utility will continue to accelerate system recovery.
body_zh: |
  印尼國家電力公司PLN於2026年6月6日起，對棉蘭（Medan）及北蘇門答臘四個周邊縣市實施輪流停電。事件起因是兩天前的一場強風豪雨，於6月4日將北蘇門答臘共12座輸電鐵塔掀倒，造成兩條主要供電線路中斷。能源政策研究機構「基本服務改革研究所」（Institute for Essential Services Reform，IESR）將此次倒塌事件定性為全國電網韌性的警訊，並要求政府展開全面調查。

  ## 輸電設施受損情況

  此次損毀涉及兩條輸電走廊。其一為加浪（Galang）至西芒庫（Simangkuk）之間的275千伏特超高壓架空輸電線路（SUTET），其中三座鐵塔——T18、T19、T20——完全倒塌，另有兩座（T17、T21）出現結構變形。其二為德賓廷吉（Tebing Tinggi）至錫羅坦（Sei Rotan）之間的150千伏特高壓架空輸電線路（SUTT），從T77至T82共六座連續鐵塔相繼倒地，T76號鐵塔則發生彎曲變形。

  IESR指出，加浪至西芒庫的275千伏特走廊是「蘇門答臘電力幹線」（Tol Listrik Sumatra）的一部分，屬於國家級戰略輸電計畫，於2019年6月正式啟用，是相對新建的基礎設施資產。

  ## 輪流限電時程及受影響區域

  PLN北蘇門答臘區域單位（PLN UID Sumut）表示，目前正採取有序的輪流負載管理措施，以避免停電範圍進一步擴大，同時加緊進行搶修作業。受影響區域涵蓋棉蘭、賓佳（Binjai）、德里沙達（Deli Serdang）、沙達貝達蓋（Serdang Bedagai）及朗卡（Langkat）部分地區。PLN UID Sumut通訊主任達瑪·薩普特拉（Darma Saputra）於6月6日表示，上述地區用戶每天最多面臨兩次停電，每次停電時間約為三小時。

  PLN財務、通訊暨行政資深經理埃弗隆·盧姆班加歐（Efron Lumban Gaol）表示，PLN正對系統進行即時監控，並致力將對用戶的衝擊降至最低。目前該公司正興建緊急應急鐵塔，以加速恢復北蘇門答臘電網供電。PLN就停電事件向受影響用戶致歉。

  ## 各方呼籲展開調查

  IESR執行長法比·圖米瓦（Fabby Tumiwa）於6月8日指出，輸電鐵塔在設計上本應能夠承受豪雨、雷擊及強風等印尼常見天氣條件，而國際設計標準亦適用於此類基礎設施。他指出，此次倒塌事件發生在蘇門答臘另一起全島大規模停電事故的數週之後，不能僅以惡劣天氣一語帶過。

  圖米瓦在一份書面聲明中表示：「此次事故的主要原因，不能立即歸咎於惡劣天氣。真正需要釐清的是，為何相對新建的基礎設施在面對此類天氣條件時，竟會接連發生損毀。」

  IESR表示，一項國家戰略計畫的新建資產出現此等故障，顯示電網韌性與設計標準有必要進行深度檢討。該機構呼籲能源礦產資源部，特別是負責產業監管的電力總局（Directorate General of Electricity，DJK），展開全面技術稽核，並要求進行獨立調查，以透明方式釐清此次故障究竟源於設計缺陷、材料劣化，還是該戰略輸電計畫在施工執行上的問題。

  ## 調查參考標竿

  IESR以西班牙2025年大停電作為參考案例，指出西班牙電網營運商、監管機構與政府共同檢視一起局部事故何以演變為全國危機，找出加劇因素並解決系統性根本問題。IESR表示，印尼同樣需要進行類似的調查：不僅要追查現場的直接原因，更要釐清為何系統在面對設計階段本應納入考量的條件時，仍然發生失效。

  IESR表示，蘇門答臘接連發生重大停電事故，已證實全國基礎設施規劃存在嚴重缺口，有必要透過透明調查加以因應，並更新設計與維護標準。PLN則表示，全體人員正全力投入，儘快恢復正常供電，並將持續加速電網復原作業。
