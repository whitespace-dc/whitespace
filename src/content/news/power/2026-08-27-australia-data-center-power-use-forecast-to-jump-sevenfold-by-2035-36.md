body_en: |
  Australia's data centers are on course to consume nearly seven times more electricity within a decade, straining a grid already navigating the retirement of coal and gas plants, the Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) said in its annual Electricity Statement of Opportunities.

  Data centers are projected to draw 34 terawatt-hours (TWh) from the National Electricity Market (NEM) — the interconnected grid covering eastern and southern Australia — in the 2035-36 financial year. That would lift the sector's share of NEM demand to 13%, up from 3% today. One terawatt-hour is roughly the annual electricity consumption of 200,000 average Australian households.

  ## Scale of the buildout

  The projected growth reflects Australia's push to capture what Commonwealth Bank of Australia estimates could be A$150 billion (US$108 billion) in data center investment by 2030. The federal government is pushing to ensure new facilities run on renewable energy, though that policy has met opposition from some regional administrations, AEMO noted.

  Data centers present a particular grid-management challenge because, unlike variable residential or commercial loads, they operate at relatively constant draw around the clock and across seasons — AEMO described them as similar to "large industrial loads." That flat, persistent demand profile puts pressure on the grid during off-peak periods when overall consumption is lowest, and can emerge faster than the generation and transmission infrastructure needed to serve it, risking supply bottlenecks, higher consumer prices, and increased reliance on fossil fuels.

  ## Supply additions and cancellations

  On the generation side, approximately 9 gigawatts (GW) of new generation and storage capacity reached full output in the 2025-26 financial year, double the record set the prior year. One gigawatt is roughly the output of a large coal-fired power station. AEMO chief executive Daniel Westerman said the surge in new capacity had improved the near-term reliability outlook.

  "A significant amount of new capacity is expected to be delivered between now and the early 2030s, helping to replace retiring generation and support growing electricity demand," Westerman said in a statement accompanying the report. "Beyond 2030, the next wave of investment will be critical to maintaining reliability."

  Not all planned supply has materialized. More than a third of the data center projects AEMO listed in last year's statement have since been canceled, the operator reported, underscoring the gap between announced pipelines and projects that reach completion.

  ## Retiring generation

  The demand surge arrives as the grid simultaneously loses firm dispatchable capacity. Some 13 GW of coal-fired generation and nearly 2 GW of gas generation are now scheduled for retirement within the decade. Coal and gas plants provide power that can be called on regardless of weather conditions, a characteristic that intermittent renewable sources such as wind and solar cannot replicate without storage.

  ## What it means for the grid

  The combination of fast-rising data center demand, large-scale fossil fuel retirements, and an uneven project completion rate sets up a reliability test for Australia's electricity system in the second half of the 2030s. AEMO's report identifies the period beyond 2030 as the window in which a sustained second wave of generation and storage investment will be required to keep the system balanced. The operator's warning that demand can come online faster than infrastructure can be built to serve it frames that investment timing as the central policy and commercial challenge of the decade ahead.
body_zh: |
  澳洲能源市場營運商（AEMO）在最新一期年度《電力機遇聲明》中指出，澳洲資料中心的用電量未來十年內將成長近七倍，對一個正處於燃煤與天然氣電廠退役過渡期的電網而言，將帶來沉重壓力。

  依據預測，資料中心至2035至36財政年度，將從涵蓋澳洲東部與南部的互聯電網——國家電力市場（NEM）——每年抽取34太瓦時（TWh）的電力。屆時，資料中心在NEM總需求中的占比，將從目前的3%大幅躍升至13%。作為參考，一太瓦時大約相當於澳洲20萬個普通家庭的年度用電量。

  ## 建設規模

  上述成長預測，反映了澳洲積極爭取資料中心投資的企圖心。澳洲聯邦銀行估計，至2030年，相關投資規模可望達到1,500億澳元（約1,080億美元）。澳洲聯邦政府正力推新設施採用再生能源，不過AEMO指出，這項政策已在部分地方政府層級遭遇阻力。

  資料中心在電網管理上帶來特殊挑戰：與浮動不定的住宅或商業用電需求不同，資料中心全天候、跨季節地維持相對穩定的電力消耗——AEMO將其比擬為「大型工業負載」。這種平穩且持續的需求特性，在整體用電量最低的離峰時段同樣會對電網形成壓力；加上資料中心的需求成長往往快過與之配套的發電與輸電基礎設施的建設速度，恐引發供電瓶頸、推高民眾電費，並加重對化石燃料的依賴。

  ## 供電新增與取消

  在供電端，2025至26財政年度約有9吉瓦（GW）的新發電與儲能容量正式投入滿載運轉，是前一年度創下紀錄的兩倍。一吉瓦大致相當於一座大型燃煤電廠的發電輸出量。AEMO執行長丹尼爾．韋斯特曼（Daniel Westerman）表示，新增容量的大幅躍升已改善了近期的供電可靠度展望。

  「預計從現在到2030年代初期，將有大量新產能陸續投入，有助於填補退役機組所留下的缺口、支撐日益成長的電力需求，」韋斯特曼在隨報告同步發布的聲明中表示。「2030年後，下一波投資將是維持供電可靠度的關鍵所在。」

  然而，並非所有規劃中的供電計畫都能如期實現。AEMO指出，去年度聲明中所列的資料中心項目，已有逾三分之一宣告取消，此一現象凸顯出已公告的開發管線與最終落地完工之間的顯著落差。

  ## 退役發電機組

  在需求急速攀升的同時，電網也正面臨穩定可調度容量的流失。未來十年內，約13吉瓦的燃煤發電機組與近2吉瓦的天然氣發電機組預計將陸續退役。燃煤與天然氣電廠的核心優勢，在於可依需求隨時調度出力、不受天候條件左右——這是風力與太陽能等間歇性再生能源在缺乏儲能配套的情況下所無法複製的特性。

  ## 對電網的影響

  資料中心需求快速攀升、大規模化石燃料發電機組退場，加上項目實際完工率參差不齊，三重因素相互疊加，將在2030年代後半對澳洲電力系統形成嚴峻的可靠度考驗。AEMO報告將2030年後定位為關鍵視窗，屆時必須啟動持續且大規模的第二波發電與儲能投資，方能維持系統供需平衡。該營運商警告，資料中心需求可能比基礎設施建設更快到位，這也使投資時機的精準拿捏，成為未來十年最核心的政策與商業挑戰。
