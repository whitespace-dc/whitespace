body_en: |
  PJM Interconnection's 2028/29 capacity auction came up 6,831 megawatts short of the grid operator's own reliability requirement, and the Department of Energy has for the sixth time ordered a retiring Pennsylvania power plant to remain available, as rapidly growing data center demand strains both PJM's generation reserves and the regulatory processes designed to govern large-load growth.

  ## The capacity shortfall and backstop procurement

  PJM's July Base Residual Auction procured 138,318 MW through the Reliability Pricing Model. Including fixed-resource-requirement capacity, total procured capacity reached 149,181.6 MW UCAP, which was 6,831.3 MW below PJM's Reliability Requirement for the 2028/29 delivery year.

  To address the gap, PJM filed a Reliability Backstop Procurement (RBP) proposal with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) on July 31 after its board of managers advanced the plan. The procurement is targeted to begin September 30, subject to FERC approval, with results expected in December. Resources secured through the RBP would receive fixed prices for terms of up to 15 years, at a maximum weighted-average willingness to pay of $555 per MW-day. New generation resources covered by bilateral agreements or otherwise brought to the system by large loads would reduce the amount PJM procures through the backstop.

  ## Eddystone: a sixth emergency extension

  The DOE issued an order on August 21 under Section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act, requiring PJM and Constellation Energy to keep 760 MW of generation at the Eddystone Generating Station in Pennsylvania available through November 20. The order covers Units 3 and 4, each rated at 380 MW and capable of running on natural gas or oil. Both units were scheduled to retire on May 31, 2025; the August 21 directive is the sixth DOE order extending or preserving their availability.

  The order requires PJM to economically dispatch the Eddystone units when needed and specifies that the units "shall not be considered a capacity resource," meaning they do not count toward PJM's formal capacity obligations.

  DOE's reliability analysis projects approximately 25 GW of load growth in PJM's footprint, with 15 GW attributed to data centers, against roughly 17 GW of announced fossil-fuel retirements. DOE modeling estimated around 430 loss-of-load hours under average weather and up to 1,052 hours in a worst-case scenario, with a maximum unserved load of approximately 21.3 GW. The DOE characterized these as stress scenarios rather than forecasts, illustrating why the combination of load growth and retirements constitutes a reliability concern.

  ## Data center demand and the pace problem

  PJM separately forecasts 32 GW of total load growth by 2030, with 30 GW expected to come from large loads, primarily data centers. PJM also recorded a preliminary all-time peak load of 168,158 MW on July 2.

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, said the fundamental problem is timing. "Load is arriving faster than replacement generation, and transmission can become executable," he said. DOE's order attributes the reliability concern to a "timing mismatch between resource retirements, load growth and the pace of new generation entry."

  Darryl Lawrence, Pennsylvania's consumer advocate and director of the state's Office of Consumer Advocate, said PJM's reliability position has changed significantly after decades of ample reserve margins. "During extreme weather conditions, PJM's system is fragile," Lawrence said. "And this is not something that we've seen in my time in the business, which is 20-plus years." He compared the scale of new load growth to the period when steel mills were being built.

  ## The stakeholder process that produced no result

  PJM had attempted to address large-load policy through its Critical Issue Fast Path (CIFP) process, an accelerated mechanism for resolving priority policy questions through stakeholder votes. Launched in 2025, the process generated 12 distinct proposals but produced no consensus; all 12 were voted down and none advanced as a recommendation to PJM's board of managers.

  A transcript of a July 23 FERC technical conference on PJM governance showed how sharply participants disagreed about the outcome. Pennsylvania Deputy Secretary of Policy Jacob Finkel, who attended a postmortem at PJM's Valley Forge headquarters, described the mood among members as self-congratulatory. "The members went around patting themselves on the back for what a thoughtful, deliberative, creative process they had just gone through," Finkel said. His own assessment: "This is a failure. You have not advanced the ball."

  Osnato argued the problem was structural. The stakeholder process was being asked to settle a question involving participants with materially different economic interests and conflicting views on who should pay for new generation. "At some point, more alternatives do not create consensus. They create more ways to avoid making the underlying decision," he said.

  Joseph Bowring, president of Monitoring Analytics and PJM's independent market monitor, said the process retained value but was distorted by participants' incentives. "Stakeholders were generally pursuing their narrow self interest and ignoring the broader public interest in efficient, competitive markets that assign costs and risks to those that cause them, in this case the data center load," Bowring told Data Center Knowledge.

  ## Policy direction versus technical implementation

  Several participants at the FERC conference argued the CIFP's failure reflected a category error: the process was asked to make policy rather than work through implementation.

  Abe Silverman, assistant research scholar at Johns Hopkins University's Ralph O'Connor Sustainable Energy Institute, said the problem arose "when we ask committees to decide policy." Large-load integration should begin with PJM, its board, and the states establishing the policy direction, with stakeholders then addressing technical and implementation questions. "We would have saved so many hours of meetings," Silverman said.

  Pamela Quinlan, executive director of the New Jersey Energy Resilience Bank, made a similar argument while cautioning against abandoning the technical stakeholder process. Direction should come from leadership and the board; stakeholders should provide the technical expertise for implementation, she said.

  PJM's board ultimately drew on the CIFP record to develop its own large-load framework. The January decision addressed load forecasting, "bring your own new generation" (BYONG) arrangements through which large loads can contract directly for new generation, expedited interconnection for new generators, and a reliability backstop.

  ## Who pays

  The cost allocation question runs through both the RBP and the Eddystone orders.

  Bowring said PJM's backstop procurement proposal would shift costs to customers who are not driving the new load. "The RBP process would require other customers to pay for data center load, despite PJM's assertions to the contrary," he said. "The RBP approach is going in the wrong direction."

  On Eddystone, Constellation has reached an agreement with PJM to recover the costs of complying with the DOE order, including operating and required maintenance costs, with PJM allocating those costs using the same approach it applies to capacity costs. The DOE order requires PJM to use economic dispatch to minimize costs to ratepayers but does not resolve who ultimately bears the cost of retaining retiring generation.

  Lawrence said Pennsylvania's large-load tariff applies a "but for" test: if infrastructure would not be built without a specific customer, that customer should bear the cost. Applying that test to Eddystone is more difficult, he said, because electricity cannot be traced to a specific generating unit or data center.

  Elizabeth Whitney, managing principal at Meguire Whitney, said the Trump administration's declaration of a national energy emergency has created a broader framework for repeated use of Section 202(c) authority, which has historically functioned as a short-term reliability tool. "If there is a statutory benefit to being in an emergency situation, then there's going to be political pressure to find an emergency to justify that use," she said. Repeated reliance on emergency authority raises the question of how long such measures can remain in effect before they effectively become part of normal resource planning, she added.

  Google's Brian George, the company's senior lead for U.S. energy markets, told FERC that Google has billions of dollars invested in PJM and operates data centers throughout the region. He said the company needs greater certainty about market rules before committing capital to additional power resources. "We can't do that without the certainty that the market rules are going to exist in such a way that we know that investment is going to be worthwhile," George said.

  ## Forecast uncertainty and the evidence problem

  Underlying the reliability debate is uncertainty about how much data center demand will materialize and when. Data center load accounts for 15 GW, or 60 percent, of the 25 GW of PJM load growth in DOE's model, but that demand is less certain in timing and scale than a retiring power plant.

  "A generator retirement is relatively binary. A 15 GW data-center forecast is not," Osnato said.

  Lawrence raised a specific forecasting problem: the same data center project can appear in multiple utility territory projections simultaneously while developers negotiate for service with different providers, potentially causing more than one utility to count the same project in its load forecast even though only one can ultimately serve it. "We potentially have a double-counting problem," he said.

  Whitney said power constraints could cause developers to adjust plans in ways that alter projections. "I don't know that we can really reliably trust any modeling right now that is even three to five years out," she said.

  Osnato said the uncertainty sharpens rather than diminishes the evidentiary standard grid planners should apply before acting on large-load projections. "If forecast large-load growth is consequential enough to justify preventing physical generation from retiring, then the durability of that demand assumption becomes part of the reliability question itself," he said.

  ## What comes next

  PJM's backstop procurement is targeted to run through September and October, with results expected in December. Eddystone's current extension runs through November 20.

  Osnato said the two policy tracks address different parts of the same problem on different timelines. "Eddystone addresses the supply side of an existing timing gap. PJM's large-load reforms address the demand-entry side of the future gap." PJM's recent large-load reforms change the conditions under which future data centers connect to the grid but do not resolve the immediate problem of generation already scheduled to retire.

  ## 容量缺口與備援採購

  PJM七月份的基準殘餘拍賣（Base Residual Auction）依照可靠度定價模型（Reliability Pricing Model）共採購138,318 MW。加計固定資源需求容量後，總採購容量達到149,181.6 MW UCAP，仍較PJM對2028/29年度所設定的可靠度需求低了6,831.3 MW。

  為彌補此一缺口，PJM董事會通過相關方案後，於7月31日向聯邦能源監管委員會（FERC）提交可靠度備援採購（Reliability Backstop Procurement，RBP）計畫。採購預計於9月30日啟動，惟須待FERC核准，結果預計於12月公布。透過備援採購取得的資源將獲得固定價格，合約期最長15年，加權平均最高願付價格為每MW·日555美元。若大型負載透過雙邊協議或其他方式自行引進新發電資源，PJM可相應減少備援採購的規模。

  ## Eddystone電廠：第六度緊急延壽

  DOE於8月21日依據《聯邦電力法》（Federal Power Act）第202(c)條發布命令，要求PJM與星座能源（Constellation Energy）維持賓州Eddystone發電廠760 MW的發電能力，有效期至11月20日。此命令涵蓋3號與4號機組，各380 MW，可燃燒天然氣或燃油。兩組機組原訂於2025年5月31日退役；8月21日的命令是DOE第六度延長或保留其可用狀態。

  命令要求PJM在有需要時以經濟調度原則運行Eddystone機組，並明確規定這些機組「不得視為容量資源」，意即不計入PJM正式容量義務。

  DOE的可靠度分析預估，PJM服務範圍內的負載將成長約25 GW，其中15 GW來自資料中心，同期間已宣布退役的化石燃料發電機組則約達17 GW。DOE模型估算，在平均氣候條件下，每年約有430個「失負荷小時」（loss-of-load hours），最惡劣情境下可達1,052小時，最大未供電負載約為21.3 GW。DOE將這些定性為壓力情境，而非正式預測，藉以說明負載成長與機組退役合力構成可靠度隱憂的原因。

  ## 資料中心需求與時程問題

  PJM另行預測，至2030年總負載將成長32 GW，其中30 GW預計來自大型負載，主要為資料中心。PJM同時記錄到7月2日的初步史上最高峰值負載，達168,158 MW。

  Persistence Analytics Group創辦人Neil Osnato指出，根本問題在於時序。「負載增加的速度，超過替代發電資源上線及輸電線路落實執行的速度，」他說。DOE的命令也將此次可靠度疑慮歸因於「機組退役、負載成長與新增發電進場速度之間的時序錯位」。

  賓州消費者倡議辦公室主任Darryl Lawrence表示，PJM的可靠度處境與數十年來備用容量充裕的局面相比，已有重大改變。「在極端天候條件下，PJM的系統相當脆弱，」Lawrence說，「這是我從事這一行逾20年來未曾見過的情況。」他將此次新增負載的規模，比喻為當年鋼鐵廠大舉興建的時代。

  ## 利害關係人程序無疾而終

  PJM曾試圖透過「關鍵議題快速通道」（Critical Issue Fast Path，CIFP）程序處理大型負載政策，這是一種透過利害關係人投票加速解決優先政策問題的機制。此程序於2025年啟動，共產生12項不同提案，但最終未獲共識；12項提案全數遭到否決，無一以建議案形式提交PJM董事會。

  一份7月23日FERC技術研討會的逐字稿顯示，與會者對結果看法分歧極大。賓州政策副部長Jacob Finkel曾出席PJM在Valley Forge總部召開的檢討會，他描述現場成員的氣氛是自我表揚。「成員們互相拍拍背，慶賀彼此剛完成了一場多麼周詳、審慎、富有創意的過程，」Finkel說。他本人的評語則是：「這是一次失敗。你們根本沒有向前推進。」

  Osnato認為問題出在結構上。利害關係人程序被要求解決的問題，牽涉經濟利益截然不同、且對誰應為新增發電買單見解相左的各方參與者。「在某個時間點後，更多的選項並不會帶來共識，只會創造出更多迴避核心決定的方式，」他說。

  Monitoring Analytics總裁、PJM獨立市場監管機構負責人Joseph Bowring表示，程序本身仍有價值，但被參與者的利益動機所扭曲。「利害關係人普遍追求自身狹隘利益，忽視了更廣泛的公共利益——即在有效率、具競爭性的市場中，將成本與風險歸咎於造成者，在此案中便是資料中心負載，」Bowring告訴Data Center Knowledge。

  ## 政策方向與技術執行之別

  FERC研討會上，多位與會者認為，CIFP的失敗反映出一種根本性的分類錯誤：該程序被要求制訂政策，而非討論執行細節。

  約翰霍普金斯大學Ralph O'Connor永續能源研究所助理研究學者Abe Silverman表示，問題源於「我們請委員會來決定政策」。大型負載整合應先由PJM、其董事會及各州確立政策方向，再由利害關係人處理技術與執行問題。「這本可以省下無數小時的會議，」Silverman說。

  紐澤西州能源韌性銀行執行長Pamela Quinlan也提出類似論點，同時警告不應放棄技術性利害關係人程序。方向應由領導層與董事會提供；利害關係人則提供執行所需的技術專業，她說。

  PJM董事會最終援引CIFP的記錄，自行制定大型負載框架。1月份的決定涵蓋負載預測、允許大型負載直接與新發電資源簽約的「自備新增發電」（bring your own new generation，BYONG）安排、新發電機組的快速併網，以及可靠度備援機制。

  ## 費用由誰買單

  費用分攤問題貫穿了備援採購（RBP）與Eddystone命令兩條政策線。

  Bowring表示，PJM的備援採購提案將把費用轉嫁給並非新增負載來源的其他用戶。「RBP程序將要求其他用戶為資料中心負載買單，儘管PJM有不同的說法，」他說，「RBP的做法走錯了方向。」

  在Eddystone電廠方面，Constellation已與PJM達成協議，由PJM就其遵循DOE命令的相關費用（含運營及必要維護費用）給予補償，PJM將採用與容量費用相同的分攤方式處理這些成本。DOE命令要求PJM採用經濟調度原則以降低用戶負擔，但並未解決誰最終須承擔保留退役電廠成本的問題。

  Lawrence表示，賓州的大型負載費率適用「但若非」（but for）檢驗標準：若某項基礎設施若非特定用戶的需求便不會興建，則費用應由該用戶承擔。他說，將此標準套用於Eddystone較為困難，因為電力無法追溯至特定發電機組或特定資料中心。

  Meguire Whitney管理合夥人Elizabeth Whitney表示，川普政府宣布全國能源緊急狀態，已為重複援引第202(c)條授權創造了更寬廣的框架；此授權在歷史上主要作為短期可靠度工具使用。「若在緊急狀態下存在法定利益，政治上就會有壓力去找到緊急狀態，以正當化該權力的行使，」她說。她補充，反覆依賴緊急授權引發了一個問題：這類措施在持續多久後，實際上將成為常態資源規劃的一部分。

  谷歌（Google）美國能源市場資深主管Brian George告訴FERC，谷歌在PJM區域投入了數十億美元，並在全區運營資料中心。他表示，公司在承諾追加電力資源投資前，需要對市場規則有更高的確定性。「在市場規則能夠以讓我們確信投資值得的方式確立之前，我們無法這樣做，」George說。

  ## 預測不確定性與舉證問題

  可靠度爭議的底層，是對資料中心需求最終規模及其實現時序的不確定性。在DOE模型中，資料中心負載佔25 GW總成長量的15 GW，即60%，但其時序與規模的確定性遠不及一座退役電廠。

  「發電機組退役相對二元。15 GW的資料中心預測則不然，」Osnato說。

  Lawrence提出一個具體的預測問題：同一個資料中心開發案，可能同時出現在多個電力業者的服務區域預測中——因為開發商在與不同業者談判供電合約期間，可能導致不只一家電力業者將同一案件計入負載預測，但最終只有一家能夠實際供電。「我們可能存在重複計算的問題，」他說。

  Whitney表示，電力供應限制可能促使開發商調整計畫，進而改變預測結果。「我不確定我們現在是否真的能夠可靠地信任任何三至五年後的模型，」她說。

  Osnato表示，不確定性不但沒有降低電網規劃者在採信大型負載預測前應達到的舉證標準，反而提高了這道門檻。「若預測中的大型負載成長足以正當化阻止現有發電機組退役，那麼這些需求假設的可持續性本身，就成了可靠度問題的一部分，」他說。

  ## 後續發展

  PJM的備援採購預計於9月至10月間進行，結果預計於12月公布。Eddystone的現行延壽命令有效期至11月20日。

  Osnato表示，兩條政策線在不同時間軸上分別處理同一問題的不同面向。「Eddystone處理的是現有時序缺口的供給面；PJM的大型負載改革則處理的是未來缺口的需求進場面。」PJM近期對大型負載的改革，改變了未來資料中心併入電網的條件，但無法解決已排定退役的電廠所帶來的眼前問題。
