  ## LNG storage enters data center infrastructure planning

  Data centers that co-locate on-site liquefied natural gas (LNG) storage with behind-the-meter power generation are now subject to a more complex and coordinated fire protection regime, fire protection consultancy Jensen Hughes said in an analysis of the sector. The firm argues that treating LNG fuel systems as core infrastructure rather than ancillary utilities is the defining factor in whether a facility achieves stable, resilient operations.

  The shift is being driven by the scale of power demand from artificial-intelligence workloads. Hyperscale and large enterprise data centers are turning to natural gas-fueled behind-the-meter generation to sidestep delays and uncertainties tied to utility interconnection. As a backstop against gas supply interruptions, some projects are adding on-site LNG storage that can be vaporized on demand to keep turbines running when pipeline supply falls short.

  ## Why LNG is entering the data center fuel equation

  LNG — natural gas that has been cooled to roughly minus 162 degrees Celsius, reducing it to liquid form for storage and transport — occupies approximately one six-hundredth the volume of the same quantity of gas in its vapor state, making it a relatively space-efficient storage option. The United States currently operates more than 100 LNG storage facilities, which utilities use to supplement pipeline flow on days of peak demand. According to Jensen Hughes, those existing facilities are not sized or positioned to serve the additional requirements of data centers during high-load periods, making on-site storage an operational necessity for some projects.

  ## The fire protection challenge of mixed-use sites

  Co-locating LNG storage, power generation equipment, and mission-critical computing infrastructure on a single site creates fire protection exposures that differ from any one of those uses alone. LNG in liquid form is not itself flammable; the hazard arises during an unintended release, when the liquid vaporizes, forms a flammable gas cloud, and can migrate to an ignition source. Standard mitigation measures, according to Jensen Hughes, include strategic separation of potential leak points from ignition sources and occupied buildings, spill containment systems, automated fire and gas detection, emergency shutdown systems, and fire water infrastructure.

  A further complication is that fire protection planning for behind-the-meter power installations and for the data center itself has frequently been conducted in parallel rather than in coordination. Where adequate physical separation exists between the two sides of a site, designers have sometimes treated them independently. Jensen Hughes contends that as facilities scale up, spacing alone is no longer a sufficient safeguard and that a unified, site-wide fire protection strategy is required.

  ## Regulatory landscape is tightening

  Two national fire protection standards govern these sites, and the regulatory weight of at least one of them is increasing. NFPA 850, the standard that addresses fire protection for electric generating plants, was upgraded from a "recommended practice" to a "standard" in its 2026 edition. The distinction matters: authorities having jurisdiction — the state and local officials who enforce building and fire codes — are likely to begin adopting and enforcing NFPA 850 requirements rather than treating them as advisory guidance.

  LNG installations are separately governed by NFPA 59A, the Standard for the Production, Storage and Handling of Liquefied Natural Gas, which sets out facility siting and fire protection requirements and is typically enforced at the state level for data center projects.

  ## Performance-based modeling over rule-of-thumb distances

  Jensen Hughes advocates for vapor dispersion and fire modeling as a foundation for design decisions at sites where LNG storage and data center operations share a footprint. That modeling, the firm said, provides defensible insight into credible fire scenarios at mixed-use sites and can be used not only to design the LNG installation's own protective systems but also to quantify and mitigate risks to the data center and other occupied structures. The approach contrasts with fixed setback distances derived from prescriptive code tables, which the firm characterizes as rule-of-thumb rather than scenario-specific.

  ## Industry significance

  The convergence of LNG process safety and data center fire protection represents a category of risk that the industry has not previously had to manage at scale. As behind-the-meter power strategies proliferate and on-site fuel storage becomes more common, the coordination between power-plant fire protection standards like NFPA 850 and LNG-specific requirements under NFPA 59A will increasingly determine the viability and insurability of large data center developments. With NFPA 850 moving from guidance to enforceable standard beginning in 2026, developers and their insurers face a more defined — and potentially more demanding — compliance baseline for new projects.

  ## LNG儲存進入資料中心基礎設施規劃

  防火保護顧問公司Jensen Hughes在一份針對產業的分析報告中指出，將液化天然氣（LNG）現場儲存設施與表後發電系統共置的資料中心，如今必須面對更加複雜且環環相扣的防火保護規範。該公司認為，能否將LNG燃料系統視為核心基礎設施，而非附屬公用設備，是決定設施能否達到穩定且具韌性營運的關鍵因素。

  這股轉變的驅動力，來自人工智慧工作負載所帶來的龐大電力需求。超大規模資料中心與大型企業資料中心，正紛紛轉向以天然氣驅動的表後發電，藉此繞開公用電力併網所衍生的延誤與不確定性。為防範天然氣供應中斷，部分專案更在現場增設LNG儲存設備，可依需求將液態天然氣汽化，確保在管線供氣不足時仍能維持渦輪機正常運轉。

  ## 為何LNG進入資料中心燃料方程式

  LNG是將天然氣冷卻至約攝氏負162度後液化所得，體積僅為同量氣態天然氣的約六百分之一，使其成為相對省空間的儲存選項。美國目前設有超過100座LNG儲存設施，供電力公司在尖峰用電日補充管線流量之用。然而Jensen Hughes指出，這些既有設施無論在規模或地點上，均不足以因應資料中心在高負載時期的額外需求，因此對部分專案而言，在現場自設儲存設備已成為營運上的必要之舉。

  ## 混合用途場址的防火挑戰

  將LNG儲存、發電設備與關鍵任務運算基礎設施共置於同一場址，所帶來的防火暴露風險，有別於上述任何單一用途本身的風險。液態LNG本身並不可燃；危害在於意外洩漏之際——液體汽化後形成可燃氣體雲，一旦飄移至點火源附近，即可能引發火災。根據Jensen Hughes的說明，標準緩解措施包括：戰略性地將潛在洩漏點與點火源及有人使用的建築物保持適當距離、設置洩漏圍堵系統、自動化火災與氣體偵測設備、緊急停機系統，以及消防用水基礎設施。

  另一項複雜因素在於，表後電力設施與資料中心本身的防火規劃，過去往往是各自並行推進，而非協調整合。若場址兩側之間存在充足的實體分隔，設計者有時會將兩者獨立處理。Jensen Hughes主張，隨著設施規模持續擴大，單靠空間距離已不再是充分的安全保障，必須建立統一的全場址防火策略。

  ## 法規環境趨嚴

  目前有兩項全國性防火保護標準規範這類場址，其中至少一項的法規效力正在提升。NFPA 850——針對電力發電廠防火保護的標準——在2026年版本中已從「建議規範」升格為「標準」。這項區別至關重要：各地權責主管機關——即負責執行建築與消防法規的州及地方官員——很可能開始採用並強制執行NFPA 850的要求，而非將其視為建議性指引。

  LNG設施另受NFPA 59A規範——即《液化天然氣生產、儲存與處理標準》——其規定涵蓋設施選址與防火保護要求，在資料中心專案中通常由州政府層級執法。

  ## 以效能模型取代經驗距離

  Jensen Hughes倡導以蒸氣擴散模擬與火災模擬，作為LNG儲存設施與資料中心共用場址時的設計決策基礎。該公司表示，此類模擬能針對混合用途場址的可信火災情境提供具說服力的洞察，不僅可用於設計LNG設施本身的防護系統，更可用來量化並降低對資料中心及其他有人使用建築物的風險。這一做法有別於源自規範性法規條文的固定退縮距離——Jensen Hughes將後者定性為經驗法則，而非針對特定情境的設計依據。

  ## 產業意義

  LNG製程安全與資料中心防火保護的匯流，代表一類產業過去從未需要大規模應對的風險類型。隨著表後電力策略日益普及、現場燃料儲存愈趨普遍，NFPA 850等電廠防火保護標準與NFPA 59A所規範的LNG特定要求之間的協調整合，將愈來愈左右大型資料中心開發案的可行性與保險承保能力。NFPA 850自2026年起由指引升格為強制標準，開發商及其保險業者在新案合規基準上，將面對更明確——且可能更嚴苛——的要求。
