  ## PLN targets 7.9 GW of geothermal capacity by 2033

  PT PLN (Persero), Indonesia's state electricity company, has announced plans to develop 110 geothermal power projects that would raise the country's installed geothermal capacity from the current 2.8 gigawatts (GW) to 7.9 GW by 2033, an addition of 5.1 GW, according to PLN Director General Darmawan Prasodjo.

  The program is embedded in PLN's 2025–2034 Electricity Supply Business Plan, known by its Indonesian acronym RUPTL, which sets out the utility's long-term generation roadmap.

  ## Project breakdown: PLN versus independent producers

  Of the 110 projects, PLN will develop 25 itself, accounting for 0.6 GW of new capacity. The remaining 85 projects, representing 4.5 GW, will be built by independent power producers (IPPs) — private companies that generate and sell electricity under contract to the grid.

  Within the IPP portion, PLN is opening two collaboration frameworks for private partners. The Geothermal Exploration & Energy Conversion Agreement (GEECA) applies to geothermal working areas (WKP) that are already under PLN's control, while the Steam Sales & Energy Conversion Agreement (SECA) applies to WKPs held by non-PLN entities. Under these two schemes, 79 projects are planned — 45 under GEECA and 34 under SECA — with projected combined capacity additions of 3.1 GW.

  "PLN is opening collaboration as broadly as possible for partners through the GEECA and SECA schemes," Darmawan said. "Through this fair collaboration, we hope to accelerate geothermal development as one of the domestic new and renewable energy sources."

  ## Government backing and energy security rationale

  The push for geothermal expansion has direct backing from President Prabowo Subianto, who has ordered the government to find alternative energy pathways to strengthen national energy sovereignty, according to the source.

  Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia, speaking at the Indonesia International Geothermal Convention and Exhibition (IIGCE 2026) in Jakarta on August 19, framed the acceleration as a means of reducing Indonesia's dependence on imported fossil fuels. "There is no other way than to accelerate geothermal implementation so it can become electricity or something else. There must be collaboration between businesses and regulators," Bahlil said.

  ## Indonesia's geothermal potential and global ambitions

  Indonesia holds an estimated geothermal potential of 23.2 GW, the largest resource base in the world, according to the source. The government has set a target for Indonesia to become the world's top geothermal energy producer through what it calls Geothermal Vision 30.1 by 2030.

  Darmawan said reaching 7.9 GW would position Indonesia as potentially the largest geothermal producer globally. Current installed capacity stands at 2.8 GW.

  Julfi Hadi, chairman of the Indonesian Geothermal Association (API), described collaboration as the essential factor in realizing that potential. He noted that geothermal energy is a base load resource — meaning it can supply electricity continuously and reliably, unlike solar or wind power, which are intermittent. "Indonesia has local resources, indigenous resources — geothermal that is the largest in the world. Geothermal energy has the special characteristic of being able to serve as a reliable base load," Julfi said. "It is the only base load renewable that exists, and it is also renewable, clean, and stretches from Sabang to Merauke."

  ## What the expansion means

  If completed as planned, the 5.1 GW addition would nearly triple Indonesia's installed geothermal capacity within the decade, making geothermal a significant pillar of the country's renewable energy mix and reducing reliance on imported fossil fuels. The GEECA and SECA frameworks are intended to lower barriers for private capital to enter the sector by providing structured contractual paths for both exploration-stage and steam-supply projects.

  The government's Geothermal Vision 30.1 target — reaching the top global position by 2030 — precedes the RUPTL's 2033 endpoint, meaning the pace of project development under the 110-project pipeline will be a key determinant of whether Indonesia meets that earlier milestone.
body_zh: |
  ## PLN目標：2033年地熱裝置容量達7.9 GW

  印尼國家電力公司PT PLN（Persero）宣布，計畫推動110項地熱發電計畫，將全國地熱裝置容量從目前的2.8 GW提升至7.9 GW，增幅達5.1 GW，預計於2033年達標。上述目標由PLN局長達爾萬·普拉索賈（Darmawan Prasodjo）對外公布。

  此一計畫納入PLN「2025—2034年電力供應事業計畫」（印尼文縮寫RUPTL），作為該公司長期電力建設藍圖的核心內容。

  ## 計畫分工：PLN自建與獨立發電業者

  110項計畫中，PLN將自行開發25項，合計新增裝置容量0.6 GW；其餘85項、合計4.5 GW則交由獨立發電業者（IPP）——即以簽約方式向電網售電的民間發電企業——負責興建。

  在IPP部分，PLN為民間合作夥伴開設兩種合作框架：「地熱探勘與能源轉換協議」（GEECA）適用於已納入PLN管轄的地熱工作區（WKP）；「蒸汽銷售與能源轉換協議」（SECA）則適用於非PLN實體持有的WKP。在此兩種機制下，共規劃79項計畫——其中GEECA涵蓋45項，SECA涵蓋34項——預計合計新增裝置容量3.1 GW。

  達爾萬表示：「PLN正透過GEECA與SECA機制，盡可能廣泛地向合作夥伴開放合作機會。透過這種公平合作模式，我們期望加速地熱開發，使其成為國內新興再生能源之一。」

  ## 政府背書與能源安全戰略

  此波地熱擴張計畫獲得總統普拉伯沃·蘇比安托（Prabowo Subianto）的直接背書。據報導，普拉伯沃已下令政府尋求替代能源路徑，以強化印尼的能源主權。

  能源暨礦產資源部長巴赫利爾·拉哈達利亞（Bahlil Lahadalia）於8月19日在雅加達舉行的「印尼國際地熱大會暨展覽（IIGCE 2026）」上發言，將加速地熱發展定位為降低印尼對進口化石燃料依賴的關鍵途徑。他說：「除了加快地熱開發，將其轉化為電力或其他用途，別無他法。企業與主管機關之間必須攜手合作。」

  ## 印尼地熱潛力與全球雄心

  據報導，印尼估計擁有23.2 GW的地熱潛力，是全球資源蘊藏量最豐富的國家。印尼政府已設定目標，要透過所謂「地熱願景30.1」（Geothermal Vision 30.1），在2030年前躋身全球最大地熱能源生產國。

  達爾萬指出，若裝置容量達到7.9 GW，印尼有望成為全球最大的地熱發電國。目前已完成的裝置容量為2.8 GW。

  印尼地熱協會（API）理事長朱爾菲·哈迪（Julfi Hadi）表示，合作是實現這一潛力的關鍵因素。他強調，地熱能源屬於基載電源——意即能夠持續穩定供電，不像太陽能或風能那樣具有間歇性。「印尼擁有本地資源、本土資源——地熱資源是全世界最豐富的。地熱能源具備可作為可靠基載電源的特殊優勢，」朱爾菲說。「它是唯一能作為基載電源的再生能源，同時也是再生、清潔的能源，縱貫薩邦至梅勞給整片土地。」

  ## 這波擴張意味著什麼

  若計畫如期完成，新增的5.1 GW將使印尼地熱裝置容量在十年內近乎增加兩倍，讓地熱成為該國再生能源結構的重要支柱，並降低對進口化石燃料的依賴。GEECA與SECA兩種框架的目的，在於為探勘階段及蒸汽供應類計畫提供結構化的合約路徑，藉此降低民間資本進入此一領域的門檻。

  政府的「地熱願景30.1」目標——於2030年前登上全球第一——比RUPTL的2033年期限更早，這意味著110項計畫的推進速度，將是決定印尼能否提前達成這一里程碑的關鍵因素。
