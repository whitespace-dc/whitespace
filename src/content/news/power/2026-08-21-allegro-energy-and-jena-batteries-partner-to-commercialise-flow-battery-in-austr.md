body_en: |
  Allegro Energy has entered a strategic partnership with Jena Batteries and its parent company Suqian Time Energy Technology to accelerate the commercial deployment of its flow battery technology, with pilot systems planned for Australia before any expansion into international markets.

  The agreement pairs Allegro's proprietary water-based microemulsion electrolyte with Jena Batteries' manufacturing and system integration expertise. Allegro, an Australian long-duration energy storage company, developed the electrolyte chemistry to address cost, safety, and performance barriers that have constrained wider adoption of long-duration storage technologies.

  ## Partnership structure

  Rather than building its own manufacturing base, Allegro is using Jena Batteries and Suqian Time Energy Technology as an established production platform. The arrangement gives Allegro access to larger-scale manufacturing capacity without having to build that infrastructure itself.

  Jena Batteries described the two companies as bringing complementary strengths. "Allegro Energy has developed a promising approach to flow battery chemistry. By combining our complementary strengths, we are creating the foundation for safe, scalable and cost-effective long-duration energy storage solutions," a company spokesperson said.

  ## The technology

  Allegro's microemulsion electrolyte is water-based and non-flammable, and uses materials the company describes as readily available. The chemistry is designed for applications requiring repeated cycling over longer periods — a profile that suits electricity grids adding more wind and solar generation, and data centres seeking storage that can handle uneven demand patterns.

  Thomas Nann, Allegro's chief executive, said the company focused on developing the underlying chemistry and is now relying on its partners' experience to convert that into deployable systems at scale. "Allegro has spent years developing the chemistry. Jena Batteries brings significant manufacturing and system integration capability. Combining those strengths gives us a pathway to take an Australian-developed technology and prove that it can be manufactured and deployed at commercial scale," Nann said.

  ## Data centre demand

  The partnership comes as long-duration storage has drawn growing interest from data centre operators seeking storage capable of handling rapid cycling and irregular load profiles. The same demand driver applies to electricity grids integrating more wind and solar capacity.

  That interest is also prompting broader debate about the limits of lithium-ion batteries in some stationary storage settings. While lithium-ion remains a major part of the storage market, flow batteries and other chemistries are being examined for applications that require longer discharge periods and frequent cycling.

  ## The Australian cleantech challenge

  The partnership also addresses a recurring challenge for Australian clean technology developers: connecting domestic intellectual property with industrial-scale production. Allegro's approach — using an established manufacturing platform rather than building capacity from scratch — offers a potentially faster route to commercial deployment if pilot systems perform as expected.

  Nann framed the model as a way to prevent Australian-developed technology from remaining confined to the research stage. "The opportunity for Australia isn't simply to deploy batteries invented and manufactured somewhere else. We have the science and engineering capability to develop important new storage technologies here. Partnerships like this give Australian innovation a credible pathway into global manufacturing and global markets," he said.

  The arrangement also reflects the broader structure of the battery industry, where innovation, manufacturing, and project integration are often spread across different countries and specialist companies, the source material noted.

  ## What comes next

  Nann said the industry's central challenge has shifted from proving the need for long-duration storage to executing deployment at scale. "Long-duration storage is no longer principally a question of whether the world needs it. The challenge is how we manufacture and deploy it at the scale the energy transition now requires," he said.

  The companies plan to deploy pilot systems in Australia first, with international markets to be considered only after those pilots are completed.
body_zh: |
  澳洲長時儲能公司Allegro Energy日前宣布，與Jena Batteries及其母公司宿遷時代能源科技達成策略合作協議，加速旗下液流電池技術的商業化部署。雙方計畫先在澳洲進行試驗系統部署，完成後再考慮進軍國際市場。

  此次合作將Allegro自主研發的水基微乳化電解液，與Jena Batteries在製造及系統整合方面的專業能力相結合。Allegro開發這套電解液化學技術，正是為了克服長時儲能技術在成本、安全性與效能上的瓶頸，從而推動此類技術更廣泛地落地應用。

  ## 合作架構

  Allegro並未選擇自行建立製造基地，而是借助Jena Batteries與宿遷時代能源科技現有的生產平台。這項安排讓Allegro得以取得更大規模的製造產能，同時無需自行投入建置相關基礎設施。

  Jena Batteries表示，雙方各有所長、相輔相成。該公司發言人指出：「Allegro Energy在液流電池化學領域開發出極具潛力的方案。透過整合雙方的互補優勢，我們正為安全、可擴展且具成本效益的長時儲能解決方案奠定基礎。」

  ## 技術特點

  Allegro的微乳化電解液以水為基礎，不具可燃性，且所使用的材料據該公司表示均可輕易取得。這套化學技術專為需要長時間、反覆充放電的應用場景而設計——這樣的特性恰好契合持續擴大風電與太陽能發電比例的電力網路，以及需要應對不穩定負載模式的資料中心。

  Allegro執行長Thomas Nann表示，公司多年來專注於底層化學技術的研發，現在則有賴於合作夥伴的經驗，將這項技術轉化為可大規模部署的系統。他說：「Allegro多年來深耕化學技術的開發，Jena Batteries則擁有強大的製造與系統整合能力。兩者強強聯合，為我們開闢了一條路徑，讓這項澳洲自主研發的技術得以在商業規模下完成製造與部署。」

  ## 資料中心需求

  此次合作的背景，正值長時儲能技術引發資料中心業者日益濃厚的興趣。這些業者正在尋求能夠應對快速充放電循環與不規律負載特性的儲能方案。同樣的需求驅動力，也適用於持續整合更多風電與太陽能容量的電力網路。

  這股興趣同時引發了業界更廣泛的討論，探討鋰離子電池在部分固定式儲能應用場景中的局限性。儘管鋰離子電池仍是儲能市場的重要組成，但液流電池及其他化學體系，正被用於研究需要更長放電時間與頻繁循環的應用情境。

  ## 澳洲清潔科技的挑戰

  此次合作也回應了澳洲清潔科技開發商長期面臨的一項挑戰：如何將本土智慧財產與工業規模的生產能力相連結。Allegro的做法——借助既有製造平台，而非從零開始建置產能——若試驗系統表現符合預期，有望提供一條更快速通往商業化部署的道路。

  Nann將這套模式定位為防止澳洲自主研發技術止步於研究階段的解方。他表示：「澳洲的機會，絕不只是部署由其他地方發明、製造的電池。我們具備在本土開發重要新型儲能技術的科學與工程實力。這樣的合作，為澳洲的創新提供了一條可信的路徑，通往全球製造與全球市場。」

  此次合作安排也折射出電池產業的整體結構——創新、製造與專案整合往往分散於不同國家與專業公司之間。

  ## 下一步計畫

  Nann表示，業界的核心挑戰已從「證明長時儲能的必要性」，轉向「如何在能源轉型所需的規模下落實製造與部署」。他說：「長時儲能是否為全球所需，如今已不再是主要問題。挑戰在於，我們如何以能源轉型當前所需的規模來製造並部署它。」

  雙方計畫首先在澳洲部署試驗系統，待試驗完成後，才會評估進軍國際市場的可能性。
