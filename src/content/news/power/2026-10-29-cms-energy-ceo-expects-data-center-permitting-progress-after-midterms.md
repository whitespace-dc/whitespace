body_en: |
  CMS Energy CEO Garrick Rochow predicted that a contested data center project drawing up to 1 gigawatt (GW) from Consumers Energy's Michigan grid would see "more progress" on local permitting following next week's midterm elections, he told investors and analysts on the company's third-quarter earnings call Tuesday. The comments came as CMS quietly removed a pipeline slide showing its multi-gigawatt large-load backlog from the quarterly presentation materials.

  CMS Energy is the parent of Consumers Energy, which operates regulated gas and electric utilities in Michigan.

  ## Data center permitting and pipeline

  Rochow declined to name the operator but said the project — which Consumers announced an agreement to serve earlier this year — was "continuing to progress through zoning in multiple locations" after a permitting setback in April. He attributed much of the public opposition to election-cycle politics.

  "Some of the spin that you see leading up to the election does not equal reality," Rochow said.

  The omission of the large-load funnel slide — which had appeared in Consumers' previous several quarterly earnings presentations — drew notice. Rochow said the decision was intentional, comparing the pipeline to football: execution to the end zone matters more than charting individual plays.

  Consumers has energized approximately 450 megawatts (MW) of new large load in its territory out of 745 MW covered by signed contracts in its five-year plan. Those contracts include what Rochow described as a "medium-sized data center" alongside semiconductor, food processing, and manufacturing facilities. The data center under the 1-GW agreement is not included in those figures.

  CMS Energy Chief Financial Officer Sri Maddipati said each gigawatt of incremental large load could drive between $2 billion and $5 billion in capital investment for the utility — a figure that underscores the financial stakes of permitting outcomes for the project.

  ## Earnings and NorthStar restructuring

  CMS Energy earned $0.39 per share in the third quarter of 2026, down from $0.92 per share in the same period of 2025. The company said the decline reflects asset impairment and restructuring charges tied to NorthStar Clean Energy, its non-regulated clean energy development and independent power production subsidiary.

  CMS has agreed to or completed sales covering more than 35% of NorthStar's assets, with the bulk of the remainder in advanced negotiations. The company aims to complete the restructuring by year-end. CMS previously said it would retain certain Michigan assets, including two gas-fired peaker plants, a 770-MW waste- and gas-fired cogeneration facility, and about 500 MW of solar.

  The restructuring will free at least $500 million for redeployment into the regulated utility business, the company said. Maddipati suggested that estimate is conservative.

  "I would be disappointed if we are not above the $500 million funding benefit," Maddipati said.

  ## Campbell coal plant and regulatory costs

  Neither Rochow nor Maddipati addressed the J.H. Campbell Generating Complex during Tuesday's call, but CMS Energy disclosed in a required securities filing this week that it has incurred $303 million in net costs through the end of September operating the approximately 1,420-MW coal-fired plant under the U.S. Department of Energy's Section 202(c) emergency authority — the federal provision allowing the DOE to order power plants to stay online to protect grid reliability. That net figure accounts for $344 million in market revenues received from the Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO), the regional grid manager covering much of the central United States.

  Campbell was originally scheduled for retirement in May 2025 but has continued operating at DOE's direction. CMS Energy said it plans to petition the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), the federal body overseeing interstate electricity markets, for full recovery of its net operating costs. Earlier petitions covering the first three 90-day order periods remain pending before FERC.

  In September, a federal appeals court invalidated DOE's original emergency order, ruling that it improperly displaced states' regulatory authority. The ruling came in response to litigation filed by environmental groups and attorneys general from Michigan and two other MISO states. CMS Energy said it expects DOE to appeal the decision.

  ## Rate case and longer-term investment plans

  Consumers Energy revised its requested revenue requirement in a pending Michigan Public Service Commission (MPSC) rate case downward to $443 million, from a prior request of $456 million. The utility is seeking a 51.75% equity ratio and a 10.25% return on equity — the regulated profit margin allowed on its asset base — and expects a final MPSC order by early April 2027.

  In a separate investor presentation earlier this month, CMS Energy said it plans to invest more than $17 billion in Consumers' electric system over the next five years and grow its combined gas and electric rate base at roughly 10.5% annually over that period.

  Consumers' most recently filed integrated resource plan envisions adding nearly 16 GW of new clean energy resources and 4.1 GW of battery storage by 2040 in pursuit of Michigan's statutory target of 100% clean energy by that date.
body_zh: |
  CMS Energy執行長Garrick Rochow週二在該公司第三季法說會上向投資人與分析師表示，一項從Consumers Energy密西根電網抽用最高達1吉瓦（GW）電力、飽受爭議的資料中心開發案，在下週期中選舉過後，地方許可審查程序將見到「更多進展」。就在此番發言的同時，CMS Energy悄悄將過去數季法說會簡報中例行出現的多吉瓦大型用電客戶開發管線投影片從本季報告中移除。

  CMS Energy是Consumers Energy的母公司，後者在密西根州經營受監管的天然氣與電力公用事業。

  ## 資料中心許可審查與開發管線

  Rochow婉拒透露業主名稱，但表示該案——Consumers Energy今年稍早已宣布簽署供電協議——在今年四月遭遇許可審查挫折後，目前正「在多個地點持續推進分區申請程序」。他將大部分來自社區的反對聲浪歸因於選舉週期的政治操作。

  「選前所見到的部分炒作，與現實並不相符，」Rochow說。

  大型用電開發管線投影片遭到移除一事引發市場側目。Rochow表示，此一決定出於刻意考量，並以美式足球為喻：能否成功推進至達陣區才是關鍵，逐一記錄每個進攻動作意義不大。

  Consumers Energy在其五年計畫的7億4,500萬瓦（MW）簽約容量中，已在轄區內正式通電約4億5,000萬瓦的新增大型用電設施。這些合約涵蓋Rochow所形容的一座「中型資料中心」，以及半導體、食品加工與製造業設施。前述1吉瓦協議所涉及的資料中心，並不計入上述數字。

  CMS Energy財務長Sri Maddipati表示，每增加1吉瓦的大型用電負載，可為該公用事業帶動20億至50億美元的資本支出——此一數字凸顯出許可審查結果對該開發案財務影響之重。

  ## 獲利表現與NorthStar重整

  CMS Energy 2026年第三季每股盈餘為0.39美元，較2025年同期的0.92美元大幅下滑。公司表示，獲利衰退主要反映旗下非管制潔淨能源開發暨獨立發電子公司NorthStar Clean Energy的資產減損與重整費用。

  CMS Energy已就逾35%的NorthStar資產完成或達成出售協議，其餘大部分資產亦已進入深度談判階段。該公司目標在今年底前完成重整。CMS Energy先前表示，將保留若干密西根州資產，包括兩座燃氣尖峰電廠、一座770 MW的廢棄物暨天然氣汽電共生設施，以及約500 MW的太陽能資產。

  此次重整將為受監管的公用事業本業釋出至少5億美元的再部署資金。Maddipati更暗示此一估計偏保守。

  「若我們最終未能超越5億美元的資金效益，我會感到失望，」Maddipati說。

  ## Campbell燃煤電廠與法規成本

  週二的法說會上，Rochow與Maddipati均未提及J.H. Campbell發電廠，但CMS Energy本週在依規申報的證券文件中揭露，截至九月底，該公司在美國能源部第202(c)條緊急授權下持續營運這座約1,420 MW燃煤電廠，已累計淨支出3億300萬美元。能源部得依此聯邦條款命令發電廠維持運轉，以保障電網穩定。上述淨支出數字已扣除從中部獨立系統運營商（MISO，負責管理美國中部大部分地區電網的區域調度機構）所獲得的3億4,400萬美元市場收入。

  Campbell電廠原訂於2025年5月除役，但在能源部指令下持續運轉至今。CMS Energy表示，計畫向聯邦能源監管委員會（FERC，負責監管跨州電力市場的聯邦機構）申請全額追償其淨營運成本。涵蓋前三個90天命令期間的較早申請案，目前仍在FERC審理中。

  今年九月，一家聯邦上訴法院宣告能源部最初的緊急命令無效，裁定其不當侵奪各州的監管權限。此一判決源於環保團體及密西根州與另外兩個MISO成員州的州檢察長提起的訴訟。CMS Energy表示，預期能源部將提起上訴。

  ## 費率申請與長期投資計畫

  Consumers Energy在密西根州公共服務委員會（MPSC）待審費率申請案中，將其要求的收入調增金額從先前的4億5,600萬美元下修至4億4,300萬美元。該公用事業正申請51.75%的股本比率及10.25%的股本報酬率（即監管機關允許的資產基礎獲利上限），並預期MPSC將於2027年四月初作出最終裁決。

  在本月稍早另行舉辦的一場投資人說明會上，CMS Energy表示，計畫在未來五年內對Consumers Energy的電力系統投入逾170億美元，並預計在此期間以每年約10.5%的幅度提升天然氣與電力合計費率基礎。

  Consumers Energy最新提交的整合資源計畫，規劃在2040年前新增近16 GW的潔淨能源資源及4.1 GW的電池儲能設施，以追求密西根州法定的2040年達成100%潔淨能源供電目標。
