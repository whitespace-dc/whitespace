body_en: |
  Singapore's Energy Market Authority (EMA) has granted conditional approval for two companies to import a combined 900 megawatts (MW) of electricity generated from solar photovoltaic and battery energy storage systems in Johor, Malaysia, the regulator announced on Aug. 7.

  The approvals go to Sembcorp Utilities for 300 MW and Southern Solar Alliance — a wholly owned subsidiary of Malaysian developer Ditrolic Energy Holdings — for 600 MW. Both companies are targeting commercial operations around 2029, EMA said.

  At that combined scale, the imported electricity would be sufficient to meet the annual power demand of one million four-room public housing (HDB) flats in Singapore, according to EMA's figures.

  ## Where the electricity comes from

  Sembcorp Utilities said its project will draw on a large-scale development at Linggiu Reservoir in Johor, comprising an approximately 2.2 gigawatt-peak (GWp) floating solar photovoltaic installation paired with up to 4.3 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of battery energy storage. The floating solar array will generate electricity; the batteries will store surplus generation for dispatch as needed.

  Sembcorp said the project is designed to meet energy demand in both Singapore and Malaysia and will use existing and future subsea cable infrastructure between the two countries to move power across the border.

  ## What conditional approval means

  A conditional approval is the first of three regulatory stages EMA requires before a company can import electricity commercially. It signals that EMA has found the proposal technically and commercially viable. The developer then proceeds with further surveys and feasibility studies.

  If the company can demonstrate it meets requirements in both Singapore and the host country, EMA issues a conditional licence, indicating the project has reached an advanced development stage. The final step is the full importer licence, which allows commercial operations to begin.

  Before either project can proceed to later stages, EMA said both companies must obtain all required approvals from relevant jurisdictions, conclude power purchase agreements with buyers, secure sufficient financing, and meet various project development milestones.

  ## Context within Singapore's broader import programme

  The two new approvals bring the total number of electricity import projects that have received conditional approvals or licences from EMA to 13, with a combined proposed capacity of 9.25 gigawatts (GW). Those projects span solar, wind and hydropower sourced from Malaysia, Indonesia, Cambodia, Vietnam and Australia.

  Singapore has set a target to import around 6 GW of electricity by 2035, which would represent approximately one-third of the country's projected energy needs at that time. The power sector currently accounts for 40 percent of Singapore's total national emissions, EMA said, making low-carbon electricity imports a central plank of the country's decarbonisation strategy.

  In October 2025, Singapore granted separate conditional approval to import 1 GW of low-carbon electricity — primarily hydropower — from Sarawak.

  ## Cross-border infrastructure

  The existing electricity interconnection between Singapore and Peninsular Malaysia allows up to 1 GW of bi-directional power flows. Singapore Energy Interconnections, Malaysian utility Tenaga Nasional Berhad and SP Group have signed a Joint Development Agreement to study a second interconnection link of up to 2 GW between the two countries, EMA said.

  ## Industry significance

  Victor Nian, founding co-chairman of the independent think-tank Centre for Strategic Energy and Resources, said the approvals help ensure Singapore's clean energy imports remain diverse. Broadening the mix of source countries and technologies reduces exposure to any single supply disruption. Nian added that growing experience in cross-border electricity trade could position Singapore as a regional clean energy trading hub.

  EMA said it will continue to engage companies with credible and commercially viable import proposals that can contribute to power sector decarbonisation.
body_zh: |
  新加坡能源市場管理局（EMA）8月7日宣布，有條件批准兩家企業從馬來西亞柔佛州進口合計900百萬瓦（MW）、由太陽能光伏及電池儲能系統所產生的電力。

  此次獲批企業分別為勝科公用事業（Sembcorp Utilities），獲准進口300 MW；以及南方太陽能聯盟（Southern Solar Alliance）——馬來西亞開發商Ditrolic Energy Holdings的全資子公司——獲准進口600 MW。EMA表示，兩家企業均預計於2029年前後啟動商業運營。

  按此合計規模，進口電力足以滿足新加坡一百萬戶四房式組屋（HDB）的年度用電需求，此為EMA所引用之數據。

  ## 電力來源

  勝科公用事業表示，其項目將依託柔佛州令吉水庫（Linggiu Reservoir）的大型開發計畫，包含約2.2吉瓦峰值（GWp）的浮式太陽能光伏裝置，並搭配高達4.3吉瓦時（GWh）的電池儲能設施。浮式太陽能陣列負責發電，電池則儲存多餘電量，按需調度供應。

  勝科表示，該項目旨在同時滿足新加坡與馬來西亞兩地的能源需求，並將借助兩國之間現有及未來的海底電纜基礎設施，實現跨境輸電。

  ## 有條件批准的意涵

  有條件批准是EMA在企業正式商業進口電力之前所要求三個監管階段中的第一步。此一批准意味著EMA已認定該方案在技術與商業層面具備可行性，業者隨後將推進進一步勘察與可行性研究。

  若業者能證明其符合新加坡及電力來源國的各項要求，EMA將頒發有條件許可（conditional licence），表明項目已進入高階開發階段。最後一步則為完整的進口商許可（importer licence），取得後方可正式展開商業運營。

  EMA表示，在兩項目進入後續階段之前，兩家企業均須取得相關司法管轄區的所有必要批准、與買方簽訂購電協議、確保充足融資，並達成各項項目開發里程碑。

  ## 新加坡更廣泛進口計畫的背景

  此次兩項新批准，使獲EMA頒發有條件批准或許可的電力進口項目總數達到13個，合計擬議裝機容量為9.25吉瓦（GW）。這些項目涵蓋太陽能、風能及水力發電，電力來源遍及馬來西亞、印尼、柬埔寨、越南及澳洲。

  新加坡已設定目標，於2035年前進口約6吉瓦電力，約占屆時該國預測能源需求的三分之一。EMA指出，電力部門目前占新加坡全國總排放量的40%，因此進口低碳電力是該國去碳化策略的核心支柱。

  2025年10月，新加坡曾另行有條件批准，從砂拉越進口1吉瓦、以水力發電為主的低碳電力。

  ## 跨境基礎設施

  新加坡與馬來西亞半島之間現有的電力互聯系統，目前允許最高1吉瓦的雙向電力流通。新加坡能源互聯公司（Singapore Energy Interconnections）、馬來西亞國家能源公司（Tenaga Nasional Berhad）及新加坡電力集團（SP Group）已簽署聯合開發協議，就兩國之間建設第二條最高達2吉瓦的互聯線路展開研究，EMA表示。

  ## 產業意義

  獨立智庫「戰略能源與資源中心」（Centre for Strategic Energy and Resources）創始共同主席Victor Nian表示，此次批准有助確保新加坡清潔能源進口的多元化。擴大電源來源國與技術的組合，可降低對任何單一供應中斷的曝險。他補充，隨著跨境電力交易經驗不斷積累，新加坡有望確立其作為區域清潔能源交易樞紐的地位。

  EMA表示，將持續與具備可信度及商業可行性的進口提案企業保持溝通，以推動電力部門的去碳化進程。
