body_en: |
  Global investors are conditioning large-scale capital commitments to Indonesia's power sector on clearer project financing structures, tariff mechanisms, and coal-plant retirement schedules, the Asia Investor Group on Climate Change (AIGCC) said Monday.

  The call comes as Indonesia's electricity planning document — the RUPTL 2025-2034 (Rencana Usaha Penyediaan Tenaga Listrik, or Power Supply Business Plan) — projects a need for $188 billion to add 69.5 gigawatts (GW) of generation capacity by 2034. About three-quarters of that new capacity is planned to come from renewable energy and energy storage. The plan relies on private capital, channeled through independent power producers (IPPs), for approximately 73 percent of the total investment required.

  ## The core investor complaint

  AIGCC CEO Rebecca Mikula-Wright said the obstacle is no longer an absence of planning. "The challenge is no longer a lack of planning — it's alignment and implementation of existing policy frameworks," she said in a written statement Monday. Investors are not asking Indonesia to be more ambitious, she said; they are asking for a clear path to mobilizing financing capital.

  AIGCC published these findings in a report titled *Investor Expectations for Power-Sector Transition Roadmaps in Asia*, which maps the specific conditions investors require before committing capital to power-sector transitions across the region.

  ## What investors say is missing

  The report identifies several specific gaps that are limiting investment. Investors want certainty on procurement mechanisms, tariffs, payment guarantees, foreign exchange risk allocation, government support structures, and the financial health of PT PLN (Persero), Indonesia's state electricity utility and the country's primary offtaker for power generation contracts. Grid construction readiness and public access to project pipelines are also cited as preconditions for investment commitments.

  The report also flags captive power plants — coal-fired generators that supply electricity directly to industrial estates rather than through the national grid — as an unresolved complication. Investors want a defined retirement schedule for both grid-connected coal plants and captive facilities, including financing structures and compensation arrangements for shutdowns, the capacity that would replace them, and a framework for managing affected workers and communities through what is referred to as a just transition.

  ## JETP pathway remains non-binding

  Indonesia participates in the Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP), an international financing coalition that produced its own Comprehensive Investment and Policy Plan (CIPP). The AIGCC report notes that the CIPP offers a more ambitious, investor-oriented transition pathway but is not binding, leaving its relationship to the official RUPTL targets unresolved.

  ## Five priorities AIGCC sets for policymakers

  AIGCC outlined five specific actions it is calling on Indonesian policymakers to take:

  - Align the national energy transition roadmap, the power-sector plan, and the JETP framework, including emissions-reduction targets and rules governing captive plants.
  - Clarify procurement mechanisms, tariffs, payment guarantees, exchange rate risk arrangements, government support instruments, and PLN revenue projections so individual projects carry bankable financing structures.
  - Set a firm direction for coal plant retirement — covering grid-connected and captive facilities — including compensation, replacement capacity, and just-transition provisions.
  - Ensure grid and technology readiness while opening project pipeline information to investors.
  - Improve transparency by disclosing carbon market assumptions, publishing transition progress reports, and holding regular dialogues with investors and other stakeholders.

  ## Regional significance

  Mikula-Wright said Indonesia's position makes it critical to Southeast Asia's broader energy transition, and that investors have already begun looking at the country as a destination. "A coherent, investable roadmap will enable them to commit at a scale commensurate with the country's growth potential," she said.

  The arithmetic of the RUPTL plan leaves little room for shortfalls in private capital. With roughly three dollars out of every four projected to come from IPPs, the revenue models, risk allocation frameworks, and project lists that AIGCC is requesting are, in the group's framing, not technical footnotes but the core of the plan itself.

  AIGCC's five-point recommendation set is published alongside its *Investor Expectations for Power-Sector Transition Roadmaps in Asia* report, released Monday.
body_zh: |
  亞洲氣候變遷投資人聯盟（AIGCC）週一表示，全球投資人正以更清晰的專案融資架構、費率機制及燃煤電廠退場時程為前提，決定是否對印尼電力部門做出大規模資本承諾。

  此一呼籲，源自印尼電力規劃文件——RUPTL 2025-2034（即《電力供應業務計畫》，Rencana Usaha Penyediaan Tenaga Listrik）——預計需要1,880億美元，於2034年前新增69.5吉瓦（GW）的發電容量。其中約四分之三的新增容量規劃來自再生能源及儲能設施。該計畫依賴透過獨立發電商（IPP）引入的民間資本，預計佔總投資需求的約73%。

  ## 投資人核心訴求

  AIGCC執行長Rebecca Mikula-Wright表示，障礙已不再是規劃不足。「挑戰不再是缺乏規劃，而是現有政策框架的協調與落實，」她在週一的書面聲明中說。她指出，投資人並非要求印尼提高目標，而是希望看到一條明確的融資資本動員路徑。

  AIGCC在一份題為《亞洲電力部門轉型路線圖的投資人期望》（*Investor Expectations for Power-Sector Transition Roadmaps in Asia*）的報告中公布上述發現，該報告詳細列出投資人在對亞洲各地電力部門轉型做出資本承諾前所要求的具體條件。

  ## 投資人認為尚待解決的問題

  報告指出幾項正在限制投資的具體缺口。投資人希望在採購機制、費率、付款保證、外匯風險分配、政府支持架構，以及印尼國營電力公司PT PLN（Persero）的財務健全狀況等方面獲得確定性——PLN是印尼發電合約的主要售電方。電網建設的就緒程度及專案管線資訊的公開取得，也被列為投資承諾的先決條件。

  報告亦點出「自用電廠」問題——即直接供電給工業區、未接入國家電網的燃煤發電機組——視之為尚未解決的複雜因素。投資人希望看到針對併網燃煤電廠及自用電廠制定明確的退場時程，包括關廠的融資架構與補償安排、替代容量規劃，以及透過所謂「公正轉型」（just transition）框架妥善照顧受影響勞工與社區的方案。

  ## JETP路線仍屬非約束性

  印尼參與「公正能源轉型夥伴關係」（JETP），這是一個國際融資聯盟，已推出其「綜合投資與政策計畫」（CIPP）。AIGCC報告指出，CIPP提供了一條更具企圖心、更以投資人為導向的轉型路徑，但並不具約束力，其與官方RUPTL目標之間的關係至今仍懸而未決。

  ## AIGCC對政策制定者提出的五項優先事項

  AIGCC概述了呼籲印尼政策制定者採取的五項具體行動：

  - 整合國家能源轉型路線圖、電力部門計畫與JETP框架，涵蓋減碳目標及自用電廠管理規範。
  - 釐清採購機制、費率、付款保證、匯率風險安排、政府支持工具及PLN營收預測，使個別專案具備可融資的財務架構。
  - 為燃煤電廠退場確立明確方向，涵蓋併網與自用電廠，包括補償安排、替代容量規劃及公正轉型條款。
  - 確保電網與技術的就緒程度，同時向投資人開放專案管線資訊。
  - 強化透明度，包括揭露碳市場假設、發布轉型進度報告，並定期與投資人及其他利害關係人展開對話。

  ## 區域重要性

  Mikula-Wright表示，印尼的地位使其對東南亞整體能源轉型至關重要，且投資人已開始將該國視為資金去向。「一份連貫且具可投資性的路線圖，將使他們得以按照與印尼成長潛力相稱的規模做出承諾，」她說。

  RUPTL計畫的財務數字幾乎沒有為民間資本短缺留下任何空間。預計每四美元的投資中，約有三美元將來自獨立發電商；在AIGCC的論述框架中，投資人所要求的收益模式、風險分配架構與專案清單，並非技術性注腳，而是整個計畫的核心所在。

  AIGCC的五點建議已隨其《亞洲電力部門轉型路線圖的投資人期望》報告一同於週一正式發布。
