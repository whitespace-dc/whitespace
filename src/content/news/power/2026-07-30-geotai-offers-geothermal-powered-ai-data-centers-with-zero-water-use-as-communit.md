body_en: |
  GEOT.Ai, a startup structured as a public benefit corporation, is deploying modular artificial intelligence data centers powered by closed-loop geothermal energy that consumes no water after initial well drilling, as community opposition to conventional data center development intensifies in the United States and United Kingdom.

  CEO and co-founder Jenifer Muir said the company designs and deploys what it calls AI Factories — small, modular, turnkey facilities sited in existing industrial or urban environments — at scales ranging from five megawatts to one gigawatt. Speaking to Capacity Media ahead of Metro Connect Fall 2026, Muir framed the company's model as a direct response to a breakdown in trust between data center operators and the communities that host them.

  ## Why communities are pushing back

  Data center developments approved by governments in Michigan and Essex, UK have proceeded despite local opposition, Muir noted. She identified the root cause of that opposition as the gap between what operators promise and what they actually consume. "The lack of trust comes in, for example, when a data centre secures buy-in from the community by making certain promises, and then in practice consumes far more water than promised, or creates brownouts," she said.

  Los Angeles, where Muir is based, already experiences brownouts she attributed in part to AI data centers coming online in surrounding areas. The city has a population of 11 million. Those outages, even when they last only an hour or two, affect hospitals and court authorities processing millions of dollars in goods entering North America every minute, she said.

  ## The scale of the grid problem

  The rate of demand growth sharpens the pressure. Muir cited AMD CEO Dr. Lisa Su's announcement at CES in January that AMD forecast ten times the current compute capacity by 2030. Muir said that projection implies grid capacity would need to grow roughly a hundredfold within five years — a pace she described as completely unprecedented.

  "The data-compute revolution of AI requires a completely different paradigm for power and cooling," Muir said. She described addressing that gap as the obligation behind GEOT.Ai's founding.

  ## How the geothermal system works

  GEOT.Ai uses advanced closed-loop geothermal heat extraction. In the company's design, heat drawn from underground directly powers server racks through immersion cooling and direct-to-chip cooling. No water is consumed at any point in the system's operating lifecycle; only a small amount is used during the initial drilling of the geothermal well, the company said.

  The deployment strategy targets existing industrial or urban sites rather than agricultural land or undeveloped areas. Muir said this allows the company to repurpose sites it describes as defunct or abandoned, revitalizing local economies while avoiding land conversion.

  "Instead of going into large open agricultural areas or land that has yet to be developed, we deploy at the edge," she said.

  ## Governance and community profit-sharing

  GEOT.Ai operates as a public benefit corporation, a legal structure that subjects it to stricter reporting requirements than a standard company. Its board of directors includes representatives covering investor interests, digital integrity, and public benefit oversight.

  The company commits 20% of all profit to the communities it serves: 10% paid directly to those communities and 10% directed to a land preservation trust, Muir said. The structure is encoded in bylaws designed to generate shareholder value while meeting public benefit obligations.

  "We've structured this in the form of a public benefit trust," she said. "It holds us to our promise that, alongside the innovation AI offers, we maintain firm grounding in governance and in the communities we serve."

  ## The policymaker education gap

  Muir argued that the disconnect between technology leaders and community or civil leaders is itself a structural problem. Technology leaders are advancing AI at an unprecedented pace, she said, while boards of supervisors and town councils are experts in social impact and economic development, not technology — making it difficult for them to evaluate what is being proposed or promised.

  She called for forums to provide genuine transparency and education for policymakers and community leaders, so that both groups are current on what AI infrastructure development involves and what it demands.

  ## Dedicated power supply as the long-term fix

  Muir's broader argument is that AI infrastructure should draw on its own dedicated, firm power supply — meaning reliable, dispatchable generation — rather than compete with existing residential and commercial consumers on shared grids. Firm power refers to generation that can be counted on regardless of weather or time of day, in contrast to intermittent sources like solar or wind without storage.

  "I believe AI merits and requires its own firm-based power supply, with its own set of transparency, governance and rules," she said. "In that kind of structure, AI can genuinely thrive."

  GEOT.Ai was founded last year, according to Muir. The company is presenting at Metro Connect Fall 2026.
body_zh: |
  GEOT.Ai是一家以公共利益公司（public benefit corporation）型態運作的新創企業，正在部署由閉迴路地熱能源驅動的模組化人工智慧（AI）資料中心。公司表示，這套系統完成初期鑿井作業後，在整個運作周期中完全不消耗水資源。與此同時，隨著美國和英國各地社區對傳統資料中心開發的組織性反彈日益激烈，該公司的模式正引發市場廣泛關注。

  執行長暨共同創辦人珍妮佛・穆爾（Jenifer Muir）表示，公司設計並部署其稱為「AI工廠」（AI Factories）的小型模組化交鑰匙設施，選址鎖定既有工業或都市環境，規模從5百萬瓦到1吉瓦不等。穆爾在出席Metro Connect Fall 2026大會前接受《Capacity Media》採訪時，將公司的商業模式定位為針對資料中心業者與主辦社區之間信任崩解所提出的直接回應。

  ## 為何社區開始強力反彈

  穆爾指出，美國密西根州及英國艾塞克斯（Essex）已獲政府核准的資料中心開發案，儘管當地居民強烈反對，仍持續推進。她認為，反彈的根源在於業者承諾與實際消耗之間的落差。「信任的崩解，舉例來說，就發生在資料中心業者以特定承諾取得社區認可之後，在實際運作上卻消耗了遠超承諾的水量，或造成電壓驟降，」她說。

  穆爾的根據地洛杉磯已頻繁出現電壓驟降，她將部分原因歸咎於周邊地區陸續上線的AI資料中心。洛杉磯擁有1,100萬人口，這類停電事件即便只持續一兩個小時，也會衝擊醫院運作，以及每分鐘監管數百萬美元貨物進入北美通關業務的法院機關。

  ## 電網問題的規模

  需求成長的速度正在加劇電網壓力。穆爾引用超微（AMD）執行長蘇姿丰博士（Dr. Lisa Su）今年一月在CES大會上的預測——超微預估，到2030年全球運算容量將成長至現今的十倍。穆爾表示，這項預測意味著電網容量需要在五年內擴增約一百倍，她形容這是「史無前例的速度」。

  「AI所帶動的資料運算革命，需要一套截然不同的電力供應與散熱典範，」穆爾說。她將填補這道缺口視為創立GEOT.Ai的核心使命。

  ## 地熱系統的運作原理

  GEOT.Ai採用先進的閉迴路地熱熱能萃取技術。在公司的設計中，從地下抽取的熱能直接透過浸沒式冷卻（immersion cooling）與晶片直接冷卻（direct-to-chip cooling）為伺服器機架提供動力。公司表示，系統在整個運作生命周期中完全不消耗水資源，僅在初期鑿設地熱井時需使用少量用水。

  部署策略鎖定既有工業或都市用地，而非農業用地或尚未開發的區域。穆爾說，這讓公司得以活化其所描述的廢棄或閒置場址，在重振當地經濟的同時，也避免了土地用途的轉換。

  「我們不走進大片農業用地或尚待開發的土地，而是在邊緣地帶進行部署，」她說。

  ## 治理架構與社區利潤分配

  GEOT.Ai以公共利益公司的法人型態運作，相較於一般公司，這種結構須接受更嚴格的揭露要求。其董事會涵蓋投資人利益、數位誠信及公共利益監督等代表席次。

  穆爾表示，公司承諾將所有獲利的20%回饋給其所服務的社區：其中10%直接支付給社區，另外10%則撥入土地保育信託。這套架構已明文訂入公司章程，旨在於創造股東價值的同時，切實履行公共利益義務。

  「我們以公共利益信託的形式建構這套框架，」她說。「這讓我們對自己的承諾負責——在AI帶來的創新之外，我們同樣在治理層面和我們所服務的社區中保持穩固的立足點。」

  ## 政策制定者的知識落差

  穆爾認為，科技領袖與社區或民政領袖之間的脫節，本身就是一個結構性問題。她說，科技領袖正以前所未有的速度推進AI，而郡議員和鄉鎮議會的成員是社會影響與經濟發展方面的專家，而非科技領域的行家，這使他們難以評估業者所提出的方案，或判斷業者所做出的承諾是否可信。

  她呼籲建立論壇，為政策制定者和社區領袖提供真正的透明度與教育管道，讓兩個群體都能及時掌握AI基礎設施開發的實際內涵與需求。

  ## 專屬電力供應才是長遠解方

  穆爾的核心主張是：AI基礎設施應仰賴自身專屬的、穩定型電力供應——亦即可靠、可調度的發電能源——而非與既有住宅和商業用戶在共用電網上相互競爭。所謂「穩定型電力」（firm power），指的是不受天氣或時段影響、可穩定供應的發電方式，有別於沒有搭配儲能設施的太陽能或風能等間歇性電源。

  「我認為AI值得擁有、也確實需要自己的穩定型電力供應，並配備一套屬於AI自身的透明度、治理機制與規範，」她說。「在這樣的架構下，AI才能真正蓬勃發展。」
