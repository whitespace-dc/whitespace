body_en: |
  Wood Mackenzie has warned that data center projects in Malaysia's Johor state could account for 40% of the state's total electricity demand by 2035, an increase of around 24 percentage points, as a rapidly expanding project pipeline threatens to outpace grid infrastructure readiness.

  The findings appear in a WoodMac report titled "Powering Johor's Data Centre Boom: Supply, Demand, and Grid Constraints." Alvin Tan, WoodMac's research analyst for power and renewables, said the central challenge has shifted from whether power is available to where it is available. "Areas attracting the highest concentration of data centre investment are also where grid infrastructure is under the greatest pressure, making transmission and distribution readiness a key determinant of future growth," Tan said.

  ## Capacity already exceeds state electricity demand

  Johor's aggregate data center power capacity reached approximately 3.8 gigawatts (GW) as of September, nearly one and a half times the state's current total electricity demand, according to the report. As of 2025, Johor had 27 active data center projects and a pipeline of approximately 50 additional projects as of April 2025.

  Johor emerged as the region's primary data center destination after Singapore paused new data center development between 2019 and 2022. The state now holds one of the fastest-growing pipelines in Southeast Asia, Tan said.

  ## Policy measures accelerating development

  Several programs have reduced barriers to data center development in Johor. The Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone, Malaysia's National Data Centre Framework, and Tenaga Nasional Bhd's (TNB) Green Lane Pathway — which streamlines grid connection approvals and timelines — have collectively accelerated project qualification and construction. WoodMac warned that together these initiatives risk enabling data center growth to outpace grid supply readiness.

  ## Generation supply and the coal retirement risk

  Johor currently holds approximately 6.8 GW of installed generation capacity, primarily sourced from natural gas and coal. That supply base faces a material reduction: 2.1 GW of coal-fired capacity is expected to retire during the 2030s under Malaysia's New Energy Transition Roadmap (NETR), the national framework governing the country's shift toward cleaner power sources.

  WoodMac identified Malaysia's NewGen26 program as critical to maintaining grid reliability through the coal retirement period. NewGen26 is an open tender targeting 6 to 8 GW of new gas-fired generation capacity, designed to sustain long-term reliability and support future industrial growth.

  ## Renewables and mitigation options

  The Southern Johor Renewable Energy Corridor is expected to provide a partial offset to coal plant retirements. Planned projects in Mersing and Kota Tinggi within the corridor could add four gigawatts-peak (GWp, a measure of rated solar output under standard test conditions) of solar capacity integrated with battery storage, which the report said would help cushion the impact of future coal retirements.

  Near-term solutions identified by WoodMac include stronger on-site substations at individual data center facilities and deployment of decentralized solar generators. Longer-term, the report calls for dedicated renewable energy infrastructure built specifically to serve the data center sector.

  ## Significance for regional grid planning

  WoodMac's concern centers on execution timing rather than resource availability in aggregate. The risk, Tan said, is that physical infrastructure — substations, transmission upgrades, and new generation capacity — will not be completed on the timelines the sector requires. "Delays in substations, transmission upgrades or new generation projects could ultimately become the limiting factor on future expansion," he said.

  The report frames infrastructure planning pace, not investment appetite, as the binding constraint on Johor's continued growth as a data center hub.
body_zh: |
  研究機構伍德麥肯茲（Wood Mackenzie）示警，馬來西亞柔佛州的資料中心開發案到2035年時，可能占全州總電力需求的40%，較目前大幅攀升約24個百分點。隨著開發管線急速擴張，電網基礎建設能否跟上腳步，已成為業界最大隱憂。

  上述發現出自伍德麥肯茲一份題為《為柔佛資料中心熱潮供電：供需格局與電網限制》（Powering Johor's Data Centre Boom: Supply, Demand, and Grid Constraints）的報告。伍德麥肯茲電力與再生能源研究分析師譚爾文（Alvin Tan）表示，核心挑戰已從「電力是否充足」轉變為「電力在哪裡」。他說：「吸引最多資料中心投資的地區，也正是電網基礎建設壓力最大的地方，因此輸配電整備程度將成為未來成長的關鍵決定因素。」

  ## 裝置容量已超越全州電力需求

  報告指出，截至9月，柔佛州資料中心的總電力容量已達約3.8吉瓦（GW），相當於全州目前總電力需求的近1.5倍。截至2025年，柔佛州共有27個活躍的資料中心開發案；截至2025年4月，另有約50個案件列於開發管線中。

  柔佛州之所以崛起為區域首選資料中心目的地，部分原因在於新加坡曾於2019年至2022年間暫停新建資料中心的開發許可。目前柔佛州擁有東南亞成長速度最快的開發管線之一，譚爾文表示。

  ## 政策措施加速發展腳步

  多項政策措施已大幅降低在柔佛州建設資料中心的門檻，包括柔佛—新加坡特別經濟區、馬來西亞國家資料中心框架，以及馬來西亞國家能源公司（Tenaga Nasional Bhd，TNB）推出的「綠色通道計畫」（Green Lane Pathway）——後者可簡化電網接入的審批程序與所需時程。伍德麥肯茲警告，上述措施若同步推進，有可能使資料中心的成長速度超越電網供給的整備程度。

  ## 發電供給與燃煤退場風險

  柔佛州目前擁有約6.8吉瓦的裝置發電容量，主要來自天然氣與燃煤。然而，這一供給基礎面臨實質縮減的風險：根據馬來西亞《新能源轉型路線圖》（New Energy Transition Roadmap，NETR）——即指引該國邁向更清潔能源結構的國家框架——預計有2.1吉瓦的燃煤發電容量將於2030年代陸續退場。

  伍德麥肯茲點名馬來西亞的「NewGen26計畫」是維繫燃煤退場期間電網穩定的關鍵。NewGen26為一開放招標計畫，目標引進6至8吉瓦的新建燃氣發電容量，設計初衷在於維持長期供電可靠性，並支撐未來工業發展需求。

  ## 再生能源與緩解選項

  柔佛南部再生能源走廊預計可部分填補燃煤電廠退場後的供給缺口。走廊內文丁（Mersing）與古打丁宜（Kota Tinggi）的規劃案，合計可新增4吉瓦峰（GWp，即太陽能板在標準測試條件下的額定輸出）的太陽能容量，並整合電池儲能系統；報告認為此舉將有助於緩衝未來燃煤退場的衝擊。

  伍德麥肯茲點出的近期解決方案包括：強化個別資料中心設施內的自有變電站，以及部署分散式太陽能發電設備。就長期而言，報告呼籲專門為資料中心產業建置專屬的再生能源基礎設施。

  ## 對區域電網規劃的重要意涵

  伍德麥肯茲的核心關切在於執行時程，而非整體資源是否充足。譚爾文指出，風險在於實體基礎建設——包括變電站、輸電線路升級及新建發電容量——無法在產業所需的時程內完工。他說：「變電站、輸電升級或新建電源計畫的延誤，最終可能成為限制未來擴張的決定性因素。」

  報告的核心論點是：制約柔佛州持續成長為資料中心樞紐的關鍵所在，不是投資意願，而是基礎建設規劃的推進速度。
