body_en: |
  The Tennessee Valley Authority has entered a $1.7 billion aggregate payment agreement with an unnamed data center customer under its new large-load tariff, the federal utility disclosed in a Nov. 12 filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

  The tariff, which took effect Oct. 1, applies to data center customers requesting more than 5 MW of capacity. "Capacity commitment charges under the agreement are based on a rate of approximately $1.5 million per MW of requested capacity above 5 MW," the filing states. TVA described the pricing as reflecting its "current cohort-based pricing methodology, which may be revised for future customer cohorts."

  The tariff will be phased in through 2029. That phase-in period applies to existing customers, allowing them to retain their current rate structure for a transition period, according to a Nashville Scene report cited by the utility's disclosures.

  ## CEO transition and investment rationale

  TVA CEO Mike Skaggs, speaking on a Nov. 13 earnings call, said the new data center rate structure has supported the utility's investment program. Those investments include extending the operational lives of existing nuclear generating units and what Skaggs described as expanding the nuclear fuel and supply hub in the East Tennessee area.

  Skaggs had come out of retirement to serve as TVA's interim chief executive following the retirement of former CEO Don Moul. TVA's board confirmed him as permanent CEO at a Nov. 10 meeting, according to Clifton Lowry, the utility's vice president of financial planning and investor relations, who spoke on the same earnings call.

  Separately, TVA recently received the first small modular reactor (SMR) construction permit issued by the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission, for a BWRX-300 reactor at the Clinch River Site in Oak Ridge, Tennessee. The filing notes, however, that TVA "has not yet decided whether to construct a reactor at the site."

  ## Capital program and gas generation

  President and CFO Tom Rice said TVA is in a "strong financial position" to execute a $13 billion capital investment plan over the next three years — on top of more than $23 billion in investments the utility has made since 2020.

  The capital plan includes 1.5 GW (gigawatts) of new natural gas generation approved by the TVA board in August and targeted to come online by 2032. One gigawatt is roughly the output of a large conventional power plant, enough to serve several hundred thousand homes under typical load conditions. TVA's board approved that gas facility in August, and the filing states the action "closes out" the utility's previous, now-defunct Middle Tennessee Energy Solution project, with all associated funding transferred to the new gas project.

  As of Sept. 30, TVA had already spent $463 million on long-lead-time equipment originally procured for the earlier project, equipment it now expects to apply to the new gas facility. TVA could spend up to an additional $3.1 billion to complete it.

  ## Supply chain and cost pressures

  TVA's SEC filing flags ongoing execution risk. The utility said it "continues to experience supply chain pressures resulting from inflation, tariffs and other trade restrictions, material constraints, and labor availability." Those factors have contributed to project delays, limited availability of critical materials, and higher costs for both materials and labor, the filing states.

  TVA said it anticipates that "inflationary and tariff pressures will persist into 2027" and that continued or escalating pressure "could result in more substantial operational impacts and increased pressure on power rates," though the utility said challenges have so far been managed with limited disruption to operations.

  ## Significance

  The $1.7 billion deal is the first publicly disclosed contract under TVA's new large-load data center tariff and establishes a visible market price — $1.5 million per MW above the 5 MW threshold — for the cohort-based pricing structure the utility intends to use as a template for future customers. The tariff represents a structural shift in how TVA recovers the grid investment costs that large data center loads require, linking capacity charges directly to the incremental megawatts customers request rather than spreading those costs across the broader rate base.

  TVA said it anticipates inflationary and tariff-related cost pressures will persist through at least 2027.
body_zh: |
  田納西河谷管理局（TVA）與一家未具名資料中心客戶簽訂了一份總金額高達17億美元的協議，適用其針對用電需求超過5 MW客戶所推出的全新大負載費率方案。TVA於11月12日向美國證券交易委員會（SEC）提交的申報文件中揭露了這份合約。

  此費率方案自10月1日起生效，適用於申請容量超過5 MW的資料中心客戶。文件指出：「本協議的容量承諾費用，依據的是超出5 MW部分每MW約150萬美元的費率。」TVA表示，此一定價方式反映了該機構「現行的批次定價方法，未來客戶批次之費率可能據此調整。」

  此費率方案將分階段推行至2029年。根據《Nashville Scene》的報導，分階段機制適用於現有客戶，讓他們在過渡期間得以沿用原有費率結構。

  ## 執行長交接與投資依據

  TVA執行長Mike Skaggs於11月13日的法說會上表示，新版資料中心費率結構已支撐了該機構的投資計畫，相關投資包括延長現有核能機組的運轉壽命，以及他所描述的擴建東田納西地區核燃料與供應樞紐。

  Skaggs是在前任執行長Don Moul退休後，從退休狀態復出擔任TVA代理執行長。TVA財務規劃暨投資人關係副總裁Clifton Lowry在同一場法說會上透露，TVA董事會已於11月10日的會議中正式確認Skaggs出任常任執行長。

  另外，TVA近期也獲得美國核能管制委員會（NRC）核發的首張小型模組化反應爐（SMR）建設許可，許可對象為位於田納西州橡樹嶺（Oak Ridge）Clinch River廠址的BWRX-300型反應爐。然而，文件中也指出，TVA「尚未決定是否在該廠址興建反應爐」。

  ## 資本計畫與天然氣發電

  總裁兼財務長Tom Rice表示，TVA財務狀況穩健，足以在未來三年內執行130億美元的資本投資計畫，而自2020年以來，該機構已累計投入逾230億美元。

  資本計畫涵蓋由TVA董事會於8月核准的1.5 GW（吉瓦）新增天然氣發電容量，預計於2032年前投入運轉。一吉瓦的發電量大致相當於一座大型傳統發電廠的輸出，在典型用電條件下可供數十萬戶家庭使用。董事會於8月核准上述天然氣設施，文件亦指出，此舉「正式終止」了該機構已廢止的中田納西能源方案（Middle Tennessee Energy Solution），所有相關資金均已移撥至新的天然氣計畫。

  截至9月30日，TVA已為原先採購的長交期設備支出4億6,300萬美元，這批設備原本是為前述廢止計畫所訂購，現預計挪用於新的天然氣設施。TVA在後續工程上可能還需再追加最多31億美元的支出。

  ## 供應鏈與成本壓力

  TVA在SEC申報文件中亦點出持續存在的執行風險。該機構表示，「持續面臨通貨膨脹、關稅及其他貿易限制、物料短缺以及勞動力不足所帶來的供應鏈壓力」，這些因素導致工程延宕、關鍵物資供應受限，以及材料與人工成本上揚。

  TVA預估「通膨與關稅壓力將延續至2027年」，並指出若壓力持續或進一步惡化，「可能對營運造成更顯著的衝擊，並對電費費率帶來更大壓力」。不過，TVA表示，目前相關挑戰均已在對營運影響有限的情況下妥善因應。

  ## 重要意義

  這份17億美元的合約，是TVA新版大負載資料中心費率方案下首宗公開揭露的合約，確立了明確的市場價格——超出5 MW門檻部分每MW 150萬美元——作為該機構批次定價結構的範本，供未來各批次客戶參考。此費率方案代表TVA在回收大型資料中心負載所需電網投資成本上的結構性轉變，將容量費用直接與客戶申請的增量MW數掛鉤，而非將這些成本分攤至更廣泛的費率基礎。

  TVA表示，預計通膨及關稅相關成本壓力至少將持續至2027年。
