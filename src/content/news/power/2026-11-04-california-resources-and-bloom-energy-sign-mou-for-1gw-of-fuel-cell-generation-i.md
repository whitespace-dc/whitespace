body_en: |
  California Resources Corporation (CRC) and Bloom Energy have signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding (MOU) to evaluate the development of 1 gigawatt or more of fuel cell generation capacity across California, with data centers among the primary target customers.

  The agreement pairs Bloom's solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs) — stationary generators that convert fuels into electricity through an electrochemical process without combustion, sold under the name Bloom Box — with carbon capture and storage infrastructure controlled by CRC. The partnership covers front-of-the-meter utility-connected projects and behind-the-meter on-site installations across Central and Northern California.

  ## Project structure and scope

  The two companies said they will identify offtake customers including data centers, utilities, community choice aggregators (locally governed entities that procure electricity on behalf of residents), municipalities, and other industrial users. CRC will manage projects and complete interconnection and permitting work; Bloom will supply the fuel cells and third-party carbon capture equipment.

  Behind-the-meter projects would be sited across CRC's Central Valley footprint to provide on-site power to industrial customers. Front-of-the-meter projects would connect to utility offtake on the grid.

  CRC's carbon management subsidiary, Carbon TerraVault (CTV), is expected to handle transportation and permanent underground storage of captured carbon dioxide. The partners said the arrangement could capture and store up to three million metric tons of CO2 per year.

  ## How the technology works and why capture is central

  Bloom's SOFC units generate electricity by passing fuel — predominantly natural gas at present, though the units are compatible with hydrogen — across ceramic plates in an electrochemical reaction rather than burning it. The companies said the absence of combustion produces a concentrated CO2 exhaust stream, which they claim makes carbon capture more efficient and less costly compared with capturing emissions from conventional combustion generators.

  "Our non-combustion technology makes carbon capture more efficient and economically viable at scale, opening a new path to carbon-neutral power generation and addressing the decarbonization needs of large critical infrastructure," said Aman Joshi, Bloom's chief commercial officer.

  Francisco Leon, CRC's president and chief executive, framed the deal around California's power supply constraints. "California needs more clean, firm, reliable, and affordable power generated here at home. We are uniquely positioned with land, vast subsurface storage, and CO2 transportation and storage expertise," Leon said. "When combined with Bloom's non-combustion technology with highly efficient carbon capture, we are positioned to deliver clean and affordable energy to power data centers and the grid at scale."

  ## Bloom's broader data center pipeline

  The CRC agreement extends a string of large-scale fuel cell commitments Bloom has secured across the data center sector. Earlier in 2026, Bloom expanded an agreement with Oracle to supply the cloud company with up to 2.8 gigawatts of SOFC capacity, up from an initial 1.2 gigawatts agreed the prior year. Deployments at Oracle's Jupiter data center in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, have faced opposition to a natural gas pipeline extension planned to supply the facility.

  Bloom also holds agreements with colocation provider Equinix for deployments across 19 data centers totaling more than 100 megawatts, and with U.S. utility American Electric Power (AEP) for up to 1 gigawatt of SOFCs to power artificial intelligence data centers off the main grid. In October 2026, AEP signed a 20-year power purchase agreement with Google that committed the company to offtake 702 megawatts of power from a Bloom-technology fuel cell generation project near Cheyenne, Wyoming.

  ## What comes next

  The non-binding nature of the MOU means the 1-gigawatt target remains contingent on offtake agreements, permitting outcomes, and carbon capture economics that the parties have not yet disclosed. The companies said their immediate next steps include completing the interconnection and permitting work needed to bring projects online.
body_zh: |
  加州資源公司（California Resources Corporation，CRC）與Bloom Energy已簽署一份不具約束力的合作備忘錄（MOU），評估在加州開發合計逾1吉瓦燃料電池發電容量的可行性，資料中心為主要目標客戶之一。

  此協議將Bloom的固態氧化物燃料電池（SOFC）——以電化學方式將燃料轉換為電力、無需燃燒的固定式發電設備，商品名為「Bloom Box」——與CRC旗下的碳捕集與儲存基礎設施結合。合作範圍涵蓋加州中部及北部以電網為接點的前置電表（front-of-the-meter）市電連接型專案，以及裝設於現場的後置電表（behind-the-meter）自用型安裝。

  ## 專案架構與規模

  兩家公司表示，將共同尋找購電客戶，對象包括資料中心、電力公司、社區選擇聚合商（由地方自治機構代表居民採購電力的實體）、市政機關及其他工業用戶。CRC將負責統籌管理專案，並完成電網併接及許可申請作業；Bloom則負責供應燃料電池及第三方碳捕集設備。

  後置電表專案將部署於CRC在中央谷地（Central Valley）的既有據點，為工業客戶提供現場電力。前置電表專案則接入電力公司系統，透過電網進行輸售。

  CRC旗下的碳管理子公司Carbon TerraVault（CTV）預計負責捕集二氧化碳的輸送及永久地下封存作業。雙方表示，此安排每年最多可捕集並封存300萬公噸二氧化碳。

  ## 技術原理與碳捕集的核心地位

  Bloom的SOFC裝置透過讓燃料——目前主要為天然氣，但亦相容氫氣——在陶瓷板上進行電化學反應而發電，全程無需燃燒。兩家公司指出，非燃燒方式可產生濃度較高的二氧化碳廢氣，他們主張這使碳捕集較傳統燃燒式發電機更具效率，成本也更低。

  Bloom首席商務長Aman Joshi表示：「我們的非燃燒技術使碳捕集更具效率，在大規模部署上更具經濟可行性，為碳中和發電開闢全新路徑，同時解決大型關鍵基礎設施的去碳化需求。」

  CRC總裁兼執行長Francisco Leon則以加州電力供應限制為切入點，詮釋這筆交易的意義。Leon表示：「加州需要在地生產更多清潔、穩定、可靠且實惠的電力。我們擁有土地、廣大的地下儲存空間，以及二氧化碳輸送與封存的專業能力，處於得天獨厚的地位。」他補充：「結合Bloom的非燃燒技術與高效碳捕集，我們有能力以規模化方式，為資料中心及電網提供清潔且實惠的能源。」

  ## Bloom更廣泛的資料中心業務佈局

  此次與CRC的協議，延續了Bloom近年來在資料中心領域陸續簽署的一系列大規模燃料電池承諾。2026年稍早，Bloom擴大了與甲骨文（Oracle）的合作協議，將SOFC供應容量上限從前一年議定的1.2吉瓦提升至2.8吉瓦。甲骨文位於新墨西哥州多納安納縣（Doña Ana County）Jupiter資料中心的部署計畫，因一條擬議中的天然氣管線延伸工程遭當地反對而面臨阻礙。

  Bloom亦與主機代管供應商Equinix簽有協議，在其19座資料中心部署合計逾100百萬瓦的容量；並與美國電力公司AEP（American Electric Power）簽有最多1吉瓦SOFC的協議，在主電網以外為人工智慧資料中心供電。2026年10月，AEP與Google簽署一份20年購電協議，Google承諾採購Wyoming州夏安（Cheyenne）附近一座採用Bloom技術燃料電池發電專案所生產的702百萬瓦電力。

  ## 後續展望

  由於此備忘錄不具約束力，1吉瓦的目標仍有賴各方尚未揭露的購電協議、許可申請結果及碳捕集經濟效益方能落實。兩家公司表示，當務之急為完成專案上線所需的電網併接及許可申請作業。
