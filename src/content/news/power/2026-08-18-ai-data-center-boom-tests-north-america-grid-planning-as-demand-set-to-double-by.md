  ## AI load growth compounds existing grid stress

  North America's electric grid is entering a period of demand growth that utilities, grid operators, and planners describe as unlike anything in decades, driven by the simultaneous expansion of artificial intelligence data centers, domestic manufacturing, and electrification across transportation and buildings. The North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC), the body responsible for grid reliability across the continent, projected 224 gigawatts (GW) of summer peak demand growth over the next ten years in its 2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment — the highest projected ten-year growth rate since 1995, with data centers and other large-load customers identified as key drivers.

  At the same time, analysis by Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL), published in its 2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report, estimated that U.S. data center electricity consumption could grow from approximately 176 terawatt-hours (TWh) in 2023 to between 325 and 580 TWh by 2028. One terawatt-hour is roughly the annual electricity use of 90,000 average U.S. homes. The range reflects deep uncertainty about the pace and concentration of AI infrastructure build-out.

  ## Interconnection backlogs move to the center of investment decisions

  As demand accelerates, access to the transmission grid — the high-voltage network that moves power from generators to load centers — has become a primary constraint. Utilities and grid operators are processing growing volumes of interconnection requests from generation developers, storage providers, data center operators, and industrial customers. The transmission systems now being asked to serve this growth were largely planned and built before current demand levels were anticipated, according to the Utility Dive analysis.

  What was once treated as a technical scheduling problem now carries direct economic consequences, the article argues. For hyperscale data center operators and advanced manufacturers, interconnection timelines are becoming as material to site selection as land availability, workforce, or access to capital. Grid readiness is, in practical terms, functioning as a competitive differentiator among regions competing for large-scale investment.

  The planning challenge is structural: commitments for new transmission infrastructure must be made years before the load actually materializes. A single large data center campus can add hundreds of megawatts to a local grid — enough to require significant infrastructure upgrades — and several projects arriving in the same region within a short window can fundamentally alter regional investment priorities.

  ## Uncertainty, not volume, is the hardest planning problem

  Industry planners and grid operators frame the core difficulty not as the total quantity of power AI will require, but as the inability to forecast with confidence where and when that demand will appear. Traditional utility planning was built around a single, relatively stable forecast of future load. That model is no longer adequate, according to the analysis.

  Utilities are instead being pushed toward evaluating multiple plausible futures simultaneously, accounting for the interplay of AI expansion, broader electrification, manufacturing growth, policy shifts, and market forces. The planning objective shifts from producing an accurate single forecast to identifying infrastructure investments that retain value across a wide range of possible outcomes.

  ## Planners turn to analytics and flexible interconnection tools

  In response, utilities are exploring a combination of advanced analytical tools and revised interconnection frameworks. The options under examination include phased interconnection strategies — which allow load to come online in stages as infrastructure catches up — along with demand response programs, flexible service agreements, and large-customer load management arrangements. These approaches do not replace the need for transmission investment or grid modernization, but provide additional mechanisms for managing the timing mismatch between demand growth and infrastructure delivery.

  The broader industry push is toward what planners describe as proactive, data-driven planning: using advanced analytics, digital workflows, high-performance computing, and AI-enabled planning tools to evaluate more scenarios more quickly, identify grid constraints earlier, and prioritize capital expenditure with greater confidence.

  ## Grid readiness to determine where AI investment flows

  The analysis positions grid readiness as the variable most likely to determine where AI-related investment concentrates over the coming decade. Regions and utilities that can process interconnection requests faster, demonstrate transmission capacity, and offer greater certainty on delivery timelines are expected to attract a disproportionate share of hyperscale data center and advanced manufacturing projects.

  NERC's 2025 assessment, projecting 224 GW of summer peak demand growth, represents the clearest public benchmark for the scale of infrastructure buildout the industry now faces over the next ten years.

  ## AI負載成長加劇電網既有壓力

  北美電網正進入一個數十年來罕見的需求成長期。電力公司、電網營運商與規劃人員均表示，這波需求浪潮前所未見，主要驅動力來自人工智慧資料中心的同步擴張、國內製造業回流，以及交通運輸與建築領域的電氣化趨勢。北美電力可靠性協會（NERC）負責監管全大陸的電網可靠性，其2025年長期可靠性評估報告預測，未來十年夏季尖峰需求將增長224吉瓦（GW）——這是自1995年以來最高的十年期預測成長率，資料中心及其他大型用電客戶被列為主要驅動因素。

  與此同時，勞倫斯柏克萊國家實驗室（LBNL）在其2024年美國資料中心能源使用報告中估計，美國資料中心用電量可能從2023年的約176太瓦時（TWh），於2028年前成長至325至580太瓦時之間。一太瓦時約相當於9萬戶美國家庭的年均用電量。這個預測區間反映出，外界對AI基礎設施建設速度與集中程度的深層不確定性。

  ## 電網連接積壓問題成為投資決策核心

  隨著需求加速，進入輸電網路——即將電力從發電廠輸送至用電中心的高壓網路——的管道已成為首要制約因素。電力公司與電網營運商正處理來自發電開發商、儲能業者、資料中心營運商及工業用戶的大量電網連接申請。根據《Utility Dive》的分析，目前被要求承擔這波需求成長的輸電系統，大多是在遠低於當前用電水準的年代所規劃與建設的。

  報告指出，過去被視為技術性排程問題的電網連接，如今已帶來直接的經濟後果。對於超大規模資料中心營運商與先進製造業者而言，電網連接時程對選址決策的影響，已與土地取得、勞動力供應或資本管道同等重要。電網整備度，在實際意義上，已成為各地區競爭大型投資時的差異化競爭優勢。

  這項規劃挑戰具有結構性本質：新輸電基礎設施的承諾，必須在負載實際出現的數年前便作出決策。單一大型資料中心園區可為當地電網帶來數百百萬瓦（MW）的新增需求——足以觸發重大基礎設施升級——若同一地區在短期內湧入多個此類專案，更可能從根本上改變區域投資優先順序。

  ## 不確定性，而非總量，才是最難解的規劃課題

  業界規劃人員與電網營運商將核心難題界定為：問題不在於AI最終將消耗多少電力，而在於無法有把握地預測這些需求將在何時、何地出現。傳統電力公司的規劃模式建立在對未來負載的單一、相對穩定預測之上，然而這套模式已不再適用，分析如此指出。

  電力公司如今被迫同時評估多種可能的未來情境，並考量AI擴張、廣泛電氣化、製造業成長、政策轉向及市場力量之間的交互影響。規劃目標也從產出一個精確的單一預測，轉變為找出在各種可能情境下均能保有價值的基礎設施投資方案。

  ## 規劃人員轉向資料分析與彈性電網連接工具

  對此，電力公司正探索結合先進分析工具與修訂後電網連接框架的應對方案。研議中的選項包括分階段電網連接策略——允許負載隨基礎設施跟進而分批上線——以及需量反應計畫、彈性服務協議，及針對大型用戶的負載管理安排。這些做法並不能取代輸電投資或電網現代化的必要，但提供了額外機制，以管理需求成長與基礎設施交付之間的時間落差。

  更廣泛的業界推動方向，指向規劃人員所稱的「主動式、資料驅動規劃」：運用先進分析技術、數位工作流程、高效能運算及AI輔助規劃工具，更快速地評估更多情境、更早識別電網瓶頸，並以更高信心度排定資本支出的優先順序。

  ## 電網整備度決定AI投資流向

  分析報告將電網整備度定位為未來十年最可能決定AI相關投資集中地點的關鍵變數。能夠更快速處理電網連接申請、展示充足輸電容量，並提供更高交付時程確定性的地區與電力公司，預計將吸引超乎比例的超大規模資料中心及先進製造業專案。

  NERC 2025年評估報告預測夏季尖峰需求將成長224吉瓦，是目前針對業界未來十年所需面對之基礎設施建設規模，最清晰的公開基準指標。
