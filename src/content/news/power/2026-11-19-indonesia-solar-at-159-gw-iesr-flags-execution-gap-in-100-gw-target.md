body_en: |
  Indonesia's installed solar capacity stood at approximately 1.59 gigawatts as of the first quarter of 2026, leaving the country with less than 2 percent of a government target of 100 GW within three years, the Institute for Essential Services Reform (IESR) said Wednesday at the online launch of its Indonesia Solar Energy Outlook 2027 report.

  IESR Executive Director Fabby Tumiwa said the principal obstacle is not planning ambition but execution, particularly in how state utility PT PLN (Persero) procures and delivers solar projects. The gap between PLN's published plans and actual installations has consistently held back the sector, he said.

  ## Planning targets versus delivered capacity

  PLN's Electricity Supply Business Plan — known by its Indonesian acronym RUPTL — set a target of adding 777 megawatts of solar capacity in 2025. Actual additions fell well short of that figure, the IESR said, without specifying the delivered amount. One megawatt is roughly enough to supply several hundred homes under average conditions; 777 MW would have represented a meaningful step in a national buildout.

  The pattern continued into 2026. PLN launched a competitive tender for a 1.2-gigawatt solar project in April 2026, but as of November 18 the utility had not disclosed a winner or a construction schedule.

  "We see a large gap between what is planned and what is executed. This is what we need to resolve if we want to accelerate solar development in Indonesia," Tumiwa said.

  ## Procurement and tender process

  The IESR said the 100-GW target cannot be reached through a business-as-usual approach and that the government and PLN need to overhaul their approach to planning, procurement, and supporting infrastructure.

  On procurement, IESR recommends adopting reverse auctions — a competitive format in which suppliers bid prices down — underpinned by strict technical standards. Procurement decisions also need to be supported by assured grid capacity, completed permitting, and confirmed construction sites before tenders are issued, Tumiwa said.

  IESR also recommends that PLN publish a regular, predictable tender calendar so developers, investors, banks, and equipment suppliers can plan their commitments with confidence.

  ## Grid readiness and storage

  Grid infrastructure is a second binding constraint, according to IESR. Solar additions need to be matched by upgrades to transmission and distribution networks to ensure that generated power can be absorbed by the system. IESR recommends directing large-scale solar projects first to grids already capable of handling the output while reinforcement work proceeds elsewhere.

  Battery energy storage systems, or BESS, are also necessary, IESR said, to stabilize supply and manage the variability inherent in solar generation.

  The grid also needs to become more operationally flexible. One mechanism, Tumiwa said, is retrofitting existing coal-fired steam power plants — known in Indonesia as PLTU — so they can modulate output as solar penetration rises and load patterns shift.

  IESR noted that PLN's own investment capacity is limited and that external investment support for the utility will be needed to advance grid modernization and BESS deployment at the required scale.

  ## Rooftop solar quota

  Tumiwa recommended revising the rooftop solar quota established under Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources Regulation No. 2 of 2024. The quota was introduced when PLN faced excess generation capacity; that rationale, IESR said, is no longer consistent with the current priority of accelerating the energy transition. Any revision must still account for available capacity at individual grid substations, Tumiwa added.

  ## Significance

  IESR's report identifies procurement delays, grid constraints, limited PLN investment capacity, and an outdated rooftop solar policy as independent obstacles to accelerated deployment. Structural changes across all four areas, the organization said, are required if the 100-GW target is to be met. As of the report's release date, the 1.2-GW tender PLN opened in April 2026 had still not produced a disclosed winner or project timeline.
body_zh: |
  印尼太陽能裝置容量截至2026年第一季約為1.59吉瓦，距政府三年內達成100吉瓦的目標尚不及2%。基本服務改革研究所（IESR）週三在《2027年印尼太陽能展望》報告線上發布會上公布了這項數據。

  IESR執行長Fabby Tumiwa表示，主要障礙並非規劃層面的企圖心不足，而在於執行層面——尤其是國有電力公司PT PLN（Persero）在太陽能計畫的採購與交付方式上的問題。他說，PLN公布的計畫與實際裝置量之間的落差，長期以來持續阻礙了這個產業的發展。

  ## 規劃目標與實際裝置容量

  PLN的《電力供應業務計畫》（以印尼文縮寫RUPTL為人所知）設定2025年新增777百萬瓦太陽能容量的目標。IESR表示，實際新增量遠低於這一數字，但未說明具體交付量。就規模而言，1百萬瓦在一般用電條件下約可供應數百戶家庭；777百萬瓦原本將是全國建設進程中的一大步。

  這種模式延續到了2026年。PLN於2026年4月就一項1.2吉瓦的太陽能計畫啟動競爭性招標，但截至11月18日，該電力公司尚未公布得標者或施工時程。

  Tumiwa表示：「我們看到規劃與執行之間存在巨大落差。若要加速印尼太陽能發展，這正是我們必須解決的問題。」

  ## 採購與招標流程

  IESR表示，以現行方式運作不可能達成100吉瓦的目標，政府與PLN必須全面改革規劃、採購及配套基礎設施的作法。

  在採購方面，IESR建議採用逆向競標機制——一種由供應商競相壓低價格的競爭格式——並以嚴格的技術標準作為支撐基礎。Tumiwa表示，採購決策在招標發出之前，還需要確保電網容量充足、許可申請完成，以及施工場址確定。

  IESR也建議PLN定期公布可預期的招標時間表，讓開發商、投資人、銀行及設備供應商能夠有信心地規劃自身的投入承諾。

  ## 電網整備與儲能

  根據IESR的說法，電網基礎設施是第二個關鍵制約因素。太陽能裝置容量的增加必須伴隨輸配電網路的升級，以確保發出的電力能被系統有效吸收。IESR建議，在其他地區進行電網強化工程的同時，優先將大型太陽能計畫導入已具備承載能力的電網。

  IESR表示，電池儲能系統（BESS）也是必要的配套措施，用以穩定供電並管理太陽能發電固有的間歇性問題。

  電網在運營上也需要更具彈性。Tumiwa表示，其中一項機制是對現有燃煤蒸汽發電廠（印尼稱為PLTU）進行改造，使其能在太陽能滲透率上升、負載型態轉變時靈活調節輸出。

  IESR指出，PLN自身的投資能力有限，若要推動電網現代化及所需規模的BESS建設，將需要來自外部的投資支援。

  ## 屋頂型太陽能配額

  Tumiwa建議修訂依據能源礦產資源部2024年第2號規定所設立的屋頂型太陽能配額制度。該配額制度的設立背景是PLN當時面臨發電容量過剩的問題；IESR表示，這一設立理由已不符合當前加速能源轉型的優先方向。Tumiwa補充說，任何修訂仍須考量各地輸電變電站的實際可用容量。

  ## 重要意義

  IESR的報告指出，採購延宕、電網制約、PLN投資能力有限，以及屋頂型太陽能政策過時，是阻礙加速部署的四項獨立障礙。該組織表示，若要實現100吉瓦的目標，必須在上述四個領域全面推動結構性改革。截至報告發布之日，PLN於2026年4月開啟的1.2吉瓦招標案仍未公布得標者或計畫時程。
