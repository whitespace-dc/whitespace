body_en: |
  Indonesia's state electricity company PT PLN faces mounting financial pressure from take-or-pay clauses in its power purchase agreements with private developers, as the country accelerates a large-scale renewable energy buildout, PLN's president director said.

  Of the 69.5 gigawatts (GW) of new generation capacity planned — enough to power hundreds of millions of homes — approximately 75%, or around 55 GW, will come from independent power producers (IPPs), private developers that sell electricity to PLN under long-term contracts, according to PLN President Director Suroso Isnandar.

  ## What take-or-pay means for PLN

  A take-or-pay clause in a power purchase agreement (PPA) — the contract under which PLN buys electricity from private generators — obligates the buyer to pay for a contracted volume of power regardless of whether that power is actually consumed or dispatched onto the grid. Under this structure, PLN remains liable for payments even when electricity output from IPPs exceeds what the grid can absorb at a given time.

  The scale of IPP involvement in Indonesia's expansion plan makes this obligation significant. With 55 GW of the planned addition tied to private contracts carrying take-or-pay terms, PLN's payment commitments are locked in against generation volumes that may not align with actual demand or grid conditions.

  ## Renewable energy ambitions drive IPP reliance

  The pressure on PLN's finances arises directly from Indonesia's push to accelerate the development of new and renewable energy (NRE). The country's expansion program leans heavily on private capital through IPPs to meet its capacity targets, but that reliance transfers contractual risk back to PLN in the form of guaranteed payment obligations.

  Suroso Isnandar highlighted the consequence: the large share of privately developed capacity brings with it unavoidable PPA liabilities under the take-or-pay framework, even when not all energy produced is absorbed by the system.

  ## Significance for Indonesia's energy transition

  The scrutiny of take-or-pay terms points to a structural tension in Indonesia's energy planning. Attracting private investment in renewable generation typically requires the revenue certainty that take-or-pay contracts provide to developers. However, those same guarantees concentrate demand and dispatch risk at PLN, the sole offtaker, and can strain the utility's finances if grid integration lags behind generation growth.

  With 55 GW of contracted private capacity in the pipeline under these terms, the financial exposure embedded in PLN's PPA portfolio is set to grow as Indonesia pursues its renewable energy targets.

  在規劃中的695億瓦（69.5 GW）新增發電裝置容量中——足以供應數億個家庭的用電需求——約75%、即大約550億瓦（55 GW），將由獨立發電業者（IPP）提供。所謂IPP，係指依長期合約向PLN售電的私人發電開發商。上述數字由PLN總裁蘇羅索·伊斯南達爾親口公布。

  ## 照付不議條款對PLN的意義

  購電協議（PPA）中的「照付不議」條款，係指PLN向私人發電業者購電時所簽訂的合約中，明定一項規定：無論實際消耗或調度上網的電量為何，買方均須依約支付合約所載明的電力量費用。在這種架構下，即便IPP的發電量超出電網在特定時段所能吸收的上限，PLN仍須履行付款義務。

  IPP在印尼擴建計畫中所佔的規模，使這項義務格外沉重。由於規劃新增容量中有多達550億瓦綁定於含有「照付不議」條款的私人合約，PLN的付款承諾已被鎖定在特定發電量上，而實際需求或電網條件未必能與之相符。

  ## 再生能源雄心推動對IPP的高度依賴

  PLN財務所承受的壓力，直接源自印尼加速發展新能源與再生能源（NRE）的政策推力。印尼的電力擴建計畫高度仰賴透過IPP引入的民間資本，以達成裝置容量目標；然而這種依賴方式，卻將合約風險以保證付款義務的形式，回頭轉嫁給了PLN。

  蘇羅索·伊斯南達爾特別指出其中的後果：大比例的私人開發容量，在「照付不議」框架下帶來了無可迴避的購電協議負債，即便系統實際上並未完全消耗所生產的電力，這些負債依然存在。

  ## 對印尼能源轉型的重要意義

  外界對「照付不議」條款的高度關注，揭示了印尼能源規劃中一個結構性矛盾。吸引私人資本投入再生能源發電，通常需要「照付不議」合約所能賦予開發商的收益確定性；然而，這些保證同時也將需求與調度風險集中在PLN這個唯一的電力買受方身上，一旦電網整合速度跟不上裝機增速，便可能對這家電力公司的財務造成嚴峻衝擊。

  在上述條款框架下，目前已有550億瓦的私人簽約容量列入開發管線，PLN購電協議組合中所蘊含的財務曝險，將隨著印尼積極追求再生能源目標的腳步持續擴大。
