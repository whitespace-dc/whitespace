body_en: |
  President Donald Trump declared on Oct. 21 that new, large-scale data centers must generate all of their own electricity, sell or return excess power to local communities or the grid, and make financial deals with host communities, a directive that would require many projects already under way to reassess energy strategies already locked in if translated into formal policy.

  Trump posted the statement on Truth Social, writing that new and large data centers are "mandated by me to make their own electricity" and that developers should pay community residents a "dividend" to build local support. If his position were formalized, developers would face difficult questions about whether existing plans would need to change, according to Data Center Knowledge.

  ## What the industry says about onsite generation

  Onsite generation at AI data centers is technically achievable but broadly impractical as a universal requirement. Patrick Hughes, senior vice president of strategy, technical, and industry affairs at the National Electrical Manufacturers Association (NEMA), said the technology exists, whether combined-cycle gas plants or solar paired with battery storage, but making it work reliably at industry scale is a different problem.

  "It's possible for a standalone AI data center to provide sufficient onsite power to fuel its own operations," Hughes said. "But that doesn't mean it's feasible for a single AI data center — let alone the whole fleet of AI data centers — to supply 100% of their own power and operate reliably 24/7; that requires significant operational management and maintenance."

  Hughes contributed to the AI Data Center Energy Performance Framework, a technical standard co-developed by NEMA, the American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers (ASHRAE), and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL), which sets guidance on safety, resilience, and grid interaction for AI data center design.

  ## Equipment and supply chain barriers

  Equipment availability poses one of the most immediate practical constraints. Many components required for gas-fired, nuclear, and battery-backed onsite power systems are already in short supply, with some turbine models effectively sold out through 2031.

  AI data centers are already competing with utilities, commercial buildings, transportation, and industrial manufacturers for turbines, switchgear, transformers, batteries, and specialized labor. Permitting timelines, fuel supply chains, interconnection rules, and community considerations also vary widely by location, Hughes said.

  AI training and inference facilities have power densities and load profiles that differ sharply from traditional data centers and from facilities that have historically relied on co-located generation, adding further complexity to any broad bring-your-own-power mandate.

  ## Existing power agreements and clean-energy commitments

  A blanket onsite power requirement would collide with existing contractual and sustainability commitments. Many projects already under construction have secured grid connections, signed power purchase agreements (PPAs, long-term contracts to buy electricity from a specific generator), or otherwise locked in their energy strategy.

  The Stargate project in Abilene, Texas, illustrates the mixed-source reality already in operation. The project is using natural gas, fuel cells, and gas engines during construction and to power its first operating data centers. Developers are also planning solar and wind additions; Abilene was selected in part because of its strong solar irradiance, abundant undeveloped land, and absence of major nearby urban electricity loads. With developers targeting a total capacity of 1.2 gigawatts (GW) once all phases are complete, wind and solar are expected to supplement other sources.

  Applied Digital's campus in Ellendale, North Dakota offers a second example of developers aligning with available generation rather than building their own. The company arranged 400 megawatts (MW) of stranded wind power (electricity produced by wind farms with no nearby demand to absorb it) to serve the campus. Applied Digital also recently signed a deal with data center developer Soluna to use output from Soluna's 150 MW Briscoe wind farm for a new Texas data center.

  "We have said for a long time that the constraint in AI is energy access, not chips," Soluna CEO John Belizaire said. "We built Soluna to bring load to the generation, so the power would be ready when the demand arrived."

  ## Hybrid model seen as the practical path

  If Trump's position were formalized as policy, any workable rule would likely need to accommodate a range of configurations rather than mandate complete self-sufficiency. The AI Data Center Energy Performance Framework does not prescribe onsite or grid power exclusively; it addresses onsite and offsite renewables, intelligent energy storage, and grid-interactive design across the full range of facility configurations.

  "There's no single answer, as the right onsite generation strategy will inevitably depend on each facility's load, site, and regulatory landscape," Hughes said.

  For many facilities, the most practical outcome under any new policy is a hybrid model combining onsite generation and storage with a maintained grid connection. If Trump's directive were translated into formal policy, projects already under construction with existing grid connections and PPAs would face questions about whether those arrangements would need to change.

  美國總統唐納·川普於10月21日宣布，新建大型資料中心必須全部自行發電，並將多餘電力售予或回饋地方社區或電網，同時須與所在社區達成財務協議。若此方向正式成為政策，許多已在施工中的計畫將被迫重新評估原本已確定的能源策略。

  川普在社群平台Truth Social發文，表示新建大型資料中心「受我強制要求，必須自行發電」，並呼籲開發商向社區居民支付「股利」，以爭取在地支持。據《Data Center Knowledge》報導，若其立場正式化為政策，開發商將面臨既有計畫是否需要變更的棘手問題。

  ## 業界對現址自行發電的看法

  AI資料中心在現址自行發電在技術上雖可行，但若作為全行業的統一要求，在實務上卻有相當困難。美國全國電氣製造商協會（NEMA）策略、技術暨產業事務資深副總裁派翠克·休斯（Patrick Hughes）表示，無論是複循環燃氣電廠，還是搭配電池儲能的太陽能系統，技術本身確實存在，但要在產業規模下穩定運作，是另一個層次的問題。

  休斯表示：「讓一座獨立的AI資料中心自行供應足夠的電力以維持運作，技術上是可以辦到的。但這並不代表單一AI資料中心——更遑論整個AI資料中心群——能夠100%自行供電並全天候穩定運作；這需要高度複雜的操作管理與維護工作。」

  休斯參與制訂了「AI資料中心能源效能框架」（AI Data Center Energy Performance Framework），這是由NEMA、美國採暖、製冷與空調工程師學會（ASHRAE）及太平洋西北國家實驗室（PNNL）共同開發的技術標準，針對AI資料中心設計的安全性、韌性及電網互動提供指引。

  ## 設備與供應鏈的障礙

  設備的取得是最直接的實務限制之一。燃氣發電、核能及電池備援的現址供電系統所需的許多元件，如今已面臨嚴重短缺，部分型號的渦輪機實際上已售罄至2031年。

  AI資料中心已在與電力公用事業、商業建築、交通運輸及工業製造商爭搶渦輪機、開關設備、變壓器、電池及專業人力。休斯指出，許可審核時程、燃料供應鏈、電網併接規範及社區因素也因地區而有極大差異。

  AI訓練與推論設施的功率密度及負載特性，與傳統資料中心有顯著不同，也與過去仰賴共置發電設施的情況大相逕庭，這使得任何大範圍的「自備電力」強制要求更加複雜。

  ## 既有電力協議與潔淨能源承諾

  全面要求現址自行發電，將與現有的合約義務及永續承諾產生衝突。許多仍在建設中的計畫已取得電網連接資格、簽訂購電協議（PPA，即與特定發電商簽訂的長期購電合約），或以其他方式確定了能源策略。

  位於德州阿比林（Abilene）的Stargate計畫，充分展現了混合能源來源在實際運作中的樣貌。該計畫在施工期間及首批資料中心運作時，使用天然氣、燃料電池及燃氣引擎供電。開發商也規劃加入太陽能及風能；阿比林之所以雀屏中選，部分原因在於當地擁有充沛的日照條件、大量未開發土地，以及附近缺乏大型城市電力負載。隨著各期計畫全數完工後的目標總容量設定為1.2吉瓦（GW），風能與太陽能預計將作為其他電源的補充。

  Applied Digital在北達科他州艾倫代爾（Ellendale）的園區，提供了另一個開發商就地利用既有發電資源、而非自建電廠的案例。該公司安排了400百萬瓦（MW）的「擱淺風電」（即風電場所發出的電力，因周邊需求不足而無法被消化的電量）供應該園區。Applied Digital近期也與資料中心開發商Soluna簽訂協議，使用Soluna旗下150 MW的Briscoe風電場所產出的電力，供應其在德州新建的資料中心。

  Soluna執行長約翰·貝利澤爾（John Belizaire）表示：「我們長期以來一直強調，AI的瓶頸在於能源取得，而非晶片。我們創立Soluna，就是為了將負載帶到發電端，讓電力在需求出現時已備妥就緒。」

  ## 混合模式被視為務實之路

  若川普的立場正式化為政策，任何可行的規範可能都須能容納各種不同的配置方式，而非強制要求完全自給自足。「AI資料中心能源效能框架」並未單獨規定須採現址發電或電網供電；它涵蓋現址及離址再生能源、智慧儲能，以及各類設施配置下的電網互動設計。

  休斯表示：「沒有放諸四海皆準的答案，正確的現址發電策略終究取決於各設施的負載需求、所在地點及監管環境。」

  對許多設施而言，無論新政策如何規定，最務實的結果或許是採用混合模式——結合現址發電與儲能，同時維持電網連接。若川普的指令正式轉化為政策，那些仍在建設中、已取得電網連接資格並簽訂PPA的計畫，將面臨這些既有安排是否須做出改變的問題。
