body_en: |
  AI data center construction in Taiwan faces primary bottlenecks in grid access and equipment supply chain coordination rather than server delivery, with executives from Wistron and Delta Electronics warning that delays in individual components — including an imported fire suppression unit — can hold entire projects at a standstill.

  ## Power as the prerequisite

  Wistron Chief Technology Officer Shen Qing-yao said the industry's standard practice has become: secure an electricity supply before discussing anything else. He noted that the sector has taken to joking that operators now need to "bring their own bento box" — meaning, generate their own power on-site — given how difficult grid connections have become.

  Wistron's 10 MW (megawatt) data center in Tainan, in southern Taiwan, illustrates the constraint. Despite local government support, the power infrastructure could not be brought online all at once. Taiwan Power Company (Taipower) is delivering supply in two phases: 5 MW in October 2026 and a second 5 MW tranche in March 2027.

  A separately planned 40 MW facility in Hukou, in northern Taiwan, was abandoned after Wistron concluded that existing manufacturing-sector power allocations at the site could not be transferred to data center use. The company redirected its buildout to southern Taiwan.

  ## Equipment costs and lead times

  Shen said a 16 MW data center — spanning land, buildings, cooling systems, networking, heavy electrical infrastructure, and GPU (graphics processing unit) servers — requires a minimum investment of NT$30 billion.

  GPU server and rack delivery takes around six months. In practice, however, Shen said actual build-outs over the past two years have more frequently been held up by delays in PDUs (power distribution units, devices that route electricity to individual server racks) and heavy electrical equipment, rather than by the servers themselves.

  ## The single-component bottleneck

  Delta Electronics Taiwan Business General Manager Zhang Li-ye cited the company's data center in Tongluo, Miaoli County, as a case study in how supply chain fragility can undercut near-complete construction. Main engineering work was almost finished when the entire project stalled because a single fire suppression component, imported from Spain, had not arrived.

  Zhang said AI data centers now require large volumes of transformers, UPS (uninterruptible power supply) systems, power distribution equipment, and fire suppression gear — a volume and complexity that makes the traditional approach of treating each system separately no longer workable. He said the sector needs system integrators who can coordinate equipment procurement and engineering timelines from the outset.

  ## Grid connection as a separate barrier

  Wu Jin-zhong, a former deputy general manager of Taiwan Power Company who now chairs Dayuan Automotive Power, said the industry's core problem is not whether power is available but whether it can physically be delivered to a given site. Grid engineering capacity, contractor bandwidth, and heavy electrical equipment lead times can each independently extend the timeline for connecting a new facility to the public grid.

  Wu recommended that operators file power supply applications as early as possible. He also said operators should deploy energy storage systems and behind-the-meter microgrids — local electricity networks operating on the customer's side of the utility connection point — to absorb sudden load fluctuations generated by large-scale AI computing, reducing the demand spikes' impact on the broader grid.

  ## Shift in deployment constraints

  The combined picture from Wistron, Delta Electronics, and Wu reflects a change in how the industry identifies the primary limit on AI infrastructure deployment. The executives said the key constraint is no longer server delivery alone but whether power supply, equipment, and engineering can be coordinated in advance and proceed simultaneously across all workstreams. Shen indicated that each element arriving at a different time — rather than any single element being unavailable — has become the defining factor in how quickly a data center can come online.
body_zh: |
  台灣AI資料中心的建設，最主要的瓶頸並非伺服器交貨，而在於電網接入與設備供應鏈的統籌協調。緯創資通與台達電子的高層主管警告，光是某個進口零組件延誤——包括一批消防滅火設備——就足以讓整個建案陷入停擺。

  ## 電力供應是一切的前提

  緯創資通技術長沈慶堯表示，業界已形成一套標準做法：談任何事之前，先確保電力供應無虞。他指出，業者之間甚至開始流傳一句玩笑話，說現在的業者得「自帶便當」——意即自行在場址就地發電——因為申請電網接入的難度已高到如此地步。

  緯創位於台灣南部台南市的10百萬瓦（MW）資料中心，正說明了這項限制有多具體。儘管獲得地方政府支持，電力基礎設施仍無法一次到位。台灣電力公司（台電）採分兩期供電：第一期5 MW預計於2026年10月供電，第二期5 MW則延至2027年3月。

  此外，緯創原本計劃在台灣北部新竹湖口設立一座40 MW的資料中心，最終卻宣告放棄。緯創評估後認定，該地點現有的製造業用電配額無法移轉供資料中心使用，因此將整體建設方向轉移至台灣南部。

  ## 設備成本與交貨前置期

  沈慶堯表示，一座16 MW的資料中心——涵蓋土地、建築、冷卻系統、網路、重電基礎設施與GPU（繪圖處理器）伺服器——最低投資門檻為新台幣300億元。

  GPU伺服器與機架的交貨期約需六個月。然而沈慶堯指出，在過去兩年實際推進的建案中，造成延誤的因素往往不是伺服器本身，而是PDU（電源分配單元，即將電力分配至各伺服器機架的裝置）以及重電設備交貨遲緩所致。

  ## 單一零組件即可卡關全局

  台達電子台灣業務總經理張立業，以公司位於苗栗縣銅鑼的資料中心為例，說明供應鏈的脆弱性如何在工程幾近完工之際拖垮整個案場。主體工程幾乎已竣工，卻因一個從西班牙進口的消防滅火零組件遲遲未能抵台，導致整個專案被迫停擺。

  張立業表示，AI資料中心如今需要大量的變壓器、UPS（不斷電系統）、電力分配設備與消防滅火設備，其規模與複雜度使得過去「各系統分開處理」的傳統做法已不再可行。他強調，業界需要系統整合商，從專案一開始就統籌設備採購與工程時程的全面協調。

  ## 電網接入是另一道獨立的障礙

  曾任台電副總經理、現任大原汽車電力董事長的吳進忠表示，業界的核心問題不在於電力是否存在，而在於電力能否實際輸送至指定場址。電網工程容量、承包商的作業能量，以及重電設備的交貨前置期，三者各自都可能單獨拉長新機房接入公共電網的時程。

  吳進忠建議，業者應盡早提出電力供應申請。他同時建議業者部署儲能系統，以及表後微電網——即在電力公司接入點用戶側獨立運行的區域電力網路——以吸收大規模AI運算產生的瞬間負載波動，降低需求尖峰對整體電網的衝擊。

  ## 部署限制的轉變

  綜合緯創資通、台達電子與吳進忠的觀點，呈現出業界對AI基礎設施部署主要限制的認知已產生根本性轉變。幾位高層均指出，關鍵制約因素已不再只是伺服器的交貨速度，而是電力供應、設備與工程能否事先協調妥當，並在所有工作流程中同步推進。沈慶堯表示，各個環節的到位時間參差不齊——而非任何單一環節付之闕如——才是影響資料中心能否快速上線的決定性因素。
