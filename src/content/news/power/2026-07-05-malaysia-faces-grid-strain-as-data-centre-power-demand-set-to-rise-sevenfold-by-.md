  ## Malaysia faces grid strain as data centre power demand set to rise sevenfold by 2035

  Malaysia's rapid accumulation of data centre investment has produced a structural power problem: electricity consumption by data centres is projected to climb from 10,544 gigawatt-hours (GWh) today to 73,274 GWh by 2035, a near-sevenfold increase, while peak demand across Peninsular Malaysia is forecast to rise more than 57% over the same period, according to The Star.

  The surge reflects the outsized and continuous electricity appetite of data centres, which is expected to grow further as artificial intelligence applications become more widespread. A single data centre, the publication noted, may consume as much electricity as a small city.

  ## Infrastructure costs and who pays them

  Meeting projected demand will require capital expenditure running into tens of billions of ringgit over the coming decade, covering not only new generation capacity but also transmission lines, substations, reserve capacity, and grid modernisation, The Star reported.

  The government has already extended the operating life of ageing thermal power plants and accelerated new gas-fired generation to address near-term supply gaps. The publication said that while those steps are necessary in the short term, they risk locking Malaysia into greater fossil fuel dependence at a time when the country is also pursuing an energy transition.

  The central policy question is cost allocation. If Tenaga Nasional Bhd (TNB), Malaysia's national utility, is required to fund significant grid expansion, those costs must be recovered somewhere — through regulated returns, government subsidies, or electricity tariffs paid by households and businesses, the publication said.

  The Star argued the preferred outcome is one in which data centre operators bear a larger share of infrastructure costs through connection charges, dedicated power facilities, and procurement of renewable energy (RE) — power generated from sources such as solar or wind — rather than having consumers effectively subsidise the digital economy.

  ## Government conditions on approvals

  The government has indicated that new data centre approvals must be aligned with available grid capacity and that operators should develop their own RE sources to reduce reliance on the national grid, The Star reported. The publication said implementation will be decisive, warning that capacity-based screening must function as a genuine filter rather than yielding to investment attraction pressures.

  ## Economic returns under scrutiny

  The scale of power consumption relative to employment is drawing increasing scrutiny. Data centres generate far fewer jobs than conventional manufacturing investments of comparable scale, The Star noted, citing a growing analyst debate over whether the economic returns from data centres are commensurate with the demands they place on the national grid.

  The publication said Malaysia should welcome data centre investment, but not at the expense of electricity consumers — a framing that signals the government faces competing pressures as it attempts to sustain foreign investment inflows while managing grid costs and energy transition commitments.

  The government's push for operators to procure their own renewable energy and develop dedicated power facilities represents the most forward-looking policy mechanism currently in place to shift infrastructure costs away from the broader consumer base.

  ## 馬來西亞電網承壓：資料中心用電需求2035年前預計暴增七倍

  馬來西亞近年積極吸引資料中心投資，卻因此衍生了嚴峻的電力結構問題。根據《星報》（The Star）報導，馬國資料中心的用電量預計將從目前的10,544百萬瓦時（GWh）暴增至2035年的73,274 GWh，幾乎是七倍之多；與此同時，半島馬來西亞的尖峰電力需求在同期亦預計上升逾57%。

  這股電力需求的飆升，反映了資料中心龐大且不間斷的耗電特性，隨著人工智慧（AI）應用日益普及，這一需求預計還將持續擴大。《星報》指出，單一資料中心的用電量，可能高達一座小型城市的耗電規模。

  ## 基礎設施費用由誰埋單？

  《星報》報導，若要滿足預測中的用電需求，未來十年所需資本支出恐達數百億令吉，涵蓋範圍不僅限於新增發電容量，還包括輸電線路、變電站、備用容量及電網現代化工程。

  馬來西亞政府已著手延長老舊火力發電廠的運轉壽命，並加快新增燃氣發電機組，以填補近期的供電缺口。《星報》表示，雖然上述措施在短期內有其必要性，但卻可能在馬國正積極推動能源轉型之際，使其更深陷對化石燃料的依賴困境。

  核心政策問題在於成本的分攤機制。《星報》指出，若馬來西亞國家電力公司（Tenaga Nasional Bhd，TNB）須自行籌措大規模電網擴建資金，這些成本終將轉嫁至某處——無論是透過受監管的投資報酬、政府補貼，還是由一般家庭與企業承擔的電費。

  《星報》認為，最理想的結果，是資料中心業者透過連接費用、自建專屬電力設施，以及採購再生能源（RE）——亦即太陽能、風能等來源所發的電力——來承擔更大比例的基礎設施成本，而非讓消費者實質上為數位經濟進行補貼。

  ## 政府對審批案件附加條件

  《星報》報導，馬來西亞政府已表明，新的資料中心核准案件必須與現有電網容量相符，且業者應自行開發再生能源，以降低對國家電網的依賴。《星報》強調，政策的落實執行將是關鍵所在，並警告說，以容量為基礎的審核機制必須真正發揮把關作用，而非在招商引資的壓力下讓步妥協。

  ## 經濟效益備受質疑

  資料中心龐大的電力消耗相對於其所創造的就業機會，正引發越來越多的審視目光。《星報》援引分析師間日益升溫的辯論指出，資料中心創造的就業機會遠少於規模相當的傳統製造業投資，外界對於資料中心帶來的經濟回報是否與其對國家電網造成的負擔相稱，疑慮持續加深。

  《星報》表示，馬來西亞應歡迎資料中心投資，但不應以犧牲電力消費者的利益為代價——這樣的論述框架，揭示了政府正承受著多方角力的壓力，一方面試圖維持外資持續流入，另一方面又須兼顧電網成本管控與能源轉型的承諾。

  政府要求業者自行採購再生能源、自建專屬電力設施的政策方向，是目前最具前瞻性的政策機制，旨在將基礎設施成本從廣大消費者身上移轉，由資料中心業者自行承擔。
