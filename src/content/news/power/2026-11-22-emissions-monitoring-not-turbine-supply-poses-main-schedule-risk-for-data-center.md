body_en: |
  The gating factor for behind-the-meter data center power projects is not turbine procurement but the air permitting, emissions monitoring, and outside certification sequence required before a unit can legally operate, Daniel Mullen, senior manager for environmental and process monitoring at Thermo Fisher Scientific, argued in Data Center Dynamics.

  Worldwide gas turbine orders reached 846 units and 100.3 gigawatts in 2025, more than double the prior year, with lead times on new heavy-duty orders running roughly three years and delivery slots booked toward 2030, Mullen wrote. A GE Vernova executive has noted that turbines are not the gating factor slowing data center buildouts, even with order books full through 2028, he added. The constraint that project teams consistently underestimate, Mullen argued, is what comes after delivery: a fixed compliance chain with minimum durations that cannot be shortened by adding resources.

  Data centers are on track to reach roughly seven percent to 12 percent of total US electricity demand by 2028, according to the US Data Center Energy Usage Report, as cited by Mullen. Every fraction of that growth drawn from on-site combustion carries a new permitting and monitoring obligation.

  ## What CEMS is and where it fits in the compliance sequence

  A continuous emissions monitoring system, or CEMS, measures a combustion unit's exhaust continuously so that operators can demonstrate compliance with permit limits. A typical system consists of a sample interface, gas conditioning equipment, analyzers, and a data acquisition and handling system. CEMS does not replace the required commissioning process or mandatory outside source emissions testing; it must be installed and operating correctly before that outside testing can take place, Mullen wrote.

  That sequencing makes CEMS procurement an early-project concern, not a late-stage one. Integrators historically staffed for a steady volume of power-sector emissions installations are now scaling to absorb a wave of new data center projects, and shop floor and bench capacity are finite, Mullen wrote. Engaging an integrator early secures a position in the queue and keeps the CEMS schedule aligned with turbine commissioning rather than trailing behind it.

  ## Federal and state regulatory requirements

  Large stationary combustion turbines may be subject to the Environmental Protection Agency's New Source Performance Standards, known as NSPS, under 40 CFR Part 60. Applicability depends on the turbine's size, construction or modification date, fuel type, and configuration. The federal rule may require continuous emissions monitoring, continuous parameter monitoring, performance testing, or a combination, Mullen wrote.

  Federal requirements form only part of the compliance framework. State and local permitting authorities can impose additional monitoring, testing, and reporting obligations through their own regulations and permit conditions, Mullen noted. As a result, two otherwise similar turbine projects can face different monitoring requirements depending on where they are installed and how they are permitted.

  ## The certification sequence and why it cannot be shortened

  For behind-the-meter gas turbines that are not grid connected, EPA Performance Specification 2 sets the CEMS certification sequence: a seven-day calibration drift test, a linearity check, a cycle time test, verification of the data acquisition and handling system, and a Relative Accuracy Test Audit, or RATA, conducted by an independent third-party testing firm against reference methods. Each step carries minimum durations that do not shrink by assigning more personnel to the project, Mullen wrote.

  A project team that defers CEMS to the construction punch list arrives at that sequence with no schedule float remaining. A team that plans it from site selection can pre-stage integration and book a source testing firm in advance, allowing testing to begin as soon as the unit is mechanically ready rather than weeks after, Mullen wrote. His recommendation is to treat the RATA as a named milestone on the master project schedule, with the same visibility as turbine delivery, and to plan backward through its dependencies: CEMS installation, calibration gas supply, control system integration, and source testing firm availability.

  ## Technology and vendor selection

  Technology choice affects commissioning risk, Mullen argued. Equipment with a proven track record in the application reduces the likelihood that a problem during commissioning escalates into a question about whether the system is suitable at all. Field repairability matters equally: systems that trained technicians can diagnose and repair on site limit delays, while equipment requiring components to be returned to the manufacturer can extend them.

  As regulators place greater scrutiny on the environmental impact of data centers and emissions limits tighten in some jurisdictions, CEMS analyzers capable of accurately measuring lower concentrations reduce both startup and ongoing compliance risk, Mullen wrote. For operators running multi-site programs, a consistent technology and support model can reduce variability across projects, he added.

  Thermo Fisher Scientific is itself a supplier of environmental and process monitoring equipment, including CEMS analyzers for the power sector. Mullen's article advocates for earlier engagement with CEMS vendors and integrators on the basis that those supplier resources are increasingly constrained by the surge in data center installations.

  ## What it means for data center project planning

  Mullen's argument is a direct challenge to how data center power project timelines are typically structured. With turbine delivery now a known, plannable constraint, he frames the remaining schedule risk as residing in a regulatory and certification chain that starts at site selection, runs through fixed-duration steps, and ends only when the RATA is complete. That chain is insensitive to late-stage acceleration.

  As data center developers continue to book turbine delivery slots extending toward 2030, the RATA and the full certification sequence leading to it represent the final regulatory gate between hardware delivery and lawful operation of on-site generation capacity.
body_zh: |
  賽默飛世爾科技（Thermo Fisher Scientific）環境與製程監測資深經理丹尼爾·穆倫（Daniel Mullen）在《Data Center Dynamics》撰文主張，決定表後資料中心電力計畫進度的關鍵因素，並非渦輪機採購，而是機組合法運轉前所需完成的空氣許可申請、排放監測設備安裝，以及外部認證程序。

  穆倫指出，2025年全球燃氣渦輪機訂單達到846台、總容量100.3吉瓦（GW），較前一年倍增以上，新型重型機組的交貨前置時間約需三年，交貨位置已預訂至2030年前後。GE Vernova一名主管曾表示，即便訂單已排滿至2028年，渦輪機並非拖慢資料中心建設的瓶頸所在。穆倫認為，專案團隊長期低估的制約因素，在於機組交貨之後的一連串法規遵循程序——這套程序有固定的最短時程，無論投入多少人力都無法壓縮。

  根據美國《資料中心能源使用報告》（US Data Center Energy Usage Report），美國資料中心的用電量預計在2028年占全國總電力需求的7%至12%。每一分來自現場燃燒設備的發電成長，都將帶來新的許可申請與監測義務。

  ## 什麼是連續排放監測系統，它在法規遵循程序中的角色為何

  連續排放監測系統（Continuous Emissions Monitoring System，CEMS）可持續量測燃燒機組的排氣狀況，使操作人員得以證明符合許可限值。典型的CEMS由取樣介面、氣體調節設備、分析儀，以及資料擷取與處理系統所組成。穆倫指出，CEMS並不能取代必要的試運轉程序或強制性外部源排放測試；外部測試進行之前，CEMS必須先完成安裝並確認正常運作。

  這樣的順序關係，使得CEMS採購必須在計畫初期即列入考量，而非留到後期才處理。整合商歷來服務電力業排放監測安裝案的產能，如今必須大幅擴張以消化大量新增的資料中心案件，而車間與工作台的產能畢竟有限。穆倫表示，早期與整合商接洽，可搶先取得排程位置，確保CEMS的施工時程與渦輪機試運轉同步推進，而非落後其後。

  ## 聯邦與州級法規要求

  大型固定式燃燒渦輪機可能受美國環保署（EPA）依《聯邦法規》第40編第60部分（40 CFR Part 60）所訂定的「新源性能標準」（New Source Performance Standards，NSPS）規範。適用性取決於渦輪機的容量、建設或改裝日期、燃料類型及配置。穆倫寫道，聯邦規則可能要求進行連續排放監測、連續參數監測、性能測試，或上述要求的組合。

  聯邦要求只是法規遵循架構的一部分。州及地方許可主管機關可透過自身的法規和許可條件，另行附加監測、測試及申報義務。穆倫指出，因此，即便兩個條件相近的渦輪機計畫，所面對的監測要求也可能因所在地點及許可方式不同而存在差異。

  ## 認證程序及其無法壓縮的原因

  對於未與電網相連的表後燃氣渦輪機，美國環保署《性能規範》第2號（EPA Performance Specification 2）訂定了CEMS認證程序：七天的校準飄移測試、線性度確認、循環時間測試、資料擷取與處理系統的核驗，以及由獨立第三方測試機構依據參考方法執行的「相對準確度測試稽核」（Relative Accuracy Test Audit，RATA）。穆倫寫道，每個步驟都有固定的最短時程，無論增派多少人力都無法壓縮。

  若專案團隊將CEMS留到施工收尾清單才處理，到了這套程序開始時將已毫無時程緩衝。反之，若團隊從選址階段就將其納入規劃，便可預先佈署整合作業、提前預約源排放測試機構，讓測試得以在機組機械就緒後隨即展開，而非再等上數週。穆倫建議，應將RATA列為主計畫中一個明確的里程碑，與渦輪機交貨具有同等能見度，並從RATA向前逆向規劃其前置作業：CEMS安裝、校準氣體供應、控制系統整合，以及源排放測試機構的檔期確認。

  ## 技術選型與廠商選擇

  穆倫主張，技術選型會影響試運轉風險。在同類應用中具有良好實績的設備，可降低試運轉期間一旦出現問題，便演變為「系統根本不適用」之質疑的可能性。現場可修復性同樣重要：受過訓練的技術人員能夠就地診斷並修復的系統，可將延誤降至最低；而需要將零組件送回原廠的設備，則可能大幅延長停工時間。

  穆倫寫道，隨著監管機關對資料中心環境影響的審查日趨嚴格、部分地區的排放限值持續收緊，能夠精確量測較低濃度的CEMS分析儀，可同時降低啟動期間及後續長期的法規遵循風險。他補充指出，對於經營多站點計畫的業者而言，採用一致的技術與支援模式，有助於降低各計畫之間的執行差異。

  賽默飛世爾科技本身也是環境與製程監測設備的供應商，產品線涵蓋電力業使用的CEMS分析儀。穆倫在文中呼籲業界提早與CEMS廠商及整合商接洽，理由是相關廠商資源正因資料中心安裝案激增而日益緊張。

  ## 對資料中心專案規劃的意涵

  穆倫的論點，直接挑戰了資料中心電力計畫時程規劃的慣常思維。在渦輪機交貨已成為可預知、可納入規劃之制約因素的當下，他將剩餘的時程風險定位在一套從選址開始、歷經固定時程各步驟、直至RATA完成方告終的法規與認證程序之上。這套程序對後期加速無動於衷。

  隨著資料中心開發商持續預訂延伸至2030年前後的渦輪機交貨位置，RATA及其前置的完整認證程序，代表著硬體交貨與現場自備發電容量合法運轉之間的最後一道法規關卡。
