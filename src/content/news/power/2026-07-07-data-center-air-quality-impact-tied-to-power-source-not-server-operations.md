body_en: |
  Data centers produce negligible direct air pollution under standard operations, but their effect on local and regional air quality depends almost entirely on the source of their electricity and the extent to which they operate fossil-fueled generation on site.

  The distinction matters as data center power consumption grows and regulators and communities pay closer attention to where large facilities are built and how they are powered.

  ## What data centers emit on site

  A data center is a building housing servers and supporting electrical and cooling infrastructure. Under standard operating conditions it does not combust fuel and produces no flue gases. The most common on-site emission is water vapor from evaporative cooling systems.

  Because servers generate substantial heat, most modern facilities do not need on-site furnaces or boilers for space heating, removing a combustion source common in many commercial and industrial buildings. Once operational, data centers also produce relatively little vehicle traffic: they run with small headcounts and receive few deliveries after commissioning, though construction phases can be emissions-intensive.

  ## The electricity variable

  The more consequential factor is the power supply. Data centers consume electricity at scale, and the air-quality consequence of that demand tracks directly to the generation sources behind the grid.

  Several factors govern the magnitude and location of this indirect pollution:

  - **Power sourcing.** Facilities drawing a large share of electricity from renewables carry lower associated emissions because renewable generation produces no air pollutants during operation.
  - **Grid power versus on-site generation.** Most data centers draw from the grid, where large central plants typically achieve better fuel efficiency per unit of output and may incorporate emission controls unavailable in smaller generators. Facilities that use on-site power generation, whether routinely or as a grid supplement, tend to produce more localized emissions.
  - **Primary versus backup use.** Generators running continuously or frequently for primary power drive substantially greater emissions than units reserved for periodic testing and genuine emergencies.
  - **Fuel type.** Natural gas produces fewer pollutants than diesel or other higher-carbon fuels. Facilities relying on diesel for extended periods generate more local air pollution.

  ## What EPA data shows, and what it does not

  EPA analyses have found a correlation between proximity to data centers and elevated pollution levels in surrounding communities, suggesting that residents living near data centers may be exposed to higher rates of air pollution.

  The research carries significant limitations. It does not fully account for confounding variables, including the tendency to site data centers near dense urban areas where vehicle traffic and other industrial activity already elevate pollution levels. Available studies also do not consistently distinguish between facilities relying solely on grid power and those running on-site generators; the latter would have a clearer and more direct effect on local air quality.

  The evidence supports a narrower conclusion than a straightforward causal link: residents near data centers often see higher pollution levels, but existing research is not conclusive that data centers are the direct or dominant pollution source in every case.

  At the grid scale, the picture is less ambiguous. Ever-growing electricity demand from data centers almost certainly contributes to higher aggregate emissions in markets where power grids remain predominantly fossil-fueled.

  ## Why a site's impact can shift by the hour

  A facility's indirect emissions profile is not fixed. It can shift substantially within hours depending on which generation sources are supplying the grid at any given moment.

  On solar-heavy grids during daylight periods, a data center may draw power primarily from solar generation. The same facility at night may rely more heavily on fossil-fueled plants. Facilities with on-site generation face a parallel dynamic: peaks in IT workload can increase generator runtime and therefore emissions when those generators are online. Assigning a fixed air-quality impact to any single site requires detailed, time-resolved data on both grid conditions and facility operations.

  ## Operator levers for reducing local pollution

  Operators have four primary levers for reducing air-quality impact. First, power sourcing: siting facilities on grids with higher renewable penetration, entering long-term renewable energy agreements, and matching consumption with clean energy on an hourly basis where feasible. Second, minimizing on-site fossil generation: limiting diesel or gas generators to genuine emergencies and essential testing while evaluating cleaner backup alternatives. Third, efficiency: reducing total energy consumption through efficient IT hardware, advanced cooling methods including economization where climate conditions permit, and workload optimization. Fourth, demand flexibility: using demand response programs, energy storage, and workload scheduling to shift consumption toward cleaner grid hours and reduce reliance on high-emission generation periods.

  The air-quality footprint of a data center is not an inherent property of the facility type but a function of operating choices, primarily how power is sourced and how on-site generation is deployed. Facilities connected to cleaner grids or with strong renewable procurement carry lower indirect emissions than those dependent on on-site fossil-fuel generation or grids with limited renewable capacity. Operators that match consumption with clean energy on an hourly basis achieve the lowest indirect emissions profiles available under current grid conditions.
body_zh: |
  ## 資料中心空氣品質影響取決於電力來源，而非伺服器運作本身

  資料中心在正常運作條件下，幾乎不會直接產生空氣污染，但其對當地及區域空氣品質的影響，幾乎完全取決於電力的供應來源，以及場域內化石燃料發電機的使用程度。

  隨著資料中心的用電量持續攀升，監管機關與周邊社區也愈來愈關注大型設施的選址與用電方式，這項區別因此顯得格外重要。

  ## 資料中心的現場排放

  資料中心本質上是一棟容納伺服器及配套電力與冷卻基礎設施的建築物。在正常運作條件下，它不會燃燒燃料，也不會產生廢氣。現場最常見的排放物是蒸發冷卻系統所產生的水蒸氣。

  由於伺服器會產生大量熱能，多數現代設施無需在場域內設置暖氣爐或鍋爐來供暖，因此少了許多商業與工業建築常見的燃燒污染源。資料中心正式啟用後，產生的車輛交通量也相對有限——員工人數少、建置完成後的物流配送也不多，不過施工階段仍可能造成較密集的排放。

  ## 電力來源的關鍵變數

  更具決定性影響的因素，在於電力的供應方式。資料中心大規模消耗電力，而這股需求所帶來的空氣品質後果，直接反映在電網背後的發電來源上。

  幾項因素決定了間接污染的規模與分布：

  - **電力採購方式。** 若設施大量使用再生能源，相關的排放量便會較低，因為再生能源在發電過程中不會產生空氣污染物。
  - **電網供電與自備發電。** 多數資料中心從電網取電，而大型集中式發電廠通常能達到更高的燃料效率，並可能配備小型發電機所缺乏的排放控制設備。無論是常態性或作為電網補充而使用自備發電的設施，往往會產生更多在地化的排放。
  - **主力發電與備用發電。** 長期或頻繁作為主力電源運轉的發電機，所產生的排放量遠高於僅用於定期測試與真正緊急狀況的備用機組。
  - **燃料種類。** 天然氣的污染物排放量低於柴油或其他高碳燃料。長時間仰賴柴油的設施，會在當地產生更多空氣污染。

  ## 美國環保署資料的發現與侷限

  美國環保署（EPA）的分析發現，資料中心鄰近地區與較高污染濃度之間存在相關性，顯示住在資料中心附近的居民可能暴露於較高的空氣污染風險中。

  然而，這項研究存在明顯的侷限性。它未能充分排除混淆變數，包括資料中心傾向選址於交通繁忙的都會周邊地區，而這些地區本身的車輛廢氣與工業活動已使污染濃度偏高。現有研究也未能一致區分純粹仰賴電網供電的設施與使用自備發電機的設施；後者對當地空氣品質會有更直接、更明確的影響。

  現有證據所能支持的結論，比直接的因果關係更為有限：資料中心附近的居民確實常見較高的污染濃度，但現有研究尚無法確認資料中心在每個案例中都是直接或主要的污染來源。

  從電網整體規模來看，情況則較為明確。資料中心對電力的需求持續增長，在電網仍以化石燃料為主的市場中，幾乎可以確定這將推升整體排放量。

  ## 為何同一設施的影響每小時都可能不同

  設施的間接排放情況並非固定不變，可能在數小時內因電網當下的發電來源而大幅波動。

  在日照充足、太陽能比例較高的電網中，白天時段資料中心可能主要使用太陽能發電；同一設施到了夜間，則可能更依賴化石燃料電廠。使用自備發電的設施也面臨類似的動態：IT 工作負載的高峰可能增加發電機的運轉時間，在發電機上線期間相應增加排放量。要為任何單一設施指定固定的空氣品質影響，需要針對電網狀況與設施運作，取得詳細且具時間解析度的資料。

  ## 業者可採取的減污措施

  業者有四項主要手段可降低對空氣品質的衝擊。

  第一，**電力採購**：選址於再生能源滲透率較高的電網、簽訂長期再生能源購電協議，並在可行的情況下，以逐小時配比的方式確保用電來自潔淨能源。

  第二，**減少現場化石燃料發電**：將柴油或天然氣發電機的使用限制在真正的緊急狀況與必要測試，並評估更潔淨的備用能源替代方案。

  第三，**提升能源效率**：透過採用高效率的 IT 硬體、先進冷卻技術（包括在氣候條件允許時導入自然冷卻），以及工作負載優化，全面降低總用電量。

  第四，**需求彈性管理**：善用需求回應計畫、儲能系統與工作負載排程，將用電轉移至電網較潔淨的時段，並降低對高排放發電期間的依賴。

  資料中心的空氣品質足跡，並非設施類型的固有特性，而是營運選擇的結果——核心在於如何取得電力，以及如何部署現場發電設備。連接至較潔淨電網、或積極採購再生能源的設施，其間接排放量遠低於依賴現場化石燃料發電、或電網再生能源容量有限的設施。以逐小時配比方式確保用電來自潔淨能源的業者，在現有電網條件下能達到最低的間接排放水準。
