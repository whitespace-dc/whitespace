body_en: |
  Singapore said on Monday it will operate a second liquefied natural gas (LNG) terminal and broaden the geographic spread of its gas imports, as the city-state moves to reduce exposure to supply disruptions from neighboring countries including Indonesia.

  Energy, Trade and Industry Minister Tan See Leng made the announcement at the opening of Singapore International Energy Week (SIEW) 2026 on Oct. 26, describing the measures as part of a wider strategy to manage supply uncertainty as domestic energy demand grows.

  ## Second LNG terminal and import diversification

  Singapore currently operates one LNG terminal on Jurong Island. Tan said the country is expanding its LNG infrastructure with a second terminal designed to increase the capacity to receive gas from international markets. LNG is natural gas that has been cooled to liquid form for shipping by sea, allowing buyers to source from suppliers not connected by pipeline.

  Singapore already imports LNG from Australia, Africa, the Middle East, and North America, in addition to pipeline gas from Indonesia and Malaysia. Tan said the country would not rely solely on piped gas from neighboring states, and that diversifying supply sources is a central element of the strategy to reduce dependence on any single supplier region.

  Indonesia's growing domestic energy demand is among the factors Singapore must account for in its forward gas supply planning, Tan said.

  ## Infrastructure timelines versus demand growth

  Tan identified a structural tension in energy planning: the time required to build power infrastructure is measured in years to decades, while demand from emerging economic sectors can rise faster.

  He said construction of a combined-cycle gas power plant — a facility that generates electricity first through combustion gas turbines and then by recovering waste heat — typically takes four to six years. Laying transmission cables and building substations requires five to seven years. Constructing underground cable tunnels can take up to a decade.

  Data centers, by contrast, can be completed in a shorter timeframe, even though they require large volumes of electricity, Tan said. That gap makes energy demand forecasting and infrastructure timing a persistent challenge for planners.

  ## ASEAN grid connectivity

  To help address the mismatch between infrastructure lead times and accelerating demand, Singapore is promoting the development of regional energy connectivity through an ASEAN power grid. Tan said such a network would allow countries in Southeast Asia to share electricity supply and connect renewable energy generation sites with demand centers.

  Singapore's expanded LNG terminal capacity and its push for ASEAN grid integration together represent a two-track approach: securing flexible supply in the near term through diversified LNG imports, while working toward longer-term regional infrastructure that can balance generation and load across borders.
body_zh: |
  新加坡週一宣布，將啟用第二座液化天然氣（LNG）接收站，並進一步擴大天然氣進口的地理來源，以降低對印尼等鄰國供氣中斷的曝險。

  能源、貿易及工業部長陳詩龍於10月26日出席2026年新加坡國際能源週（SIEW）開幕式時宣布上述措施，並將其定位為因應國內能源需求持續成長、管理供應不確定性之整體戰略的一環。

  ## 第二座LNG接收站與進口多元化

  新加坡目前在裕廊島設有一座液化天然氣接收站。陳詩龍表示，新加坡將擴建液化天然氣基礎設施，興建第二座接收站，以提升自國際市場接收天然氣的容量。液化天然氣是將天然氣冷卻至液態後以船舶運輸的形式，使買方得以向未與管線相連的供應商採購。

  新加坡目前除透過管線從印尼與馬來西亞進口天然氣外，亦向澳洲、非洲、中東及北美採購液化天然氣。陳詩龍表示，新加坡不會單純仰賴鄰國的管線天然氣，多元化供氣來源是降低對單一供應地區依賴的核心策略。

  他指出，印尼國內能源需求持續升溫，是新加坡在進行未來天然氣供應規劃時必須納入考量的因素之一。

  ## 基礎設施建設時程與需求成長的落差

  陳詩龍點出能源規劃中存在的結構性矛盾：建設電力基礎設施所需時間以年乃至數十年計，而新興經濟部門的需求成長速度卻可能遠超於此。

  他說明，興建一座複循環燃氣發電廠——亦即先以燃燒氣渦輪機發電、再回收廢熱繼續產電的設施——通常需要四至六年；鋪設輸電纜線與興建變電站需要五至七年；建設地下電纜隧道則可能長達十年。

  相較之下，資料中心的建設時程較短，所需電量卻十分龐大。陳詩龍指出，這樣的落差使能源需求預測與基礎設施建設時程的掌握，成為規劃者長期面臨的挑戰。

  ## 東協電網互聯互通

  為協助縮短基礎設施建設前置時間與加速增長之需求之間的差距，新加坡積極倡議透過東協電網推動區域能源互聯互通。陳詩龍表示，此一網絡將使東南亞各國得以共享電力供應，並連結再生能源發電地點與用電需求中心。

  新加坡擴充液化天然氣接收站容量、同步推動東協電網整合，兩者合構成雙軌並進的策略：短期透過多元化液化天然氣進口確保靈活供氣；長期則致力於打造可跨境調度電力的區域基礎設施。
