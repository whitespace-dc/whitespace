body_en: |
  PT Pertamina Geothermal Energy Tbk. (PGEO), Indonesia's state-controlled geothermal producer, has secured US$477.87 million — approximately Rp8.6 trillion at Rp18,000 per U.S. dollar — in committed global financing for three development projects aimed at lifting the company's total installed generation capacity to 1.85 gigawatts (GW) by 2033, the company announced at a Jakarta media briefing on August 4, 2026.

  The funding comes from two multilateral lenders and is split across three discrete project tranches. Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), the Japanese government's overseas development finance arm, has committed US$158.86 million for the Lumut Balai 3 project and a separate US$148.97 million for Lumut Balai 4. The World Bank has committed US$170.04 million for the Lahendong 7-8 project, which covers two planned generating units at that field.

  ## Funding breakdown by project

  - Lumut Balai 3: US$158.86 million (JICA)
  - Lumut Balai 4: US$148.97 million (JICA)
  - Lahendong 7-8: US$170.04 million (World Bank)
  - **Total committed: US$477.87 million**

  Edwil Suzandi, PGEO's Director of Exploration and Development, said at the briefing that additional syndicated lenders are also standing by. "There are many more syndication funders today on standby waiting for PGE," Suzandi said, according to Bisnis.com. "So our challenge is how we make the projects commercial so that we can enter the execution phase."

  His remarks signal that the constraint on PGEO's expansion is not access to capital but rather progressing individual projects to a stage where financing can be drawn down — a common bottleneck in geothermal development, where resource confirmation and permitting must precede construction.

  ## First-half 2026 financial position

  PGEO disclosed its first-half 2026 results at the same briefing. Total assets stood at US$2.97 billion at the end of June, down 2.23% year to date. Total liabilities fell 2.80% year to date to US$961.18 million, composed of US$198.35 million in short-term liabilities and US$762.83 million in long-term liabilities — the latter representing 79.4% of the total liability stack. Both categories declined from end-2025 levels, by 7.44% and 1.52% respectively.

  Total equity came to US$2.0 billion, down 1.95% year to date from US$2.04 billion at the end of 2025. The company's debt-to-equity ratio (the ratio of total liabilities to equity) was 0.48 times, indicating liabilities at less than half of equity. Return on assets — a measure of how efficiently the company generates earnings from its asset base — improved to 2.68% in the first half of 2026 from 2.27% in the prior comparable period.

  Suzandi said the company expects its track record to support competitive financing terms going forward. "We are very optimistic that PGE, based on experience, can obtain financing at more competitive pricing. So we can guarantee that we will be able to obtain that," he said, as quoted by Bisnis.com.

  ## What the financing means for capacity targets

  Geothermal energy produces electricity by tapping underground heat reservoirs and, unlike solar or wind, generates power around the clock regardless of weather. One gigawatt of installed capacity is roughly equivalent to the output of a large conventional power station and is sufficient to supply electricity to millions of homes, depending on the grid.

  PGEO's target of 1.85 GW by 2033 represents a multi-project buildout across at least two existing geothermal fields — Lumut Balai in South Sumatra and Lahendong in North Sulawesi. The secured commitments from JICA and the World Bank provide the debt financing foundation for those expansions, though Suzandi's comments indicate that converting committed funding into active construction depends on completing commercialization steps for each project.

  The company's broader pipeline of standby syndicated financing, which Suzandi referenced without specifying amounts or lenders, suggests the project execution timeline, rather than funding availability, will determine how quickly PGEO moves toward its 2033 capacity goal.
body_zh: |
  印尼國營地熱能源業者PT Pertamina Geothermal Energy Tbk.（PGEO）宣布，已獲得共計4億7,787萬美元——按每美元兌1萬8,000印尼盾計算，約合盧比8.6兆元——的全球融資承諾，資金將分配至三項開發專案，目標是在2033年前將公司總裝置發電容量提升至1.85吉瓦（GW）。上述消息由PGEO於2026年8月4日在雅加達舉行的媒體說明會上正式對外宣布。

  此輪融資來自兩家多邊貸款機構，分三個獨立專案批次撥付。日本國際協力機構（JICA）——日本政府的對外開發融資部門——已承諾為Lumut Balai 3專案提供1億5,886萬美元，並另行承諾為Lumut Balai 4專案提供1億4,897萬美元。世界銀行則承諾為Lahendong 7-8專案提供1億7,004萬美元，涵蓋該地熱田兩座計畫中的發電機組。

  ## 資金明細（按專案）

  - Lumut Balai 3：1億5,886萬美元（JICA）
  - Lumut Balai 4：1億4,897萬美元（JICA）
  - Lahendong 7-8：1億7,004萬美元（世界銀行）
  - **承諾總額：4億7,787萬美元**

  PGEO勘探與開發董事Edwil Suzandi在說明會上表示，還有更多銀行聯貸方正在候場。「目前有許多聯貸資金提供者在等待PGE，」Suzandi表示，「因此我們的挑戰在於如何讓這些專案具備商業可行性，以便進入執行階段。」上述引述出自Bisnis.com的報導。

  他的發言顯示，制約PGEO擴張步伐的，並非取得資本的管道，而是如何推動各別專案達到可動用融資的階段——這是地熱開發領域常見的瓶頸：資源確認與許可程序必須在施工動工前完成。

  ## 2026年上半年財務狀況

  PGEO同步在說明會上揭露了2026年上半年的財務成果。截至6月底，公司總資產為29億7,000萬美元，較年初下滑2.23%。總負債則較年初減少2.80%，至9億6,118萬美元，其中短期負債1億9,835萬美元，長期負債7億6,283萬美元——後者占總負債的79.4%。兩類負債均較2025年底水準下降，分別減少7.44%及1.52%。

  股東總權益為20億美元，較2025年底的20億4,000萬美元下滑1.95%。公司負債權益比（總負債對股東權益之比率）為0.48倍，顯示負債規模不及股東權益的一半。資產報酬率——衡量公司從資產基礎創造獲利效率的指標——由上年同期的2.27%提升至2026年上半年的2.68%。

  Suzandi表示，公司預期過往的財務紀錄將有助於爭取更具競爭力的融資條件。「我們非常樂觀，PGE憑藉既有經驗，能夠以更具競爭力的定價取得融資，我們可以保證能夠做到，」他說，此話亦引自Bisnis.com的報導。

  ## 融資對裝置容量目標的意義

  地熱能源透過抽取地下熱源儲層來發電，與太陽能或風能不同，它不受天氣影響，可全天候穩定供電。1吉瓦的裝置容量，大致相當於一座大型傳統電廠的輸出功率，視電網條件而定，足以供應數以百萬計的家庭用電。

  PGEO設定的2033年1.85吉瓦目標，涉及橫跨至少兩座現有地熱田的多專案建設計畫——分別是位於南蘇門答臘的Lumut Balai，以及位於北蘇拉威西的Lahendong。JICA與世界銀行的承諾融資為上述擴建計畫奠定了債務融資基礎；不過，Suzandi的說法也指出，承諾融資能否轉化為實際施工，仍有賴於各專案完成商業化流程。

  Suzandi雖提及公司尚有更廣泛的銀行聯貸候補融資管道，但未說明具體金額或貸款方。這意味著，推動PGEO邁向2033年裝置容量目標的關鍵變數，將是各專案的執行時程，而非資金是否充裕。
