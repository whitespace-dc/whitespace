body_en: |
  During red alerts that triggered rotating brownouts across Luzon and the Visayas in May, more than a dozen coal-fired generating units sat on forced outage, with several dark for over a year and one offline since 2019, according to Philippine grid operator advisories. Industry data and analysis point to deferred maintenance on a fleet that carries close to three-fifths of the country's electricity supply as the primary driver.

  Coal's share of Philippine generation rose from roughly one-tenth in the 1990s to around 60 percent today, according to data cited by Rappler and the International Centre for Sustainable Carbon. The grid was rebuilt with the expectation that these plants would provide steady baseload. Most of the units on forced outage during the May alerts were coal-fired, and with retirement timelines for new and existing capacity stretching toward 2060, the fleet remains the dominant generation source through the country's energy transition.

  ## Why units keep tripping

  The outage problem is not confined to ageing equipment. Some of the units with the worst forced-outage records have operated for less than a decade, according to data compiled by the Center for Energy, Ecology, and Development (CEED), a Philippine energy research organization — pointing to maintenance and asset strategy as a significant factor alongside plant age.

  One structural driver is cycling. Coal generators are engineered for steady output over long periods, but the grid now requires them to ramp up and down throughout the day to balance variable solar and wind power being fed into the system. The Institute for Climate and Sustainable Cities has linked this cycling pattern to faster mechanical wear and a rising rate of unplanned outages.

  Higher ambient temperatures add further stress to under-maintained units. Heat cuts cooling margins and drives stator, rotor, and bearing temperatures upward; insulation life roughly halves for every 10°C rise in operating temperature. On the steam-turbine side, thermal expansion narrows clearances between moving parts, raising the risk of rubbing damage to thrust collars and shafts.

  Mid-life overhauls — standard practice in many power markets around a plant's 20th year of operation — have been applied thinly across much of the Philippine fleet, according to a Manila Bulletin report from May 2026. Through the early 2020s, the fleet's forced-outage rate averaged close to 9 percent, above expected levels, according to data from TransitionZero, an energy transition analytics organization.

  ## The case for maintenance

  Writing in BusinessWorld, Joko Sutopo, head of Services for Southeast Asia at Sulzer — a Swiss industrial engineering and manufacturing company with a local sales and service presence in Metro Manila — argues that proactive maintenance of the existing fleet offers the fastest and most cost-effective path to recovering lost grid capacity. Sulzer provides rotating equipment services and engineering support for power generation, water, and industrial sectors in the Philippines.

  The conventional pattern across the fleet, Sutopo writes, is to run a unit until a failure occurs and then compete for a repair slot, allowing faults to grow and extending outage durations. A condition-based, predictive maintenance program instead continuously monitors vibration, temperature differentials, and signs of cracking in steam turbine components, allowing operators to act before a minor fault forces a full outage. Inspections focused on the parts most prone to failure — blade roots and seal areas — can catch problems while the needed fix is still small and the unit is still running.

  Sulzer's recommended schedule calls for visual checks every 8,000 to 12,000 operating hours, a minor inspection at around 25,000 hours, and a major overhaul at roughly 50,000 hours, with the interval resetting after each shutdown.

  ## Repair cases

  Sulzer provided two cases illustrating the capacity recoverable without full equipment replacement.

  At a 77.5-megawatt geothermal plant in the Philippines, a rotor more than 40 years old had repeatedly failed after earlier repairs applied cold metal transfer welding on high-stress areas, leaving weaker joints that accelerated cracking. Sulzer traced the root cause and rebuilt the rotor using submerged arc welding with 12-percent chromium steel, a material chosen for its resistance to erosion, corrosion, and stress corrosion cracking. The rotor returned to service ahead of schedule, and Sulzer estimated the repair prevented $2.87 million in further production losses. The operator subsequently applied the same method to two additional rotors.

  At a 1,200-megawatt thermal power plant in Vietnam, a lubrication failure destroyed most of the steam-path components in a turbine-generator. The original equipment manufacturer recommended replacing all four rotors. Sulzer instead weld-repaired the unit and reverse-engineered thousands of replacement parts, returning the plant to service at 45 percent below the cost of full replacement.

  Sutopo notes that this type of repair capability is now available domestically in the Philippines. Independent service providers can deploy portable machining lathes, balancing machines, and welding kits on-site, reducing the lead times that have historically extended outage periods when components must be sourced from abroad.

  ## Fleet implications

  The units that tripped during the May red alerts will remain part of the grid's generation base regardless of what new capacity comes online; retirement timelines for the coal fleet run toward 2060. With renewable capacity still being built, the existing fleet carries the grid's reliability through the transition, Sutopo argues, and the megawatts recoverable through systematic maintenance and targeted repair represent real additional supply — though the article does not quantify a total figure for the Philippine fleet as a whole.

  The Department of Energy has conducted a review of plant performance, according to the Manila Bulletin, though the article does not indicate whether any mandatory maintenance standards or inspection timelines for the coal fleet have been established as a result.

  五月間呂宋島與米沙鄢地區爆發紅色警戒、各地輪流停電期間，逾十二座燃煤發電機組陷入強制停機狀態，其中數座停機時間已逾一年，一座更自2019年起便未再發電，菲律賓電網業者公告及業界分析數據均有所記載。業界數據與分析報告指出，承擔全國近五分之三電力供應的燃煤機隊長期延宕維護，是此次事件的主因。

  根據Rappler與「國際永續碳中心」（International Centre for Sustainable Carbon）所引用的數據，燃煤發電占菲律賓電力結構的比重，已從1990年代的約一成攀升至如今的約六成。電網的重建規劃本以這些電廠穩定提供基載電力為前提。五月警戒期間，強制停機機組以燃煤機組為主；而新建與現有機組的除役時程均延伸至2060年前後，意味著這支機隊仍將在能源轉型過程中持續扮演主力角色。

  ## 機組頻繁跳脫的根本原因

  停機問題並不侷限於老舊設備。菲律賓能源研究機構「能源生態暨發展中心」（Center for Energy, Ecology, and Development，CEED）所彙整的數據顯示，強制停機紀錄最差的部分機組，運轉年限不過十年，顯示維護策略與資產管理，和機組本身老化同樣都是不容忽視的因素。

  其中一項結構性因素在於「轉換式運轉」（cycling）。燃煤機組的設計初衷是長時間穩定輸出，但現行電網卻要求機組全天反覆升降載，以平衡太陽能與風力持續饋入系統所帶來的波動。「氣候與永續城市研究所」（Institute for Climate and Sustainable Cities）已將此種運轉模式，與機械磨耗加速及非計畫停機率上升連結在一起。

  環境溫度升高，更進一步加重維護不足機組的負擔。高溫壓縮冷卻裕度，導致定子、轉子與軸承溫度攀升；運轉溫度每上升攝氏10度，絕緣材料的使用壽命便縮短約一半。就蒸汽輪機側而言，熱膨脹縮小了動件之間的間隙，推力軸環與主軸遭受磨擦損傷的風險因此升高。

  根據《馬尼拉公報》（Manila Bulletin）2026年5月的報導，業界通行的中期大修——通常在機組運轉屆滿20年前後執行——在菲律賓機隊中的普及程度仍相當有限。能源轉型分析機構「TransitionZero」的數據顯示，整個2020年代初期，菲律賓燃煤機隊的強制停機率平均接近9%，高於正常預期水準。

  ## 維護的必要性

  蘇爾壽（Sulzer）是一家在馬尼拉大都會設有本地銷售及服務據點的瑞士工業工程與製造公司，業務涵蓋電力發電、水務及工業部門的旋轉設備服務與工程支援。該公司東南亞服務部門主管Joko Sutopo在《商業世界》（BusinessWorld）撰文指出，針對現有機隊進行積極性維護，是以最快速度、最具成本效益的方式補回失去的電網容量的選擇。

  Sutopo在文中指出，菲律賓機隊長期以來的慣常模式，是讓機組一路運轉至故障發生，才搶著排隊等待維修，不僅讓故障持續惡化，更拉長了停機時間。相對地，狀態導向（condition-based）的預測性維護方案，可持續監測振動量、溫差，以及蒸汽輪機組件的裂縫跡象，讓業者得以在小問題演變成全面停機之前及時處置。聚焦於最易損部位——葉片根部與密封區域——的定期檢測，往往能在修復工作仍相對輕微、機組尚未被迫停機時便揪出問題所在。

  蘇爾壽建議的維護時程為：每運轉8,000至12,000小時進行目視檢查，約25,000小時進行小修，約50,000小時進行大修，每次停機後重新起算間隔時數。

  ## 實際修復案例

  蘇爾壽提供了兩個案例，說明在不全面汰換設備的前提下可回收的容量規模。

  菲律賓一座77.5百萬瓦地熱電廠，有一支機齡逾40年的轉子，因前次維修於高應力區段採用冷金屬過渡焊接（cold metal transfer welding），留下強度較弱的焊接接點，導致裂縫加速擴展而反覆故障。蘇爾壽追溯根本原因，改以埋弧焊接（submerged arc welding）工法搭配12%鉻鋼重新製作轉子；該材料因兼具抗侵蝕、抗腐蝕及抗應力腐蝕裂縫的特性而獲選用。轉子提前完成修復並重新投入運轉，蘇爾壽估計此次修復避免了287萬美元的進一步生產損失。業主隨後將相同工法應用於另外兩支轉子。

  在越南一座1,200百萬瓦的火力發電廠，潤滑系統失效導致汽輪發電機蒸汽通路內的組件幾乎全毀。原廠建議更換全部四支轉子；蘇爾壽則改採焊接修復方式，並對數千個替換零件進行逆向工程，最終讓電廠以低於全面汰換成本45%的費用恢復運轉。

  Sutopo指出，這類修復能力目前已可在菲律賓本地取得。獨立服務廠商可攜帶可移式車床、動平衡機與焊接設備直接進場施工，從而縮短過去因零件須從海外採購而歷史性拉長停機周期的等待時間。

  ## 機隊整體影響

  五月紅色警戒期間跳脫的機組，無論新增容量如何成長，仍將繼續留在電網的發電結構中；燃煤機隊的除役時程延伸至2060年前後。在再生能源容量仍持續建置之際，現有機隊承擔著能源轉型期間的電網可靠度；Sutopo認為，透過系統性維護與針對性修復所能回收的百萬瓦數，代表真實可用的額外供電容量——儘管文中並未就菲律賓整體機隊的可回收規模給出量化數字。

  根據《馬尼拉公報》報導，能源部已就電廠績效展開審查，但文中未透露此次審查是否已催生針對燃煤機隊的強制性維護標準或檢測時程規定。
