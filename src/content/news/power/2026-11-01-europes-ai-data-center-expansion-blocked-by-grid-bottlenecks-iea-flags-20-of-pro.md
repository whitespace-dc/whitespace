  ## Grid constraints threaten Europe's AI infrastructure buildout

  Europe's rapid expansion of data centers to support artificial intelligence workloads is running into a hard infrastructure barrier: the continent's power grids cannot reliably deliver electricity where it is needed, even when surplus renewable generation exists. The International Energy Agency estimates that around 20 percent of planned data center projects across Europe are at risk of delays due to electricity supply bottlenecks, according to analysis published by Data Center Dynamics.

  All five of Europe's major data center hubs — Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris, and Dublin — are hitting capacity limits, the analysis states. The constraint is not a shortage of power generation. At peak times, renewables produce more electricity than Europe can consume, and at historically low cost. The bottleneck is the transmission and distribution infrastructure required to move that power to load centers.

  ## Solar curtailment signals a systemic pattern

  The dynamic is not new. European solar generation capacity expanded faster than grid infrastructure, storage, and local demand could absorb, producing a familiar outcome: negative prices during peak solar hours and rising curtailment, in which generators are paid to switch off because the grid has no room for their output. In Spain, approximately 16 percent of solar generation was curtailed in the first quarter of 2026, double the rate from a year earlier, and investors began selling assets at fractions of their original valuations, according to the analysis. Solar deployment is now slowing across Europe, the piece states, not because clean power demand has fallen but because projects were built without adequate coordination with surrounding systems.

  The data center buildout is following the same pattern, the analysis argues, but at greater speed and higher stakes. Generation capacity, demand growth, and grid investment have each proceeded on separate tracks, creating a system in which adding new large loads to the grid compounds stress rather than balancing it.

  ## Operators turn to gas and nuclear

  With grid-connected clean power unavailable at the scale and reliability operators require, data center developers are pursuing alternatives. In the United Kingdom, more than 100 data centers have submitted requests for gas connections, in some cases as primary supply rather than backup, according to The Guardian, cited in the analysis.

  North American technology companies are moving further toward nuclear. Microsoft has structured a 20-year agreement to restart a nuclear facility in the United States. Amazon acquired a nuclear-adjacent campus in West Texas to secure dedicated power supply. Google has committed to small modular reactors — factory-built nuclear units smaller than conventional plants — in Tennessee, with first power targeted around 2030. Those moves, the analysis notes, illustrate the direction large operators take when grid infrastructure cannot deliver clean power reliably. Nuclear produces very low greenhouse gas emissions but carries high upfront costs, long development timelines, and unresolved public acceptance issues, the piece states.

  ## The energy hub model

  One structural response gaining traction is the energy hub: co-locating solar generation, battery storage, and data center load at a single grid connection point. Rather than multiple large, uncoordinated connections to an already strained grid, the model bundles and balances power assets locally before presenting a single, manageable load profile to the network operator.

  A study by Duke University found that grids could absorb up to 100 gigawatts — roughly the output of 100 large power stations — of new load without infrastructure upgrades, provided large users are willing to reduce consumption for less than one percent of the time. The energy hub model is designed to enable exactly that kind of demand flexibility.

  The concept has been demonstrated in Ireland, where a data center developer, an energy company, and the national grid operator EirGrid jointly built a shared substation serving both a data center and an offshore wind farm. By planning and funding the infrastructure together rather than sequentially, both projects connected to the grid two years faster than they would have otherwise, according to the analysis.

  ## Investment is already moving toward integrated models

  Large technology companies are beginning to integrate power development directly into their capital programs. Alphabet, Google's parent, acquired energy developer Intersect Power in 2025 for $4.75 billion specifically to develop generation alongside new data center capacity. Meta, Amazon, Google, and Microsoft together accounted for roughly half of the 56 gigawatts of clean power purchase agreements — long-term contracts to buy renewable electricity — signed globally in 2025.

  ## What comes next

  The analysis concludes that the near-term risk for Europe is a bifurcation: operators who cannot secure grid-connected clean power will lock in gas or nuclear supply, potentially for decades, undermining both climate commitments and the continent's ability to attract AI investment. The longer-term risk is competitiveness: as investment gravitates toward reliable infrastructure, so will leading AI companies and the talent they employ.

  The central coordination problem — generation, storage, and demand planned separately and connected to grids one project at a time — is identified as the priority to address. The Ireland substation case is cited as evidence that co-planned infrastructure can accelerate connection timelines materially, with the analysis pointing to that model as the basis for scaling grid capacity without waiting for large-scale transmission upgrades.

  ## 電網限制威脅歐洲AI基礎設施建設

  歐洲為支撐人工智慧工作負載而快速擴建資料中心，卻撞上一道實體基礎設施的高牆：即便再生能源出現供給過剩，歐洲大陸的電力網路仍無法穩定地將電力輸送至需求所在。據《Data Center Dynamics》援引國際能源總署（IEA）的分析，歐洲約兩成規劃中的資料中心計畫，正面臨因電力供應瓶頸導致延誤的風險。

  歐洲五大資料中心樞紐——法蘭克福、倫敦、阿姆斯特丹、巴黎、都柏林——全數逼近容量上限。然而問題根源並非發電量不足：尖峰時段，再生能源的發電量已超過整個歐洲的消耗量，且成本處於歷史低點。瓶頸在於將這些電力輸送至用電中心所需的輸配電基礎設施。

  ## 太陽能棄電折射出系統性問題

  這種困境並非首次出現。歐洲太陽能發電容量的擴張速度，遠超電網基礎設施、儲能設備與當地需求所能吸納的速度，導致一個熟悉的結果：太陽能發電尖峰時段出現負電價，以及不斷攀升的棄電率——業者被強制停機，是因電網已無空間容納其發電量。據前述分析，西班牙2026年第一季約有16%的太陽能發電量遭到棄電，是一年前的兩倍，投資人也開始以原始估值的零頭拋售資產。文章指出，歐洲的太陽能部署目前正在放緩，原因並非清潔能源需求下滑，而是過去建置時未與周邊系統充分協調所致。

  該分析認為，資料中心的大規模建置正以更快的速度、更高的代價，重蹈同樣的覆轍。發電容量、需求成長與電網投資各行其道，形成一個惡性循環：在電網上新增大型負載，非但無助於平衡，反而加劇壓力。

  ## 業者轉向燃氣與核能

  由於電網連接的清潔能源在規模與穩定性上均無法滿足需求，資料中心開發商開始尋求替代方案。據分析援引《衛報》報導，在英國已有逾百座資料中心提交燃氣接線申請，部分案例中，燃氣甚至被定位為主要供電來源，而非備用電力。

  北美科技公司則進一步轉向核能。微軟簽署了一份長達20年的協議，以重啟美國一座核能設施。亞馬遜在德州西部收購了一處毗鄰核電廠的園區，以確保專屬電力供應。谷歌則承諾在田納西州部署小型模組化反應爐（SMR）——這種工廠預製的核能機組規模小於傳統核電廠——目標是在2030年前後首次發電。分析指出，這些動作清楚說明了當電網基礎設施無法穩定供應清潔能源時，大型業者所選擇的方向。核能的溫室氣體排放量極低，但前期成本高昂、開發時程漫長，公眾接受度問題亦尚未解決，文章如此指出。

  ## 能源樞紐模式

  一種正逐漸獲得關注的結構性應對方案，是「能源樞紐」：將太陽能發電、電池儲能與資料中心負載集中於同一個電網接入點。這一模式的核心思路，是避免在已承壓的電網上增設多個龐大且缺乏協調的連接點，改為在接入電網之前，先將電力資產在本地統一整合與調度，向電網業者呈現單一、可管理的負載輪廓。

  杜克大學一項研究發現，若大型用電戶願意在不到1%的時間內削減用電，電網可在無需進行基礎設施升級的前提下，額外承受高達100吉瓦（GW）的新增負載——相當於100座大型電廠的總輸出功率。能源樞紐模式正是為實現這種需求彈性而設計。

  這一概念已在愛爾蘭獲得實際驗證：一家資料中心開發商、一家能源公司，與愛爾蘭國家電網業者EirGrid，共同建造了一座同時服務資料中心與離岸風電場的共用變電所。據分析，透過聯合規劃與共同出資，而非各自循序推進，兩個計畫的電網接入時程均比原本縮短了兩年。

  ## 投資已向整合模式靠攏

  大型科技公司開始將電力開發直接納入資本支出計畫。谷歌母公司Alphabet於2025年以47.5億美元收購能源開發商Intersect Power，明確是為了在新建資料中心的同時同步開發發電資產。據分析，Meta、亞馬遜、谷歌與微軟四家公司，合計約占2025年全球簽署的56吉瓦清潔能源購電協議（長期買電合約）的半數。

  ## 後續展望

  分析的結論指出，歐洲近期面臨的風險是走向兩極分化：無法取得電網連接清潔能源的業者，將被迫鎖定燃氣或核能供應，時間跨度可能長達數十年，不僅有礙氣候承諾的實現，也將削弱歐洲大陸吸引AI投資的能力。長期風險則是競爭力的喪失：隨著投資流向基礎設施更可靠的地區，領先的AI公司及其所雇用的人才也將隨之遷移。

  分析將「發電、儲能與需求各自規劃、逐一接入電網」這一核心協調問題，列為亟需優先解決的課題。愛爾蘭變電所案例被引述為佐證，說明聯合規劃的基礎設施能夠大幅縮短接入時程。分析指出，在等待大規模輸電升級到位之前，這一模式是擴大電網容量的可行基礎。
