body_en: |
  Enhanced geothermal developer Fervo Energy faces transmission constraints across its 42-gigawatt western pipeline that could slow project rollout and limit revenues at its flagship Utah development, according to a Monday research note from Jefferies and a May 5 analysis by market intelligence platform MLQ.ai, both issued after the company's initial public offering last month.

  ## Transmission shortfall at Cape Station

  The most acute constraint sits at Cape Station, Fervo's 500-megawatt (MW) project in Utah. MLQ.ai said the company holds approximately 290 MW of interconnection and transmission rights for Cape Station Phase II, less than the 384 MW of combined contracted capacity it holds under expanded power purchase agreements (long-term electricity supply contracts, or PPAs) with Southern California Edison (SCE) and the Clean Power Alliance. "If additional transmission capacity cannot be secured, revenues will be reduced or contracts may be modified," MLQ.ai said. The platform also noted that Fervo "depends on third-party transmission infrastructure for all current revenue and contracted [power purchase agreements]."

  ## Pipeline exposure

  Jefferies equity analyst Julien Dumoulin-Smith said the problem extends across Fervo's full 42-GW pipeline of ten projects in Nevada, Utah, and Idaho. Most sites occupy rural areas with limited existing infrastructure, and the constraints "could slow [Fervo's] anticipated roll-out," he wrote. Management has "highlighted this as a risk factor for full 384MW," Dumoulin-Smith said.

  Management's proposed mitigation is to connect generation directly to customers behind the meter — a configuration that links a power source to an on-site customer rather than routing electricity onto the public transmission grid. Dumoulin-Smith said Fervo's management "appears more bullish than we initially considered" on that avenue. In May, Sarah Jewett, Fervo's senior vice president of strategy, told TechCrunch the company is "seeing an increasing amount of behind the meter commercial interest."

  ## Regulatory push

  Fervo has also pressed regulators for faster transmission development. In March, the company submitted comments on a California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) concept paper titled "Working Concepts in Transmission Financing and Ownership," arguing the CPUC has committed to procuring clean firm resources including geothermal but "needs to take urgent action to unlock delivery through new transmission."

  ## Cape Station schedule and the May blowout

  Cape Station is scheduled to begin commercial operations later this year, initially delivering 100 MW. A well blowout occurred at the site on May 25, first reported by Axios. Fervo said there were no injuries or environmental damage and that the incident would not affect the project's timeline.

  The blowout nonetheless drew significant investor attention. Dumoulin-Smith said it "highlighted the degree of [investor's] nerves," with client inquiries focused on project impact, timeline, and cost "despite limited details on what had actually happened." The volume of inbound questions, he wrote, "underscores how critical near-term execution on Phase I will be to shares."

  ## Investor outlook

  Overall investor feedback since the IPO has "skewed positive," according to Dumoulin-Smith. He described investors as "engaged and appear receptive to the story," with questions centering on how to build conviction around what he called "first-of-a-kind [enhanced geothermal]" technology. "Growth is compelling, but the next few months remain critical as investor scrutiny will be at its peak for both execution and potential setbacks ahead of Phase I [commercial operations date]," he wrote.

  Beyond its current ten-project pipeline, Fervo has identified additional prospective lease areas in Nevada, Utah, Idaho, California, New Mexico, and Washington.
body_zh: |
  增強型地熱開發商Fervo Energy在橫跨美國西部、總計42 GW的開發管線中面臨輸電限制，此情況可能拖慢專案推進速度，並壓縮其猶他州旗艦開發案的營收——根據傑富瑞（Jefferies）週一發布的研究報告，以及市場情報平台MLQ.ai於五月五日公布的分析，兩份報告均於該公司上月完成首次公開發行（IPO）後發布。

  ## Cape Station 輸電缺口

  最嚴峻的限制出現在Cape Station——Fervo位於猶他州的500 MW開發案。MLQ.ai指出，Fervo持有的Cape Station第二期併網與輸電權約為290 MW，低於其與南加州愛迪生電力（SCE）及清潔電力聯盟所簽訂的擴大購電協議（PPA，即長期電力供應合約）所涵蓋的合計384 MW合約容量。「若無法取得額外輸電容量，營收將遭到壓縮，或合約將須調整，」MLQ.ai表示。該平台並指出，Fervo「所有現有營收及合約購電協議，均仰賴第三方輸電基礎設施」。

  ## 開發管線的整體曝險

  傑富瑞股票分析師朱利安·杜穆蘭-史密斯（Julien Dumoulin-Smith）表示，問題擴及Fervo橫跨內華達州、猶他州與愛達荷州共十個專案、總計42 GW的完整開發管線。大多數選址位於現有基礎設施匱乏的偏遠地區，這些限制「可能拖慢〔Fervo〕預期的推進腳步」，他在報告中寫道。他並指出，管理層「已將此列為全384 MW的風險因素」。

  管理層提出的因應方案，是將發電直接連接至表後客戶——此一配置讓電源直接對接場址內的用電戶，而非透過公共輸電網路輸送。杜穆蘭-史密斯表示，Fervo管理層在這條路徑上「比我們最初預期的更為樂觀」。今年五月，Fervo策略資深副總裁莎拉·朱伊特（Sarah Jewett）向TechCrunch表示，公司「正看到愈來愈多表後商業需求的興趣」。

  ## 監管推動

  Fervo也積極向監管機關爭取加速推動輸電建設。今年三月，該公司就加州公用事業委員會（CPUC）一份題為「輸電融資與所有權工作概念」的概念文件提交意見書，主張CPUC已承諾採購包含地熱在內的清潔穩定資源，但「必須採取緊急行動，透過新建輸電線路打通輸送瓶頸」。

  ## Cape Station 運轉時程與五月井噴事件

  Cape Station預計於今年稍晚啟動商業運轉，初期輸出100 MW。五月二十五日，該場址發生一起井噴事故，最先由Axios報導。Fervo表示，事故未造成人員傷亡或環境損害，也不影響專案時程。

  儘管如此，這起井噴事件仍引發投資人高度關注。杜穆蘭-史密斯表示，此事「凸顯出投資人的緊張情緒」，客戶詢問集中在專案影響、時程及成本，「儘管關於實際發生情況的資訊十分有限」。他寫道，大量湧入的問詢「突顯出第一期〔商業運轉〕近期執行成效對於股價的關鍵性」。

  ## 投資人展望

  杜穆蘭-史密斯表示，自IPO以來整體投資人反應「偏向正面」。他形容投資人「參與積極，對這個故事似乎抱持接受的態度」，提問集中於如何對其所稱的「首創型〔增強地熱〕」技術建立投資信念。「成長前景引人入勝，但未來數月仍然關鍵，因為隨著第一期〔商業運轉日期〕臨近，投資人對執行面及潛在挫折的審視將達到高峰，」他寫道。

  除現有十個專案的開發管線之外，Fervo已在內華達州、猶他州、愛達荷州、加州、新墨西哥州及華盛頓州識別出更多潛在租賃區域。
