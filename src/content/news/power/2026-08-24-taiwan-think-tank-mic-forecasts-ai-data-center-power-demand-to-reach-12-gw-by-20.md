body_en: |
  Taiwan's Market Intelligence & Consulting Institute (MIC), a government think tank, projects that AI data center (AIDC) electricity demand on the island will reach between 1.1 and 1.2 gigawatts (GW) by 2035, up from roughly 303 megawatts (MW) today, MIC Deputy Director Lin Bo-qi said in an interview published Sunday. The forecasts are intended to give regulators a quantitative benchmark for balancing computing capacity against energy allocation, Lin said.

  MIC's projections place AIDC demand at 0.8 to 0.9 GW by 2030, rising to 1.1 to 1.2 GW by 2035.

  ## Scale relative to Taiwan's grid

  MIC said concerns about electricity shortfall should be weighed against the overall size of Taiwan's power system. As of 2025, the island's total installed generation capacity stands at approximately 70 GW. AIDC actual power consumption in 2025 is about 303 MW — roughly 0.4 percent of that total. Even at the 2035 upper-end projection of 1.2 GW, AI data centers would account for only 1 to 2 percent of Taiwan's installed capacity, MIC said.

  The more pressing concern, MIC said, is regional grid balance. Current policy follows a supply-demand matching principle to avoid excessive concentration of demand in northern Taiwan, which already carries high grid loads.

  Taiwan's state-owned utility Taipower has separately stated it has reserved approximately 2 to 3 GW of additional installed capacity for computing-power applications.

  ## How MIC built its forecast

  MIC said it derived its projections by combining International Energy Agency (IEA) global trends with benchmarks from Japan, South Korea, and Singapore, and by applying projections of AI adoption rates in Taiwan's manufacturing sector.

  According to Ministry of Economic Affairs planning, AI adoption in Taiwan's manufacturing sector is set to rise from 22.7 percent in 2024 to 74 percent by 2030, reaching a high-penetration scenario by 2035. MIC said that trajectory, applied to computing-power demand modeling, produces the 1.1 to 1.2 GW estimate for 2035.

  ## International policy comparisons

  MIC reviewed how peer countries manage data center power demand to inform its recommendations.

  South Korea has imposed strict screening on facilities in high-load zones such as Seoul, and uses tax incentives to steer developers toward regions with greater grid headroom. Japan has tightened after-the-fact audits to curb the practice of developers securing grid capacity they do not ultimately use. Singapore applies an overall capacity quota with competitive bidding, and enforces strict requirements on Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) — a ratio measuring how efficiently a facility converts incoming electricity into useful computing work, where a lower figure indicates less overhead loss. MIC recommended Taiwan progressively tighten its own PUE standards as technology matures.

  ## Recommendations for Taiwan's approval framework

  MIC proposed extending the scope of regulatory review beyond the current framework, which focuses primarily on grid headroom and is assessed by the Bureau of Energy and Taipower. Future reviews, MIC said, should incorporate industrial-benefit criteria: whether a data center's computing capacity substantively supports domestic industries and startup companies, whether it prioritizes locally manufactured servers and information and communications technology hardware to build a supply-chain cluster, and whether it delivers measurable economic benefit to local communities.

  For site selection, MIC identified six criteria regulators should apply: land availability, power supply, water availability, proximity to submarine cable landing points, community engagement, and natural disaster risk. The institute recommended prioritizing locations where power supply exceeds demand and where sites sit close to existing substations.

  MIC also suggested Taiwan study practices in the United States and Japan of requiring private AI data centers to make a portion of their computing capacity available to academic institutions, research bodies, and startup companies, to strengthen Taiwan's domestic AI ecosystem.
body_zh: |
  ## MIC預測台灣AI資料中心用電需求　2035年將達1.2GW

  台灣政府智庫資策會（MIC）預測，島內AI資料中心（AIDC）的用電需求將從目前約303百萬瓦（MW）成長至2035年的1.1至1.2十億瓦（GW）。資策會副主任林伯齊於週日發布的專訪中表示，此一預測旨在提供主管機關一套量化基準，以平衡算力部署與電力分配之間的取捨。

  依據資策會預測，AI資料中心用電需求將於2030年達到0.8至0.9GW，2035年進一步攀升至1.1至1.2GW。

  ## 相對於台灣電網的規模

  資策會指出，對於電力短缺的疑慮，應放在台灣整體電力系統的規模下審視。截至2025年，全島總裝置容量約為70GW，而AI資料中心今年的實際用電量約為303MW，僅占總裝置容量的約0.4%。即便以2035年上限預測值1.2GW計算，AI資料中心用電也不過占台灣總裝置容量的1至2%。

  資策會表示，更迫切的問題在於區域電網的供需平衡。現行政策依循供需對應原則，避免需求過度集中於電網負載已相當沉重的北部地區。

  台電方面則另行表示，已為算力應用預留約2至3GW的額外裝置容量。

  ## 預測模型的建構方式

  資策會說明，此次預測係結合國際能源總署（IEA）的全球趨勢、日本、南韓及新加坡的標竿數據，並套用台灣製造業AI導入率的預測值後得出。

  根據經濟部規劃，台灣製造業AI導入率預計從2024年的22.7%提升至2030年的74%，並於2035年達到高度滲透的情境。資策會表示，將此一成長軌跡代入算力需求模型，即可推算出2035年1.1至1.2GW的預測值。

  ## 國際政策比較

  資策會也梳理了同類國家管理資料中心用電需求的方式，作為政策建議的參考依據。

  南韓針對首爾等高負載地區的資料中心設施實施嚴格審查，並以租稅優惠引導業者進駐電網容量較為充裕的地區。日本則強化事後稽核機制，以遏制業者取得電網容量後未實際使用的情形。新加坡採行整體容量配額加競標制度，並對電源使用效率（PUE）訂有嚴格規範——PUE係衡量設施將輸入電力轉換為有效算力之效率的比值，數值愈低代表額外耗損愈小。資策會建議台灣隨技術發展逐步提高PUE標準。

  ## 對台灣審核框架的建議

  資策會建議擴大監管審查範疇，現行框架主要由能源局與台電就電網容量進行評估，未來應進一步納入產業效益指標：包括資料中心算力是否實質支援本土產業及新創企業、是否優先採購本地製造的伺服器與資通訊硬體以形成供應鏈聚落，以及是否為在地社區帶來可衡量的經濟效益。

  在選址方面，資策會提出六項主管機關應採用的評估指標：土地可用性、電力供應、水源供應、鄰近海底電纜登陸點的距離、社區溝通參與，以及天然災害風險。資策會建議優先考慮電力供給大於需求、且鄰近現有變電站的地點。

  此外，資策會也建議台灣參考美國及日本的做法，要求私人AI資料中心將部分算力開放供學術機構、研究單位及新創企業使用，以強化台灣本土AI生態系的發展能量。
