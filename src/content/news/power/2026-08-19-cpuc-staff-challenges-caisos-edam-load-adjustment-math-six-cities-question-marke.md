body_en: |
  California Public Utilities Commission staff and six Southern California municipal utilities have challenged the California Independent System Operator's methodology for calculating load adjustments in the Extended Day-Ahead Market, filing comments that question specific figures and the transparency of the underlying calculations.

  ## Disputed adjustments

  CPUC's Energy Division staff, in comments filed Aug. 12, said it could not determine why CAISO made a 592.50 megawatt load adjustment on June 11 in response to a deficiency of Imbalance Reserve Up (IRU) capacity. IRU refers to reserves kept on standby to cover unexpected shortfalls between electricity supply and demand.

  Staff noted that CAISO's operating procedure requires it to procure 100 percent of the IRU requirement at a 97.5 percent confidence level when the day-ahead load forecast exceeds 42,000 megawatts. The day-ahead load forecast for June 11 was 37,000 megawatts, making the trigger condition unclear, according to the filing.

  The Aug. 4 adjustment drew sharper scrutiny. CPUC staff said the 3,590 MW adjustment appeared "larger than one would expect." At the time, the system appeared "fully resourced" to meet its published requirement by covering 90 percent of potential outcomes, representing roughly 900 MW of procurement. Moving to the 97.5 percent threshold should have produced a more modest increase, staff wrote, but the actual adjustment was 3,590 MW on top of the existing requirement, bringing the total to approximately 4,400 MW, nearly five times the 90 percent figure.

  "It is difficult to understand how moving from the 90 percent uncertainty to the 97.5 percent uncertainty would increase the requirement nearly fivefold (i.e., from ~900 MW to 4,400, or ~900 MW + ~3600 MW)," CPUC staff wrote.

  ## What the data do not show

  Energy and regulatory analyst Michael Cade, commenting to Utility Dive, said the core issue is transparency rather than a confirmed error. "The strongest element of Energy Division's comments is that currently available public data don't allow stakeholders to reproduce the calculations behind some of CAISO's key results," he said. "That discrepancy alone doesn't show that the adjustments were wrong. CAISO uses a separate, higher uncertainty benchmark to calculate them."

  Cade added that "Energy Division's concern is that the published data don't show how that calculation produced adjustments of 592.5 MW and 3,590 MW."

  CPUC staff noted that CAISO indicated in a previous meeting that it had run a counterfactual comparing CAISO day-ahead market results with EDAM results, and asked that those findings be presented at the next Market Performance and Planning Forum meeting.

  ## Six Cities challenge cost-benefit accounting

  The California "Six Cities," comprising the municipal utilities of Anaheim, Azusa, Banning, Colton, Pasadena, and Riverside, filed separate comments on Aug. 13 asking CAISO to conduct an ongoing cost-benefit analysis of EDAM. The cities cited what they described as "extremely high resource commitments" made by CAISO and market participants against "negligible benefits" in the market's first two months.

  EDAM generated gross benefits of $11.38 million for May and June combined across the entire market. Of that amount, $5.96 million was attributed to the CAISO Balancing Authority Area (BAA), the geographic zone CAISO operates. However, the CAISO BAA transferred $2.67 million in congestion revenues to PacifiCorp, the market's only non-CAISO participant, representing roughly 45 percent of those attributed gross benefits.

  Congestion revenues are collected when electricity flows across constrained transmission paths and are distributed according to market rules.

  The Six Cities wrote that benefits received from participating in EDAM and the Western Energy Imbalance Market (WEIM), a real-time balancing market that preceded EDAM, "should be at least roughly proportional to responsibility for market costs."

  Cade said a similar transparency concern applies to the benefits estimate itself. "CAISO reports the total and its allocation among the three participating balancing areas, but not the underlying counterfactual results or a dollar breakdown by benefit component," he said.

  ## Congestion revenue allocation also questioned

  Powerex Corp., a marketer of wholesale electricity, submitted comments on Aug. 14 asking CAISO to report congestion revenues based on where market participants actually paid the cost. The company said it was "unclear how much of the $37.6 million in congestion revenues associated with constraints in the California ISO BAA were paid by market participants in PacifiCorp West and in PacifiCorp East," the two geographic sub-areas of PacifiCorp's service territory.

  ## What comes next

  EDAM, the first Western day-ahead market, launched in May. PacifiCorp was its inaugural non-CAISO participant and remains the only one. Portland General Electric is scheduled to join the market on Oct. 1.

  The stakeholder comments were submitted in response to CAISO's Market Performance and Planning Forum Q3 meeting, held July 30. The Q4 meeting, at which CPUC staff has asked CAISO to present its counterfactual analysis, is tentatively scheduled for Oct. 29.
body_zh: |
  加州公用事業委員會（CPUC）能源部門人員與南加州六家市立電力公司，就加州獨立系統運營商（CAISO）在延伸日前市場（EDAM）中的負載調整計算方式提出質疑，分別提交意見書，對特定數字提出異議，並要求CAISO公開更完整的計算依據。

  ## 爭議調整量

  CPUC能源部門人員於8月12日提交的意見書指出，他們無法釐清CAISO為何在6月11日因不平衡備用上調容量（IRU）不足，而做出592.5百萬瓦（MW）的負載調整。IRU是指為因應電力供需之間突發性短缺所保留的備用容量。

  意見書指出，依據CAISO的操作程序，當日前負載預測超過4萬2,000 MW時，CAISO須以97.5%的信賴水準採購100%的IRU需求量。然而，6月11日的日前負載預測僅為3萬7,000 MW，使得觸發條件並不明確。

  8月4日的調整則引發更嚴格的審視。CPUC人員指出，3,590 MW的調整量「遠超出合理預期」。當時，系統在覆蓋90%潛在情境的條件下，看似已「充分取得資源」以滿足已公告的需求量，對應採購量約為900 MW。意見書指出，提升至97.5%信賴水準，應只會帶來幅度較為有限的增量，但實際調整量卻在既有需求基礎上再加計3,590 MW，使總量達到約4,400 MW，幾乎是90%情境數字的五倍。

  「很難理解，從90%不確定性移至97.5%不確定性，需求量為何會增加近五倍（亦即從約900 MW增至4,400 MW，即約900 MW加上約3,600 MW）」，CPUC人員在意見書中寫道。

  ## 公開數據無從驗算

  能源與法規分析師麥可‧凱德（Michael Cade）在接受《Utility Dive》採訪時表示，問題的核心在於透明度，而非確認有誤。「能源部門意見書最有力的一點，是現有公開數據無法讓利害關係方重現CAISO若干關鍵結果背後的計算過程，」他說，「這項落差本身並不能證明調整量有誤。CAISO採用的是另一套較高不確定性基準來進行計算。」

  凱德補充道：「能源部門所關切的，是已公布的數據無法呈現，該計算過程如何得出592.5 MW與3,590 MW的調整量。」

  CPUC人員指出，CAISO曾在先前的會議中表示，已進行反事實比較分析，對照CAISO日前市場結果與EDAM結果，並要求CAISO在下次「市場績效與規劃論壇」會議上提報相關發現。

  ## 六城市質疑成本效益計算

  由阿納海姆、阿蘇薩、班寧、科爾頓、帕薩迪納與河濱六座城市市立電力公司組成的「加州六城市」，於8月13日提交獨立意見書，要求CAISO對EDAM進行持續性的成本效益分析。六城市指出，CAISO與市場參與者承擔了「極高的資源投入」，但EDAM上路首兩個月的效益卻「微乎其微」。

  EDAM在5月與6月合計為整個市場創造了1,138萬美元的毛效益。其中，596萬美元歸屬於CAISO電力平衡責任區（BAA）——即CAISO所營運的地理區域。然而，CAISO電力平衡責任區另將267萬美元的壅塞收益轉付給太平洋電力公司（PacifiCorp），後者目前是市場上唯一的非CAISO參與者，此金額約佔該區毛效益的45%。

  壅塞收益是指電力流過受限輸電路徑時所收取的費用，並依市場規則進行分配。

  六城市在意見書中指出，從EDAM與其前身西部能源不平衡市場（WEIM，一個早於EDAM上路的即時平衡市場）參與所獲得的效益，「應與承擔的市場成本責任大致成比例」。

  凱德表示，效益估算本身也存在類似的透明度疑慮。「CAISO公布了總額及其在三個參與電力平衡責任區之間的分配，但未揭露背後的反事實分析結果，也沒有提供依效益項目細分的金額明細，」他說。

  ## 壅塞收益分配亦受質疑

  批發電力業者Powerex公司於8月14日提交意見書，要求CAISO依市場參與者實際支付成本的地點來呈報壅塞收益。該公司表示，目前「不清楚加州ISO電力平衡責任區內輸電壅塞相關的3,760萬美元壅塞收益中，有多少是由太平洋電力公司西區與太平洋電力公司東區的市場參與者所支付」，而這兩個地理分區正是太平洋電力公司服務範圍的次級區域。

  ## 後續發展

  EDAM是西部地區首個日前市場，於5月正式上線。太平洋電力公司是首家加入的非CAISO參與者，目前也是唯一一家。波特蘭一般電力公司（Portland General Electric）預計將於10月1日加入市場。

  上述各方意見書均是針對CAISO於7月30日舉行的「市場績效與規劃論壇」第三季會議所提交。第四季會議暫定於10月29日召開，CPUC人員已要求CAISO屆時提報其反事實分析結果。
