body_en: |
  The North American Electric Reliability Corp. (NERC), the organization responsible for overseeing the reliability of the bulk power system in North America, found no areas at high risk of resource shortfalls heading into the 2026-2027 winter season, but identified parts of the Midwest, Southeast and New England where fuel supply and transmission constraints could strain grid operators during extreme cold, the agency said Nov. 18 in its annual Winter Reliability Assessment.

  On-peak capacity resources — the generation available during periods of peak electricity demand — grew by 26.2 gigawatts (GW) compared with the 2025-26 winter, a 2.4% increase. One gigawatt is roughly enough to power 750,000 average U.S. homes. NERC attributed the growth primarily to additions of battery storage and gas-fired generation, as well as a slowdown in generator retirements.

  "In many ways, the industry is on better footing going into winter this year," Mark Olson, NERC's manager of reliability assessments, said on a call discussing the report.

  ## El Niño adds uncertainty

  Despite the capacity gains, Olson cautioned that winter remains "the toughest operating period for the North American electric grid." The El Niño weather pattern — a climate cycle associated with warmer-than-average winters in parts of North America — introduces uncertainty rather than a reliable buffer, he said. "The reality is that extreme storms with ice and abnormal cold temperatures can lie within those averages," Olson said. "El Niño is a variable that adds to uncertainty for the winter."

  NERC said it is urging heightened vigilance from industry stakeholders in light of that uncertainty.

  ## Where constraints pose the greatest risk

  The assessment identified specific subregions where the combination of extreme weather and infrastructure limits could cause supply to fall short of demand.

  In New England, constrained natural gas pipeline infrastructure has been a recurring concern in NERC's assessments. Bob Tallman, a senior engineer at NERC, said on the call that the region is rated at elevated risk because its gas pipeline capacity is limited, leaving power generators exposed when demand for heating fuel peaks simultaneously with demand for electricity.

  In the Northeast Power Coordinating Council's Québec and Maritimes regions, supply "can become strained during extreme winter peak demand," Tallman said.

  In the SERC Reliability Corp. East area — a region covering much of the southeastern United States — resources can fall short of extreme peak demand "during morning and low solar output periods of the day," Tallman said. Solar generation drops to zero overnight and is reduced during morning hours, leaving the grid more dependent on dispatchable fuel-based generators at the same time heating demand is highest.

  In the Southwest Power Pool (SPP) and Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO) territories, which together cover a large portion of the central United States, NERC identified subregions with localized transmission constraints. Those constraints could make it difficult to import power from neighboring areas during a wide-area winter weather event that simultaneously limits local generation and increases reliance on those imports.

  ## Fuel supply the common thread

  Across the flagged regions, fuel supply risk is a consistent thread. NERC officials noted that peak winter electric heating demand frequently coincides with higher residential natural gas consumption. That overlap can leave single-fuel power plants — generators that run only on natural gas and do not hold long-term fuel supply contracts — unable to operate when their output is most needed.

  The assessment did not identify any region as facing a high probability of controlled outages or load shedding, but the elevated and elevated-risk designations signal areas where operators will need contingency plans if temperatures fall well below seasonal norms.

  The findings carry practical implications for grid planners and operators in the identified regions, who will need to coordinate fuel procurement, transmission scheduling and demand-response programs ahead of potential cold snaps. NERC said it has been in contact with industry stakeholders about the need for extra vigilance through the season.
body_zh: |
  北美電力可靠性公司（NERC）——負責監管北美主幹電力系統可靠性的機構——於11月18日公布2026-2027年冬季可靠性評估報告，指出進入本冬季後，北美各地區並無出現電力資源嚴重短缺的高風險地區，但仍點名中西部、東南及新英格蘭部分區域，認為燃料供應與輸電限制可能在極端低溫期間對電網調度造成壓力。

  尖峰容量資源——即尖峰用電時段可調用的發電量——相較2025-2026年冬季增加了26.2 GW（吉瓦），成長幅度達2.4%。1 GW的發電量約可供應75萬戶美國一般家庭用電。NERC指出，此次容量成長主要來自電池儲能與天然氣發電機組的新增，以及發電機組除役速度的放緩。

  「從許多方面來看，業界進入今年冬季的基礎比往年更為穩固，」NERC可靠性評估主任Mark Olson在討論報告的電話會議上表示。

  ## 聖嬰現象增添不確定性

  儘管容量有所增加，Olson仍警告冬季依然是「北美電網最嚴峻的運行時期」。聖嬰現象（El Niño）——一種與北美部分地區冬季氣溫偏暖有關的氣候循環——帶來的是更多不確定性，而非可靠的緩衝，他說。「現實是，夾帶冰雪與異常低溫的極端風暴，完全可能出現在那些平均值之中，」Olson說，「聖嬰現象是一個增添冬季不確定性的變數。」

  NERC表示，鑒於上述不確定性，已呼籲業界利害關係人保持高度警惕。

  ## 哪些地區面臨最大供應風險

  此次評估點名了多個次級地區，認為在極端天氣與基礎設施限制的雙重壓力下，這些地方的供電可能無法滿足需求。

  在新英格蘭，天然氣管線基礎設施容量不足一直是NERC歷年評估中反覆出現的隱憂。NERC資深工程師Bob Tallman在電話會議上表示，該地區之所以被列為較高風險等級，是因為天然氣管線容量有限，當暖氣用氣需求與電力需求同時攀升時，發電業者極易陷入燃料短缺困境。

  在東北電力協調委員會轄下的魁北克（Québec）與濱海省份地區，「在冬季極端用電尖峰期間，供電可能趨於緊張，」Tallman說。

  在SERC可靠性公司東部區域——涵蓋美國東南大部分地區——電力資源可能在「每日清晨及太陽能出力偏低的時段」無法應付極端尖峰需求，Tallman指出。太陽能發電在夜間停止輸出、清晨出力有限，導致電網在暖氣需求最旺盛的同一時段，更加仰賴可調度的燃料發電機組。

  在西南電力池（Southwest Power Pool，SPP）與中大陸獨立系統運營商（Midcontinent Independent System Operator，MISO）轄區——合計涵蓋美國中部大片地區——NERC發現部分次級地區存在局部輸電限制。這些限制可能在廣域冬季天氣事件發生時，導致難以從周邊地區輸入電力；而在此情況下，當地發電能力本身已受限，對外部輸入的依賴反而更高。

  ## 燃料供應是共同隱憂

  綜觀上述被點名的各地區，燃料供應風險是貫穿始終的共同主軸。NERC官員指出，冬季電暖需求尖峰往往與居家天然氣用量攀升的時間高度重疊。這種供需交疊，可能使單一燃料電廠——即僅燃燒天然氣、且未持有長期燃料供應合約的發電機組——在最需要其出力的時刻，反而無法正常運行。

  此次評估並未將任何地區列為面臨大規模強制停電或分區限電的高機率區域，但「較高風險」等級的認定，意味著這些地區的業者在氣溫大幅低於季節常態時，必須備妥應急計畫。

  相關評估結果對上述地區的電網規劃者與業者而言具有實質意義——他們需要在可能的寒流來臨之前，提前協調燃料採購、輸電排程與需求回應計畫。NERC表示，已就本季保持額外警惕的必要性，與業界利害關係人展開溝通。
