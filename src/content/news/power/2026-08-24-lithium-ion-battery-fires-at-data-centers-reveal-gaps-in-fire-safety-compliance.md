body_en: |
  Data centers' increasing reliance on lithium-ion batteries for backup power has been linked to a series of damaging fires, and industry practitioners say compliance with existing fire codes leaves a significant gap between the risks those batteries introduce and the measures most project teams deploy to manage them.

  ## Four incidents in recent years

  Four fires linked to lithium-ion battery systems at data centers illustrate the scale of the problem.

  In September 2024, a fire at a Singapore data center damaged two battery rooms and two power supply rooms; firefighters took 36 hours to contain it. More recently, a reported battery explosion at a South Korean data center sparked a blaze that halted more than 600 online government services, including tax, postal, and mobile ID systems, and required nearly 200 firefighters working for ten hours to bring under control. Two separate fires at a Virginia facility last September were also attributed to lithium-ion batteries. At a Microsoft data campus in Goodyear, Arizona, a thermal runaway incident caused a small explosion inside a battery room.

  ## How lithium-ion fires develop

  The central hazard that lithium-ion batteries pose is thermal runaway — a self-accelerating chain reaction triggered when a battery cell becomes damaged, overheated, or defective. Once started, it spreads rapidly between cells, generating intense heat and flammable gases and producing fires that are extremely difficult to suppress. The 36-hour containment time in Singapore reflects how quickly suppression options narrow once the process begins, according to Aaron Velardi, head of North American development at Ramtech North America, writing in Data Center Dynamics.

  Despite those hazards, lithium-ion batteries play a critical role in uninterruptible power supply — or UPS — systems, providing essential backup power during grid outages. They are also widely used to power tools and temporary equipment during data center construction.

  ## Standards in place, but inconsistently applied

  The U.S. National Fire Protection Association's NFPA 855 standard sets minimum safety requirements for the design, installation, and operation of stationary energy storage systems — fixed battery installations, as distinct from portable devices — covering siting, fire protection, ventilation, and emergency response. Velardi notes that implementation of NFPA 855 remains inconsistent, varying between jurisdictions, project teams, and the authorities having jurisdiction, known as AHJs, that oversee specific sites. In practice, many projects focus on achieving minimum compliance rather than developing comprehensive fire prevention strategies, he writes.

  The NFPA is preparing to publish a new Battery Safety Code, designated NFPA 800, which the association describes as the first full-lifecycle standard for battery safety. It will cover manufacturing, transport, installation, operation, emergency response, and end-of-life management. The code was developed through an accelerated process in response to rapid growth in lithium-ion adoption across construction, energy, and industrial sectors, and is expected to be released on a provisional basis shortly.

  ## Construction phases present heightened exposure

  Large data center campuses frequently bring portions of a site online while other sections remain under construction, meaning live infrastructure and active building works coexist within the same environment. During construction phases, fire safety requirements are often interpreted more flexibly, Velardi writes, widening the gap between the risks present on site and the measures deployed to manage them. Because lithium-ion batteries also power tools and temporary operations during construction — not only UPS systems in completed facilities — the hazard extends across the full project timeline.

  ## Detection and suppression approaches are shifting

  Some project teams are moving away from sole reliance on gaseous suppression systems, which discharge a gas agent to displace oxygen and extinguish fire, while continuing to use pre-action sprinkler systems, which require a confirmed detection signal before releasing water. Interest in water mist systems designed for high-density environments is also growing, according to Velardi.

  Wireless fire detection systems are gaining adoption because they can maintain continuous coverage across complex or rapidly changing construction sites without requiring hardwired infrastructure. Real-time monitoring, automated alerts, and auditable response records provide earlier visibility into abnormal conditions. Velardi describes early detection as the best available opportunity to prevent escalation, because once thermal runaway begins, intervention options narrow rapidly.

  ## Industry significance

  The consequences of a single fire extend well beyond the immediate site. Extended commissioning delays, insurance complications, and redesign work typically follow incidents involving battery systems or high-voltage infrastructure. For facilities supporting AI workloads and cloud services, downtime and recovery costs compound those losses, Velardi notes. Incidents can also affect wider service networks dependent on new capacity coming online, as the South Korean case — in which more than 600 government services were interrupted — illustrates.

  As data centers grow in scale and power density to support AI workloads, the concentration of energy at any single facility increases the potential damage from a single incident. The expected provisional release of the NFPA 800 Battery Safety Code will mark the first regulatory attempt to govern battery safety across a product's full lifecycle, from manufacturing through end-of-life management.
body_zh: |
  資料中心對鋰離子電池備用電力的依賴程度持續攀升，這股趨勢已與多起嚴重火災事故相互牽連。業界人士指出，遵守現行消防法規，仍難以填平電池帶來的實際風險，與多數專案團隊所部署管理措施之間的巨大落差。

  ## 近年四起事故

  近年來，與鋰離子電池系統相關的資料中心火災事故共有四起，深刻凸顯問題的嚴重性。

  2024年9月，新加坡一處資料中心發生火災，兩間電池室與兩間電源供應室受損，消防人員歷時36小時才將火勢控制住。較近期，南韓一座資料中心傳出電池爆炸事故，引發大火，超過600項政府線上服務隨之中斷，涵蓋稅務、郵政及行動身分證等系統，近200名消防人員奮戰約10小時才將火勢撲滅。去年9月，維吉尼亞州一處設施連續發生兩起火災，同樣歸因於鋰離子電池。亞利桑那州古迪爾（Goodyear）一處微軟資料中心園區的電池室，則發生熱失控事故，引發小規模爆炸。

  ## 鋰離子電池火災的成因

  鋰離子電池最核心的危險在於熱失控（thermal runaway）——一種在電池芯受損、過熱或出現瑕疵時所觸發的自我加速連鎖反應。一旦啟動，便在電池芯之間迅速蔓延，產生強烈高熱與易燃氣體，形成極難撲滅的火災。Ramtech North America北美業務開發總監Aaron Velardi在《Data Center Dynamics》撰文指出，新加坡長達36小時的控火過程，正說明熱失控一旦展開，可用的抑制選項便急速縮窄。

  儘管存在上述危險，鋰離子電池在不斷電系統（UPS）中仍扮演不可或缺的角色，於電網中斷時提供必要備用電力。在資料中心施工期間，鋰離子電池也廣泛用於電動工具及臨時設備的供電。

  ## 現行標準存在，落實卻參差不齊

  美國全國消防協會（NFPA）的NFPA 855標準，為固定式儲能系統——即固定安裝的電池設施，有別於可攜式裝置——的設計、安裝與操作，訂立了最低安全要求，涵蓋選址、消防保護、通風及緊急應變等面向。Velardi指出，NFPA 855的執行情況參差不齊，因地方管轄區、專案團隊，以及負責監管特定場址的主管機關（AHJ）不同而有所差異。他在文中寫道，實務上許多專案僅以達到最低合規標準為目標，而未建立全面的火災預防策略。

  NFPA正籌備發布新的《電池安全法規》，代號NFPA 800，協會將其定位為首部涵蓋電池完整生命週期的安全標準，內容涵蓋製造、運輸、安裝、操作、緊急應變及生命週期終止管理。該法規係因應鋰離子電池在建築、能源及工業領域的快速普及，以加速程序進行制定，預計近期以暫行版本發布。

  ## 施工階段的高風險暴露

  大型資料中心園區往往採分批上線模式——部分區域已投入運作，其他區域仍在施工，致使現有基礎設施與施工作業並存於同一環境。Velardi寫道，施工期間，消防安全要求往往被以較為寬鬆的方式詮釋，進一步擴大了現場實際風險與已部署管理措施之間的落差。由於鋰離子電池不僅應用於竣工設施的UPS系統，在施工期間也為電動工具及臨時作業供電，這項潛在危害因此延伸至整個專案的完整時間軸。

  ## 偵測與抑制方式的轉變

  部分專案團隊正逐步擺脫對氣體抑制系統的單一依賴——此類系統透過釋放氣體驅離氧氣來達到滅火目的——同時持續採用預動式灑水系統，後者須在接獲確認的偵測信號後才會釋放水源。Velardi指出，專為高密度環境設計的細水霧系統也日益受到業界重視。

  無線火災偵測系統因能在複雜或快速變動的施工現場維持持續防護覆蓋，且無需鋪設硬接線基礎設施，正獲得越來越廣泛的採用。即時監控、自動警報及可稽核的應變紀錄，有助於及早掌握異常狀況。Velardi表示，及早偵測是防止事態失控升級的最佳機會，因為熱失控一旦啟動，可採取的干預選項便迅速縮窄。

  ## 產業影響

  單一火災事故的衝擊遠超出事發場址本身。涉及電池系統或高壓基礎設施的事故，通常會引發一連串後果：試運轉延誤、保險理賠複雜化，以及設計重工。Velardi指出，對於支援AI工作負載與雲端服務的設施而言，停機與復原成本更將使損失雪上加霜。事故也可能衝擊仰賴新增容量上線的更廣泛服務網路，南韓案例便是明證——超過600項政府服務因此中斷。

  隨著資料中心為支援AI工作負載持續擴大規模與功率密度，單一設施的能源集中程度不斷提高，單一事故可能造成的損害也隨之放大。預計近期以暫行版本發布的NFPA 800《電池安全法規》，將成為首次嘗試從製造到生命週期終止管理，對電池安全進行全生命週期規範的法規里程碑。
