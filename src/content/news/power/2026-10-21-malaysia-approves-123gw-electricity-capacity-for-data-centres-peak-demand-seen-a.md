body_en: |
  Malaysia had approved electricity supply capacity totalling 12.3 gigawatts (GW) for data centre projects as of August, with peak demand from the sector forecast to reach 8.39 GW by 2030, Deputy Prime Minister Datuk Seri Fadillah Yusof said in a written parliamentary reply.

  Fadillah, who also serves as Energy Transition and Water Transformation Minister, said the government would expand generation capacity and strengthen grid infrastructure in phases to accommodate the sector's growth. These steps would not compromise the stability or reliability of national electricity supply, he added.

  The disclosure came in response to a question by Syerleena Abdul Rashid (PH-Bukit Bendera), who asked about approved utility capacity for data centre projects, demand projections to 2030, and safeguards for domestic consumers.

  ## Consumer cost protection

  Fadillah said additional costs arising from data centres' large utility demands would not be passed on to other consumers. The government would apply a cost causation principle, under which the party responsible for generating additional system requirements bears the related expenses.

  Two existing mechanisms support that commitment. The Ultra-High Voltage tariff, which took effect on July 1 last year, applies to new and existing data centres drawing supply at high-voltage levels above 50 kilovolts (kV) up to 230 kV, and at extra-high-voltage levels above 230 kV. The government has also raised the Connected Load Charge, a penalty mechanism intended to deter data centres from applying for electricity capacity beyond their actual requirements, to promote efficient use of approved supply.

  ## Renewable energy mandate

  Data centres must source at least 30% of their total demand capacity from renewable energy. That threshold can be met through the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme or the Solar for Self-Consumption initiative. The sector is also encouraged to develop on-site generation facilities to reduce dependence on the national grid. The Energy Commission is preparing a framework and guidelines to govern self-generation for data centres and other industries, Fadillah said.

  ## Water allocation and oversight

  On water, Fadillah said data centre projects in Selangor, Johor, and Negri Sembilan had received combined approval to use 445.04 million litres a day between last year and 2030. Of that total, 63.90 million litres a day had been approved for centres already in operation as of June, representing 14.36% of total approved water demand.

  Every proposed data centre development is assessed by the Data Centre Task Force, which considers available water resources, water supply system capacity, and the needs of existing users. Projects assessed as posing a risk to water supply reserve margins must address the capacity shortfall before they can proceed.

  The National Water Services Commission (SPAN) has issued guidelines requiring data centres to improve water-use efficiency and use alternative sources for cooling where possible. A separate water tariff of between RM5.30 and RM5.38 per cubic metre applies to data centres in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan, effective August 1 last year.

  ## Significance and outlook

  The parliamentary disclosure provides the most detailed official accounting to date of Malaysia's data centre utility commitments, quantifying both the electricity capacity already approved and the outer limit of projected demand through the decade. The figures, alongside the tariff and penalty frameworks described by Fadillah, indicate that the government is managing rapid sector expansion while attempting to ring-fence its cost impact on residential and commercial electricity and water users.

  Any future adjustments to water or electricity tariffs will be assessed on the basis of actual service delivery costs, affordability, and national economic interests, Fadillah said. The Energy Commission's framework for data centre self-generation facilities remains in preparation.

  ## 馬來西亞核准12.3GW資料中心用電容量，2030年尖峰需求預估達8.39GW

  馬來西亞副總理拿督斯里法迪拉·優素夫在一份書面國會答覆中表示，截至今年8月，政府已核准總計12.3吉瓦（GW）的資料中心供電容量，並預估該產業至2030年的尖峰需求將達8.39吉瓦。

  法迪拉同時兼任能源轉型與水務轉型部長，他表示政府將分階段擴充發電容量、強化電網基礎設施，以因應產業的快速成長，且上述措施不會危及全國電力供應的穩定性與可靠性。

  此次揭露源自雪麗娜·阿都拉昔（公正黨－峇吉里峇打區）的質詢，她詢問資料中心的核准用電容量、2030年前的需求預測，以及保障一般消費者的相關機制。

  ## 消費者費用保護

  法迪拉表示，資料中心龐大用電需求所衍生的額外成本，不會轉嫁給其他消費者。政府將採用「成本歸因原則」，由造成額外系統需求的當事方自行承擔相關費用。

  目前已有兩項現行機制支撐此一承諾。其一為「超高壓電費率」，自去年7月1日起生效，適用於以高壓（50千伏以上至230千伏）及超高壓（230千伏以上）方式取電的新舊資料中心。其二為「接戶負載費」，此一罰則機制旨在遏阻資料中心申請超出實際需求的電力容量，藉此促進核准供電量的有效利用，政府已調高此費率。

  ## 再生能源強制要求

  資料中心必須從再生能源取得至少30%的總需求容量，此門檻可透過「企業再生能源供應計畫」或「自用太陽能發電計畫」來達成。政府亦鼓勵資料中心業者自建發電設施，以降低對國家電網的依賴。法迪拉表示，能源委員會正在研擬一套管理框架與指引，以規範資料中心及其他產業的自發電行為。

  ## 用水配額與監管

  在用水方面，法迪拉表示，雪蘭莪、柔佛及森美蘭三州的資料中心專案，已獲核准於去年至2030年間合計每日使用4億4504萬公升的水量。其中，截至今年6月已投入營運的資料中心每日核准用水量為6390萬公升，佔總核准用水需求的14.36%。

  每一項資料中心開發申請均須經「資料中心專案工作小組」審查，評估項目包括現有水資源、供水系統容量及現有用戶需求。若評估結果顯示該專案對供水儲備水位構成風險，業者必須先解決容量不足的問題，方可繼續推進。

  國家水務服務委員會（SPAN）已發出指引，要求資料中心提升用水效率，並在可行情況下採用替代水源進行冷卻。針對半島馬來西亞及納閩的資料中心，自去年8月1日起另行適用每立方公尺5.30至5.38令吉的獨立水費率。

  ## 重要意義與前景展望

  此次國會揭露是迄今官方對馬來西亞資料中心電力與用水承諾最為詳盡的說明，不僅量化了已核准的電力容量，也確立了本十年需求預測的上限。上述數字，連同法迪拉所述的電費及罰則機制，顯示政府正在積極管理產業的快速擴張，同時力求將成本衝擊與住宅及商業用戶的電費、水費切割區隔。

  法迪拉表示，未來任何水費或電費的調整，均將依據實際服務提供成本、民眾負擔能力及國家經濟利益加以評估。能源委員會針對資料中心自發電設施的管理框架，目前仍在研擬當中。
