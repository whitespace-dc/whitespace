  DayOne Data Centres and Blue Whale Energy have signed a memorandum of understanding to study a distributed virtual power plant (VPP) in Singapore, a project that would connect battery storage at DayOne's planned SG1 data centre with batteries at other commercial and industrial sites across the city-state.

  A VPP combines energy assets at different locations and manages them through software and control systems so that geographically dispersed sites can operate as a single resource for the electricity grid. In this case, the aim is to pool battery storage across several sites so that electricity drawn from the grid can be adjusted in real time when needed.

  ## What the study will examine

  The initial work is expected to include a joint front-end engineering design (FEED) study covering storage configuration, safety, metering and telemetry, controls, operational safeguards, and possible arrangements with participating commercial and industrial facilities, according to the companies.

  The partners also plan to engage with authorities and market stakeholders to understand the technical requirements and conditions for participation in relevant market schemes — specifically how a distributed system of this kind would fit within Singapore's existing electricity market rules.

  Part of the assessment will examine potential participation in Singapore's Demand Response and Interruptible Load programmes, which are designed to encourage businesses to reduce electricity consumption during periods of market stress and grid contingencies. That element of the study will depend on applicable market rules and regulatory approvals.

  ## The anchor site and the aggregator

  DayOne's SG1 development is set to serve as the anchor asset in the proposed study. By connecting SG1's battery storage to systems installed at third-party commercial and industrial locations, the partners want to test whether a cluster of assets can respond to grid conditions in a coordinated way.

  Blue Whale Energy, also based in Singapore, specialises in battery energy storage systems and VPP aggregation services. Its role in the study will focus on the technical, operational, and commercial arrangements needed to combine multiple battery assets into one coordinated platform.

  DayOne operates data centres across Asia Pacific and Europe, with a footprint spanning 10 markets including Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Japan, Hong Kong, Finland, and Spain.

  ## Pressure on grid infrastructure

  The study reflects growing pressure on electricity systems as demand from artificial intelligence and cloud computing rises, with data centre operators and grid managers seeking ways to make better use of existing power infrastructure, particularly during periods of peak demand.

  According to the companies, a coordinated distributed storage network could help reduce strain on the grid at key moments and may limit the need for some network upgrades that would otherwise be required to meet peak demand.

  "Singapore's digital economy depends not only on computing infrastructure but also on the physical systems that support it, including energy," said Jamie Khoo, chief executive officer of DayOne. "We believe data centers should play an active role in strengthening the broader power ecosystem they rely on and creating long-term value."

  Gabriel Lim, founder and chief executive officer of Blue Whale Energy, said the project would assess whether a central data centre site can be combined with other facilities to create a workable commercial and operational model. "Coordinated energy storage could create value by bringing together an anchor data center and suitable commercial and industrial sites," Lim said.

  ## Significance for constrained markets

  The study raises a question that extends beyond Singapore: how data centre operators in constrained power markets can add digital infrastructure without placing disproportionate pressure on power networks. If the VPP model proves technically and commercially viable, it could offer a template for other markets where grid capacity is a binding constraint on data centre growth.

  The next steps for the project include completing the FEED study and engaging with Singapore's electricity market authorities to determine the conditions under which a distributed VPP of this kind could participate in grid stability programmes.

  ## DayOne與藍鯨能源攜手 擬在新加坡建立分散式虛擬電廠

  DayOne資料中心與藍鯨能源已簽署合作備忘錄，共同研究在新加坡推動一套分散式虛擬電廠（VPP）方案。這項計畫將把DayOne預定開發的SG1資料中心所配置的電池儲能系統，與新加坡各地其他商業及工業場址的電池設備串聯起來。

  虛擬電廠的概念是透過軟體與控制系統，統一調度分布在不同地點的能源資產，讓地理上彼此分散的場址能夠作為單一資源共同支援電力網路。在這項方案中，目標是將多個場址的電池儲能資源匯聚在一起，以便在有需要時即時調整從電網汲取的用電量。

  ## 研究範疇

  根據兩家公司的說法，前期工作預計將包括一項聯合初步工程設計（FEED）研究，涵蓋儲能系統配置、安全規範、電表與遙測設備、控制系統、操作防護措施，以及與參與商業和工業設施之間的可能合作安排。

  雙方合作夥伴亦計畫與主管機關及電力市場相關利害關係人進行溝通，以釐清參與相關市場機制所須符合的技術要求與條件，尤其是這類分散式系統如何在新加坡現行電力市場規則的框架下運作。

  此次評估還將審視潛在參與新加坡「需求回應」及「可中斷負載」計畫的可行性。這兩項計畫旨在鼓勵企業在電力市場緊張或電網出現緊急狀況時降低用電需求。相關研究進度將視適用的市場規則與法規核准情形而定。

  ## 主力場址與聚合業者

  DayOne的SG1開發案預計作為這項研究的核心錨點資產。透過將SG1的電池儲能系統與安裝在第三方商業及工業場址的系統相互連結，雙方合作夥伴希望驗證一組資產群能否以協調一致的方式回應電網狀況。

  同樣總部位於新加坡的藍鯨能源，專精於電池儲能系統及虛擬電廠聚合服務。在這項研究中，其角色將著重於整合多組電池資產、建構統一協調平台所需的技術、營運及商業安排。

  DayOne在亞太及歐洲地區經營多座資料中心，業務遍及新加坡、馬來西亞、印尼、泰國、日本、香港、芬蘭及西班牙等10個市場。

  ## 電網基礎建設的壓力

  這項研究反映出電力系統所承受的日益沉重壓力——隨著人工智慧與雲端運算需求持續攀升，資料中心業者與電網管理者都在積極尋求更有效利用現有電力基礎設施的方法，尤其是在尖峰用電期間。

  兩家公司表示，一套協調整合的分散式儲能網路，有助於在關鍵時刻舒緩電網壓力，或許也能降低部分原本為應付尖峰需求而必須進行的電網升級需求。

  DayOne執行長Jamie Khoo表示：「新加坡的數位經濟不僅仰賴運算基礎設施，同樣也有賴於支撐它的實體系統，包括能源。我們相信，資料中心應積極參與強化其所依存的整體電力生態系，並創造長遠價值。」

  藍鯨能源創辦人暨執行長Gabriel Lim則表示，這項計畫將評估一座核心資料中心場址能否與其他設施結合，建立可行的商業與營運模式。「協調整合的能源儲存，可以透過整合一個錨點資料中心與適合的商業及工業場址來創造價值，」Lim說道。

  ## 對供電受限市場的意義

  這項研究所引發的議題，其意義已超越新加坡本身：在電力供應受限的市場中，資料中心業者究竟能如何擴建數位基礎設施，同時又不對電網造成過度壓力？若虛擬電廠模式在技術與商業層面均被證實可行，便可能為其他同樣面臨電網容量成為資料中心成長瓶頸的市場，提供一套可供參考的範本。

  這項計畫的後續步驟包括：完成FEED研究，並與新加坡電力市場主管機關進行溝通，以確認此類分散式虛擬電廠在何種條件下得以參與電網穩定計畫。
