body_en: |
  Ratepayer advocates from seven states and PJM Interconnection's independent market monitor have called on the grid operator to postpone its December capacity auction, arguing that speculative data center load forecasts could force 67 million consumers to absorb between $5 billion and $10 billion in unnecessary electricity costs.

  The advocates — consumer offices from Pennsylvania, Ohio, New Jersey, Delaware, West Virginia, Maryland and Illinois — sent a letter Wednesday to PJM's board asking it to delay the base capacity auction for the 2029/30 delivery year, currently scheduled to run Dec. 9 through Dec. 15, "until data center loads, both real and speculative, can be removed from the load forecast." The letter requests a written response from PJM's board no later than Nov. 23.

  A capacity auction is the mechanism by which a grid operator contracts with power generators and demand-response providers to ensure enough supply will be available to meet peak electricity demand years in advance. Consumers ultimately pay these capacity costs through their electric bills.

  ## The market monitor's warning

  The advocates' letter relies heavily on a Nov. 12 report by Monitoring Analytics, PJM's independent market monitor — a third-party watchdog that evaluates whether the grid operator's markets function competitively. The report warned that PJM's current auction demand forecast includes data centers that have been canceled or are unlikely to reach their projected demand levels.

  Including that speculative load in the auction would, according to Monitoring Analytics, cause consumers to procure more capacity than is actually needed — at a cost the monitor placed at $5 billion to $10 billion above what customers would otherwise pay. Cumulatively, the monitor's Nov. 12 report found that existing and forecast data center demand incorporated into PJM's capacity market has already raised capacity costs by $29.4 billion across all customers.

  The advocates said the monitor's recommendation is not simply to update PJM's load numbers. "It is to fundamentally change how forecasted data-center demand is treated in the capacity auction so that existing consumers are not required to subsidize speculative or uncommitted data center demand," they wrote.

  The letter also cited a Nov. 3 complaint filed by utility holding company Exelon at the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), which challenged PJM's three-year forward capacity auction structure partly over uncertainty in forecasting data center load and the resulting risk of excess capacity costs.

  ## PJM's position

  PJM pushed back on the delay request. Jeffrey Shields, a spokesman for the grid operator, told Utility Dive that the December auction includes an established price cap that acts as a safeguard against how high capacity prices can rise, and that postponing the auction at this stage could destabilize the market and increase the risk of power interruptions.

  "The system urgently needs capacity to meet rising demand," Shields said. "Electricity demand is growing faster than new power supplies can be built."

  Shields drew a distinction between PJM's planning obligations and the policy decisions that drive load growth. "While decisions about whether data centers and other large electricity users can connect to the grid rest with policymakers and utilities, PJM must plan for the demand those decisions create," he said.

  Delaying the auction would require PJM to seek approval from FERC, the federal agency that oversees wholesale electricity markets.

  ## Current market rules and pending reforms

  Underlying the dispute is a structural feature of PJM's capacity market rules: under the current framework, data center companies are not required to pay directly for the capacity and infrastructure costs their load growth creates. Instead, those costs are spread across all consumers on the grid.

  PJM has proposed a two-part reform in response to pressure from states, federal regulators and consumer groups: a framework to facilitate bilateral contracts — direct agreements between large loads and generators outside the standard capacity auction — and a separate reliability backstop procurement for large loads that do not secure bilateral contracts. The grid operator is also pursuing reforms to its interconnection rules for large loads and new generation. Those efforts remain pending.

  The advocates noted that PJM planned to publish its final "planning parameters" for the upcoming auction on Nov. 19, a step that could remove some canceled or delayed data center projects from the auction's demand curve. However, they said that action falls short of what the market monitor recommends and would not resolve the underlying problem.

  "Proceeding with an auction that incorporates both real and speculative data-center demand, despite the [market monitor's] findings, would undermine that commitment and expose consumers to billions in potentially avoidable costs," the advocates wrote, referencing a statement of principles endorsed in January by PJM and the governors of its 13 member states, which held that large-load forecasts must be "real and verified."

  ## What comes next

  The outcome will likely depend on whether PJM's board responds to the advocates' Nov. 23 deadline and whether FERC chooses to act — either on the advocates' delay request or on Exelon's pending complaint — before the auction's Dec. 9 start date. PJM's publication of updated planning parameters on Nov. 19 will offer an initial indication of how many data center projects the grid operator is willing to remove from its demand forecast ahead of the auction.
body_zh: |
  七州消費者倡議團體及PJM互聯公司（PJM Interconnection）獨立市場監管機構，聯合敦促這家電網業者延後十二月的容量拍賣。他們警告，投機性的資料中心負載預測可能迫使六千七百萬名消費者多負擔五十億至一百億美元的不必要電力費用。

  來自賓夕法尼亞州、俄亥俄州、紐澤西州、德拉瓦州、西維吉尼亞州、馬里蘭州及伊利諾州的消費者辦公室，於週三聯名致函PJM董事會，要求延後原訂十二月九日至十五日舉行的二○二九至二○三○供電年度基本容量拍賣，直到「資料中心的真實與投機性負載均能從負載預測中移除」為止。信中要求PJM董事會不得晚於十一月二十三日以書面回覆。

  所謂容量拍賣，是電網業者與發電商及需求響應供應商簽訂合約的機制，目的是確保數年後的高峰用電需求有足夠供給可資調配。這些容量費用最終將透過電費帳單轉嫁給消費者。

  ## 市場監管機構的警告

  倡議團體的信函大量援引PJM獨立市場監管機構Monitoring Analytics於十一月十二日發布的報告。該機構為第三方監察單位，負責評估PJM市場是否具有競爭性。報告警告，PJM目前的拍賣需求預測中，納入了已取消或不太可能達到預測用電量的資料中心。

  根據Monitoring Analytics的分析，將這些投機性負載納入拍賣，將導致消費者採購超出實際所需的容量，相關費用比消費者原應支付的金額多出五十億至一百億美元。累計而言，十一月十二日的報告指出，PJM容量市場中已納入的現有及預測資料中心需求，已使所有用戶的容量費用合計增加了二百九十四億美元。

  倡議團體指出，市場監管機構的建議並非僅是更新PJM的負載數字，而是「從根本上改變容量拍賣對預測資料中心需求的處理方式，使現有消費者不必為投機性或未經承諾的資料中心需求買單」。

  信函還援引公用事業控股公司Exelon於十一月三日向聯邦能源管理委員會（FERC）提出的投訴。Exelon在投訴中挑戰PJM三年期前瞻容量拍賣架構，部分原因正是資料中心負載預測的不確定性，以及由此衍生的超額容量費用風險。

  ## PJM的立場

  PJM對延後拍賣的請求予以反駁。電網業者發言人傑弗里·希爾茲（Jeffrey Shields）向《Utility Dive》表示，十二月的拍賣設有既定價格上限，可防止容量價格無限攀升；若在此時延後拍賣，可能動搖市場穩定、增加供電中斷的風險。

  「系統迫切需要容量以應對持續上升的需求，」希爾茲說，「電力需求的成長速度已超過新電力供應的建設速度。」

  希爾茲區分了PJM的規劃責任與推動負載成長的政策決策。「儘管資料中心及其他大用電戶能否接入電網，決定權在於政策制定者與公用事業公司，但PJM必須為這些決策所創造的需求進行規劃，」他說。

  延後拍賣將要求PJM向負責監管批發電力市場的聯邦機構FERC申請核准。

  ## 現行市場規則與待議改革

  這場爭議的核心，在於PJM容量市場規則中一項結構性特徵：依現行框架，資料中心業者無需直接承擔其負載成長所帶來的容量及基礎設施費用，這些費用反而分攤給電網上的全體消費者。

  PJM在各州、聯邦監管機構及消費者團體的壓力下，提出了雙軌改革方案：其一是促進雙邊合約的框架，讓大型用電戶得以在標準容量拍賣體系之外，直接與發電商簽訂協議；其二是針對未能取得雙邊合約的大型用電戶，設立獨立的可靠性備用採購機制。電網業者亦正推動大型用電戶及新發電設備的併網規則改革，相關措施目前均尚待核定。

  倡議團體指出，PJM計畫於十一月十九日公布本次拍賣的最終「規劃參數」，此舉或可將部分已取消或延宕的資料中心專案從拍賣需求曲線中移除。然而，他們認為此舉仍未達市場監管機構的建議標準，無法解決根本問題。

  「在市場監管機構已提出上述調查結果的情況下，仍推進納入真實與投機性資料中心需求的拍賣，將破壞既有承諾，並使消費者暴露於數十億美元本可避免的額外費用之下，」倡議團體寫道。信函援引PJM及其十三個成員州州長於今年一月共同背書的原則聲明，聲明明確要求大型負載預測必須「真實且經過驗證」。

  ## 後續發展

  事件走向很可能取決於兩個關鍵因素：PJM董事會是否在倡議團體十一月二十三日的截止期限前作出回應，以及FERC是否選擇在十二月九日拍賣開始前有所行動——無論是回應倡議團體的延後申請，還是處理Exelon懸而未決的投訴。PJM預計於十一月十九日公布更新後的規劃參數，屆時將初步揭示這家電網業者願意在拍賣前從需求預測中移除多少資料中心專案。
