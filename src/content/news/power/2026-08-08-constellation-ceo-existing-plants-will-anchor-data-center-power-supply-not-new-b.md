body_en: |
  Constellation Energy's chief executive said Thursday that existing power plants — not new construction — will carry the bulk of data center electricity demand in the near term, pushing back against calls for hyperscalers to source only newly built generation.

  "We're never going to build this economy if we have to wait for new power plants to be built before we can connect any data center," Joseph Dominguez, Constellation's president and CEO, said during a second-quarter earnings conference call. "The bedrock of building out at least this early phase of the data economy is going to rely heavily, in my view, on existing generation."

  The remarks address a live policy debate around the Ratepayer Protection Pledge, an initiative that calls on planned data centers to supply their own new generation rather than draw on existing grid capacity.

  ## The capacity argument

  Dominguez argued that the existing transmission and generation system carries enough available capacity to serve consumers during more than 99% of all hours in a year. The constraint, he said, is not total energy supply but a narrow window of peak demand hours.

  "We have a peak capacity concern, not an energy concern," he said. "The secret sauce here is to deal with the handful of peak hours that present reliability concerns, and at the same time to harvest the stranded capacity that exists every other hour of the year."

  Batteries, demand response programs — in which large customers voluntarily reduce usage during grid stress — and peaking resources can manage those critical hours, he said. The remaining hours, he argued, are where Constellation's existing fleet provides the most value.

  "That's where our fleet becomes extraordinarily valuable because it's a fixed-price, clean energy resource that they could count on for decades," Dominguez said.

  Baltimore-based Constellation is an independent power producer — a company that generates and sells electricity but does not own distribution lines to homes and businesses — with approximately 55 gigawatts (GW) of total generation capacity. One gigawatt is roughly enough to power 750,000 average U.S. homes. About 22 GW of that fleet is nuclear, and the company said it sees potential for approximately 1.1 GW of additional output through reactor uprates — engineering modifications that increase a plant's licensed power level.

  ## Texas interconnection pause

  Constellation has projects tied to data centers that have been caught in a pause of Texas's Batch Zero interconnection process, Dominguez said. Batch Zero refers to a queue of large electricity load requests — primarily data centers — that the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), the state's grid operator, has temporarily held pending additional review.

  The queue totals approximately 474 GW of large load interconnection requests, the vast majority from data centers. Texas Governor Greg Abbott asked that applicants provide information on their power supply plans, expected electricity and water consumption, and any public financial assistance received.

  Dave Dardis, Constellation's chief of external affairs and growth officer, said on the call that the required information can be submitted quickly and that the company does not expect a significant delay.

  "We see this as a temporary measure that we think is manageable by the industry," Dardis said, noting that lawmakers approaching midterm elections have an incentive to move quickly.

  ## ERCOT price dynamics

  Wholesale power prices in ERCOT are currently low, a condition Dominguez attributed to battery storage and other resources coming online ahead of the large load growth that data centers are expected to bring. He said he expects prices to recover as data centers are built and consumption rises.

  "The market will start to tighten up as data centers get built, and you start to see the market come into more or less equilibrium," he said.

  ## Brazos Valley sale and DOJ obligations

  Also on Thursday, Constellation confirmed the sale of its 606-megawatt (MW) gas-fired Brazos Valley power plant in Texas to LS Power for $860 million, equivalent to approximately $1,420 per kilowatt of capacity. The transaction followed a competitive bidding process.

  "It was clear from a very competitive process that buyers recognize the long-term value of gas-fired assets with the potential for even higher utilization rates," said Shane Smith, Constellation's chief financial officer.

  The sale will satisfy Constellation's divestiture obligations under its settlement with the U.S. Department of Justice stemming from the company's acquisition of Calpine, a large natural gas generator. Total gross proceeds from assets sold under that DOJ agreement are expected to reach approximately $5.9 billion, Smith said.

  ## Earnings

  Constellation reported second-quarter adjusted operating earnings of $2.55 per share, up from $1.91 per share in the same period a year earlier. Smith attributed the increase to the Calpine acquisition, higher capacity revenue, and other factors.

  ## Industry implications

  Dominguez's comments signal that Constellation intends to position its existing nuclear and other generation assets as a near-term solution for data center operators seeking long-term, fixed-price power contracts, rather than waiting for new capacity additions. The argument runs counter to grid advocates who contend that data center load growth should be paired with incremental new supply to avoid drawing down reserves that residential and commercial customers rely on.

  The resolution of the Texas Batch Zero review process will determine when Constellation's affected data center projects can move forward toward commercial operation.

  Constellation Energy執行長週四表示，在短期內，現有電廠——而非新建機組——將承擔資料中心電力需求的大宗，並對超大規模業者應僅使用新建機組供電的呼聲予以反駁。

  「如果每連接一座資料中心都必須等待新電廠建成，我們的經濟根本發展不起來，」Constellation總裁兼執行長Joseph Dominguez在第二季財報電話會議上表示，「在我看來，建構資料經濟——至少這個早期階段——將大量倚賴現有機組。」

  此番言論回應了圍繞「電費支付者保護承諾」（Ratepayer Protection Pledge）的現行政策辯論。這項倡議呼籲規劃中的資料中心自行興建新機組供電，而非從現有電網容量中抽取電力。

  ## 容量論述

  Dominguez主張，現有輸電與發電系統具備充足的可用容量，足以在一年中逾99%的時間內服務用戶。他表示，限制所在並非總體電力供應，而是少數幾個尖峰負載時段。

  「我們面臨的是尖峰容量問題，而非電力供應問題，」他說，「關鍵在於處理少數幾個存在可靠性疑慮的尖峰時段，同時挖掘全年其他時間所存在的閒置容量。」

  電池儲能、需量反應方案——即大型用戶在電網吃緊時自願降低用電量——以及尖峰備用資源，均可管理這些關鍵時段，他說。而在其餘時間，正是Constellation現有機組最能發揮價值之處。

  「正因如此，我們的機組才格外珍貴——那是一種固定電價的清潔能源資源，客戶可以在數十年內持續仰賴，」Dominguez說。

  總部位於巴爾的摩的Constellation是一家獨立發電業者——即只負責發電與售電、不持有住宅與商業配電線路的公司——總裝置容量約55吉瓦（GW）。1吉瓦大致可供應75萬戶美國一般家庭用電。其中核電約22吉瓦，該公司表示，透過反應爐功率提升（reactor uprate）——亦即提高電廠核准輸出功率的工程改造——可望再增加約1.1吉瓦的額外產能。

  ## 德州併網申請暫停

  Dominguez表示，Constellation部分與資料中心相關的專案，受困於德州「批次零」（Batch Zero）併網程序的暫停。「批次零」是指德州電力可靠度委員會（ERCOT）——即該州電網營運機構——目前暫緩受理的一批大型用電申請排程，主要來自資料中心。

  這批排程合計約有474吉瓦的大型負載併網申請，絕大多數來自資料中心。德州州長Greg Abbott要求申請方提供供電計畫、預期用電量與用水量，以及所獲公共財政補助等相關資訊。

  Constellation外部事務暨成長長Dave Dardis在電話會議上表示，所需資訊可迅速提交，公司預計不會出現明顯延誤。

  「我們認為這是一項暫時性措施，業界應可因應，」Dardis說，並指出臨近期中選舉的民選官員有加快推動的誘因。

  ## ERCOT電力價格走勢

  目前ERCOT批發電力價格偏低，Dominguez將此歸因於電池儲能等資源已搶先上線，而資料中心預期帶來的大規模負載成長尚未實現。他預期，隨著資料中心陸續建成、用電量增加，電價將回升。

  「隨著資料中心陸續建成，市場將逐漸趨緊，並回歸大致均衡，」他說。

  ## Brazos Valley出售及司法部義務

  週四，Constellation亦確認已將位於德州、裝置容量606百萬瓦（MW）的Brazos Valley天然氣電廠，以8.6億美元出售給LS Power，相當於每千瓦容量約1,420美元。此次交易經過競爭性競標程序。

  「從競爭激烈的競標過程可以清楚看出，買方認可天然氣資產的長期價值，以及其進一步提升使用率的潛力，」Constellation財務長Shane Smith表示。

  本次出售將履行Constellation依據與美國司法部（DOJ）達成的和解協議所承擔的資產剝離義務，該協議源於公司收購大型天然氣發電商Calpine一事。Smith表示，依據該司法部協議出售的資產，預計總毛收益將達約59億美元。

  ## 財報表現

  Constellation公布第二季調整後每股營業獲利2.55美元，高於去年同期的1.91美元。Smith將此歸因於收購Calpine的貢獻、較高的容量收益及其他因素。

  ## 產業意涵

  Dominguez的言論顯示，Constellation打算將旗下現有核電及其他發電資產，定位為資料中心業者的近期解方——這些業者尋求長期固定電價合約，而非等待新增產能到位。這一主張與電網倡導者的立場相左；後者認為，資料中心負載成長應與新增供電同步，以避免壓縮住宅與商業用戶所仰賴的備用容量。

  德州「批次零」審查程序的結果，將決定Constellation受影響的資料中心專案何時得以進入商業運轉。
