body_en: |
  PJM Interconnection filed a plan at the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) on Friday to hold a one-time reliability backstop auction aimed at closing a 6.8-gigawatt (GW) capacity shortfall left by the grid operator's last scheduled procurement, with the auction window running from Sept. 30 to Oct. 21.

  The shortfall emerged from PJM's most recent base capacity auction, which covers the delivery year beginning in mid-2028. PJM — the regional transmission organization that manages the electric grid across 13 states and the District of Columbia — failed in that auction to secure enough capacity to meet its 20% reserve margin target, a threshold designed to limit the probability of an unexpected, large-scale power outage to no more than once every 10 years.

  ## What drove the gap

  PJM attributed the shortfall primarily to surging data center load forecasts that have not been matched by new generation coming online. The grid operator estimates that data center and other large-load demand could grow by up to 70 GW across its footprint by 2038 — more than double the 6.8-GW gap the backstop auction is meant to fill.

  The Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC), an environmental advocacy group, said the backstop auction could result in PJM paying up to $20 billion for new power plants to supply data centers built through 2027.

  ## Two-part structure

  PJM's proposal has two components. The first is the backstop auction itself. The second is a facilitated process for data centers and power suppliers to reach bilateral contracts directly. Any bilateral deals concluded before the auction begins would reduce the auction's capacity procurement target, according to PJM.

  PJM proposed an overall price cap of $555 per megawatt-day (MW-day) for the backstop auction, a significant increase from the $325/MW-day cap that applied to its last base capacity auction. A megawatt-day is the standard unit used in PJM's capacity market to price a generator's commitment to be available when the grid needs it.

  ## Cost allocation and state role

  Costs from the backstop auction would be spread across PJM zones based on each zone's proportional share of the procurement target and then passed down to the utilities and other load-serving entities — the companies that buy wholesale power to supply retail customers — within those zones, according to PJM's filing.

  PJM said the plan's success depends on individual states taking action to shield retail customers from potential cost shifts. The grid operator stated in its filing: "The RBP framework relies on each of the PJM states to refine, for purposes of cost allocation, which retail loads — including which 'large loads,' as each state defines such loads — should be allocated the costs of resources procured through the RBP."

  ## Separate large-load curtailment proposal

  PJM plans to file a second, separate proposal at FERC on Aug. 7, according to Jeffrey Shields, a PJM spokesman. That filing, called the Interim Resource Adequacy Service — previously referred to as "connect and manage" — would allow PJM to curtail large loads that do not have their own dedicated power supplies when the grid approaches emergency conditions. The rules would apply to data centers that come online after June 1, 2027.

  Under the plan, PJM would create a registry of all large loads, which the grid operator said "will provide the critical data transparency needed to establish load reduction priorities for retail customers."

  Tom Rutigliano, a senior advocate for Climate and Energy at NRDC, said in a press release: "Requiring future data centers to bring their own power might be the single best thing they could have done to stabilize the grid. Now PJM needs to finish the job by supporting state efforts to manage demand from existing data centers, enabling rapid deployment of energy storage, and accelerating the connection of new energy projects to the grid."

  ## What comes next

  The backstop auction is structured to conclude ahead of PJM's next base capacity auction, which covers the 2029/30 delivery year. PJM said it aims to announce the backstop auction results by Dec. 2, giving the market a cleared picture of new capacity commitments before that subsequent procurement begins. The outcome of the FERC review of both filings will determine whether the auction proceeds as proposed.

  ## PJM向FERC提案舉辦備援競標，填補6.8GW電力缺口

  PJM Interconnection週五向美國聯邦能源監管委員會（FERC）提交計畫，擬舉辦一次性可靠性備援容量競標，以彌補該電網運營商上一輪採購後留下的6.8吉瓦（GW）容量缺口，競標窗口訂於9月30日至10月21日。

  此次缺口源於PJM最近一次基礎容量競標——該輪競標所涵蓋的供電年度自2028年中期起算。PJM是負責管理跨13個州及哥倫比亞特區電網的區域輸電組織，卻未能在該次競標中取得足夠容量，達成其設定的20%備用容量目標。該目標旨在將大規模突發停電的機率控制在每10年不超過一次的水準。

  ## 缺口成因

  PJM將此次短缺主要歸因於資料中心負載預測急遽攀升，而新增發電容量的上線速度卻遠跟不上需求。該電網運營商估計，至2038年，其管轄範圍內的資料中心及其他大型負載需求最多可增加70GW——超過此次備援競標擬填補的6.8GW缺口的兩倍有餘。

  環保倡議團體「自然資源保護委員會」（NRDC）表示，此次備援競標可能導致PJM為供應2027年前建設完工的資料中心，斥資高達200億美元興建新電廠。

  ## 雙軌架構

  PJM的提案包含兩個部分：第一是備援競標本身；第二是協助資料中心與電力供應商直接簽訂雙邊合約的促進機制。PJM表示，若有任何雙邊合約在競標開始前即告成立，將可相應縮減競標的容量採購目標。

  PJM為此次備援競標設定的整體價格上限為每百萬瓦日（MW-day）555美元，較上一次基礎容量競標所適用的325美元上限大幅提高。「百萬瓦日」是PJM容量市場中用來衡量發電機在電網需要時保持可用狀態之承諾的標準計價單位。

  ## 費用分攤與各州角色

  根據PJM的申報文件，備援競標所產生的費用將依各PJM分區佔採購目標的比例進行分攤，再轉嫁至各分區內的電力公司及其他負載服務實體——即向零售用戶供應批發電力的企業。

  PJM表示，此方案能否成功，有賴各州採取行動，保護零售用戶免受潛在費用轉嫁的衝擊。PJM在申報文件中指出：「備援競標框架有賴PJM各州就費用分攤目的，釐清哪些零售負載——包括各州自行定義的『大型負載』——應承擔透過備援競標採購資源所產生的費用。」

  ## 大型負載削減之獨立提案

  PJM發言人傑佛瑞．席爾茲（Jeffrey Shields）表示，PJM計畫於8月7日另行向FERC提交第二份獨立提案。該提案名為「過渡性資源充裕服務」（Interim Resource Adequacy Service），此前曾被稱為「接入並管理」（connect and manage）方案，將允許PJM在電網接近緊急狀態時，對沒有專屬自備電源的大型負載實施削減。此規定將適用於2027年6月1日後上線的資料中心。

  依據該計畫，PJM將建立所有大型負載的登記名冊，並表示此舉「將提供關鍵的資料透明度，以便為零售用戶建立負載削減優先順序」。

  NRDC氣候與能源資深倡議員湯姆．魯提利亞諾（Tom Rutigliano）在新聞稿中表示：「要求未來的資料中心自備電源，或許是穩定電網所能採取的最重要單一舉措。現在PJM需要完成後續工作，支持各州管理現有資料中心需求的努力、推動儲能設施的快速部署，並加速新能源專案併網。」

  ## 後續進程

  備援競標的設計目標是在PJM下一輪基礎容量競標（涵蓋2029/30供電年度）啟動前完成。PJM表示，預計最晚於12月2日公布備援競標結果，讓市場在後續採購展開之前，能對新增容量承諾有清晰的掌握。FERC對兩份申報文件的審查結果，將決定競標是否能依提案方式如期進行。
