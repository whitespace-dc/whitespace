body_en: |
  Australia's plan to impose what Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called the world's first national energy and environmental rules on data centers stalled Tuesday after Queensland and the Northern Territory declined to back the framework, leaving the policy without the unanimous state and territory support required to proceed.

  ## How the framework would work

  Albanese laid out the proposal in a policy speech earlier in July: new data centers would be required to add at least as much electricity generation to the grid as they consume. Operators would also be expected to build renewable generation, minimize water use, maximize energy efficiency, and fund any additional water infrastructure required. Bringing those obligations into a single national framework would be a global first, Albanese said, though he provided few details on how the policy would work in practice.

  Federal, state and territory energy ministers met virtually Tuesday under the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council to discuss the proposed controls. Under the policy's structure, all states and territories must endorse the plan for it to take effect nationally.

  ## Queensland and Northern Territory resistance

  Queensland had signaled its opposition before the meeting. Premier David Crisafulli stated publicly that the state does not support imposing renewable power requirements in order to remain an attractive market for investors. After Tuesday's gathering, Queensland Energy Minister David Janetzki said the state would support proposals that delivered affordable, sustainable power but would "not support underdeveloped ideas that hand increased power to Canberra at the expense of Queenslanders."

  The Northern Territory joined Queensland in opposing most measures, including the introduction of a national regulatory set, according to a government statement issued after the council meeting.

  The federal government said it would continue working with states, territories and market bodies on a detailed policy design, with the potential for introduction of more stringent local requirements. The ministers are scheduled to meet again in September.

  ## Scale of the sector at stake

  The standoff comes as Australia's data center industry, fueled by demand for artificial intelligence and hyperscale operators including Microsoft and Meta, is expanding sharply. Data center investment could reach A$150 billion (approximately US$105 billion) by 2030, according to Commonwealth Bank of Australia associate economist Lucinda Jerogin. Six gigawatts of potential capacity are planned, driven by appetite for hyperscale cloud and AI infrastructure, she said.

  Australia ranked second only to the United States in dollars invested in the sector in 2024, according to real estate group Knight Frank. In a June report, Bloomberg Intelligence analysts led by Matt Ingram identified Australia as one of Asia's top locations for data center construction, citing high renewable energy potential, a stable political environment, and strong connectivity to the rest of Asia via low-latency submarine cables. A surge in data center investment is also helping support the country's slowing economy.

  ## Public support, industry caution

  Despite state-level resistance, the proposed rules carry broad public backing. A YouGov survey commissioned by Australia's Climate Council found that 82% of respondents agreed new data centers should be required to pay for the renewable energy and storage infrastructure needed to meet their power demands.

  Rob Nicholls, a senior researcher at the University of Sydney's Centre for AI, Trust and Governance, said the policy cannot function without unanimous agreement from all jurisdictions. "The policy the PM has announced doesn't work unless there's buy-in from all the states and territories," Nicholls said. "Part of the reason you have a policy is to avoid a race to the bottom from the states."

  Data Centres Australia, the industry body whose members include Google, AirTrunk and Microsoft, said it supports the principle that new electricity demand should be backed by new supply, noting that many operators and customers already underwrite renewable energy. Chief Executive Officer Belinda Dennett said the group wants clarity on three specific implementation questions: whether the compliance obligation falls on the data center operator or the tenant, when that obligation takes effect, and whether energy usage is measured against the facility's actual electricity consumption or its nameplate capacity, meaning the maximum theoretical output.

  ## What the September meeting must resolve

  Australia is among a growing number of countries attempting to regulate artificial intelligence infrastructure without stifling investment while ensuring sufficient power supplies for energy-intensive data centers. The federal government and the jurisdictions that back the proposal face the task of winning over Queensland and the Northern Territory before the next ministerial meeting, scheduled for September, or accepting that the national uniformity the framework was designed to deliver will not be achievable in its current form.

  澳洲計畫對資料中心實施首相安東尼・阿爾巴尼斯所稱「全球首創」的全國能源與環境規範，但週二在昆士蘭州與北領地相繼拒絕背書後，政策陷入停滯，無法取得推行所需的所有州與領地一致支持。

  ## 框架運作方式

  阿爾巴尼斯在七月初的政策演說中概述這項提案：新建資料中心必須向電網新增至少等同其用電量的發電容量。業者還須興建再生能源設施、最小化用水量、提升能源效率，並自行負擔額外水利基礎設施所需費用。阿爾巴尼斯表示，將上述義務納入單一全國框架將是全球首例，但對於政策的實際執行細節，他並未多作說明。

  聯邦、各州及領地的能源部長週二透過「能源與氣候變遷部長理事會」召開視訊會議，討論這項管制提案。依據政策架構，所有州與領地必須一致背書，該框架才能在全國層面生效。

  ## 昆士蘭州與北領地的反對立場

  昆士蘭州在會議召開前便已表態反對。州長大衛・克里薩弗利公開聲明，該州不支持強制採用再生能源要求，以免有損對投資人的市場吸引力。會議結束後，昆士蘭州能源部長大衛・亞內茨基表示，該州支持能帶來「平價且永續電力」的方案，但「不支持將更多權力拱手讓給坎培拉、損害昆士蘭民眾利益的半生不熟想法。」

  北領地則在理事會會議結束後發表的政府聲明中，表明反對大多數措施，包括引入全國統一監管框架。

  聯邦政府表示，將持續與各州、領地及市場機構就詳細政策設計展開協商，並保留在地方層面引入更嚴格要求的可能。各部長預計將於九月再度會面。

  ## 產業規模與利益所在

  這場僵局發生之際，澳洲資料中心產業正在人工智慧需求以及微軟、Meta等超大規模業者的帶動下急速擴張。根據澳洲聯邦銀行副研究員盧辛達・傑羅金的估算，澳洲資料中心投資額到2030年可望達到1,500億澳幣（約合1,050億美元）。她指出，超大規模雲端與人工智慧基礎建設的強勁需求，已帶動高達六吉瓦（GW）的潛在產能規劃。

  根據房地產顧問公司萊坊（Knight Frank）的數據，2024年澳洲在資料中心領域的投資金額僅次於美國，居全球第二。彭博智庫分析師馬特・英格拉姆帶領的團隊在六月份的報告中，將澳洲列為亞洲最具吸引力的資料中心建設地點之一，理由包括：豐沛的再生能源潛力、穩定的政治環境，以及透過低延遲海底電纜與亞洲各地的緊密連通性。資料中心投資熱潮同時也為澳洲趨緩的整體經濟注入支撐力道。

  ## 民意支持，業界存疑

  儘管州層面阻力重重，這項擬議規範仍獲得廣泛民意支持。澳洲氣候委員會委託YouGov進行的調查顯示，82%的受訪者同意新建資料中心應自行負擔滿足用電需求所需的再生能源與儲能基礎設施費用。

  雪梨大學人工智慧、信任與治理研究中心資深研究員羅伯・尼科爾斯表示，若無所有轄區一致同意，政策便無法有效運作。「首相所宣布的政策，若未能獲得所有州與領地的支持，就難以落實，」尼科爾斯說。「制定這項政策的部分原因，正是為了防止各州之間競相降低標準的惡性競爭。」

  代表Google、AirTrunk、微軟等業者的業界組織澳洲資料中心協會（Data Centres Australia）表示，原則上支持新增電力需求應由新增供給來支撐，並指出許多業者與客戶已自行承購再生能源。執行長貝琳達・丹尼特表示，協會希望就三項具體執行問題獲得明確答覆：合規義務究竟落在資料中心業者還是承租方；義務何時生效；以及用電量的計算基準是設施實際耗電量，還是銘牌容量（即最大理論輸出值）。

  ## 九月會議待解難題

  澳洲是全球眾多嘗試監管人工智慧基礎建設國家之一，目標是在不扼殺投資的前提下，確保能源密集型資料中心擁有充足的電力供應。聯邦政府與支持提案的轄區，面臨在九月下次部長級會議前爭取昆士蘭州與北領地點頭的挑戰；若無法達成共識，就必須接受一個現實：這套原本旨在實現全國統一標準的框架，在現行形式下恐怕無法如願推行。
