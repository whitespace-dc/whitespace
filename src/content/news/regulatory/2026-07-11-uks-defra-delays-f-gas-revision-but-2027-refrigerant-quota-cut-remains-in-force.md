body_en: |
  The UK's Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (DEFRA) announced in May that it was delaying its proposed revision to Great Britain's hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) phase-down rules, but industry trade body FETA warned this week that the underlying reduction in refrigerant quota still takes effect on 1 January 2027 under existing legislation.

  Under Great Britain's current F-gas regulation — the rules governing the supply and use of fluorinated greenhouse gas refrigerants — the HFC quota is scheduled to fall from 31% of baseline levels to 24% on that date. That represents a 22.6% reduction in refrigerant availability even without any revised framework, according to FETA, a manufacturers' group serving the refrigeration, air conditioning, and heat pump sector.

  "The delayed phasedown decision may have postponed a major regulatory change, but the direction of travel remains unchanged," said Martyn Cooper, FETA's commercial manager.

  ## DEFRA's consultation and why it stalled

  DEFRA launched a consultation in November on a revised GB HFC phase-down. That consultation proposed cutting the quota from 31% of baseline to 16.2% from 1 January 2027 — a far steeper reduction than what current law already requires. The November consultation did not address wider issues such as product bans, servicing restrictions, or changes to training and certification requirements.

  The proposed timeline alarmed much of the supply chain. "For manufacturers, contractors, wholesalers and end users alike, such a sharp reduction raised concerns about supply chain resilience, refrigerant pricing, retrofit demand and the industry's ability to transition at the required pace," Cooper said.

  DEFRA announced in May that it required further work before any final decisions could be made. The department did not provide a revised timetable.

  ## Great Britain's divergence from EU and Northern Ireland rules

  While the European Union revised its F-gas regulation in 2024, Great Britain still operates under the original regulation — known as 517/2014 — and its original phase-down steps. Northern Ireland, under existing arrangements, continues to follow EU F-gas legislation, leaving businesses in England, Scotland, and Wales operating under a different and now-outdated framework.

  DEFRA published a review of the GB F-gas regulation in December 2022. Industry stakeholders then waited nearly three years for clarity on the next phase of the HFC phase-down strategy before the November consultation was launched.

  ## What FETA proposed as an alternative

  FETA argued for a more measured approach than the consultation proposed. The federation called for gentler quota reductions at both the 2027 and 2030 milestones, saying this would give industry more time to adapt while still delivering meaningful emissions reductions. FETA described its proposal as designed to align with DEFRA's longer-term trajectory while avoiding what it called a sudden supply shock in the late 2020s.

  ## The risk of misreading the delay

  FETA acknowledged that the delay provides breathing space in a market already managing skills shortages, rising heat pump deployment, and evolving sustainability requirements. However, it warned explicitly against treating the pause as a signal to slow transition planning.

  "There is a danger that the announcement could be interpreted as a pause in the transition. It is not," Cooper said. "DEFRA has been clear that the existing GB F-Gas Regulation remains fully in force."

  The body called on contractors and end users to continue prioritizing lower global warming potential (GWP) refrigerants where technically and economically feasible, on equipment manufacturers to sustain investment in future-proof product portfolios, and on wholesalers and service providers to prepare for tighter refrigerant availability and shifting customer demand.

  FETA also noted that F-gas legislation extends beyond quota allocations. Existing product bans and placing-on-the-market restrictions remain in force and will continue to shape technology choices across the sector.

  ## What the delay means for industry

  For businesses in England, Scotland, and Wales, the practical consequence is a market in which the next regulatory step is known but the step after that is not. The 22.6% reduction in HFC quota takes effect on 1 January 2027 under current law regardless of when DEFRA finalizes any revised phase-down framework. That reduction — from 31% of baseline to 24% — is less severe than the consultation's proposed cut to 16.2%, but FETA said it will still create supply chain challenges the market must prepare for.

  FETA said it is continuing to lobby parliament and is pushing for more detail and specific timelines from DEFRA on the revised phase-down framework.
body_zh: |
  英國環境、食品與農村事務部（DEFRA）於今年5月宣布延後推進大不列顛氫氟碳化物（HFC）逐步削減規則的修訂計畫，但業界製造商組織FETA本週警告，現行法規所規定的冷媒配額削減，仍將依既有立法於2027年1月1日起正式生效。

  根據大不列顛現行F-gas法規——即規範氟化溫室氣體冷媒供應與使用的相關規定——HFC配額預計將在屆時從基準量的31%降至24%。FETA指出，即便沒有任何修訂框架，此舉仍代表冷媒可用量將減少22.6%。FETA為服務冷凍、空調與熱泵產業的製造商公會。

  FETA商務經理馬丁·庫珀（Martyn Cooper）表示：「逐步削減決策的延後，或許推遲了一項重大法規變革，但政策方向並未改變。」

  ## DEFRA的諮詢過程及其停滯原因

  DEFRA於去年11月啟動了大不列顛HFC逐步削減修訂方案的公眾諮詢。該諮詢案提議自2027年1月1日起，將配額從基準量的31%大幅削減至16.2%——遠比現行法律所要求的更為嚴苛。11月的諮詢案並未觸及更廣泛的議題，例如產品禁令、維修限制，或培訓與認證要求的變更。

  提案時程令供應鏈各方大為憂慮。庫珀說：「對製造商、承包商、批發商和終端用戶而言，如此急劇的削減幅度引發了各界對供應鏈韌性、冷媒定價、改裝需求，以及業界能否跟上轉型步伐的疑慮。」

  DEFRA於5月宣布，在做出任何最終決定之前，仍需進行更多研究。該部門並未提供修訂後的時程表。

  ## 大不列顛與歐盟及北愛爾蘭法規的分歧

  歐盟已於2024年修訂其F-gas法規，但大不列顛目前仍沿用最初的法規——即517/2014號條例——及其原定的逐步削減步驟。北愛爾蘭依現行安排，持續適用歐盟的F-gas法規，導致英格蘭、蘇格蘭和威爾斯的企業須在一套不同且如今已顯落後的框架下運作。

  DEFRA於2022年12月發布了大不列顛F-gas法規的審查報告。此後業界利害關係人等待了將近三年，才等到去年11月諮詢案的啟動，以期釐清HFC逐步削減策略的下一階段方向。

  ## FETA提出的替代方案

  FETA主張採取比諮詢案所提議更為穩健的做法。該聯合會呼籲在2027年和2030年兩個里程碑上採取較為緩和的配額削減幅度，認為此舉可給予業界更多適應時間，同時仍能實現有意義的減排效果。FETA將其方案定位為既能與DEFRA的長期政策方向接軌，又能避免在2020年代後期引發其所稱的「突發性供應衝擊」。

  ## 誤讀延遲訊號的風險

  FETA承認，在市場已同時面臨技術人才短缺、熱泵部署規模攀升及永續發展要求持續演變等挑戰之際，此次延後確實提供了一定的喘息空間。然而，FETA明確警告，不應將這次暫緩視為放慢轉型規劃步調的信號。

  庫珀說：「此次公告存在被解讀為轉型暫停的風險，但事實並非如此。DEFRA已明確表示，現行大不列顛F-gas法規依然完全有效。」

  FETA呼籲承包商和終端用戶在技術與經濟條件可行的情況下，持續優先採用較低全球暖化潛勢（GWP）的冷媒；呼籲設備製造商持續投資具前瞻性的產品組合；並呼籲批發商與服務供應商為冷媒供應趨緊及客戶需求轉變預做準備。

  FETA亦指出，F-gas立法的範疇不僅限於配額分配。現行產品禁令及上市限制仍持續有效，並將持續影響整個產業的技術選型方向。

  ## 延遲對業界的實質意義

  對英格蘭、蘇格蘭和威爾斯的企業而言，實際情況是：下一個法規步驟已然明確，但之後的步驟仍是未知數。根據現行法律，HFC配額將於2027年1月1日起削減22.6%，無論DEFRA何時完成修訂後逐步削減框架的定案程序，此一削減均將生效。這項削減——從基準量的31%降至24%——雖不如諮詢案提議的削減至16.2%來得嚴峻，但FETA表示，業界仍須預先因應此舉所帶來的供應鏈挑戰。

  FETA表示，正持續遊說國會，並積極向DEFRA爭取提供修訂後逐步削減框架的更多細節及具體時程。
