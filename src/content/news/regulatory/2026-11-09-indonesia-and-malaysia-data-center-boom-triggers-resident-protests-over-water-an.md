body_en: |
  Residents of Batam, Indonesia have held protests at a data center cluster operated by several Singapore- and Hong Kong-based technology companies, demanding restoration of water supply after shortages left households unable to cook or bathe, as regional authorities move to impose new resource requirements on a rapidly expanding artificial intelligence infrastructure sector.

  ## Protests and the Nongsa Digital Park dispute

  The demonstrations targeted Nongsa Digital Park, a special economic zone on Batam island that hosts data centers operated by Singapore-headquartered BW Digital, DayOne, and Princeton Digital Group, as well as Hong Kong-based Gaw Capital Partners, according to AFP reporting. At one protest in October, residents arrived carrying buckets; police officers were observed filling them at the site.

  Residents in affected communities described going without water for extended periods. In the coastal village of Mata Ikan, people used seawater for sanitation. Government-distributed water trucked to communities was described by residents as "smelly" and "yellowish."

  The water shortages in surrounding communities were caused by a combination of drought linked to the El Niño climate cycle, which depleted the nearby Nongsa Reservoir, and an electrical fault that disrupted distribution, said Ginda Alamsyah, a spokesman for water utility operator Air Batam Hilir. The tech park itself draws from a separate source, the Duriangkang Reservoir, an industry source told AFP.

  Batam Mayor and BP Batam head Amsakar Achmad said in October that recent disruptions to water distribution "was not caused by data centres or AI or investors entering that sector," attributing the decline to "weather and climate factors." Neither BP Batam nor Indonesia's ministry for economic affairs, which regulates Nongsa Digital Park, responded to AFP requests for comment.

  ## Regulatory response

  In late September, Batam authorities directed data center developers to build seawater desalination plants to supply their own cooling water and avoid drawing further on municipal resources.

  Gaw Capital Partners acknowledged "the distress and frustration expressed by local residents in Nongsa and surrounding Batam communities." The company said its facilities deploy "closed-loop" water systems — which recirculate water internally rather than continuously drawing fresh supply — combined with "advanced engineering, efficient architectural designs and renewable energy sourcing."

  Air Batam Hilir said it supports investment policies but noted that water demand must be managed carefully. The utility is working to increase capacity across Batam's six reservoirs through dredging, with cloud seeding under consideration for dry periods.

  ## Regional expansion and resource pressure

  The Batam dispute reflects broader tensions across Southeast Asia as data center construction accelerates to meet demand from artificial intelligence workloads. Singapore restricted new data center development between 2019 and 2022 because of resource constraints, redirecting investment to neighboring Indonesia and Malaysia.

  Data center capacity in Southeast Asia is projected to triple from 2025 levels by 2030, driven by a tenfold increase in AI use, according to a report by consulting firm KPMG. That scale of growth puts pressure on both water supplies and electricity grids that remain heavily reliant on fossil fuels, AFP reported.

  In Malaysia's Johor state, which borders Singapore to the north, data center capacity has expanded from 10 megawatts (MW) roughly five years ago to 2.1 gigawatts (GW) today — a 200-fold increase — according to researcher James Chai of the ISEAS – Yusof Ishak Institute, who described Johor as "one of the largest emerging AI infrastructure clusters in the world." One gigawatt is roughly the output of a large power plant, giving a sense of the sector's scale in the state.

  By 2035, data centers are forecast to consume one-third of Malaysia's total national energy supply, Malaysia's deputy prime minister told Parliament in July. The government said at that time it would approve new data center projects only after verifying sufficient energy and water supply capacity.

  Johor residents have separately protested dust pollution and flash flooding linked to data center construction as developers build in closer proximity to residential areas. Mohd Fadhil Md Din of Malaysia's University of Technology described Johor as being at "a critical inflection point," saying it has the potential to become Southeast Asia's leading sustainable data center hub if resource management and spatial planning are handled carefully.

  ## Community opposition and one withdrawal

  Resident opposition has succeeded in at least one case. In Kota Damansara on the outskirts of Kuala Lumpur, a developer withdrew its planning permission application in August after neighbors organized online, lobbied their member of parliament, and collected more than 9,000 petition signatures opposing a proposed data center.

  Malaysia's Human Resources Minister Ramanan Ramakrishnan said following the withdrawal that large-scale projects "that could affect the residents' lives" require public input, while stating he was not opposed to development or investment broadly.

  ## What the disputes signal for the sector

  The conflicts in Batam and Johor illustrate the resource allocation pressures that regulators in Indonesia and Malaysia now face as AI infrastructure investment continues to scale. The KPMG projection of capacity tripling by 2030, combined with Malaysia's forecast of data centers consuming one-third of national electricity by 2035, points to intensifying competition for water and power before the regulatory frameworks governing those resources have fully adapted.

  Malaysia has said it will condition future data center approvals on verified supply adequacy; Batam has moved to require developers to source their own cooling water through desalination rather than draw on municipal systems.
body_zh: |
  印尼巴淡島（Batam）居民近日聚集在一處資料中心園區外舉行抗議，要求恢復正常供水——水荒已導致當地居民無法煮食、沐浴。與此同時，印尼與馬來西亞的地方主管機關也開始對快速擴張的人工智慧（AI）基礎建設產業施加新的資源管制。根據顧問業者KPMG的預測，東南亞資料中心容量預計在2030年前較2025年水準成長三倍。

  ## 抗議事件與農莎數位園區爭議

  此次示威行動矛頭指向農莎數位園區（Nongsa Digital Park）——位於巴淡島的一個特別經濟區，園區內設有多家科技公司的資料中心，包括總部位於新加坡的BW Digital、DayOne及Princeton Digital Group，以及香港的基滙資本（Gaw Capital Partners）。上述資訊均來自法新社的報導。今年10月，抗議居民手提水桶現身現場，警察也被目擊在該處協助居民裝水。

  受影響社區的居民描述，他們已連續多日無水可用。在沿海漁村瑪達伊坎（Mata Ikan），居民甚至以海水充當衛生用水。政府以水車配送到社區的用水，據居民形容「有異味」且「呈黃色」。

  供水業者Air Batam Hilir的發言人金達·阿拉姆夏（Ginda Alamsyah）表示，周邊社區缺水是多重因素交織所致：受聖嬰現象（El Niño）氣候週期影響的乾旱耗盡了附近的農莎水庫（Nongsa Reservoir），加上電力故障進一步造成供水系統中斷。一名業界消息人士向法新社透露，科技園區本身另有水源，取水自杜里安康水庫（Duriangkang Reservoir），與居民用水系統分開。

  巴淡市長暨巴淡工業局（BP Batam）首長安沙卡爾·阿赫馬德（Amsakar Achmad）10月表示，近期供水中斷「並非資料中心、AI或進駐投資者所造成」，他將問題歸因於「天候與氣候因素」。BP Batam及印尼負責監管農莎數位園區的經濟事務部，均未回應法新社的置評請求。

  ## 監管應對

  9月下旬，巴淡當局要求資料中心開發商自行興建海水淡化廠，以提供冷卻用水，避免繼續消耗市政供水資源。

  基滙資本對此回應，表示認識到「農莎及巴淡周邊社區居民所承受的困境與挫折感」。該公司指出，其設施採用「封閉式循環」水系統——在內部循環再利用用水，而非持續汲取新鮮水源——並結合「先進工程技術、高效能建築設計與再生能源應用」。

  Air Batam Hilir則表示支持投資政策，但強調必須審慎管理用水需求。該供水業者目前正透過疏浚作業提升巴淡島六座水庫的蓄水容量，乾旱期間亦考慮採用人工增雨技術。

  ## 區域擴張與資源壓力

  巴淡島的爭議折射出東南亞更廣泛的緊張態勢。隨著AI運算需求大幅增長，資料中心建設正在加速推進。新加坡曾因資源限制，於2019年至2022年間暫停核發新資料中心的開發許可，致使大量投資轉往鄰近的印尼與馬來西亞。

  根據KPMG的報告，受AI使用量十倍成長的驅動，東南亞資料中心容量預計在2030年前較2025年水準成長至三倍。如此規模的擴張，對供水及仍高度依賴化石燃料的電網均形成沉重壓力。

  在與新加坡接壤的馬來西亞柔佛州（Johor），資料中心容量從約五年前的10百萬瓦（MW）躍升至今日的21億瓦（2.1 GW），成長了200倍。這項數據來自尤索夫伊薩東南亞研究院（ISEAS–Yusof Ishak Institute）研究員詹姆士·柴（James Chai），他將柔佛形容為「全球規模最大的新興AI基礎建設聚落之一」。1 GW大致相當於一座大型發電廠的輸出功率，由此可見資料中心產業在該州的龐大規模。

  馬來西亞副總理今年7月在國會表示，預計到2035年，資料中心的能源消耗將達到全國總用電量的三分之一。政府當時亦宣布，未來僅在確認具備充足能源與水資源供應後，才會核准新的資料中心建案。

  柔佛州居民亦曾就資料中心建設引發的揚塵污染及閃洪問題發起抗議，起因是開發商在距離住宅區更近的地點大規模施工。馬來西亞理工大學的莫哈末·法迪爾·馬丁（Mohd Fadhil Md Din）形容柔佛正處於「關鍵轉捩點」，指出若能妥善管理資源並審慎規劃空間配置，柔佛有潛力躍升為東南亞首屈一指的永續資料中心樞紐。

  ## 居民反對與一次撤案

  居民抗議至少在一個案例中取得了成效。在吉隆坡近郊的哥打白沙羅（Kota Damansara），一名開發商在今年8月撤回了規劃許可申請，起因是當地居民在網路上組織動員、遊說選區國會議員，並徵集逾9,000份反對興建資料中心的請願連署。

  馬來西亞人力資源部長拉馬南·拉馬克里希南（Ramanan Ramakrishnan）在該案撤回後表示，「可能影響居民生活」的大型開發案需要廣徵民意，但他同時強調，本身並不反對發展或招商引資。

  ## 爭議對產業的啟示

  巴淡島與柔佛州的衝突，清楚呈現出印尼與馬來西亞監管機關在AI基礎建設投資持續擴大之際所面臨的資源分配壓力。KPMG預測2030年前容量將成長三倍，加上馬來西亞估算2035年資料中心將消耗全國三分之一電力，兩相對照，在水資源與能源管理相關法規尚未完備之前，水電資源的競爭只會日趨激烈。

  馬來西亞已表明，未來資料中心開發案的核准將以確認供應充足為前提；巴淡島則已要求開發商透過海水淡化自行解決冷卻用水需求，而非繼續仰賴市政供水系統。
