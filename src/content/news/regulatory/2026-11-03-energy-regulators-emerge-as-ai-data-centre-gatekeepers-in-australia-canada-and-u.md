body_en: |
  Energy regulators and central governments in Australia, Canada and the United Kingdom have become the decisive gatekeepers for large artificial intelligence data centre projects, as a series of high-profile approval setbacks establishes that permitting, not capital, now sets the pace of the sector's infrastructure build.

  ## Australia: a federal vacuum stalls the territory's largest project

  Energy North said it is no longer working to a June 2028 first-power date for Project Ares, a 1-gigawatt AI campus planned for a cattle station almost 700 kilometres from the nearest city in Australia's Northern Territory. The company told Reuters the date was "indicative" and said it would confirm timing once the Northern Territory Environment Protection Authority (NT EPA) sets terms for an environmental impact statement.

  The NT EPA suspended its own seven-week deadline for setting those assessment terms on 7 October, after determining it needed guidance from the federal government, which has not yet published its policy expectations for data centres. The regulator identified 10 potential impacts from the roughly 190-square-kilometre campus, including strain on water systems and disruption of Aboriginal sacred sites, and said it had low confidence that Energy North's proposed mitigation measures would manage significant impacts.

  Project Ares is designed as an off-grid campus with 1 gigawatt of IT load, backed by 3,000 megawatt-peak of solar generation, 16 gigawatt-hours of battery storage, and up to 1,038 megawatts of gas-fired generation to firm supply when solar output falls short. Energy North declined to identify its financiers.

  Reuters described the delay as the largest timing setback yet for an Australian data centre. It comes less than two weeks after NextDC lost almost 42% of its market value in a single trading session after community opposition halted grid connection works at its M3 campus in Melbourne. The Northern Territory government is reportedly in talks with 12 separate data centre-related projects.

  ## Alberta: the power plant clears, the data centre does not

  In Canada's Alberta province, the Alberta Utilities Commission (AUC), the provincial power regulator, approved a 920-megawatt natural gas plant on 23 October to supply Beacon Data Centers' planned Heartland hub in Sturgeon County, northeast of Edmonton. The approval covers the generation facility only, not the associated 800-megawatt data centre, although Sturgeon County has separately issued a master development permit for the wider project.

  AI company Anthropic is in talks to lease capacity at the Heartland campus, The Globe and Mail reported on 30 October, citing two sources. Neither company commented and no capacity, term or price was disclosed. If concluded, the deal would follow Anthropic's lease at EverWind's Point Tupper campus in Nova Scotia, its first in Canada, and its first Australian lease in Queensland.

  Sturgeon County already hosts Meta's 1-gigawatt, CAD 13-billion campus, announced in July. Opposition is growing elsewhere in Alberta: landowners near Beacon's planned Indus site, east of Calgary, have contested its gas plant plans there, and Samson Cree Nation, the lone intervenor in the Sturgeon County proceeding, had not decided its next step as of The Globe and Mail's reporting. In Ontario, the municipality of Oakville has imposed what the article describes as the province's first data centre moratorium.

  ## UK: grid access becomes the policy lever

  The United Kingdom has moved furthest in converting grid access into a direct policy instrument. The Data Centre Covenant, published alongside Chancellor John Healey's Budget on 28 October, establishes five tests that data centre operators must satisfy: energy costs, community investment, environmental standards, job creation and security.

  Strategic data centres that pass the tests can have grid capacity reserved or reallocated for them. The most exceptional projects may receive temporary on-site generation rights while awaiting a permanent grid connection. A Ministerial Control Centre, chaired by the minister for AI, will decide which sites qualify. The government has not yet defined what constitutes a strategic data centre.

  The cost of grid delays was visible before the covenant was published. Microsoft withdrew from technology company Nscale's planned campus at Loughton after being told grid power might not arrive until the early-to-mid 2030s. Critics have questioned the trade-off: Foxglove, a legal firm that challenges data centre planning applications in court, told The Observer that strategic sites would receive "a golden ticket to skip the grid connection queue," with nothing binding them to deliver the community benefits they promise.

  ## Risk begins to be priced

  As project timelines lengthen, the financial sector is formalising its exposure. In Singapore, a group of insurers, brokers and industry bodies is developing a regional data centre insurance facility with an initial capacity of US$200 million. Singapore's national development minister, Chee Hong Tat, disclosed the initiative on 2 November; backers include MSIG, Gallagher Re, Scor, Samsung Re and the Cybersecurity Association Singapore, with a memorandum of understanding due to be signed that week.

  The facility will cover construction, operations, supply chain, design and cyber risks. Minister Chee described existing cover for the sector as fragmented and cited estimates placing Asia-Pacific data centre capacity at about 32 gigawatts today, rising to 57 gigawatts by 2030. The Singapore effort follows US lenders that have begun pricing community opposition risk directly into financing terms for domestic projects.

  ## The shift and what it means

  Across three jurisdictions, the pattern is consistent: the decisive approval venue has moved up the regulatory stack. Local planning authorities are no longer the final word. In Australia, the absence of federal data centre policy has become the primary bottleneck. In Alberta, the provincial power regulator's timeline for approving generation assets determines when data centre construction can begin. In the UK, a ministerial panel will control access to constrained grid capacity.

  The financial consequences are measurable. NextDC's single-day 42% market-value loss demonstrated that community opposition can become a material financing risk. The Singapore insurance initiative formalises that exposure as a standalone product rather than a risk absorbed informally within broader construction policies.

  The next determinants in each market are: when the NT EPA sets terms for Project Ares' environmental impact statement, and whether Australian federal data centre policy arrives first; whether the UK government publishes a definition of a strategic data centre; and whether Anthropic's reported talks with Beacon result in a signed lease, alongside what step Samson Cree Nation takes in the Alberta proceeding.

  ## 澳洲：聯邦政策缺位拖住領地最大計畫

  Energy North表示，其計劃在澳洲北領地距最近城市約700公里的牧場興建的1吉瓦AI園區「阿瑞斯計畫」（Project Ares），已不再以2028年6月作為首次供電目標日期。該公司向路透社表示，此前的時間表僅屬「參考性質」，並稱待北領地環境保護局（NT EPA）確定環境影響聲明的評估條件後，才會公布正式時程。

  NT EPA於10月7日暫停其原訂七週的評估條件設定期限，原因在於該機構需要聯邦政府的政策指引，而聯邦政府迄今尚未公布有關資料中心的政策預期。監管機構就這座占地約190平方公里的園區列出10項潛在衝擊，包括對水資源系統的壓力，以及對原住民神聖遺址的干擾，並表示對Energy North所提緩解措施能否有效應對重大衝擊信心不足。

  阿瑞斯計畫規劃為一座離網AI資料中心園區，IT負載達1吉瓦，配備裝置容量3,000百萬峰瓦（MWp）的太陽能發電設施、16吉瓦時電池儲能系統，以及最高1,038百萬瓦的燃氣備援機組，以便在太陽能出力不足時維持穩定供電。Energy North拒絕透露其融資方身份。

  路透社將此次延誤描述為澳洲資料中心迄今最大的時程挫折。事件距NextDC因社區反對導致其墨爾本M3園區電網接入工程被迫停工、股價單日暴跌近42%，相距不到兩週。北領地政府據報正與多達12個資料中心相關計畫進行洽談。

  ## 亞伯達：電廠獲批，資料中心仍待審

  在加拿大亞伯達省，省級電力監管機構亞伯達公用事業委員會（AUC）於10月23日核准了一座920百萬瓦天然氣電廠，以供電給Beacon Data Centers計劃在埃德蒙頓東北方史特金縣（Sturgeon County）開發的Heartland樞紐。此次批准僅涵蓋發電設施本身，不包括配套的800百萬瓦資料中心，但史特金縣已就整體計畫另行核發了總體開發許可。

  AI公司Anthropic正就租用Heartland園區容量展開洽談——《環球郵報》於10月30日援引兩名知情人士的說法報導了這一消息。雙方均未置評，租用容量、合約期限及價格均未披露。若交易達成，這將繼Anthropic在新斯科細亞省EverWind公司Point Tupper園區簽訂加拿大首份租約，以及在昆士蘭簽訂澳洲首份租約之後，再添一筆重要協議。

  史特金縣已是Meta旗下一座1吉瓦、造價130億加元大型園區的所在地，該計畫於7月宣布。亞伯達其他地區的反對聲浪也在升溫：卡加利以東Beacon計劃中的Indus場址附近地主，已就當地的天然氣電廠計畫提出異議；薩姆森克里族（Samson Cree Nation）是史特金縣審議程序中唯一的介入方，截至《環球郵報》報導時，該族群仍未決定下一步行動。在安大略省，奧克維爾（Oakville）市已實施禁建令，據報這是全省首個針對資料中心的凍結措施。

  ## 英國：電網接入成為政策槓桿

  英國在將電網接入轉化為直接政策工具方面走在三國最前端。《資料中心公約》與財政大臣約翰．希利（John Healey）的預算案一同於10月28日發布，確立了資料中心業者必須滿足的五項審查標準：能源成本、社區投資、環境標準、就業創造與安全。

  通過審查的策略性資料中心可獲得電網容量的預留或重新分配。對於最具指標意義的計畫，政府更可在其等待永久電網接入期間，授予臨時場址自備電源的許可。由AI事務部長擔任主席的「部長級管控中心」（Ministerial Control Centre）將負責裁定哪些場址具備入選資格。目前政府尚未定義何謂「策略性資料中心」。

  電網延誤的代價在公約發布前便已顯現。微軟因獲告知電力供應可能要到2030年代初至中期才能就位，已從科技公司Nscale位於勞頓（Loughton）的計劃園區撤出。批評人士對此取捨提出質疑：專門在法院挑戰資料中心規劃申請的法律機構Foxglove告訴《觀察家報》，策略性場址將獲得「插隊電網接入的黃金門票」，且沒有任何具約束力的機制確保其兌現所承諾的社區效益。

  ## 風險開始被納入定價

  隨著計畫時程不斷拉長，金融業正式開始將相關風險納入定價框架。在新加坡，一批保險公司、再保險經紀商及業界組織正聯手籌建一個區域性資料中心保險機制，初始承保容量為2億美元。新加坡國家發展部長徐芳達（Chee Hong Tat）於11月2日公布此一倡議，參與方包括MSIG、嘉勒再保險（Gallagher Re）、法國再保險集團Scor、三星再保險（Samsung Re）及新加坡網路安全協會，相關諒解備忘錄預計於同週完成簽署。

  此一機制將承保建設、營運、供應鏈、設計及網路安全等各類風險。徐部長表示，目前業界既有保障分散且不完整，並引用估計數據指出，亞太地區資料中心容量目前約為32吉瓦，預計到2030年將增至57吉瓦。新加坡的舉措呼應了美國借貸方開始將社區反對風險直接計入國內計畫融資條款的趨勢。

  ## 格局轉變及其意涵

  縱觀三個司法管轄區，模式高度一致：最終核准場所已在監管層級上移。地方規劃主管機關不再是最後一道關卡。在澳洲，聯邦資料中心政策的缺位已成為首要瓶頸；在亞伯達，省級電力監管機構核准發電資產的時程，決定了資料中心何時才能開工興建；在英國，部長級審查委員會將掌控吃緊電網容量的接入資格。

  財務後果有跡可循。NextDC單日市值重挫42%，清楚說明社區反對可演變為重大融資風險。新加坡的保險機制，則將這種風險正式化為獨立保險產品，而非繼續以模糊方式隱含在廣泛的工程建設保險政策之中。

  各市場接下來的關鍵節點在於：北領地環保局何時確定阿瑞斯計畫的環境影響評估條件，以及澳洲聯邦資料中心政策能否搶先出爐；英國政府是否將發布「策略性資料中心」的明確定義；以及Anthropic與Beacon的傳聞洽談是否簽成正式租約，和薩姆森克里族在亞伯達審議程序中的下一步行動。
