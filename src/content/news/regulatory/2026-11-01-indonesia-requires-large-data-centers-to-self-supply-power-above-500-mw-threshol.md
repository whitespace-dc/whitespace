body_en: |
  Indonesia's state investment agency Danantara is requiring data center developers whose facilities demand more than 500 megawatts (MW) of electricity — roughly enough to power a small city — to arrange their own independent energy supply rather than drawing from the national grid, according to reporting by Kontan.

  The threshold of 500 MW is significant in the context of hyperscale data centers, which are large warehouse-scale facilities built to serve cloud computing and artificial intelligence workloads. A 500 MW data center is among the largest class of such facilities globally.

  ## Policy rationale and investment implications

  Danantara's requirement is designed to redirect the electricity burden of the largest data center projects away from state utility PLN (Perusahaan Listrik Negara), Indonesia's national electricity company, and onto the investors themselves. By doing so, the policy could drive new capital into power generation, including clean and renewable energy sources, as operators seek to meet their own supply obligations.

  However, the Indonesian Solar Energy Association (Asosiasi Energi Surya Indonesia) has warned that the requirement carries risks. The association is calling on the government to provide electricity procurement options that are flexible, transparent, and competitive, according to Kontan. Without those guardrails, the mandate to self-supply could add materially to project costs and erode Indonesia's competitiveness as a destination for data center investment.

  ## Competing pressures

  The policy sits at the intersection of two pressures facing Indonesian policymakers: the need to attract large-scale digital infrastructure investment, and the need to manage strain on the national power grid. Large data centers place concentrated, continuous electricity loads on grid infrastructure, and requiring self-supply at scale removes that burden from PLN.

  At the same time, requiring investors to build or procure their own generation capacity — particularly at the 500 MW scale — raises the capital requirements and complexity of entering the Indonesian market. The Indonesian Solar Energy Association's call for flexible and competitive options suggests the clean energy industry sees an opportunity in the policy but wants assurance that the procurement framework will not disadvantage particular technologies or lock investors into costly arrangements.

  ## What comes next

  The Indonesian Solar Energy Association is actively lobbying the government to design the self-supply framework with flexibility and transparency as core features, signaling that the details of implementation remain under discussion as of late October 2026.

  ## 印尼要求500MW以上大型資料中心業者自行籌備電力供應

  印尼國家投資機構丹南塔拉（Danantara）規定，凡是電力需求超過500百萬瓦（MW）的資料中心開發商——大約相當於一座小型城市的用電量——必須自行安排獨立能源供應，不得仰賴國家電網，印尼財經媒體《Kontan》對此有詳細報導。

  500MW這道門檻，在超大規模資料中心的語境下意義重大。超大規模資料中心是專為雲端運算與人工智慧工作負載而建的倉儲級巨型設施，500MW的資料中心在全球同類設施中已屬規模最大的一級。

  ## 政策立意與投資影響

  丹南塔拉此項規定的用意，是將規模最龐大的資料中心專案所帶來的用電壓力，從國家電力公司PLN（Perusahaan Listrik Negara）身上轉移到投資者本身。藉由這樣的安排，此政策有望引導新資本流入電力建設，包括潔淨能源與再生能源，因為業者必須自行承擔供電義務。

  然而，印尼太陽能協會（Asosiasi Energi Surya Indonesia）提出警示，認為此規定存在風險。該協會呼籲政府提供兼具彈性、透明度與競爭性的電力採購選項，《Kontan》亦有相關報導。若缺乏相應的配套保障，強制自供電力的規定可能大幅推高專案成本，進而侵蝕印尼在資料中心投資目的地市場上的競爭力。

  ## 兩股壓力的拉鋸

  此政策夾在印尼政策制定者所面臨的兩股壓力之間：一方面需要吸引大規模數位基礎設施投資，另一方面又必須管控國家電網所承受的負荷。大型資料中心會對電網基礎設施形成集中且持續的用電壓力，要求業者在超大規模層次自行供電，便可將這股壓力從PLN身上卸除。

  與此同時，要求投資者自行建設或採購發電容量——尤其是500MW這個量級——將提高進入印尼市場所需的資本門檻與複雜程度。印尼太陽能協會呼籲採購框架應具備彈性與競爭性，這意味著潔淨能源業界雖然看好此政策所帶來的機遇，但也希望確認採購制度不會讓特定技術處於不利地位，或迫使投資者陷入成本高昂的安排。

  ## 後續發展

  印尼太陽能協會正積極向政府遊說，要求將彈性與透明度列為自供電框架設計的核心原則。這顯示截至2026年10月下旬，執行細節仍在討論之中。
