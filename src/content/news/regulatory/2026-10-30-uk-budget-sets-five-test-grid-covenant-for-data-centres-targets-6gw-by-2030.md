body_en: |
  The UK government unveiled a Data Centre Covenant in its Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026, requiring operators seeking state help with grid connections to pass five tests, as ministers target a near-tripling of national AI data centre capacity from approximately 2 gigawatts (GW) to 6GW by 2030.

  The five tests are mandatory only for sites seeking a "strategic" designation, which unlocks the covenant's most substantial support: reserved or reallocated grid capacity and, in cases where other measures fall short, temporary on-site generation while a permanent connection is arranged. The government has not yet defined what qualifies a site as strategic. A new Ministerial Control Centre, chaired by the minister for AI, will make that determination. Strategic sites must also agree a Local Benefit Plan committing substantial investment in their host communities. All operators are encouraged to meet the tests voluntarily.

  ## Planning framework for large projects

  Alongside the covenant, the government published a draft National Policy Statement for Data Centres, open for consultation until 8 January 2027. The statement covers large projects — indicatively those with at least 100 megawatts (MW) of IT load — seeking development consent through the Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project (NSIP) regime, which applies to infrastructure too large or complex for standard local planning.

  The Budget's AI and cybersecurity package totals more than £700 million, including £140 million in additional funding for the Sovereign AI Unit and £60 million for Sovereign AI's Research and Development Procurement Scheme. The first-year capital allowance for qualifying investment rises from 40% to 50%, reducing the upfront tax cost of new builds.

  Healey, framing the measures as a sovereignty question, told MPs that "nowhere is the necessary role for the state clearer than in AI."

  ## A queue that grows faster than the covenant can clear it

  The covenant's practical utility depends on a grid connection problem it does not directly solve. Ofgem, the UK energy regulator, has said the connection queue grew 460% in the first half of 2025, with data centres accounting for at least 73GW of pipeline applications. At a Capacity Media roundtable in London, 70% of infrastructure leaders said they expect connections to take more than 18 months.

  The consequences are already visible. Earlier in October, Microsoft withdrew from Nscale's planned Loughton site — once described as the UK's largest sovereign AI data centre — after the site was told grid power might not arrive until the early-to-mid 2030s.

  The covenant's core levers — reserving future capacity, reallocating connections freed by others, and transferring pre-2030 contracts — primarily move designated sites higher in the existing queue rather than shortening it overall, according to Capacity Media's analysis. The on-site generation provision could bridge some waiting time in the interim.

  Peter Griffiths, founder and executive chairman of Argyll Data Development, called the covenant "potentially the more significant announcement" in the Budget, ranking it ahead of the additional £60 million for the Sovereign AI Fund. "Funding British AI companies achieves little if the infrastructure they depend on cannot get connected to the grid," he said. Griffiths argued that Scotland's renewable resources make it a natural place to integrate generation, storage and AI compute, rather than "treating every new data centre as another demand on an already constrained grid." Scotland has separately tightened oversight: councils must now notify ministers of any data centre application exceeding 50MW, and such projects require an environmental impact assessment.

  ## Industry welcomes direction, questions the gaps

  TechUK said the covenant recognises domestic data centre capacity as central to economic growth, sovereign compute and national resilience, and that the new grid powers could remove long-standing investment barriers. The trade body flagged two concerns: "strategic data centre" remains undefined, and the UK remains uncompetitive on energy affordability, risking the loss of digital infrastructure investment to other markets.

  Mark Boost, chief executive of UK cloud provider Civo, said the government had "set out the right principles" on procurement and backing British small and medium-sized enterprises, but that "the real test is yet to come." UK public cloud spending "has defaulted to US hyperscalers for years," he said, warning the plans would go awry "if its procurement bravery fails to match its rhetorical ambition." Civo is developing sovereign AI models with Locai Labs under a project called Project Mercury.

  ## Digital connectivity as a missing dimension

  Lee Myall, chief executive of Neos Networks, welcomed the covenant's grid focus but said the same joined-up thinking must extend to digital connectivity. Neos Networks research found that connectivity constraints have forced 38% of large UK businesses to keep AI workloads more centralised than planned, while 34% have moved workloads to another region entirely. "Digital connectivity must be considered alongside power, planning and other infrastructure priorities," Myall said.

  ## Cybersecurity funding follows confirmed government outage

  The Budget's cyber allocation arrives after the government confirmed on 22 October that 32 services had been taken offline while PHP2Shell security patches were applied, though ministers said they were not aware of any confirmed compromise of central government systems. Michael Vallas, global technical principal at Goldilock Secure, said success should be measured by "how much of the country is resilient," not only by the number of attacks prevented.

  ## What determines the covenant's impact

  For developers, the covenant's value hinges on two questions the Budget left open: how many sites the government classifies as strategic, and how quickly reserved capacity reaches those sites once classified. The National Policy Statement consultation closes on 8 January 2027.
body_zh: |
  英國政府於2026年10月28日在秋季預算案中發布《資料中心承諾》，要求尋求國家協助電網接入的業者通過五項測試，部長們同時訂下目標，要在2030年前將全國AI資料中心容量從約2吉瓦（GW）近乎三倍擴增至6GW。

  五項測試僅對尋求「策略性」認定的場址具強制效力；取得此認定後，業者可解鎖承諾中最實質的支援：保留或重新分配的電網容量，以及在其他措施不足時，於等待永久接入期間取得臨時現場發電設施。政府目前尚未定義何種場址符合策略性認定資格，新設立、由AI部長主持的「部長級管控中心」將負責作出裁定。策略性場址還須簽署《地方利益計畫》，承諾對所在社區進行重大投資。政府鼓勵所有業者自願達到這五項測試標準。

  ## 大型專案規劃框架

  政府在發布承諾的同時，也公告了《資料中心國家政策聲明》草案，開放諮詢至2027年1月8日。該聲明適用於大型專案——指示性門檻為IT負載至少達100百萬瓦（MW）——這些專案須透過《國家重要基礎設施專案》（NSIP）機制申請開發許可；NSIP機制適用於規模或複雜程度超出一般地方規劃範疇的基礎設施。

  預算案的AI與網路安全配套總額超過7億英鎊，其中包括給「主權AI單位」的1.4億英鎊額外撥款，以及主權AI研發採購計畫的6,000萬英鎊。符合資格的投資之首年資本扣除率從40%提升至50%，降低新建設施的前期稅務成本。

  希利在向國會議員闡述相關措施時，將其定性為主權問題，表示「在任何領域，國家所扮演的必要角色都不如AI來得清晰明確。」

  ## 排隊速度超越承諾的消化能力

  承諾的實際效用，取決於一個它並未直接解決的電網接入問題。英國能源監管機構Ofgem表示，2025年上半年電網接入申請排隊量暴增460%，其中資料中心的申請案便佔了至少73GW。在Capacity Media於倫敦舉辦的圓桌論壇上，70%的基礎設施主管表示，他們預計接入等待時間將超過18個月。

  後果已顯而易見。10月初，微軟宣布退出Nscale計畫在洛頓（Loughton）興建的場址——這個園區曾被譽為英國最大的主權AI資料中心——起因是該場址被告知電網供電可能要到2030年代初至中期才能到位。

  根據Capacity Media的分析，承諾的核心機制——保留未來容量、重新分配他人釋出的接入名額，以及移轉2030年前的合約——主要是讓獲指定場址在現有排隊中向前推進，而非整體縮短等待時間。現場發電條款或可在過渡期間彌補部分等待缺口。

  Argyll Data Development創辦人暨執行董事長彼得·格里菲斯（Peter Griffiths）稱承諾「可能是」預算案中「更具重要意義的公告」，其重要性甚至超過主權AI基金的額外6,000萬英鎊撥款。他表示：「如果英國AI企業所依賴的基礎設施無法接入電網，那麼資助這些企業也無濟於事。」格里菲斯主張，蘇格蘭的再生能源資源使其成為整合發電、儲能與AI運算的天然場所，而非「把每個新資料中心都視為對本已吃緊的電網再添一筆需求」。蘇格蘭已另行加強監管：地方議會現須向部長通報任何超過50MW的資料中心申請案，且此類專案須進行環境影響評估。

  ## 產業肯定方向，質疑遺漏之處

  TechUK表示，承諾認可了國內資料中心容量在經濟成長、主權運算及國家韌性方面的核心地位，新的電網授權或可消除長期以來阻礙投資的障礙。該業界團體同時提出兩點疑慮：「策略性資料中心」定義仍屬空白，且英國在能源可負擔性方面競爭力不足，恐使數位基礎設施投資流向其他市場。

  英國雲端服務業者Civo執行長馬克·布斯特（Mark Boost）表示，政府在採購政策及扶持英國中小企業方面「確立了正確原則」，但「真正的考驗尚未到來」。他說，英國公共雲端支出「多年來一直依賴美國超大規模業者」，並警告若「採購行動的勇氣無法與口頭上的雄心壯志相稱」，這些計畫恐將落空。Civo目前正與Locai Labs合作，在一項名為「水星計畫」（Project Mercury）的專案下開發主權AI模型。

  ## 數位連線：被遺漏的面向

  Neos Networks執行長李·麥奧（Lee Myall）對承諾聚焦電網一事表示歡迎，但指出同樣整體性的思維也必須延伸至數位連線。Neos Networks研究發現，連線限制已迫使38%的英國大型企業將AI工作負載的集中程度維持在高於原定計畫的水準，另有34%的企業已將工作負載完全遷移至其他地區。麥奧表示：「數位連線必須與電力、規劃及其他基礎設施優先事項一併納入考量。」

  ## 網路安全資金繼政府確認服務中斷後到位

  預算案的網路安全撥款，恰在政府於10月22日確認32項服務被迫下線後到位——當時相關服務正在套用PHP2Shell安全修補程式；不過部長表示，尚未掌握中央政府系統遭確認入侵的跡象。Goldilock Secure全球技術主任邁可·瓦拉斯（Michael Vallas）表示，成效應以「全國有多少比例具備韌性」來衡量，而非僅以阻止攻擊的次數論斷。

  ## 決定承諾影響力的關鍵

  對開發商而言，承諾的價值取決於預算案留下的兩個未解問題：政府將把多少場址認定為策略性場址，以及獲得認定後，保留容量能多快送達這些場址。《國家政策聲明》的諮詢期將於2027年1月8日截止。
