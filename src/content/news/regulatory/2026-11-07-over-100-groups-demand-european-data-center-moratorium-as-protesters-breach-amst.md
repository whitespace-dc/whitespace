body_en: |
  More than 100 activist groups launched two days of coordinated protests across Europe on November 6, calling for an immediate moratorium on new large-scale data center construction, days after US voters sided against data center development in 40 of 41 local ballot measures.

  The most visible action occurred in Amsterdam, where demonstrators broke into the construction site of a Microsoft data center being built by Pure Data Centres in the city's Sloterdijk district, in the western port area. Pure Data Centres plans to invest more than $1 billion in the facility and is backed by Brookfield-owned Oaktree. Amsterdam's mayor had banned the demonstration before it began. Riot police made several arrests. A Reuters witness counted roughly 100 people on the site; organizer Geef Tegengas told local broadcaster AT5 the figure was closer to 500. Protesters climbed cranes, hung balloons, and carried placards reading "Screw your datacentre."

  Geef Tegengas is not a formally recognized member of the Stop Dirty Data Centres coalition that organized the broader campaign, though both protests took place simultaneously, according to Data Center Dynamics. Pure Data Centres said it was committed to engaging local stakeholders and asked those on site to leave safely. Microsoft said it understood people had strong feelings about the project and respected their right to express them peacefully, but did not condone unlawful actions.

  ## Scope of the Stop Dirty Data Centres campaign

  The Stop Dirty Data Centres coalition, backed by more than 100 groups, describes its goal as a "temporary pause until social and environmental concerns are addressed" on large-scale data center development across Europe. The group says the moratorium would "force robust consideration of the harms of this infrastructure and create space for public debate about what kind of digital infrastructure society actually needs."

  Actions on November 6 and 7 were planned across 15 to 16 countries, with a march in Dublin, a protest outside the Polish parliament in Warsaw, a demonstration at a Microsoft data center in Denmark, events in east London including a walking tour of data centers in Canary Wharf, and a protest walk along the Manzanares River in Madrid. A march to the Scottish Parliament in Edinburgh is planned for November 14. Coalition coordinator Owen Espley said local campaigns were demonstrating they could "push back against the race to build data centres."

  Campaigners argue data centers strain electricity and water supplies, compete with housing for scarce land, and endanger climate commitments. Research by Germany's Öko-Institut, published for Greenpeace, projects that emissions from AI data centers could reach six times their 2023 level by 2030.

  ## US midterms as catalyst

  The European protests follow US midterm elections on November 4, in which voters in 10 states decided 41 local data center ballot measures, the largest number of such votes on record. Voters sided against data centers in every measure except one, in Harwood, North Dakota. Ohio alone accounted for 21 of the measures. The newly elected Democratic governors of Georgia, Ohio, and Texas all pledged moratoriums or tougher restrictions on new data center construction.

  Local opposition had already blocked or delayed at least 75 US data center projects worth nearly $130 billion in the first quarter of 2026, according to Capacity.

  ## Market impact

  Technology stocks fell sharply following the election results. On November 5, Nvidia closed down 9.3 percent and CoreWeave fell roughly 10 percent as the Nasdaq entered correction territory — a decline of at least 10 percent from a recent peak. A second day of losses followed on November 6 amid concern that Democratic control of Congress could bring tougher regulation of the AI sector.

  ## European restrictions already in place

  Europe has constrained data center development primarily through grid operators and planning rules rather than local ballot measures. StackPath counts 22 live or proposed restrictions across 10 European countries, led by the Netherlands and Germany with five each. No country has an outright nationwide ban.

  In the Netherlands — which has about 200 data centers planned, under construction, or in operation — a nationwide ban on new hyperscale facilities outside designated exemption areas has applied since January 1, 2024. The ban covers facilities above 70 megawatts (MW) and 10 hectares. At least seven hyperscale projects are proceeding because their permits predate the restriction, including the Pure Data Centres site in Sloterdijk and a facility in Lelystad. Plans for the Amsterdam project were filed in 2016, before the city banned new data centers within its boundaries, and the provincial government allowed it to proceed in January.

  In Ireland, data centers consumed 23 percent of the country's metered electricity in 2025, according to the Central Statistics Office. A de facto moratorium on connecting new data centers to the electricity grid ended in December 2025 and was replaced with strict criteria: any facility seeking more than 10 megavolt-amperes (MVA) of grid capacity must provide on-site dispatchable generation or storage matching its full demand. EirGrid, the grid operator, classified the greater Dublin region as fully constrained for new data center connections in May 2026. A judicial review is currently seeking to overturn those connection rules on climate-law grounds.

  A Google project in Finland faces a criminal probe over land clearances. Italy and Portugal are tightening environmental regulation for data centers.

  ## UK grid pressure and financing shifts

  In the United Kingdom, the energy regulator Ofgem estimates that 315 data center projects are in the national grid connection queue, representing 73 gigawatts (GW) of aggregate demand. UK peak electricity demand stood at 45GW in 2025, meaning the queued projects together represent more than one and a half times the country's current peak load.

  One project drawing particular scrutiny is a planned 1.5GW AI data campus proposed by Xlinks near Great Torrington in North Devon on roughly 344 hectares inside a UNESCO biosphere reserve, alongside a 1.8GW battery storage site. The project has been reported at up to £13.8 billion, and Xlinks says it would create 650 to 1,200 permanent jobs. Microsoft separately pulled out of a different UK data center project that was hit by infrastructure delays.

  The Bank of England has asked market participants about data center projects financed without a tenant in place. The Bayes UK commercial real estate lending report from October 2026 found that four lenders had stopped lending to data centers entirely.

  ## EU policy divide

  The European Commission has described expanding AI infrastructure as essential to Europe's technological sovereignty. EU technology chief Henna Virkkunen said on November 5 that Europe needed more data centers to power AI development, adding: "It's very important for citizens that they can trust that their energy bills are not skyrocketing and there's not electricity blackouts because of data centres, or they are not destroying the environment with these projects."

  European Parliament members are divided. French MEP Leïla Chaibi and Irish MEP Lynn Boylan, both of The Left group, have backed an EU-wide moratorium on large-scale builds. Portuguese Renew Europe MEP João Cotrim de Figueiredo strongly opposes a blanket moratorium, arguing Europe needs more data centers to maintain competitiveness in AI. Technology companies argue that data centers are essential to meeting global demand for AI and cloud computing and provide critical infrastructure for businesses, governments, and consumers.

  The judicial review challenging Ireland's grid connection rules on climate grounds and the Stop Dirty Data Centres march to the Scottish Parliament scheduled for November 14 represent the next near-term legal and political pressure points for the moratorium campaign.

  逾百個倡議團體於11月6日在全歐洲展開為期兩天的協調示威，要求立即對大型資料中心新建計畫實施暫停令。就在此前數日，美國選民於41項地方資料中心相關投票議案中，有40項投票反對資料中心開發，創下此類投票有史以來最多紀錄。

  此次行動中最受矚目的一幕發生在阿姆斯特丹：示威者強行闖入微軟委託Pure Data Centres在該市西部港區斯洛特戴克（Sloterdijk）興建的資料中心工地。Pure Data Centres計畫在此投入逾10億美元，背後金主為布魯克菲爾德（Brookfield）旗下的橡樹資本（Oaktree）。阿姆斯特丹市長事前已下令禁止本次示威，鎮暴警察隨後逮捕多名人士。路透社記者現場目測約有100人進入工地，組織方「Geef Tegengas」則向當地廣播電視台AT5表示，實際人數接近500人。抗議者攀上起重機、懸掛氣球，並高舉寫有「去你的資料中心」（Screw your datacentre）的標語。

  根據《資料中心動態》（Data Center Dynamics）報導，Geef Tegengas並非籌辦此次大規模行動的「停止骯髒資料中心」（Stop Dirty Data Centres）聯盟之正式成員，但兩場抗議係同步進行。Pure Data Centres表示，公司致力於與地方利害關係人溝通，並請求在場人員安全離開。微軟則表示，理解民眾對此計畫懷有強烈情緒，尊重其以和平方式表達意見的權利，但不認可任何違法行為。

  ## 「停止骯髒資料中心」活動規模

  由逾百個團體共同支持的「停止骯髒資料中心」聯盟，將其目標定位為在解決社會與環境疑慮之前，對歐洲全境大型資料中心開發實施「暫時叫停」。該聯盟表示，此暫停令將「迫使各方認真審視這類基礎設施的危害，並為公眾辯論數位社會真正需要何種基礎設施創造空間」。

  11月6日及7日的行動預計橫跨15至16個國家，包括：都柏林的遊行示威、波蘭國會外的抗議、丹麥微軟資料中心前的示威、東倫敦金絲雀碼頭資料中心徒步導覽，以及沿馬德里曼薩納雷斯河（Manzanares River）的示威遊行。另有一場前往蘇格蘭議會的遊行預定於11月14日在愛丁堡舉行。聯盟協調人歐文·埃斯普利（Owen Espley）表示，各地方行動正在證明，民間有能力「抵抗資料中心的瘋狂建設競賽」。

  倡議者主張，資料中心嚴重消耗電力與水資源，與住宅開發競逐有限土地，並對氣候承諾構成威脅。德國生態研究所（Öko-Institut）為綠色和平（Greenpeace）所做的研究預測，AI資料中心的碳排放量到2030年可能攀升至2023年水準的六倍。

  ## 美國期中選舉的催化效應

  此次歐洲抗議潮源起於11月4日的美國期中選舉。10個州共計41項地方資料中心投票議案——創下此類公投有史以來最多紀錄——選民僅對北達科他州哈伍德（Harwood）一項投下支持票，其餘40項全數遭到否決。俄亥俄州一地便佔了21項。喬治亞州、俄亥俄州及德克薩斯州的新任民主黨州長，均承諾對新資料中心建設實施暫停令或採取更嚴格管制。

  根據Capacity的資料，在2026年第一季，地方反對聲浪已阻擋或延遲了美國至少75個資料中心計畫，涉及金額近1,300億美元。

  ## 市場衝擊

  選舉結果公布後，科技股大幅下挫。11月5日，輝達（Nvidia）收盤下跌9.3%，CoreWeave跌幅約10%，那斯達克指數跌入修正區間（即較近期高點下跌至少10%）。11月6日再度出現第二波跌勢，市場憂慮民主黨掌控國會後可能對AI產業祭出更嚴格管制。

  ## 歐洲現行限制措施

  歐洲對資料中心開發的限制，主要透過電網業者及規劃法規落實，而非依賴地方公投。StackPath統計，目前歐洲10個國家共有22項已生效或規劃中的限制措施，以荷蘭和德國各5項並列最多。目前尚無任何國家實施全國性全面禁令。

  荷蘭目前約有200座資料中心正在規劃、興建或已運營。自2024年1月1日起，全國已對指定豁免區以外的新型超大規模設施實施禁令，適用對象為容量逾70百萬瓦（MW）、占地逾10公頃的設施。至少七項超大規模計畫因許可證早於禁令取得，仍可繼續推進，包括Pure Data Centres在斯洛特戴克的工地，以及萊利斯塔德（Lelystad）的一處設施。阿姆斯特丹這項計畫早在2016年便已提出申請，彼時市政府尚未宣布禁止新建資料中心，省級政府並於今年一月批准其繼續推進。

  在愛爾蘭，根據中央統計局資料，資料中心於2025年消耗了全國計量用電量的23%。允許新資料中心接入電網的事實上暫停令已於2025年12月終止，取而代之的是嚴格新規：任何申請逾10百萬伏安（MVA）電網容量的設施，必須備有等同其全部需求的現場可調度發電或儲能設備。電網業者EirGrid已於2026年5月將大都柏林地區列為新資料中心接入的全面受限區域。目前有一項司法審查，正以氣候法律為由，試圖推翻上述接入規定。

  谷歌（Google）在芬蘭的一項計畫，因土地清整問題而面臨刑事調查。義大利與葡萄牙也正在加強對資料中心的環境管制。

  ## 英國電網壓力與融資轉變

  在英國，能源監管機構Ofgem估計，目前國家電網接入申請隊列中有315個資料中心計畫，合計需求達73吉瓦（GW）。英國2025年的用電尖峰需求為45GW，意味著排隊計畫的總需求已超過英國現有尖峰負載的1.5倍以上。

  其中一個備受矚目的案例，是Xlinks公司計畫在北德文郡大托靈頓（Great Torrington）附近約344公頃的土地上，興建一座1.5GW的AI資料園區，該地點位於聯合國教科文組織（UNESCO）生物圈保護區內，並配套規劃1.8GW的電池儲能設施。據報導，該計畫投資額高達138億英鎊，Xlinks表示將創造650至1,200個永久職位。微軟則另行退出了一項因基礎設施延宕而受阻的不同英國資料中心計畫。

  英格蘭銀行（Bank of England）已就尚未確定租戶即進行融資的資料中心計畫，向市場參與者展開詢問。貝葉斯（Bayes）2026年10月發布的英國商業不動產貸款報告發現，已有四家貸款機構全面停止對資料中心提供貸款。

  ## 歐盟政策分歧

  歐盟執委會（European Commission）將擴大AI基礎設施列為歐洲科技自主的關鍵要素。歐盟科技事務主管韓娜·維爾庫寧（Henna Virkkunen）於11月5日表示，歐洲需要更多資料中心來推動AI發展，並補充道：「對公民而言，至關重要的是，他們能夠相信自己的能源帳單不會因為資料中心而飆漲、不會出現停電，這些計畫也不會破壞環境。」

  歐洲議會議員之間意見分歧。法國籍議員雷拉·柴比（Leïla Chaibi）與愛爾蘭籍議員琳恩·博伊蘭（Lynn Boylan），同屬左翼黨團，均支持在歐盟層面對大型資料中心建設實施暫停令。來自葡萄牙的復興歐洲（Renew Europe）黨團議員若昂·科特里姆·德菲格雷多（João Cotrim de Figueiredo）則強烈反對全面暫停令，主張歐洲若欲在AI領域維持競爭力，便需要更多資料中心。科技企業則主張，資料中心對於滿足全球AI與雲端運算需求不可或缺，是企業、政府與消費者的關鍵基礎設施。

  挑戰愛爾蘭電網接入規定的司法審查，以及預定於11月14日前往蘇格蘭議會的示威遊行，是暫停令運動在近期最具代表性的法律與政治施壓節點。
