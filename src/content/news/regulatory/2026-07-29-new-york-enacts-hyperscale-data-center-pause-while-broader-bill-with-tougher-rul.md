body_en: |
  New York Gov. Kathy Hochul on July 14 signed what law firm DLA Piper describes as the first state moratorium on new hyperscale data centers in the country, issuing an executive order that pauses state environmental permits for facilities capable of consuming 50 megawatts or more of electricity for up to one year. The order buys time for policymakers to build out a regulatory framework, but a broader piece of legislation already passed by the state legislature — one that would impose requirements on existing facilities, not just new ones — remains unsigned.

  The scale of demand growth driving the action is substantial. The New York Independent System Operator (NYISO), the state's electricity grid operator, saw its large-load interconnection queue — the pipeline of projects seeking to connect to the grid — grow from six projects totaling roughly 1,045 megawatts in 2022 to 48 proposals totaling approximately 12 gigawatts as of December 31, according to an analysis by law firm Harris Beach Murtha. One gigawatt is roughly the output of a large power plant.

  ## What the executive order does

  The executive order pause will remain in effect until the New York Department of Public Service completes a Generic Environmental Impact Statement assessing the potential environmental impacts of data center construction and operation in the state, including effects on energy demand, water use and quality, air quality, and noise levels, according to the state.

  Although the order is directed at future data centers, it could affect projects already moving through the permitting pipeline, according to an analysis by law firm Davis Wright Tremaine. Projects with approval pending from the state's Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) as of July 14 could be impacted, while those proceeding solely through local permitting processes that do not require DEC approval would not be affected.

  "For companies developing, financing, or operating data centers, the Executive Order serves as a reminder that successful projects increasingly require coordination across land use, environmental permitting, energy, technology transactions, commercial contracting, financing, and regulatory compliance," Davis Wright Tremaine's analysis states.

  ## The unsigned bill and its wider reach

  Hochul issued the executive order to give the government time to work through the Responsible Data Center Development Act, designated A11560, which the state legislature passed on June 4 but which the governor has not yet signed. The key difference between the bill and the executive order is scope, according to DLA Piper.

  The executive order applies a single 50-MW threshold, while the bill uses lower, variable peak-load thresholds of 1, 5, and 20 megawatts — bringing far more facilities within its reach. The two instruments also differ on how they handle existing projects. The bill carves out modifications, renewals, reissuances, and recertifications of existing approvals, as well as projects that commenced construction on or before its effective date. The executive order contains no such grandfathering mechanism; its applicability turns on DEC permitting status, not construction commencement, according to DLA Piper.

  ## Requirements under A11560

  If signed, A11560 would impose a series of obligations on data center operators, according to analyses by Harris Beach Murtha and Davis Polk.

  Data centers with a peak load of at least 5 MW would be required to procure at least one-third of their electricity from renewable sources by 2030, with higher percentages required in later years. They would also need to meet energy-efficiency goals to be developed by the New York State Energy Research and Development Authority (NYSERDA).

  Large new data centers and any existing facility adding 20 MW or more of load would be required to fund a host-community benefits program covering residential energy upgrades, community infrastructure, and measures to mitigate water and wastewater impacts, according to Harris Beach Murtha.

  The bill could also affect operators using water-intensive cooling designs, according to Davis Polk. The DEC would be required to assess whether new or amended regulations are needed for water withdrawal programs as applied to data centers, with a report due within 12 months.

  ## Local government role

  Under A11560, the Empire State Development Fund — a state agency that provides grants and loans to projects — would have 60 days from enactment to publish a framework giving localities more say in data center siting decisions. That framework would address the creation of a community investment fund, funded by data center companies, and would include direction on local infrastructure investments, prevailing wage and project labor agreements, and reporting on economic metrics, according to a Davis Polk analysis.

  ## What comes next

  The executive order and the unsigned bill together represent two distinct regulatory tracks with different thresholds, grandfathering rules, and scopes. The moratorium under the executive order will lift only once the Department of Public Service finishes its Generic Environmental Impact Statement process; the timeline for that review has not been set. A11560 awaits Hochul's decision on whether to sign it into law.
body_zh: |
  紐約州長凱西・霍楚爾於7月14日簽署了一道行政命令，律師事務所DLA Piper將其形容為全美首例針對新建超大型資料中心的州級暫停令。該命令對耗電量達50百萬瓦（MW）以上的設施暫停發放州環境許可，期限最長一年。此舉旨在為政策制定者爭取時間、建立完整的監管框架。然而，州議會已通過一項覆蓋範圍更廣的法案——不只針對新建設施，也涵蓋現有設施——目前仍等待霍楚爾州長簽署。

  促成此次行動的需求成長規模相當可觀。根據律師事務所Harris Beach Murtha的分析，紐約州電網運營商「紐約獨立系統運營商」（NYISO）的大型用電併網申請佇列——即等待接入電網的專案管線——從2022年的6個專案、總計約1,045 MW，暴增至截至去年12月31日的48個提案、總計約12吉瓦（GW）。1吉瓦大致相當於一座大型發電廠的輸出功率。

  ## 行政命令的內容

  根據紐約州官方說明，此次行政命令的暫停期將持續至紐約州公共服務部完成「通用環境影響評估說明」（Generic Environmental Impact Statement）為止。該評估將針對資料中心在紐約州的建設與營運可能產生的環境影響進行全面審查，涵蓋對能源需求、用水量及水質、空氣品質以及噪音水準的影響。

  儘管此命令針對的是未來新建的資料中心，但根據律師事務所Davis Wright Tremaine的分析，它也可能影響到目前已在申請許可流程中的專案。截至7月14日，凡仍待州環境保護局（DEC）核准的專案，都可能受到波及；而那些僅循地方許可程序、無須DEC核准的專案，則不受影響。

  Davis Wright Tremaine的分析指出：「對於開發、融資或營運資料中心的企業而言，此行政命令提醒業者，成功推進專案越來越需要跨越土地使用、環境許可、能源、科技交易、商業合約、融資及法規合規等多個層面的統籌協調。」

  ## 尚未簽署的法案及其更廣泛的影響範圍

  霍楚爾州長發布行政命令，正是為了爭取時間，以便政府研議編號A11560的《負責任資料中心發展法》（Responsible Data Center Development Act）。該法案由州議會於6月4日通過，但州長至今尚未簽署。根據DLA Piper的分析，法案與行政命令之間的核心差異在於適用範圍。

  行政命令以單一的50 MW為門檻，而該法案則採用較低的浮動尖峰負載門檻——分別為1 MW、5 MW及20 MW——使更多設施納入規管範圍。兩者在處理既有專案的方式上也有所不同。該法案將現有核准的修改、續期、重新核發及重新認證，以及在法案生效日前已動工的專案排除在外。行政命令則無此類「祖父條款」保護機制；其適用範圍取決於DEC的許可狀態，而非施工動工時間，DLA Piper如此分析。

  ## A11560法案的規範要求

  根據Harris Beach Murtha與Davis Polk的分析，若A11560獲得簽署，將對資料中心業者課以一系列義務。

  尖峰負載達5 MW以上的資料中心，將須在2030年前向再生能源採購至少三分之一的用電量，且後續年度的比例要求將逐步提高。這些設施也需達到由「紐約州能源研究與發展局」（NYSERDA）制定的能源效率目標。

  根據Harris Beach Murtha的分析，大型新建資料中心，以及任何新增負載達20 MW以上的現有設施，將須出資設立「地主社區效益計畫」，涵蓋住宅能源升級改善、社區基礎設施建設，以及減緩用水與廢水衝擊的相關措施。

  根據Davis Polk的分析，此法案也可能影響採用高耗水冷卻設計的業者。DEC將被要求評估是否有必要針對資料中心的取水計畫制定新規或修訂現行法規，並須在12個月內提交報告。

  ## 地方政府的角色

  依據A11560，負責向專案提供補助與貸款的州政府機構「帝國州發展基金」（Empire State Development Fund）須在法案生效後60天內，公布一套框架，賦予地方政府在資料中心選址決策上更大的話語權。根據Davis Polk的分析，該框架將涉及由資料中心企業出資設立社區投資基金的規劃，並包含地方基礎設施投資、現行工資及專案勞動協議，以及經濟指標報告等方面的指引。

  ## 後續發展

  行政命令與尚未簽署的法案，代表兩條截然不同的監管路徑，在門檻設定、祖父條款規定及適用範圍上均有所差異。行政命令的暫停期須待公共服務部完成通用環境影響評估說明流程後方可解除，而該審查程序的時間表目前尚未確定。A11560則靜待霍楚爾州長決定是否簽署成法。
