body_en: |
  Ofgem has proposed a refundable commitment fee and a set of deliverability milestones for large data center grid connections in Great Britain, a regulatory shift designed to clear speculative projects from a congested queue and prioritize developments with demonstrated financial and procurement readiness. The consultation closes September 16.

  The proposal targets a queue that has swollen rapidly: contracted demand offers rose from 41 gigawatts (GW) in November 2024 to 125 GW by June 2025, according to the regulator. Ofgem said data centers account for roughly 73 GW of that total, and that nonviable applications are delaying credible projects and distorting future network planning.

  ## The fee structure

  The proposed Data Centre Commitment Fee would be set between £237,500 and £712,500 per megawatt (MW), equivalent to approximately $315,000 to $946,000 per MW, or about 2.5% to 7.5% of average project costs, according to an analysis by law firm Walker Morris. For a 100 MW facility — roughly the scale of a large campus — that translates to a security deposit of approximately £23.8 million to £71.3 million. The fee would sit alongside existing transmission security requirements.

  The fee is refunded upon energization, meaning a project that successfully connects to the grid recovers the deposit in full. A project that exits the queue before energizing forfeits it.

  ## Shift from first-come to proof of delivery

  The design would alter the fundamental logic of grid access in Great Britain. The queue currently operates largely on a chronological basis. Ofgem's proposal would overlay financial capability, commercial maturity, and procurement activity as conditions for maintaining a project's position.

  Kristina Lesnjak, EMEA research manager at data center market intelligence firm DCByte, said the reform would effectively reorder the queue around credibility. "These proposals aim to make the grid queue less about who applied first and more about who can demonstrate a credible path to delivery," Lesnjak told Data Center Knowledge. "This will rule out many of the more speculative projects for prioritization straightaway, benefiting viable developments."

  Lesnjak said the risk lies in how viability is defined and enforced in practice. Projects that are credible but lack the balance sheet or established UK presence of a large hyperscaler could be disadvantaged even if their underlying development plans are sound. "The process has to be transparent and set clear milestones for developers, for what evidence is required and when," she said. "The goal should be to free up capacity from projects that are unlikely to proceed while giving serious schemes a fair opportunity to prove they can deliver."

  ## Capital access could create a two-tier market

  The commitment fee is part of a broader international pattern in which grid operators are requiring large electricity users to substantiate demand before reserving capacity. In the United States, grid operator PJM has proposed requiring many future large loads to secure sufficient generating capacity or accept curtailment during resource shortages, a parallel effort to prevent the cost of unverified or unsupported demand from falling on existing consumers, according to the source.

  In Great Britain, the likely beneficiaries are operators with a record of delivering large campuses, ready access to capital, and projects deemed strategically valuable, Lesnjak said. Smaller developers and new entrants could face delays even when their projects are technically viable. "This could lead to a two-tier market, with strategic, fast-tracked projects at the head of the queue and non-strategic schemes heavily delayed," she said. "This could benefit those major operators and hyperscalers with an existing presence in the UK over smaller players and new entrants."

  ## Implications for site selection

  The policy could also reshape where future data centers are built. Lesnjak said smaller developers may need to look beyond constrained clusters, form partnerships to finance generation capacity in lower-demand areas such as northern England and Scotland, or combine non-firm grid connections with privately owned energy assets. "In essence, smaller developers are going to need to be more creative in how they secure power for projects," she said.

  That shift would move power procurement from a downstream step — typically addressed after a site is selected — to a central variable in project planning from the outset.

  Ofgem's consultation on the Data Centre Commitment Fee and associated deliverability milestones remains open through September 16.
body_zh: |
  Ofgem提議對英國大型資料中心電網申請設置可退還的承諾費及一系列交付里程碑，此一監管轉向旨在清除塞滿申請隊伍的投機性專案，優先處理已展現財務與採購準備度的開發案。諮詢截止日期為9月16日。

  根據監管機構數據，目前待審隊伍已急速膨脹：已簽約需求要約從2024年11月的41吉瓦（GW）攀升至2025年6月的125 GW。Ofgem表示，資料中心約佔其中73 GW，不可行的申請案正拖累有實力的專案，並扭曲未來的電網規劃。

  ## 費用結構

  提議中的「資料中心承諾費」定價區間為每百萬瓦（MW）23萬7,500英鎊至71萬2,500英鎊（約合31萬5,000美元至94萬6,000美元），相當於平均專案成本的2.5%至7.5%，此為律師事務所Walker Morris的分析結果。以一座100 MW設施——大致相當於大型園區的規模——為例，此項保證金約為2,380萬至7,130萬英鎊。該費用將與現行輸電安全要求並行實施。

  保證金於專案送電時全額退還，亦即成功連接電網的專案可取回全額押金；若專案在送電前退出申請隊伍，則予以沒收。

  ## 從先到先得轉向交付能力證明

  此設計將從根本上改變英國電網接入的邏輯。目前申請隊伍基本上依申請時間先後排列。Ofgem的提案將把財務能力、商業成熟度及採購進度，覆加為維持專案排隊位置的條件。

  資料中心市場情報公司DCByte的歐洲、中東及非洲研究經理Kristina Lesnjak表示，此次改革將實質上依可信度重新排列申請隊伍。她向Data Center Knowledge表示：「這些提案的目標，是讓電網申請排隊少看誰先申請，多看誰能展示可信的交付路徑。這將立即淘汰許多投機性較強的專案，使可行的開發案受益。」

  Lesnjak指出，風險在於實際執行時如何定義與評估可行性。部分專案本身可信，但若缺乏大型超大規模業者的財務規模或既有英國市場地位，即使其開發計畫本身健全，仍可能居於不利地位。她說：「這套流程必須透明，並向開發商明確設定里程碑——哪些佐證文件是必要的、何時需要提交。目標應是釋放那些不太可能推進的專案所佔用的容量，同時給予認真的方案公平的機會來證明其交付能力。」

  ## 資本取得門檻恐造就雙層市場

  承諾費是全球電網業者要求大型用電戶在預訂容量前先證實需求這一更廣泛趨勢的一環。在美國，電網業者PJM已提案要求許多未來的大型用電戶確保足夠的發電容量，或接受資源緊缺時的削減，此舉同樣旨在防止未經核實或缺乏支撐的需求成本轉嫁給現有用戶。

  在英國，可能受益的業者是擁有大型園區交付紀錄、資本取得便利且被視為具戰略價值的企業，Lesnjak表示。規模較小的開發商及新進業者，即使其專案在技術層面可行，也可能面臨延誤。她說：「這可能導致雙層市場的出現——戰略性、獲加速審批的專案排在隊伍前端，非戰略性方案則嚴重延誤。這將使在英國已有布局的大型業者與超大規模雲端業者佔有優勢，而非有利於較小規模的業者和新進者。」

  ## 對選址策略的影響

  此政策也可能重塑未來資料中心的建設地點選擇。Lesnjak表示，較小的開發商可能需要將目光放眼至需求壅塞的聚集區之外，轉而在英格蘭北部及蘇格蘭等需求較低的地區尋求合作夥伴共同融資發電容量，或將非固定電網連接與私有能源資產結合運用。她說：「本質上，較小的開發商需要在如何為專案確保電力方面更具創意。」

  這一轉變將使電力採購從過去的下游步驟——通常在選定場址後才處理——提升為專案規劃從一開始就必須納入考量的核心變數。
