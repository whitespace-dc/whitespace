  ## FCC drafting import ban on Chinese data center optical components

  The Trump administration is preparing to ban imports of new Chinese data center components, with the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) — the U.S. regulator that oversees the country's telecommunications industry — drafting the restrictions, according to four people familiar with the matter cited by Reuters. Officials aim to announce and implement the ban before the end of 2026.

  The FCC's planned mechanism, per the sources, is to first ban imports of all new models of optical transceivers — the hardware components that convert electrical signals to light for high-speed data transmission inside data centers — and then exempt suppliers not based in China. That structure effectively targets Chinese manufacturers while preserving supply from other countries.

  ## Why the administration is acting now

  The stated purpose of the restrictions is to prevent Chinese companies from stealing data, embedding malware, or disrupting U.S. data center operations. Those data centers house the advanced chips used to train and run artificial intelligence models, making their supply chains a national-security concern, AI policy experts quoted in the reporting said.

  Policymakers in Washington are also trying to avoid repeating what they describe as the Huawei problem: Chinese telecommunications equipment that became deeply embedded in U.S. network infrastructure before restrictions were imposed, making removal slow, expensive, and incomplete.

  The FCC is stepping into a gap left by the U.S. Commerce Department, which shelved a set of planned import restrictions on China — including measures targeting data center equipment — in February 2026, following a trade-war de-escalation reached in October 2025. The FCC subsequently moved to fill that gap.

  ## FCC's expanded authority and track record

  The FCC has used a regulated entity list known as the Covered List, established by Congress, to impose similar restrictions in recent months. The agency announced bans on Chinese drones, routers, inverters, and robots in December 2025, March 2026, and again last week, the sources said.

  The agency's room to act expanded further after the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in June 2026 to broaden presidential control over specific regulatory bodies, strengthening the White House's authority over the FCC.

  ## Zhongji Innolight stands to be hardest hit

  The company most exposed to a ban is Zhongji Innolight, China's leading optical transceiver manufacturer. According to market research firm Counterpoint Research, Zhongji Innolight holds a 27% share of the global data center optical transceiver market — the largest of any single supplier — and derives approximately 90% of its revenue from outside China. The U.S. Defense Department added Zhongji Innolight to its list of companies suspected of supporting the Chinese military in June 2026.

  ## Cost impact on U.S. cloud operators; domestic supply constraints

  A ban would increase costs for U.S. cloud providers such as Amazon Web Services (AWS), which would be forced to shift purchases to domestic manufacturers including Coherent and Lumentum. Those two companies would benefit from such a shift. However, think tank reports cited in the source indicate that U.S. manufacturers currently lack the production capacity to fully replace Chinese suppliers.

  ## China's response

  China's embassy in Washington urged the United States to listen to the objective and rational voices of the business communities of both countries, to stop smearing Chinese companies and threatening them with sanctions, and warned that China will take all necessary measures in response to any actions that substantially damage its interests.

  The White House, the FCC, Zhongji Innolight, AWS, and the U.S. optical communications manufacturers named did not comment. The sources also stressed that the FCC could still modify or shelve the restrictions before any formal action is taken.

  ## FCC起草禁令，矛頭指向中國資料中心光學元件

  據路透社引述四名知情人士的說法，川普政府正準備禁止進口中國製資料中心元件，負責起草相關限制措施的是美國聯邦通訊委員會（FCC）——即美國主管電信產業的監理機關。相關官員目標在2026年底前宣布並落實這項禁令。

  據知情人士透露，FCC規劃的執行機制是：先禁止所有新型光收發器的進口，再對非中國供應商給予豁免。光收發器是將電訊號轉換為光訊號、用於資料中心內部高速資料傳輸的硬體元件。這樣的設計架構實際上是精準打擊中國製造商，同時保留其他國家的供應來源。

  ## 政府此刻出手的原因

  這項限制措施的官方目的，是防止中國企業竊取資料、植入惡意軟體，或破壞美國資料中心的正常運作。報導引述的人工智慧政策專家指出，這些資料中心儲存了用於訓練及執行AI模型的高階晶片，使其供應鏈成為國家安全層面的重要議題。

  華府政策制定者也試圖避免重蹈所謂「華為問題」的覆轍：中國電信設備在被施加限制之前，已深度嵌入美國網路基礎設施，導致後續的移除工作既耗時、耗費又不徹底。

  FCC此番行動，也是在填補美國商務部留下的空缺。商務部原本規劃了一套針對中國的進口限制措施，其中包含資料中心設備相關條款，但在2025年10月達成貿易戰降溫協議後，於2026年2月將該計畫束之高閣。FCC隨即介入，填補這道缺口。

  ## FCC擴大的職權與過往紀錄

  FCC近幾個月已援引國會授權設立的「涵蓋清單」（Covered List）監管實體名單，對類似案例實施限制。據知情人士表示，FCC分別於2025年12月、2026年3月以及上週，宣布禁止中國製無人機、路由器、逆變器及機器人的進口。

  2026年6月，美國最高法院裁定擴大總統對特定監管機構的管轄權，進一步強化了白宮對FCC的主導力，使FCC的行動空間更為寬裕。

  ## 中際旭創首當其衝

  在這項禁令中暴露風險最大的企業，是中國光收發器龍頭廠商中際旭創。據市場研究機構Counterpoint Research的數據，中際旭創在全球資料中心光收發器市場的占有率高達27%，居所有單一供應商之冠，且約90%的營收來自中國境外。2026年6月，美國國防部已將中際旭創列入疑似支持中國軍方的企業名單。

  ## 美國雲端業者成本壓力上升；國內供應能量不足

  一旦禁令生效，亞馬遜雲端服務（AWS）等美國雲端業者的採購成本將隨之攀升，被迫轉向Coherent、Lumentum等國內製造商採購。這兩家企業將因此受益。然而，報導引述的智庫報告指出，美國製造商目前的產能尚不足以完全取代中國供應商。

  ## 中國的回應

  中國駐美大使館敦促美方傾聽兩國商界客觀、理性的聲音，停止抹黑中國企業、以制裁相威脅，並警告若有任何實質損害中方利益的行動，中國將採取一切必要措施予以回應。

  白宮、FCC、中際旭創、AWS及報導點名的美國光通訊製造商均未回應置評請求。知情人士亦強調，在任何正式行動啟動之前，FCC仍可能修改或擱置相關限制措施。
