  ## Trump targets foreign grid equipment in national emergency order

  President Donald Trump declared a national emergency over the US bulk-power system on Aug. 26, directing the Energy Department to restrict certain foreign-produced grid equipment and impose conditions on equipment already in operation, a move that could add procurement delays and cost pressure to data center power projects already constrained by tight equipment supply.

  The executive order generally prohibits certain foreign-produced bulk-power system electric equipment — including associated critical software and digital capabilities — from being purchased or installed in the United States when those components pose specified national-security risks, including sabotage, unauthorized access and disruption of critical infrastructure. The order does not apply to facilities used for local electric distribution.

  The White House said the rapid growth of advanced manufacturing, artificial intelligence and defense production has increased US dependence on certain foreign components needed to provide abundant, reliable electricity, and that continued reliance on foreign sources of bulk-power equipment creates a supply-chain vulnerability.

  ## Scope: hardware, software and equipment already installed

  The order covers a broad range of electric supply-chain hardware, including transformers, generators, inverters, batteries, turbines and control systems. Agencies are also directed to consider associated software, firmware, remote-access capabilities, lifecycle maintenance and update mechanisms as part of the risk assessment.

  The restrictions extend to equipment already in service. The Energy Department can impose conditions on the continued use and operation of covered equipment. The Energy secretary must weigh reliability, safety, replacement availability and continuity of service when setting those conditions, a requirement that could make origin and software dependency part of ongoing operational decisions rather than one-time procurement reviews.

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, said the biggest practical change is that equipment origin, vendor control, software, remote access and lifecycle dependencies can now become part of the reliability decision — not just the procurement decision. Utilities could be required to answer not only whether a piece of equipment works, but whether they can continue to rely on it based on who made it, who can access it, what software it depends on and what has changed since it was installed, Osnato said.

  "The risk is not limited to the metal sitting in a substation," Osnato said, adding that the administration is treating foreign supply-chain exposure as a national-security and continuity-of-service issue rather than simply a trade concern. He cautioned that a country-of-origin label alone does not tell an operator whether the full dependency chain is safe.

  ## Reliability standards body backs collaborative approach

  The North American Electric Reliability Corp. (NERC), the organization responsible for grid reliability standards across North America, said supply-chain security is a critical and evolving issue. NERC said its Electricity Information Sharing and Analysis Center shares actionable information with industry daily on geopolitical and supply-chain security threats targeting the electricity sector, and that NERC is enhancing its supply-chain risk management standard. NERC said it will work with DOE, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, the Electricity Subsector Coordinating Council and industry as the administration implements the order, and commended the administration for the collaborative approach outlined in the order.

  ## Data center procurement in the crosshairs

  For data center developers and operators, the order arrives as power availability and infrastructure timelines have become among the most significant constraints on new projects. The equipment targeted by the order — transformers, inverters, controls and similar bulk-power components — already faces extended lead times and sourcing risks tied to AI and data center development, according to an Aug. 27 equity research note from Jefferies.

  Jefferies said the restrictions could contribute to longer lead times and higher domestic pricing, while noting that key implementation details remain unresolved until DOE issues its rules. The bank also said the order creates potential complications for equipment already installed or acquired before it was issued, particularly around servicing, retrofits and replacement, and that accelerated replacement could further extend lead times.

  A transformer or other critical component that cannot be sourced, qualified or replaced on schedule can delay a project even when generation and transmission capacity are otherwise available, the source noted.

  Osnato said the challenge will be ensuring supply-chain requirements are addressed early enough not to create problems late in a project. "The answer should not be to weaken the verification standard," he said. "It should be to make the evidence standard clearer and earlier in the process so projects do not reach construction or energization before someone discovers that a critical equipment or supply-chain assumption cannot be relied on."

  ## What comes next

  DOE has 120 days from the order's issuance to publish rules operationalizing it, including identifying specific covered equipment, suppliers and transactions. The precise impact on utilities and data center developers will depend on how those rules define scope. Osnato noted that approved equipment status is not static: "What changed since it was approved?" remains an open compliance question after rules take effect.

  ## 川普宣布國家緊急狀態，矛頭指向外國電網設備

  美國總統唐納·川普於8月26日宣布大型電力系統進入國家緊急狀態，命令能源部限制特定外國產電網設備的採購，並對現役設備的使用與運作設定相關條件。此舉可能對本已面臨設備供應吃緊的資料中心電力基礎建設項目，帶來更多採購延誤與成本壓力。

  這道行政命令原則上禁止採購或安裝特定外國產大型電力系統電氣設備——包括相關關鍵軟體及數位功能——前提是這些元件構成特定國家安全風險，例如蓄意破壞、未經授權存取，以及關鍵基礎設施遭受干擾。此命令不適用於地方電力配送設施。

  白宮表示，先進製造業、人工智慧與國防生產的快速擴張，使美國對提供充沛可靠電力所需的特定外國元件依賴程度日益加深，持續仰賴外國電力設備來源已形成供應鏈漏洞。

  ## 涵蓋範圍：硬體、軟體及現役設備

  此命令涵蓋廣泛的電力供應鏈硬體，包括變壓器、發電機、逆變器、電池、渦輪機及控制系統。各政府機關亦被要求將相關軟體、韌體、遠端存取功能、生命週期維護及更新機制一併納入風險評估。

  限制範圍更延伸至現役設備。能源部可對受管設備的持續使用與運作設定條件。能源部長在制定相關條件時，須權衡可靠性、安全性、替換設備的取得性以及服務連續性——這項要求意味著設備來源及軟體依賴性，可能成為日常營運決策的一環，而非僅限於一次性的採購審查。

  Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾·奧斯納托（Neil Osnato）表示，此命令最重大的實質改變在於：設備來源、供應商控制、軟體、遠端存取及生命週期依賴性，如今都可能成為可靠性決策的考量因素，而非僅止於採購決策。奧斯納托指出，電力業者不只需要回答某設備是否正常運作，還必須說明是否能根據製造商身分、存取權限歸屬、所依賴的軟體，以及自安裝至今有何變更，持續仰賴該設備。

  「風險不僅限於放在變電站裡的金屬設備，」奧斯納托說，並補充道，政府將外國供應鏈風險視為國家安全與服務連續性問題，而非單純的貿易議題。他提醒，光憑原產國標籤，並無法告訴業者整條依賴鏈是否安全。

  ## 可靠性標準機構支持協作推進方式

  負責制定北美電網可靠性標準的北美電力可靠性公司（NERC）表示，供應鏈安全是一項重要且持續演變的議題。NERC指出，其電力資訊共享與分析中心每日向業界分享針對電力部門的地緣政治及供應鏈安全威脅的可行情報，並正持續強化供應鏈風險管理標準。NERC表示，將在政府推行此命令的過程中，與能源部、聯邦能源監管委員會、電力子部門協調委員會及業界密切合作，並對命令所揭示的協作推進方式表示肯定。

  ## 資料中心採購首當其衝

  對資料中心開發商與業者而言，此命令正值電力取得與基礎建設時程已成為新建項目最重大制約因素之際。傑富瑞（Jefferies）8月27日發布的一份股票研究報告指出，此命令所針對的設備——變壓器、逆變器、控制系統及類似大型電力元件——本已因AI與資料中心快速發展而面臨交貨期延長及採購風險。

  傑富瑞表示，相關限制可能導致交貨期進一步拉長、國內定價上升，並指出在能源部發布實施規則之前，關鍵細節仍懸而未決。該行亦指出，此命令對命令發布前已安裝或已採購的設備，尤其在維修、改裝及替換方面，可能形成潛在的複雜情況，而加速汰換更可能進一步延長設備交貨期。

  報告指出，若變壓器或其他關鍵元件無法如期取得、完成資格審查或如期替換，即便發電和輸電容量本身已到位，仍可能導致整個項目延誤。

  奧斯納托表示，挑戰在於確保供應鏈要求能夠在夠早的階段獲得處理，以免在項目後期才引發問題。「答案不應該是降低驗證標準，」他說，「而應是讓查驗標準更加明確、更早介入，避免項目在施工或通電前才發現關鍵設備或供應鏈的前提假設根本無法成立。」

  ## 後續發展

  能源部須在命令發布後120天內公布實施規則，內容包括界定具體受管設備、供應商及適用交易範圍。相關限制對電力業者與資料中心開發商的確切影響，將取決於這些規則如何界定適用範圍。奧斯納托指出，設備的核准狀態並非靜態不變：「自獲准以來有何變更？」在規則生效後，仍將是一道懸而未決的合規課題。
