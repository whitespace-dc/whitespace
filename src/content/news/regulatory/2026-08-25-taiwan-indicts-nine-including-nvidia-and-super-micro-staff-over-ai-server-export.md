  ## Taiwan indicts nine over illegal AI server exports to China

  Taiwan prosecutors indicted nine people on Aug. 24, including employees of Nvidia and Super Micro's Taiwan units, over the alleged illegal export of high-end artificial intelligence servers to China in violation of US export controls.

  Eight defendants — among them one employee from Nvidia's Taiwan unit and two from Super Micro's Taiwan unit — were charged with breach of trust and document forgery, prosecutors in the port city of Keelung said in a statement. A ninth defendant faces charges in a related case involving the alleged siphoning of funds from a distributor company involved in the scheme.

  ## What the alleged scheme involved

  According to prosecutors, 130 B300 servers were ordered from Super Micro on the false claim that they would be installed and used at a rented server facility in Taiwan. False end-user documents were prepared to support that representation.

  Of those 130 servers, 74 were exported and delivered to Chinese customers through multiple routes: direct shipments to China, and transshipments through Indonesia, Japan, and Hong Kong. The remaining 56 servers were destined for a company in Japan, prosecutors said, but Taiwan customs officials detected irregularities and halted the export before it could proceed.

  Prosecutors said the defendants were "fully aware" that both Nvidia and Super Micro maintain "rigorous internal control procedures" regarding exports, but "colluded with one another at various levels for enormous profit, illegally exporting high-end servers, increasing corporate compliance costs, and severely damaging our nation's international image."

  Neither Nvidia nor Super Micro immediately responded to requests for comment, according to Reuters.

  ## The regulatory backdrop

  The United States has imposed export controls on advanced semiconductors since 2022, making it illegal to export or sell such chips to China without a licence. Taiwan, as the world's largest producer of advanced chips used in AI applications, has tightened its own export controls in recent years to prevent advanced technology from reaching China.

  The investigation was launched in 2026 into the suspected illegal export of servers equipped with Nvidia chips subject to those US controls.

  ## Significance

  The case highlights the compliance risks facing companies in Taiwan's technology supply chain as export control enforcement intensifies across multiple jurisdictions. The use of false end-user documentation and transshipment through third countries such as Indonesia, Japan, and Hong Kong to obscure the final destination of controlled hardware is a pattern that regulators in the US and Taiwan have flagged as a primary evasion method. Taiwan customs officials intercepting the remaining 56 servers before export indicates active monitoring of outbound shipments is now catching diversion attempts mid-stream.

  ## 台灣就非法AI伺服器出口中國案起訴九人

  台灣檢察官於8月24日對九人提起公訴，其中包括輝達（Nvidia）及超微電腦（Super Micro）台灣子公司員工，涉嫌違反美國出口管制規定，非法將高階人工智慧伺服器出口至中國。

  八名被告中，包含輝達台灣子公司一名員工及超微電腦台灣子公司兩名員工，基隆地檢署在聲明中表示，這八人遭依背信及偽造文書罪嫌起訴。第九名被告則因另一相關案件遭起訴，涉嫌從參與該計畫的經銷商公司挪用資金。

  ## 涉案手法

  根據檢察官說法，涉案人員以謊稱伺服器將安裝於台灣某租用機房並在台使用為由，向超微電腦訂購了130台B300伺服器，並偽造最終用戶文件以支持該說法。

  在這130台伺服器中，74台透過多種管道出口並交付予中國客戶，包括直接運往中國，以及經由印尼、日本和香港轉口。檢察官表示，其餘56台原定出貨至日本一家企業，但台灣海關察覺異常，在出口前即攔截阻止。

  檢察官指出，被告「明知」輝達與超微電腦對出口業務均設有「嚴格的內部控管程序」，卻「在各層級相互勾結、謀取鉅額利益，非法出口高階伺服器，不僅增加企業合規成本，更嚴重損害我國的國際形象」。

  據路透社報導，輝達與超微電腦均未立即回應置評請求。

  ## 法規背景

  美國自2022年起對先進半導體實施出口管制，規定未經許可不得將相關晶片出口或銷售至中國。台灣身為全球最大的AI應用先進晶片生產地，近年來也持續強化本身的出口管制措施，以防止先進技術流入中國。

  本案調查於2026年展開，針對涉嫌非法出口搭載受美國管制之輝達晶片伺服器一事展開追查。

  ## 案件意義

  隨著多個司法管轄區的出口管制執法力度持續升高，本案凸顯了台灣科技供應鏈業者所面臨的合規風險。偽造最終用戶文件、並藉由印尼、日本及香港等第三國轉口以掩蓋受管制硬體最終目的地，是美台兩地監管機構均已點名的主要規避手法。台灣海關在出口前成功攔截剩餘56台伺服器，顯示主管機關對出境貨物的主動監控，已能即時偵測貨物在中途遭轉運的規避行為。
