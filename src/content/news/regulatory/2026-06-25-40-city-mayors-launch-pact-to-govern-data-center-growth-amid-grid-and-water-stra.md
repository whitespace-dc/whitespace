body_en: |
  Mayors from 40 cities across four continents, including London, Phoenix, and Melbourne, have jointly launched the Global Urban Data Centres Pact, a shared framework setting standards for how data centers consume energy and water and fit into urban planning, city leaders said at London Climate Action Week this week.

  The pact responds to a global surge in computing infrastructure investment, driven largely by artificial intelligence (AI) demand, that has placed escalating pressure on electricity grids, municipal water systems, and local communities. The mayors of Phoenix and Melbourne described the initiative to Reuters.

  ## Scale of the pressure

  In Melbourne, about 50 major data centers already operate within the city and are projected to account for roughly 10% of local power demand by 2030, rising to as much as 20% by 2040 in a city of 5.5 million people, Melbourne Lord Mayor Nicholas Reece said. The facilities could collectively draw around 20 billion litres of water per year — equivalent to approximately 4% of the city's entire drinking water supply — Reece added.

  "Data centres are the biggest thing to hit the energy grid since air conditioning in the 1950s — where the rollout of air conditioning took decades, this is happening in a few short years," Reece said.

  In Phoenix, Mayor Kate Gallego said the city and surrounding region already have 225 existing or planned data centers, with further proposals that could double the area's total electricity demand. Utilities that had experienced decades of gradual, steady demand growth were now seeing expansion in a few years comparable to an entire century of prior growth, driven largely by AI-related computing, Gallego said.

  ## What the pact requires

  The Global Urban Data Centres Pact sets standards covering clean energy use, more efficient use of all resources, and better integration of data center facilities into urban planning processes, according to the mayors. The framework is intended to guide permitting and planning decisions, as well as negotiations between cities, technology companies, and national governments.

  Rules will be adapted to local conditions — the mayors noted that cooling requirements in Iceland differ substantially from those in Manila — but the shared framework is meant to provide a common baseline across signatories.

  ## Protests and competitive risk

  The pace of investment has already sparked protests in the United States, South Africa, and the United Kingdom, according to the mayors. Disputes have centered on noise, land use, and safety risks from battery energy storage systems used at the facilities, alongside broader concerns about siting large industrial infrastructure in or near residential neighborhoods, Gallego said.

  Reece said data center investment was occurring at "breakneck speed," outpacing regulation, and warned that cities faced a "race to the bottom" as governments compete to attract investment, sometimes bypassing environmental scrutiny in the process.

  ## Significance

  The pact represents an attempt by municipal governments to assert collective influence over data center development at a moment when the sector is drawing trillions of dollars in new investment globally. By aligning on planning and permitting standards across 40 cities, the signatories aim to reduce the risk that individual cities accept unfavorable local terms in exchange for economic development.

  London Mayor Sadiq Khan said in a statement that AI and digital infrastructure would play "a major role in the future prosperity of cities around the world," but that "residents are right to expect growth to be managed responsibly."

  Melbourne projects that data center power demand within the city could reach 20% of total local electricity consumption by 2040.
body_zh: |
  橫跨四大洲、共四十座城市的市長，包括倫敦、鳳凰城及墨爾本，本週在倫敦氣候行動週期間聯合發起「全球城市資料中心公約」（Global Urban Data Centres Pact）。城市領導人表示，這項共同框架針對資料中心的能源與水資源消耗，以及如何融入城市規劃，制定了共同標準，以回應人工智慧（AI）帶動的運算基礎設施投資熱潮持續超越地方法規的現況。鳳凰城與墨爾本市長分別向路透社說明了這項倡議的緣起。

  ## 壓力的規模

  墨爾本市長尼可拉斯．里斯（Nicholas Reece）表示，目前市內已有約五十座大型資料中心在運作，預計到2030年，資料中心的用電量將占當地總用電量約10%，並在這座550萬人口的城市中，於2040年前進一步攀升至多達20%。里斯補充，這些設施每年的合計耗水量可能高達約200億公升，約相當於墨爾本全市飲用水供應量的4%。

  里斯說：「資料中心對電網帶來的衝擊，堪稱是繼1950年代冷氣空調普及以來最大的一次——當年冷氣的全面推廣歷時數十年，而這一切正在短短幾年內發生。」

  鳳凰城市長凱特．加雷戈（Kate Gallego）表示，鳳凰城及周邊地區目前已有225座現有或規劃中的資料中心，若後續提案全數落地，可能使該地區的總用電需求增加至現有的兩倍。她指出，過去數十年來，當地公用事業機構所經歷的是緩慢而穩定的需求成長，如今卻在短短幾年內出現堪比過去整整一個世紀增幅的擴張，主要動力正是與人工智慧相關的運算需求。

  ## 公約的要求

  根據各市長的說明，「全球城市資料中心公約」制定了涵蓋潔淨能源使用、提升所有資源的使用效率，以及將資料中心設施更妥善地納入城市規劃程序等方面的標準。這套框架旨在引導許可審查與規劃決策，並作為城市、科技公司與中央政府之間談判的依據。

  各項規定將視當地條件進行調整——市長們指出，冰島的冷卻需求與馬尼拉存在相當大的差異——但共同框架的目的在於為所有簽署方提供一條共同的基準線。

  ## 抗議聲浪與競爭風險

  根據各市長的說法，投資擴張的速度已在美國、南非和英國引發抗議。加雷戈表示，爭議焦點集中在噪音、土地使用，以及設施所採用的電池儲能系統帶來的安全風險，同時也牽涉到在住宅區內或鄰近地區興建大型工業設施的廣泛疑慮。

  里斯表示，資料中心投資正以「令人咋舌的速度」推進，已超越法規的腳步，並警告各城市面臨「向下競逐」的風險——各地政府為了爭搶投資，有時不惜繞過環境審查。

  ## 公約的意義

  這項公約代表著各市政府試圖在資料中心建設吸引全球數兆美元新投資的關鍵時刻，藉由集體行動來彰顯影響力。透過在四十座城市間統一規劃與許可標準，各簽署方希望降低個別城市為換取經濟發展、而被迫接受不利地方條件的風險。

  倫敦市長薩迪克．汗（Sadiq Khan）在聲明中表示，人工智慧與數位基礎設施將在「全球城市未來的繁榮中扮演重要角色」，但「居民有權期待成長能夠被負責任地管理」。

  墨爾本預估，市內資料中心的用電需求到2040年可能達到當地總用電量的20%。
