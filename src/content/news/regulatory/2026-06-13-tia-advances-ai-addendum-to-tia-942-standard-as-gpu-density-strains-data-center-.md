body_en: |
  The Telecommunications Industry Association (TIA) has approved work on an addendum to TIA-942—its foundational data center infrastructure standard—specifically targeting the design and reliability challenges posed by artificial intelligence workloads, according to Tom McGarry, TIA's vice president of standards.

  The move reflects a recognition inside the standards body that AI infrastructure constitutes a third, distinct category of data center, separate from the traditional colocation and hyperscale facilities and the edge or modular sites that TIA-942 already addresses.

  ## Why AI workloads require new guidance

  AI systems depend heavily on graphics processing units (GPUs), which consume substantially more power and generate far more heat per rack than the central processing units (CPUs) that traditional data center standards were built around. McGarry identified four pressure points the addendum will need to address: higher power density at the rack level, greater reliance on liquid cooling rather than conventional air cooling, more complex and denser cabling, and structural loads heavier than facilities were originally engineered to carry.

  The core challenge, McGarry said, is preserving the same redundancy and availability levels the standard has always defined—while accommodating those changed physical conditions.

  ## The TIA-942 standard and its rating system

  TIA-942 was first published in 2005, after development began during the initial wave of large-scale data center construction. It is accredited by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI), meaning it is produced through broad, multi-stakeholder industry participation rather than by a single private organization—a characteristic McGarry cited as central to its credibility and longevity.

  The standard defines two primary objectives: how to design and build data centers, and how to maintain high availability through both planned and unplanned disruptions.

  It organizes facilities into four reliability levels:

  - **Level 1:** Basic infrastructure with minimal redundancy, comparable to a standard IT or telecommunications room.
  - **Level 2:** Component-level redundancy offering limited protection during planned maintenance or minor outages.
  - **Level 3:** Concurrent maintainability—maintenance can proceed without interrupting operations, supported by redundant systems and multiple distribution paths.
  - **Level 4:** Full fault tolerance, with complete redundancy capable of sustaining an unplanned failure without service interruption.

  McGarry said most modern operators target Level 3 or Level 4, which align with the availability expectations of enterprise and hyperscale customers.

  ## Certification program scope

  TIA operates a certification program through eight independent certification bodies operating in more than 60 countries. Those bodies are restricted to certification work and barred from providing consulting services to the facilities they assess, a separation McGarry described as essential to impartiality. TIA audits the certification bodies annually.

  The program has conducted more than 1,000 certification activities covering more than 800 data centers. Certification has taken on regulatory significance in some markets: McGarry noted that certain governments now require third-party certification for data centers handling public-sector workloads.

  ## DCE 9000 quality standard in development

  In parallel with the AI addendum, TIA's quality standards group is developing DCE 9000, a quality management system aimed at data center operators and their supply chains. McGarry described it as built on frameworks similar to ISO 9001—the internationally recognized quality management standard—but tailored to telecommunications and digital infrastructure. DCE 9000 is intended to address process consistency, risk management, and continuous improvement, complementing TIA-942's infrastructure focus with an operational performance layer.

  ## Industry participation sought

  TIA is actively recruiting participants for the AI addendum work from organizations involved in power systems, cooling technologies—particularly liquid cooling—and data center operations. The TR-42 committee, which has oversight of TIA-942, is the venue for that engagement. Organizations can also participate through TIA membership, with a contact point at membership@tiaonline.org.

  The addendum work positions TIA-942 to extend its two-decade coverage of data center infrastructure into the GPU-dense, liquid-cooled facilities that AI deployments are driving operators to build and certify.

  ## TIA推動TIA-942標準AI附錄，GPU高密度衝擊資料中心設計

  美國電信工業協會（TIA）已核准針對旗下核心資料中心基礎設施標準TIA-942開發一份附錄，專門因應人工智慧工作負載所帶來的設計與可靠性挑戰。據TIA標準副總裁湯姆．麥加里（Tom McGarry）表示，此舉源於協會內部對AI基礎設施的一項共識——AI設施已構成資料中心的第三種獨立類別，有別於TIA-942現行所涵蓋的傳統共置（colocation）與超大規模（hyperscale）機房，以及邊緣或模組化站點。

  ## 為何AI工作負載需要新規範

  AI系統高度依賴圖形處理器（GPU），其每機架的耗電量與散熱量均遠高於傳統資料中心標準所預設的中央處理器（CPU）。麥加里點出此附錄必須解決的四大壓力點：機架層級的更高電力密度、從傳統氣冷大幅轉向液冷、更複雜且更稠密的佈線，以及超出原始結構工程承載能力的設備重量。

  麥加里指出，核心挑戰在於：在因應上述物理條件改變的同時，仍須維持該標準一貫定義的備援性與可用性水準。

  ## TIA-942標準與其評級制度

  TIA-942於2005年首次發布，開發工作起步於大規模資料中心建設浪潮的初期。該標準獲美國國家標準學會（ANSI）認可，意味著其制定過程有廣泛的多方利害關係人產業參與，而非由單一私人機構主導——麥加里將此特性列為該標準公信力與長壽命的關鍵所在。

  該標準定義了兩項主要目標：如何設計與興建資料中心，以及如何在計畫性與非計畫性中斷情況下維持高可用性。

  標準將設施分為四個可靠性等級：

  - **第一級：** 基本基礎設施，備援最少，相當於一般IT或電信機房的水準。
  - **第二級：** 元件層級備援，在計畫性維護或輕微中斷期間提供有限保護。
  - **第三級：** 並行維護能力——可在不中斷營運的情況下進行維護，並有備援系統與多重配電路徑支撐。
  - **第四級：** 完全容錯，具備完整備援能力，即便發生非計畫性故障也不會中斷服務。

  麥加里表示，多數現代營運商以第三級或第四級為目標，兩者均符合企業與超大規模客戶的可用性期望。

  ## 認證計畫範疇

  TIA透過八家獨立認證機構運作認證計畫，業務遍及逾60個國家。這些機構僅限從事認證工作，嚴禁向受評估設施提供顧問服務——麥加里將此一分離原則描述為確保公正性的必要條件。TIA每年對各認證機構進行稽核。

  該計畫迄今已完成逾1,000次認證活動，涵蓋800餘座資料中心。在部分市場，認證已具備法規意義：麥加里指出，某些政府現已要求處理公部門業務的資料中心取得第三方認證。

  ## DCE 9000品質標準同步開發中

  與AI附錄同步推進的，是TIA品質標準工作組正在開發的DCE 9000——一套針對資料中心營運商及其供應鏈的品質管理系統。麥加里將其描述為建構於類似ISO 9001（國際公認品質管理標準）的框架之上，但專為電信與數位基礎設施量身調整。DCE 9000旨在解決流程一致性、風險管理與持續改善等議題，在TIA-942著重基礎設施的基礎上，另添一層營運績效的管理維度。

  ## 廣徵產業界參與

  TIA正積極向電力系統、散熱技術（尤其是液冷領域）及資料中心營運相關組織招募AI附錄的參與成員。負責監管TIA-942的TR-42委員會是參與該工作的主要管道。各組織亦可透過成為TIA會員參與其中，聯絡窗口為membership@tiaonline.org。

  此次附錄開發工作，將使TIA-942二十年來對資料中心基礎設施的規範延伸至GPU高密度、液冷化的設施——正是AI部署浪潮驅使各營運商競相興建與取得認證的新型態機房。
