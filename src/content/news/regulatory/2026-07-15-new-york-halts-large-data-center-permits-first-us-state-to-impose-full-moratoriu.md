body_en: |
  New York Governor Kathy Hochul on Tuesday signed an executive order making New York the first US state to impose a full moratorium on new large data centers, directing regulators to halt discretionary state permits for facilities consuming 50 megawatts (MW) or more until the state completes an environmental impact review.

  The order directs the New York Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) to pause permit applications for the construction or expansion of data centers at or above the 50 MW threshold, unless those applications had already been deemed complete before the order took effect. The pause runs until the state completes a Generic Environmental Impact Statement (GEIS) or for one year, whichever comes first. Local government permits are not affected.

  Covered facilities are defined as those with specialized cooling systems and continuous operations that consume, or can consume, at least 50 MW. The order exempts facilities primarily used for manufacturing, research, or education, including Empire AI and accredited colleges and universities conducting academic research, as well as medical care facilities.

  Hochul cited rising utility bills, water depletion, and community burdens as her rationale. "As data center development threatens to hike up utility bills, deplete our natural resources, and create uncertainty for New Yorkers, it's my responsibility to take action and lead," she said. She also said she would pursue legislation to repeal sales tax exemptions for large data centers.

  ## Scale of demand driving the action

  Nearly 12 gigawatts (GW) of data center load requests were in the New York Independent System Operator (NYISO) interconnection queue as of May, according to the executive order. One gigawatt of electricity can power approximately 750,000 homes. More than 8 GW of that total entered the NYISO queue during 2025 alone, the order said.

  New York currently has more than 130 data centers, according to Data Center Map, compared with more than 600 in Virginia and about 500 in Texas. The state has the eighth-most expensive retail price for residential electricity in the country, according to US Energy Department data, and its expensive land and tight power supplies have largely limited data center development relative to those larger markets.

  ## Regulatory framework to be built during the pause

  Rather than launching an entirely new regulatory process, the executive order expands an existing Public Service Commission (PSC) proceeding on large-load interconnection reforms, designated PSC Case 26-E-0045.

  The Department of Public Service will prepare the GEIS examining the effects of large data centers on energy demand, water use and quality, air quality, noise, and disproportionate impacts on disadvantaged communities.

  The order directs Empire State Development to publish a Community Investment Framework within 60 days. The guidance will help local governments negotiate benefits agreements with data center developers and will cover community investment funds, infrastructure improvements, workforce development, and project transparency.

  The Department of Public Service must also consider establishing a New York Grid Acceleration Fund, which could require data center developers to make upfront contributions for grid improvements, demand response, and clean energy resources. The fund is intended to protect ratepayers from stranded infrastructure costs if projects are delayed, scaled back, or canceled.

  The order also establishes a Data Center Interconnection Working Group to recommend reforms for connecting large new loads while maintaining what the order describes as "beneficiary pays" principles for network upgrades and resource adequacy costs.

  ## Industry warns of investment flight

  The Data Center Coalition criticized the move, saying it would redirect investment, jobs, and tax revenue to neighboring states. Dan Diorio, the organization's executive vice president of state policy and government affairs, said in a statement: "Gov. Hochul's statewide moratorium on data centers will ensure that those investments, jobs, and economic activity flow elsewhere rather than to New York."

  The coalition said data centers in 2024 supported more than 227,000 jobs in New York, contributed $49 billion to the state's gross domestic product (GDP), and generated $5.1 billion in state and local tax revenue. It argued the order could discourage additional investment, create stranded investment, and signal that New York is "closed for business."

  Hyperscalers Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Oracle did not immediately respond to requests for comment, nor did data center operators Digital Realty, Equinix, and NTT Data.

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, told Data Center Knowledge the pause could redirect some near-term investment to states with faster approvals, more available power, or clearer utility pathways. But he said it could also serve as a model for states seeking a verification process before committing utilities and communities to large new electric loads.

  "Regulatory certainty now becomes another core site-selection variable," Osnato said. "Developers do not only need speed. They need rules that investors, utilities, regulators and communities can trust."

  ## Narrower than the legislature's bill

  The executive order is narrower than legislation the New York Legislature passed earlier this year. The Responsible Data Center Development Act would have imposed a one-year moratorium on new data centers above 20 MW, less than half the 50 MW threshold set by Hochul's order. That bill has not been sent to Hochul's desk; officials in her office described it as complicated and said it would take time to work through with the legislature.

  New York is the first US state to enact a full moratorium on data centers. In April, Maine Governor Janet Mills vetoed a bill that would have imposed a similar freeze on those facilities.

  ## Broader national context

  Only one in three Americans approves of the fast pace of data center construction, and most would oppose building one in their own community, according to a recent Reuters/Ipsos poll. Dozens of state legislatures have introduced bills to rein in the effects of data centers on power bills and the environment, but none had enacted a full moratorium before New York.

  The moratorium ends either when the state finalizes its Generic Environmental Impact Statement or after one year, whichever comes first. The Community Investment Framework from Empire State Development is due within 60 days of the order taking effect.
body_zh: |
  紐約州長凱西·霍楚爾（Kathy Hochul）於週二簽署行政命令，使紐約成為全美首個對新建大型資料中心實施完整禁令的州。命令要求主管機關暫停受理耗電量達50百萬瓦（MW）以上設施的州政府裁量性許可申請，直至州府完成環境影響審查為止。

  該命令指示紐約州環境保護局（DEC）暫停受理建設或擴充達50MW門檻以上資料中心的許可申請，但命令生效前已被認定為申請完整者不在此限。暫停期限為州府完成通案環境影響說明書（GEIS）之日，或最長一年，以先到者為準。地方政府核發的許可不受影響。

  命令所涵蓋的設施，定義為配備專業冷卻系統、持續運作且耗電量達或可達50MW以上者。豁免對象包括主要用於製造、研究或教育目的的設施，涵蓋「帝國AI」（Empire AI）計畫及經認可的學院與大學所進行的學術研究，以及醫療照護設施。

  霍楚爾以電費攀升、水資源耗竭及社區負擔加重為頒布命令的理由。她表示：「資料中心的擴張正威脅推高電費、耗盡我們的自然資源，並為紐約州民帶來不確定性，採取行動、承擔領導責任是我的職責所在。」她並表示將推動立法，廢除大型資料中心的營業稅豁免優惠。

  ## 推動行動的需求規模

  根據該行政命令，截至今年5月，已有近12吉瓦（GW）的資料中心用電需求申請在紐約獨立系統運營商（NYISO）的電網併網候補隊伍中等候，其中1GW的電力約可供應75萬戶家庭使用。命令指出，上述總量中逾8GW是在2025年單一年度內湧入NYISO候補隊列的。

  根據資料中心地圖（Data Center Map）的資料，紐約州目前有逾130座資料中心，相較之下，維吉尼亞州逾600座、德克薩斯州約500座。依美國能源部數據，紐約州住宅用電零售價格在全美排名第八貴，加上地價高昂、電力供應吃緊，相較於上述較大市場，資料中心在該州的發展一直受到相當程度的限制。

  ## 暫停期間將建立的監管框架

  此次行政命令並非啟動全新的監管程序，而是擴大一項現行的公用事業委員會（PSC）程序，即針對大型用電戶電網併網改革所進行的「PSC Case 26-E-0045」案。

  公用事業服務部（Department of Public Service）將負責編製通案環境影響說明書，審查大型資料中心對能源需求、用水量與水質、空氣品質、噪音，以及對弱勢社區造成不成比例衝擊等方面的影響。

  命令要求帝國州發展局（Empire State Development）在60天內發布「社區投資框架」，協助地方政府與資料中心開發商協商利益協議，內容涵蓋社區投資基金、基礎設施改善、勞動力培育及專案透明度等項目。

  公用事業服務部還須評估設立「紐約電網加速基金」的可行性，可能要求資料中心開發商預先出資，用於電網改善、需量反應及潔淨能源資源。該基金旨在保護電費繳納者免受因專案延誤、縮減規模或取消所引發的擱置基礎設施成本損失。

  命令並設立「資料中心電網併網工作小組」，負責就大型新增用電戶的併網改革提出建議，同時維護命令所稱「受益者付費」的網路升級及資源充裕成本分攤原則。

  ## 業界警告投資將外流

  資料中心聯盟（Data Center Coalition）批評此舉將導致投資、就業機會及稅收流向鄰近州別。該組織州政策與政府事務執行副總裁丹·迪奧里奧（Dan Diorio）在聲明中表示：「霍楚爾州長對資料中心實施的全州禁令，將確保那些投資、就業機會和經濟活動流向其他州，而非留在紐約。」

  該聯盟表示，資料中心業在2024年支撐了紐約州逾22萬7千個就業機會，為該州國內生產毛額（GDP）貢獻490億美元，並創造51億美元的州及地方稅收。聯盟認為，此命令可能阻礙後續投資、造成現有投資擱置，並向外界傳遞紐約州「不歡迎投資」的訊號。

  超大規模雲端業者Alphabet、微軟（Microsoft）、Meta、亞馬遜（Amazon）及甲骨文（Oracle），以及資料中心營運商Digital Realty、Equinix和NTT Data均未立即回應置評請求。

  資料分析機構Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾·歐斯納托（Neil Osnato）告訴《Data Center Knowledge》，此次暫停可能將部分近期投資引導至審批速度更快、可用電力更充裕或電力公司路徑更明確的州別。但他也表示，此舉或可成為其他州在承諾電力公司與社區承擔大規模新增用電負荷前，尋求建立驗證程序的參考範本。

  歐斯納托說：「法規確定性現在已成為選址評估的另一項核心變數。開發商不只需要速度，他們需要的是投資人、電力公司、監管機關及社區都能信賴的規則。」

  ## 範圍較立法機構法案更為收斂

  此次行政命令的規範範圍比紐約州議會今年稍早通過的法案更為保守。州議會通過的《負責任資料中心發展法》（Responsible Data Center Development Act）原擬對20MW以上的新建資料中心實施為期一年的禁令，門檻不及霍楚爾命令所設50MW門檻的一半。該法案迄今尚未送達霍楚爾辦公室；州長辦公室官員形容該法案情況複雜，表示仍需時間與立法機構逐一磋商。

  紐約州是全美首個對資料中心頒布完整禁令的州。今年4月，緬因州州長珍妮特·米爾斯（Janet Mills）否決了一項意在對資料中心實施類似凍結措施的法案。

  ## 更廣泛的全國背景

  根據路透社與益普索（Ipsos）近期聯合民調，僅三分之一的美國人認可資料中心建設的快速步伐，且多數人表示反對在自家社區附近興建資料中心。全美已有數十個州議會提出法案，試圖遏制資料中心對電費和環境造成的影響，但在紐約之前，尚無任何一州正式頒布完整禁令。

  此次禁令將於州府完成通案環境影響說明書，或命令生效滿一年後終止，以先到者為準。帝國州發展局的社區投資框架須於命令生效後60天內完成發布。
