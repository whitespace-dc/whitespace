body_en: |
  Climate risk financial modeling firm First Street has found that 79 percent of global data center capacity faces what it calls "acute climate hazards" — including flooding, wind, and wildfire — according to a report published by the company. A further 54 percent of global capacity operates under "chronic stress conditions" such as extreme heat or water scarcity, the report states.

  The findings are based on an analysis of 97 global markets identified by real estate consultancy Cushman & Wakefield.

  ## Regional exposure to acute hazards

  Acute hazard exposure is highest in the Americas, where First Street says 86 percent of data center capacity faces these risks. The Asia-Pacific region follows at 60 percent, with Europe, the Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) at 25 percent.

  The report places major data center hubs — including Northern Virginia in the United States, Johor in Malaysia, and Marseille in France — in its highest climate-risk tier.

  ## Chronic stress conditions

  The chronic stress picture differs significantly by region. First Street reports that 89 percent of Asia-Pacific data center capacity operates under chronic stress conditions, compared with 50 percent in the Americas and 46 percent in EMEA.

  Chronic stressors such as extreme heat and water scarcity are distinct from acute events: they represent persistent baseline conditions that can degrade operations continuously rather than through discrete incidents.

  ## What the risks mean for operators

  "Exposure to extreme heat, water stress, flooding, wind, and wildfire can materially affect power availability, cooling efficiency, insurance costs, and uptime, reshaping true site readiness beyond baseline infrastructure," the report states.

  Matthew Eby, CEO and founder of First Street, argued that standard industry risk models are not equipped to capture current conditions. "Most underwriting for real assets still uses historical data, but the climate is no longer behaving the way the historical record would predict," Eby said. "As heat, drought, and water stress increase, outdated models simply don't offer a complete view of risk anymore."

  Eby added that investors who incorporate climate factors into underwriting and capital allocation decisions "will be better positioned to identify resilient markets and avoid mispriced risk."

  ## Methodology note

  Data Center Dynamics, which reported on the findings, noted that the report appears to calculate exposure by data center capacity — measured in power — rather than by the number of individual facilities, based on a market map in the report that sorts locations by total capacity. Data Center Dynamics said it had contacted First Street to confirm this methodology.

  ## Context

  First Street is not the first firm to quantify climate exposure for the data center sector. Data Center Dynamics noted that XDI, a separate climate risk analysis firm, published a report in July 2025 making similar claims about increasing risks from climate-change-induced extreme weather.

  The scale and geographic spread of data centers have grown substantially in recent years, increasing the statistical likelihood that individual facilities will be affected by climate events, according to Data Center Dynamics.

  For data center planners and investors, the First Street report frames climate risk as a direct variable in site selection, insurance pricing, and long-term operational cost — factors that have historically been treated as secondary to power availability and connectivity. The report's findings, if borne out, would affect underwriting standards and capital allocation across the industry's largest markets.
body_zh: |
  ## First Street：全球79%資料中心容量暴露於急性氣候風險

  氣候風險財務模型公司First Street發布最新報告，指出全球79%的資料中心容量面臨該公司所定義的「急性氣候危害」，涵蓋洪水、強風與野火等威脅。報告同時指出，另有54%的全球資料中心容量長期在「慢性壓力條件」下運作，包括極端高溫與水資源匱乏等問題。

  上述研究結果，係依據國際不動產顧問公司仲量聯行（Cushman & Wakefield）所辨識的97個全球市場進行分析後得出。

  ## 各地區急性危害暴露程度

  以區域分布來看，美洲的急性危害暴露程度最高。First Street指出，美洲地區86%的資料中心容量面臨上述風險；亞太地區次之，暴露比例為60%；歐洲、中東及非洲（EMEA）地區則為25%。

  報告將多個主要資料中心樞紐列為氣候風險最高等級，包括美國北維吉尼亞州、馬來西亞柔佛州，以及法國馬賽。

  ## 慢性壓力條件

  慢性壓力的地區分布情形與急性危害顯著不同。First Street報告顯示，亞太地區高達89%的資料中心容量在慢性壓力條件下運作，美洲為50%，EMEA地區則為46%。

  極端高溫與水資源匱乏等慢性壓力因子，本質上有別於急性災害事件：它們代表的是持續性的基礎背景條件，會對資料中心營運造成持續性的耗損，而非單一突發事件所帶來的衝擊。

  ## 氣候風險對營運商的影響

  報告指出：「暴露於極端高溫、水資源壓力、洪水、強風與野火之中，可能對電力供應穩定性、冷卻效能、保險成本及正常運作時間產生實質影響，從而重塑超越基礎設施本身的真實場址整備度。」

  First Street執行長暨創辦人Matthew Eby表示，現行業界標準風險模型已不足以掌握當前的實際狀況。「多數實體資產的保險核保仍仰賴歷史數據，但氣候的行為模式已不再符合歷史紀錄所能預測的規律，」Eby說，「隨著高溫、乾旱與水資源壓力持續加劇，過時的模型根本無法提供完整的風險視角。」

  Eby進一步補充，將氣候因素納入核保及資本配置決策的投資人，「將更有能力辨識具備韌性的市場，並避免承擔定價錯誤的風險。」

  ## 研究方法說明

  報告此次研究結果的媒體《Data Center Dynamics》指出，根據報告附帶的市場地圖——該地圖依各地點總容量進行排序——報告似乎是以資料中心容量（以電力規模計算）而非個別設施數量作為暴露程度的計算基準。《Data Center Dynamics》表示已就此方法論向First Street求證。

  ## 背景脈絡

  First Street並非首家量化資料中心產業氣候暴露程度的機構。《Data Center Dynamics》指出，另一家氣候風險分析公司XDI曾於2025年7月發布報告，對氣候變遷引發的極端天氣所帶來的風險提出類似警示。

  近年來，資料中心的規模與地理分布大幅擴張，統計上個別設施受到氣候事件衝擊的可能性也隨之提高，《Data Center Dynamics》如此指出。

  對於資料中心規劃者與投資人而言，First Street的報告將氣候風險定位為場址選擇、保險定價及長期營運成本的直接變數——這些因素在過去往往被視為次於電力供應與網路連結的考量。報告的研究結果一旦獲得驗證，將對業界最大市場的核保標準與資本配置方式產生深遠影響。
