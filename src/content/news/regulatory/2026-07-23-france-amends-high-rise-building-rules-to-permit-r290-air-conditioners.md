body_en: |
  France has lifted its ban on flammable refrigerants in high-rise buildings, opening the country's most tightly regulated construction category to air conditioners running on R290 (propane), a natural refrigerant with very low climate impact.

  In an order dated 13 July, the Ministry of the Interior amended the 2011 safety regulations governing the construction of high-rise buildings and their protection against fire and panic risks. Flammable refrigerants had previously been prohibited outright in refrigeration systems within those buildings.

  The amendment permits CE-marked, hermetically sealed electrical air-conditioning appliances using A3-class refrigerants — the classification for highly flammable hydrocarbons such as R290 — provided the refrigerant charge does not exceed 300 grams (roughly the weight of a small can of soda).

  ## What the charge limit means

  The 300g ceiling is central to the safety case. A hermetically sealed system with a small charge significantly limits the quantity of flammable gas that could escape in a fault condition, reducing ignition risk in occupied, high-density buildings. The requirement for CE marking means units must meet European conformity standards before they can be installed under the new rules.

  ## Regulatory context

  The change comes as European Union F-gas regulations — rules governing fluorinated greenhouse gases — are driving a phase-down of high global-warming-potential (GWP) refrigerants across the bloc and pushing the industry toward low-GWP alternatives, including flammable options such as propane. GWP is a measure of how much heat a gas traps in the atmosphere relative to carbon dioxide over a set period.

  However, EU rules allow member states to maintain local building-code exemptions, which had let France's stricter prohibition on flammable refrigerants in high-rise buildings remain in place even as the broader European transition accelerated. The 13 July order removes that exemption for small-charge, hermetically sealed units.

  ## Industry significance

  The amendment extends access to R290-based equipment to a class of buildings previously closed to it, potentially expanding the addressable market for manufacturers whose product lines have increasingly shifted toward hydrocarbon refrigerants in response to F-gas pressure. France's high-rise building category carries some of the strictest national fire and safety rules in Europe, making the regulatory change significant as a precedent for how fire-safety frameworks can accommodate low-GWP flammable refrigerants through charge and containment limits rather than outright prohibition.

  The revised regulations took effect following the 13 July ministerial order.

  ## 法國解除高層建築可燃冷媒禁令，R290空調正式獲准進駐

  法國已解除高層建築使用可燃冷媒的禁令，讓這個全國管制最嚴格的建築類別，正式向採用R290（即丙烷）作為冷媒的空調設備敞開大門。R290屬於天然冷媒，對氣候的衝擊極低。

  法國內政部於7月13日頒布命令，修訂了2011年有關高層建築興建及防火防恐慌風險的安全法規。在此之前，可燃冷媒於上述建築的冷凍空調系統中，已遭到全面禁止。

  本次修訂允許使用A3級冷媒的CE認證全密封電氣空調設備進駐高層建築。A3級為高度可燃碳氫化合物（如R290）的分類，但冷媒充填量不得超過300公克——大約相當於一小罐汽水的重量。

  ## 充填量上限的意義

  300公克的充填量上限，是本次安全論證的核心所在。全密封系統搭配少量充填量，可在設備發生故障時，大幅限制可燃氣體的外洩量，從而降低在人口密集的高層建築中引發火災的風險。CE認證的要求，則意味著設備必須符合歐洲合規標準，方可依新法規安裝。

  ## 法規背景

  此次修法的背景，是歐盟含氟溫室氣體（F-gas）法規持續推動整個歐盟區域逐步淘汰高全球暖化潛勢（GWP）冷媒，並促使業界轉向低GWP替代品，其中包括丙烷等可燃性選項。GWP是衡量某種氣體在特定時期內相對於二氧化碳的暖化能力的指標。

  然而，歐盟規定允許各成員國保留本國建築法規中的豁免條款，這使得法國針對高層建築可燃冷媒的更嚴格禁令，即便在歐洲整體轉型步伐加快之際，仍得以維持。7月13日的部長令，已就小充填量全密封設備移除了這項豁免。

  ## 對產業的意義

  本次修訂，讓R290設備得以進入此前對其關閉的建築類別，可望擴大製造商的潛在市場規模——這些製造商的產品線已在F-gas壓力下，日益轉向碳氫化合物冷媒。法國的高層建築類別適用歐洲最嚴格的國家防火安全規範之一，因此此次法規變革具有重要的先例意義：它示範了防火安全框架如何透過充填量與密封限制，而非全面禁止的方式，來容納低GWP可燃冷媒。

  修訂後的法規已於7月13日部長令頒布後正式生效。
