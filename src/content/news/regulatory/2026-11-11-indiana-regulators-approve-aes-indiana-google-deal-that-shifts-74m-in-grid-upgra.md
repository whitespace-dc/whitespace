body_en: |
  The Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission (IURC) has unanimously approved AES Indiana's plan to build roughly $737 million in generation, storage, and grid infrastructure to serve a 390-megawatt Google data center near Indianapolis, with approximately $74 million in network upgrade costs allocated to all ratepayers rather than the tech company alone.

  The 5-0 vote, reflected in the commission's Oct. 7 order, allows AES Indiana to place new transmission assets into its regulated rate base and recover the associated costs from existing and future customers — a structure that consumer advocates say transfers expenses driven by Google's load onto Indiana households and businesses.

  ## The deal's structure

  The data center is planned for Monrovia, Indiana, southwest of Indianapolis. To serve it, AES Indiana will acquire or build new generation, battery storage, and transmission assets through a subsidiary, which will hold those resources as part of the utility's rate base.

  The generation and storage portfolio consists of:
  - A 200-megawatt battery energy storage system (BESS) called Foundry Works
  - A 130-MW BESS called Hardy Hills
  - A 130-MW solar facility paired with a 130-MW BESS called Crosstrack

  Together the project adds 460 MW of four-hour battery storage and 130 MW of solar capacity. The costs for the generation and storage assets are redacted in public filings.

  AES Indiana's electric service agreement with Google is designed to ensure revenues are sufficient to reimburse at least 100% of the project's incremental direct costs, the utility said. The contract itself is heavily redacted in the public record.

  ## The $74 million dispute

  AES Indiana divided the transmission infrastructure into three categories in testimony before the commission: interconnection equipment for the new generation and storage resources; direct connection infrastructure linking the data center to the grid; and network upgrades described as primarily serving Google but also addressing broader customer demand and energy needs.

  Google will pay approximately $32 million upfront for the direct connection infrastructure. However, $73.8 million in network upgrades — including a new substation — will be placed into AES Indiana's transmission rate base and recovered through future rates charged to all customers, including residential ones.

  The Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO), the regional grid manager overseeing transmission across much of the Midwest, approved some of those network upgrades, citing reliability and load growth.

  The commission ruled that although Google's data center is the impetus for the upgrades, that does not preclude them from providing system-wide benefits. "Network upgrades, as the name implies, are upgrades or additions to the transmission network intended to provide reliability, resiliency, and stability to the system at large," the order states, adding that just as Google will be allocated a share of existing network infrastructure costs, it is reasonable for existing customers to absorb a portion of the new network upgrades.

  ## What Indiana law requires

  Indiana state law requires large load customers to reimburse at least 80% of project costs "reasonably allocable" to them. AES Indiana says its cost allocation framework does not use a "direct cost method" but instead applies a modified "slice of system" approach. The company adds that Google's service agreement separately includes an incremental-cost test designed to recover the incremental costs required to serve Google, including the network upgrades.

  Claire Rice, a spokesperson for AES Indiana, drew a distinction between how costs are spread across the system and how they are ultimately recovered. "Cost allocation and cost recovery are two different concepts," she said in an email.

  AES Indiana Vice President of Commercial Patrick Maguire said revenue from Google will "more than offset" residential customers' portion of the transmission investments. "Customers' rates are not impacted by those transmission investments at all," Maguire said. AES Indiana says Google will pay $300 million toward fixed costs through the end of the contract period at the minimum demand level, with expected total payments of approximately $770 million at the anticipated demand level.

  ## Consumer advocates and the load factor

  Consumer advocates dispute the utility's framing. Ben Inskeep, program director at the Citizens Action Coalition, an Indiana consumer advocacy group that intervened in the proceeding, said the network upgrades are specifically driven by Google's data center and characterized the commission's approval as an error. Inskeep also said the arrangement runs counter to the Trump administration's Ratepayer Protection Pledge, signed earlier this year by both Google and AES Indiana. "Instead of making the cost causer pay, AES Indiana has decided to spread the costs out to other ratepayers, and our commission has unfortunately approved that," he said.

  A second point of contention is Google's rate structure, which is based on a 90% load factor — a measure of how consistently a customer draws its contracted capacity relative to its theoretical maximum. Critics said the 90% figure amounts to a 10% discount. The commission countered that the 90% load factor "recognizes the Customer's substantial and continuous utilization of the system capacity without assuming that the Customer's maximum demand occurs exactly when the system peaks each month."

  Claire Wayner, a senior associate at clean energy nonprofit RMI, framed the broader cost allocation question around whether the grid investments would have been required absent the data center. "If that upgrade was not going to be built without the data center, without benefits modeling or just a demonstration of the benefits to the rest of the zone, it's difficult to make the claim that all ratepayers in that zone should pay for it," she said. Wayner acknowledged that aging infrastructure already slated for replacement presents a different calculus, one where broader cost socialization may be defensible if demonstrated.

  ## Cost recovery mechanism and future rate cases

  Until new base rates are established through a future rate case, AES Indiana plans to recover infrastructure costs through its Environmental Cost Recovery mechanism, netted against contract revenues. The generation and storage assets will be included in future base rate case filings.

  The commission's order says AES Indiana's accounting and ratemaking proposals enable it to "match" project costs with revenue collected through Google's service agreement and "flow" those contributions back to existing and future customers through riders. Many of the exact terms of the service agreement — including monthly energy and demand charges and riders — are redacted in the public record.

  State regulators approved a separate $71 million rate hike for AES Indiana customers in June.

  ## Significance for the industry

  The case illustrates the difficulty facing regulators and utilities as large computational loads drive some of the fastest electricity demand growth in decades. The central question of which costs are "reasonably allocable" to a specific large customer versus which provide genuine system-wide benefits has no uniform national standard, leaving the determination to individual state commissions on a case-by-case basis. The structure approved in Indiana — in which a consumer advocate says a data center's grid trigger costs are socialized across all customers while the utility argues full recovery through a separate contractual mechanism — is likely to recur in other jurisdictions facing similar requests.

  The substation planned as part of the Monrovia Project is designed for 1,200 MW of load, three times the 390 MW the data center currently requires, leaving substantial capacity for future expansion at the site.
body_zh: |
  印第安納州公用事業監管委員會（IURC）全票通過AES Indiana斥資約7.37億美元的計畫，為印第安納波利斯附近一座390 MW的Google資料中心提供電力服務，並裁定約7,380萬美元的電網升級費用由全體用電戶分攤，而非由Google單獨承擔。

  5票全數贊成的表決結果已體現於委員會10月7日的裁決書中，允許AES Indiana將新的輸電資產納入其受監管的費率基礎，並向現有及未來用電戶收取相關費用。消費者倡議人士指出，這種結構將Google用電需求所衍生的費用，轉嫁給了印第安納州的家庭與企業。

  ## 協議架構

  資料中心預計設於印第安納波利斯西南方的蒙羅維亞（Monrovia）鎮。為供應該中心電力，AES Indiana將透過一家子公司取得或興建新的發電設施、電池儲能系統及輸電資產，並將這些資源列入公用事業的費率基礎。

  發電與儲能組合包含：
  - 一座200 MW的電池儲能系統（BESS），名為Foundry Works
  - 一座130 MW的電池儲能系統，名為Hardy Hills
  - 一座130 MW太陽能設施，搭配130 MW電池儲能系統，名為Crosstrack

  整體而言，此專案共新增460 MW的四小時電池儲能容量及130 MW的太陽能發電容量。發電與儲能資產的費用在公開文件中已全數遮蔽。

  AES Indiana與Google簽訂的電力服務協議，旨在確保收入至少足以償還專案100%的遞增直接成本，該公司表示。合約本身在公開紀錄中亦有大量內容遭到遮蔽。

  ## 7,380萬美元的費用爭議

  AES Indiana在委員會作證時，將輸電基礎設施分為三大類：新發電與儲能資源的併網設備；連結資料中心與電網的直接連接基礎設施；以及被描述為主要服務Google、但同時也因應更廣泛用電戶需求與能源需要的電網升級。

  Google將預付約3,200萬美元，作為直接連接基礎設施費用。然而，7,380萬美元的電網升級費用——包括一座新變電站——將納入AES Indiana的輸電費率基礎，並透過未來向所有用電戶（包括住宅用電戶）收取的費率加以回收。

  負責監管中西部大部分地區輸電事務的區域電網管理機構「中部大陸獨立系統運營商」（MISO），已批准部分電網升級，理由是維護電力可靠性及因應負載成長。

  委員會裁定，雖然Google資料中心是推動此次升級的直接原因，但這並不妨礙升級項目同時為整體系統帶來效益。「電網升級，顧名思義，是對輸電網路進行升級或增設，旨在從整體上提升系統的可靠性、韌性與穩定性，」裁決書如此指出，並補充說明：正如Google須分攤現有電網基礎設施的部分費用，現有用電戶承擔一部分新電網升級費用同樣合理。

  ## 印第安納州法律的規定

  印第安納州法律要求大型用電客戶償還「合理歸屬於其」的專案成本中至少80%。AES Indiana表示，其成本分配框架並未採用「直接成本法」，而是採用修改版的「系統比例法」。該公司並補充，Google的服務協議另設有遞增成本測試機制，旨在回收服務Google所需的遞增成本，涵蓋電網升級費用在內。

  AES Indiana發言人Claire Rice區分了費用在系統間的分攤方式與最終的回收方式。「成本分配與成本回收是兩個不同的概念，」她在一封電子郵件中表示。

  AES Indiana商業副總裁Patrick Maguire表示，Google帶來的收入將「大幅抵消有餘」住宅用電戶在輸電投資中的分攤部分。「用電戶的費率完全不受這些輸電投資的影響，」Maguire說。AES Indiana指出，以最低需求水準計算，Google在合約期內將支付至少3億美元的固定成本；以預期需求水準計算，預估總付款金額約為7.7億美元。

  ## 消費者倡議人士與負載率爭議

  消費者倡議人士對公用事業公司的說法提出異議。印第安納州消費者倡議組織「公民行動聯盟」計畫主任Ben Inskeep在此審議程序中以利害關係人身份介入，他表示這些電網升級完全是由Google資料中心所驅動，並將委員會的批准定性為一項錯誤決策。Inskeep還指出，此安排與川普政府今年稍早頒布、Google與AES Indiana均已簽署的《用電戶保護承諾》相互矛盾。「本應讓製造費用的一方來承擔費用，AES Indiana卻決定將費用分攤給其他用電戶，而我們的委員會不幸地批准了這項安排，」他說。

  另一個爭議點在於Google的費率結構，該結構以90%的負載率為基礎——負載率是衡量用電客戶實際用電量相對於其合約容量理論上限的持續性指標。批評者認為，90%的數字實際上等同於給予10%的折扣。委員會對此回應稱，90%的負載率「反映了該用電客戶對系統容量的大量且持續性使用，而不預設每月系統尖峰時刻恰好就是該用電客戶出現最大需求的時刻」。

  清潔能源非營利機構RMI的資深研究員Claire Wayner，以一個核心問題為框架，審視更廣泛的成本分配爭議：若沒有這座資料中心，這些電網投資是否有必要進行？「如果這項升級在沒有資料中心的情況下本不會興建，且未針對該區域其餘用電戶進行效益建模或加以論證，那麼就很難主張該區所有用電戶都應為此買單，」她說。Wayner同時指出，原本就計畫汰換的老舊基礎設施面臨不同的成本計算邏輯——若能加以論證，較廣泛的費用社會化或許是可辯護的。

  ## 費用回收機制與未來費率案

  在新的基本費率透過未來費率案確立之前，AES Indiana計畫透過其「環境成本回收機制」，以合約收入抵扣後的淨額來回收基礎設施費用。發電與儲能資產將納入未來的基本費率案申請。

  委員會裁決書指出，AES Indiana的會計與費率制定提案，使其能夠將專案成本與透過Google服務協議所收取的收入「相互對應」，並透過附加費率機制，將這些貢獻「流回」現有及未來用電戶。服務協議的諸多具體條款——包括每月電力費、容量費及各項附加費率——在公開紀錄中均已遮蔽。

  州政府監管機構今年6月另行批准了AES Indiana用電戶一項7,100萬美元的費率調漲案。

  ## 對產業的意義

  此案揭示了監管機構與電力業者所面臨的難題：大型運算負載正在驅動數十年來最快速的電力需求成長。哪些費用「合理歸屬於」特定大型用電客戶、哪些又確實能為整體系統帶來效益——這一核心問題迄今缺乏全國統一標準，只能由各州委員會逐案自行裁量。印第安納州所批准的結構——消費者倡議人士認為資料中心引發的電網觸發費用被社會化分攤，而公用事業公司則主張透過獨立的合約機制完整回收——很可能在其他面臨類似申請的司法管轄區中一再重演。

  Monrovia專案所規劃的變電站設計容量達1,200 MW，是資料中心目前390 MW需求的三倍，為未來園區擴建保留了相當充裕的容量空間。
