body_en: |
  The US Commerce Department on Monday added Singapore-based Megaspeed International and six of its Southeast Asian affiliates to the Entity List, the government's export control blacklist, barring the firms from buying advanced artificial intelligence chips or other restricted technology without a US government license. Malaysia's Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry said the same day it would direct all domestic data center operators not to host, lease space to, or provide services to any of the seven companies.

  "Malaysia remains committed to a secure, transparent and rules-based trading environment and will not allow itself to be used as a channel to circumvent export controls," the ministry said in a statement. "Action will be taken against parties found to have contravened any laws."

  The Malaysia Investment Development Authority (MIDA), the country's investment promotion agency, will issue a formal notice to data center operators carrying those instructions, the ministry said.

  ## The seven companies and their structure

  Of the seven entities on the new list, Malaysia's trade ministry said two are incorporated in the country: Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd, a wholly owned Malaysian subsidiary of Megaspeed, and MSFusion Sdn Bhd, which the ministry said has reported links to Megaspeed. The US Commerce Department's Federal Register filing said all seven were added for "acting contrary to the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States." The remaining entities are affiliates registered in Singapore and Thailand.

  ## The Megaspeed affair and Nvidia's governance crisis

  Megaspeed sits at the center of a widening controversy involving Nvidia Corporation chief executive Jensen Huang. Earlier this month, Huang admitted to an affair with Alice Huang (no relation), a former Megaspeed executive who has been publicly accused of spying for the Chinese government and of targeting the Nvidia chief. Huang resigned on November 12; Nvidia's board reinstated him on November 23 after what the board described as receiving "input from senior levels of the United States government." Reports attributed the intervention to President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance personally.

  Trump holds direct financial exposure to Nvidia. The most recent presidential financial disclosure filed with the Office of Government Ethics showed he may have owned as many as 370,000 Nvidia shares as of September 30, 2026. The Financial Times reported that after Huang's reinstatement, Trump's Nvidia holdings gained an estimated $15 million.

  Megaspeed has also been publicly accused, in 2025 reporting by The New York Times and Bloomberg, of helping route embargoed Nvidia hardware into China and of deploying graphics processing units (GPUs) in Malaysia and Indonesia that appear to remotely serve Chinese customers.

  Megaspeed has consistently denied those allegations, saying it operates "fully in compliance with all applicable laws, including US export control regulations." The company said multiple government investigations had found "no evidence of any violations concerning the illicit transfer of chips or any illegal activities." Megaspeed also said Alice Huang left the company in August 2025. Nvidia said at the time that its compliance teams found no untoward activity.

  ## Nvidia's governance response and congressional scrutiny

  Nvidia's board said it remains confident Huang's reinstatement "poses no national security risk" but has formed a committee of independent directors to investigate claims that Huang was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort." The board said Huang fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating.

  The board has also established a National Security and Export Control Committee to oversee trade compliance and is conducting a search for a chief compliance officer. Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter are scheduled for closed-door transcribed interviews before the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and China Select Committees on December 1 and 2, respectively.

  California Attorney General Rob Bonta separately issued Nvidia with an investigative subpoena last week as part of a broader probe into whether the company's chips were exported to China in violation of US law. Nvidia said it will continue cooperating with government investigations and inquiries.

  ## Singapore's parallel inquiry

  Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said this week that Singapore is conducting its own inquiry into Megaspeed but is not involved with any US investigation. Wong said Singapore had previously investigated allegations against Alice Huang; that investigation did not result in criminal charges. "If there are companies that are caught breaching our laws, we will certainly take very firm enforcement actions," Wong said.

  ## Malaysia's data center exposure

  The blacklisting arrives as Malaysia has become Southeast Asia's fastest-growing data center hub. Malaysia accounts for more than half of the region's under-construction data center capacity, ahead of Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines, and Vietnam, according to market intelligence firm DC Byte.

  A separate but related risk now weighs on that expansion. Trump recently announced plans to restrict US companies from building data centers overseas, warning that those proceeding would face what he called a "tremendous tax" penalty. RHB Research said in a note that if the policy takes effect, it could trigger a temporary slowdown in data center contract awards in Malaysia. The research house cited a parallel from January 2025, when proposed US AI diffusion rules caused contract awards in Malaysia to pause for four to six months as developers adopted a wait-and-see approach.

  Overseas markets accounted for 52 percent of Alphabet's most recent annual revenue, 49 percent of Microsoft's, and 61 percent of Equinix's fiscal 2025 revenue, according to RHB Research, suggesting that curbs on self-build activity overseas could push US technology companies toward third-party data center operators. RHB Research said the restrictions could also create openings for Chinese operators: "With China likely pushing for self-sufficiency for chips in the long run in light of the latest US AI chip restrictions, there could be a possibility of China expanding further in other countries should US companies not be allowed to build DCs outside of the United States."

  Local Malaysian developers Exsim Data Centres, AIMS Group, Open DC, and YTL Data Centres, along with non-US operators NEXTDC and AirTrunk, are among those RHB Research identified as potential beneficiaries, by leasing capacity to American technology giants unable to build their own facilities overseas. AIMS Group has awarded construction contracts to Binastra Bhd and Gamuda Bhd; Exsim has also awarded a contract to Binastra, according to RHB Research.

  Huang and Teter are scheduled for closed-door congressional testimony on December 1 and 2.
body_zh: |
  美國商務部週一將新加坡商Megaspeed International及其六家東南亞關聯企業列入出口管制「實體清單」（Entity List），禁止這些企業在未取得美國政府許可的前提下採購先進人工智慧晶片或其他受管制技術。馬來西亞投資、貿易及工業部當天同步宣布，將指示境內所有資料中心業者停止為這七家企業提供託管、租賃或任何服務。

  「馬來西亞致力於維護安全、透明且以規則為基礎的貿易環境，絕不允許自身成為規避出口管制的通道，」該部在聲明中表示，「對於違反任何法律的當事人，我方將依法採取行動。」

  馬來西亞投資促進機構「馬來西亞投資發展局」（MIDA）將正式向資料中心業者發出通知，傳達上述指示。

  ## 七家企業及其組織架構

  在新列管的七家企業中，馬來西亞貿易部表示，其中兩家在馬來西亞注冊設立：一是Megaspeed的全資馬來西亞子公司Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd，另一家MSFusion Sdn Bhd則被指與Megaspeed存在關聯。美國商務部刊登於《聯邦公報》的公告指出，七家企業均因「違背美國國家安全或外交政策利益」而遭列管；其餘企業則分別在新加坡及泰國注冊。

  ## Megaspeed風波與輝達治理危機

  Megaspeed正處於一場牽連輝達（NVIDIA）執行長黃仁勳的風暴核心。本月稍早，黃仁勳公開承認與Alice Huang（與其無親屬關係）有婚外情——Alice Huang曾任Megaspeed高階主管，外界公開指控她替中國政府從事間諜活動，並以黃仁勳為目標。黃仁勳於11月12日辭職；輝達董事會在聲明稱收到「美國政府高層意見」後，於11月23日宣布恢復其職務。報導指出，出手干預者正是美國總統唐納・川普與副總統JD萬斯本人。

  川普對輝達有直接的財務曝險。依最新向美國政府倫理辦公室申報的總統財務揭露文件，截至2026年9月30日，川普持有的輝達股票最多可能達37萬股。《金融時報》報導，黃仁勳復職後，川普持有的輝達部位估計增值約1,500萬美元。

  Megaspeed同樣面臨嚴重指控。《紐約時報》與彭博社在2025年的報導中指稱，該公司協助將受禁運的輝達硬體輾轉流入中國，並在馬來西亞與印尼部署繪圖處理器（GPU），疑似遠端為中國客戶提供運算服務。

  Megaspeed始終否認上述指控，聲稱公司「完全遵守所有適用法律，包括美國出口管制法規」，並表示多項政府調查均「未發現任何涉及晶片非法轉移或違法活動的證據」。Megaspeed亦表示，Alice Huang已於2025年8月離職。輝達當時則表示，合規團隊並未發現任何不當行為。

  ## 輝達的治理因應與國會審查

  輝達董事會表示，依然相信黃仁勳復職「不構成任何國家安全風險」，但已成立一個由獨立董事組成的委員會，調查黃仁勳「遭外國情報行動針對」的相關指控。董事會表示，黃仁勳完全支持調查，並承諾全力配合。

  此外，董事會已設立「國家安全與出口管制委員會」，負責監督貿易合規事宜，並正在徵聘首席合規長。黃仁勳與輝達總法律顧問Tim Teter預計分別於12月1日及2日，出席眾議院外交委員會、情報委員會及中國特別委員會的閉門錄音聽證。

  加州司法部長Rob Bonta上週另對輝達發出調查傳票，作為調查輝達晶片是否違法出口至中國的一項更廣泛調查的一環。輝達表示，將持續配合各政府機關的調查與詢問。

  ## 新加坡的平行調查

  新加坡總理黃循財本週表示，新加坡正就Megaspeed展開自身的調查，但與美國方面的調查並無關聯。黃循財表示，新加坡先前已就Alice Huang被指控的相關事項進行過調查，惟調查結果並未促成刑事起訴。「若有企業被查出違反我國法律，我們必定會採取非常嚴厲的執法行動，」黃循財說。

  ## 馬來西亞資料中心的曝險

  此次列管行動發生之際，馬來西亞已躍升為東南亞成長最迅速的資料中心重鎮。根據市場情報公司DC Byte的數據，馬來西亞在建資料中心容量占東南亞整體逾半，領先印尼、泰國、菲律賓及越南。

  另一個相關風險也正對馬來西亞的擴張前景構成壓力。川普近期宣布計畫限制美國企業在海外興建資料中心，警告執意推進者將面臨他所稱的「巨額稅罰」。RHB Research在一份報告中指出，若該政策付諸實施，可能導致馬來西亞的資料中心合約簽訂出現短暫放緩。研究機構援引2025年1月的先例：美國提議實施的AI擴散規則曾使馬來西亞的合約簽訂暫停四至六個月，開發商普遍採取觀望態度。

  根據RHB Research的數據，海外市場占Alphabet最近一個財年收入的52%、微軟占49%，Equinix的2025財年收入中則有61%來自海外。上述數字顯示，一旦美國科技企業受限無法在海外自建設施，可能轉而向第三方資料中心業者租用容量。RHB Research並指出，相關限制也可能為中國業者創造向外擴張的機會：「鑑於最新的美國AI晶片出口限制，中國長期而言可能持續追求晶片自給自足，一旦美國企業不得在境外興建資料中心，中國業者便有可能進一步拓展在其他國家的布局。」

  本地馬來西亞開發商Exsim Data Centres、AIMS Group、Open DC及YTL Data Centres，以及非美系業者NEXTDC與AirTrunk，均被RHB Research列為潛在受益者，可望透過向無法自建設施的美國科技巨擘提供租賃容量而獲益。AIMS Group已分別將建設合約授予Binastra Bhd與Gamuda Bhd；Exsim亦已將合約授予Binastra，據RHB Research報告所述。

  黃仁勳與Teter預計分別於12月1日及2日出席國會閉門聽證。
