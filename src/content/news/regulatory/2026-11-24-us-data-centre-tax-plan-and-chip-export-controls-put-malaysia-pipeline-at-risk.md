body_en: |
  Washington's proposed tax on American companies building data centers abroad, combined with new US export licensing requirements for advanced computing chips that took effect November 19, could narrow Malaysia's new data center project pipeline, analysts said.

  ## Two regulatory pressures converge

  US President Donald Trump vowed last Friday to impose a tax on American firms that build data centers overseas, though he did not specify how the levy would be structured or under what legal authority it would be imposed.

  Separately, the US Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) enforced rules on November 19 requiring a US government licence to export, re-export, or transfer certain advanced computing chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil. Overseas data centers that already supply significant computing power to China-headquartered firms have 90 days, until February 17, 2027, to obtain a licence or face future export restrictions.

  ## New project pipeline at risk

  Loui Low Ley Yee, head of research at Malacca Securities Sdn Bhd, said local construction, property, and data-centre-related infrastructure firms could see their new project pipelines narrow if US hyperscalers — large technology companies that build and operate data centers at scale — reassess plans to invest overseas. Existing projects with secured commitments are less likely to be disrupted, Low told The Edge.

  Malaysia has approved 117 data center projects, of which 55 have begun operations; the remainder are in planning or construction phases, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani said. All 117 projects carry a combined maximum energy demand of 23,500 megawatts (MW). A further 24 applications are on hold while the government reviews the country's power and water capacity.

  ## Chip licensing could freeze loan disbursements

  For projects already under construction, an analyst who declined to be named flagged a secondary risk: banks could hold back on releasing already-approved loan funds until data center operators can demonstrate they have obtained the necessary US licences to import chips into Malaysia.

  The direct impact on Malaysian bank stocks should nonetheless be limited, the analyst said, because data-centre-related financing accounts for only a small share of the loan books at major lenders including Malayan Banking Bhd (Maybank) and CIMB Group Holdings Bhd.

  ## Chinese ownership is a minority of the market

  Of the 120 data center companies operating in Malaysia, only 17 are Chinese-owned, according to Bank Negara Malaysia. That figure suggests the BIS licensing requirement, which focuses on restricting compute access for China-linked entities, directly affects a minority of the domestic market.

  ## Semiconductor stocks seen as insulated

  Low said local semiconductor-related listed companies, including Mi Technovation Bhd, Pentamaster Corp Bhd, ViTrox Corp Bhd, UWC Bhd, and Inari Amertron Bhd, should be cushioned from the impact given their diversified customer exposures and business segments.

  ## Construction and power sectors can absorb a slowdown

  Even if data center build-outs slow, the broader construction sector should be able to manage the adjustment because Malaysia's wider infrastructure development continues, Low said. He said he favors the power and utilities sector, citing structural electricity demand, the National Energy Transition Roadmap (NETR), and continued investment in solar and renewable energy as growth drivers that should keep the sector attractive even if data-centre expansion moderates near term.

  ## Espionage allegations compound sector pressure

  The regulatory moves follow an espionage controversy that has weighed on Malaysia's stock market. Jensen Huang, chief executive of global chipmaker Nvidia, which the source describes as a leading manufacturer of the high-powered chips at the center of US export controls, acknowledged an extra-marital affair. The woman alleged to have been his partner is reported to be a Chinese spy who helped entities skirt existing US chip export restrictions.

  She was an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed International, described as a major buyer of Nvidia chips and said to have worked with Malaysian technology firms. YTL Power is among the companies named in connection with the matter and has denied claims that it was involved in the diversion of chips to China.

  Technology firms and companies in related sectors on Malaysia's stock market have collectively lost billions in market capitalisation following the allegations.

  ## What comes next

  Data center operators in Malaysia that supply computing capacity to China-headquartered clients face a February 17, 2027 deadline to secure US licences or restructure their customer arrangements under the BIS rules already in force. For projects not yet under construction, the terms and legal basis of Trump's proposed overseas data center tax remain undefined, leaving the scope of its impact on new investment decisions uncertain.
body_zh: |
  華盛頓擬對在海外興建資料中心的美國企業課稅，加上美國商務部工業安全局（BIS）自11月19日起施行的先進運算晶片出口授權要求，分析師表示，兩項政策夾擊之下，馬來西亞新建資料中心的專案管線恐將受到壓縮。

  ## 兩股監管壓力同步匯聚

  美國總統唐納·川普上週五揚言，將對在海外興建資料中心的美國企業課徵稅款，但他並未說明稅率結構為何，亦未透露課稅所援引的法律依據。

  另一方面，美國商務部工業安全局（BIS）自11月19日起，要求出口、再出口或轉讓特定先進運算晶片至馬來西亞、新加坡、印尼、泰國及巴西時，須事先取得美國政府核發的出口授權。已大量向總部位於中國的企業提供運算資源的海外資料中心，將有90天緩衝期——即至2027年2月17日——期限內須取得授權，否則將面臨未來的出口限制。

  ## 新建專案管線面臨縮減風險

  馬六甲證券（Malacca Securities Sdn Bhd）研究部主管羅麗儀（Loui Low Ley Yee）表示，若美國超大規模雲端業者——即大規模興建並營運資料中心的大型科技公司——重新評估海外投資計畫，馬來西亞本土的建設、房地產及資料中心相關基礎設施業者的新建專案管線可能隨之收窄。羅麗儀接受《The Edge》採訪時表示，已取得明確承諾的既有專案受到干擾的可能性較低。

  馬來西亞投資、貿易暨工業部長拿督斯里佐哈里·阿都加尼表示，馬來西亞已核准117座資料中心建設案，其中55座已開始運營，其餘仍處於規劃或施工階段。117座專案合計最大能源需求達2萬3,500百萬瓦（MW）。此外，另有24件申請案正處於擱置狀態，待政府完成電力及水資源容量審查後再行處理。

  ## 晶片授權要求恐凍結貸款撥付

  針對已在施工中的專案，一位不願具名的分析師提出另一項潛在風險：在資料中心業者能夠證明已取得進口晶片所需的美國授權之前，銀行可能暫緩撥付已核准的貸款資金。

  不過，該分析師指出，此事對馬來西亞銀行股的直接衝擊應屬有限，原因在於資料中心相關融資在馬來亞銀行（Malayan Banking Bhd，即Maybank）及聯昌國際集團（CIMB Group Holdings Bhd）等主要銀行的貸款帳簿中，僅佔極小比例。

  ## 中資業者僅佔市場少數

  根據馬來西亞國家銀行的數據，目前在馬來西亞營運的120家資料中心業者中，僅有17家為中資所有。此一數字顯示，BIS授權要求的核心在於限制中國相關實體取得運算資源，而直接受影響的業者僅佔馬來西亞本土市場的少數。

  ## 半導體股料可隔絕衝擊

  羅麗儀表示，馬來西亞本土上市的半導體相關公司，包括Mi Technovation Bhd、Pentamaster Corp Bhd、ViTrox Corp Bhd、UWC Bhd及Inari Amertron Bhd，由於在客戶基礎及業務部門上均較為多元，預料可緩衝相關衝擊。

  ## 建設與電力板塊有能力消化減速

  羅麗儀表示，即便資料中心建設速度放緩，整體建設板塊應仍能因應此一調整，因為馬來西亞更廣泛的基礎設施建設仍在持續推進。他表示看好電力與公用事業板塊，援引結構性電力需求、國家能源轉型路線圖（NETR），以及太陽能與再生能源的持續投資，認為上述因素將支撐該板塊的投資吸引力，即便資料中心擴建在短期內趨於緩和。

  ## 間諜指控加重產業壓力

  上述監管行動的背景，是一起令馬來西亞股市持續承壓的間諜爭議。全球晶片大廠輝達（Nvidia）執行長黃仁勳（Jensen Huang）——輝達是美國出口管制核心高性能晶片的主要製造商——承認了一段婚外情。據報導，與其有染的女性疑為中國間諜，曾協助相關實體規避現行美國晶片出口管制。

  這名女性曾在新加坡商Megaspeed International擔任高管，該公司被描述為輝達晶片的主要買家，並據稱曾與馬來西亞科技業者合作。YTL電力（YTL Power）是此次事件中被點名的業者之一，該公司已否認涉及晶片流向中國的相關指控。

  此次指控事件發生後，馬來西亞股市科技股及相關產業類股的合計市值損失達數十億元。

  ## 後續發展

  依據已生效的BIS規定，馬來西亞境內為總部設於中國的企業客戶提供運算資源的資料中心業者，將面臨2027年2月17日的截止期限——業者須在此日期前取得美國出口授權，否則須調整客戶業務安排。至於尚未動工的專案，川普擬議中的海外資料中心稅，其條件與法律依據至今仍未明朗，對新投資決策的衝擊範圍因此依然存在高度不確定性。
