body_en: |
  Malaysia's government is drafting a regulatory framework that would require data centers to source at least 30% of their electricity from renewable energy, Energy Transition and Water Transformation Minister Datuk Amar Fadillah Yusof announced last month. The policy, still being finalized, could force some of the largest power consumers in the country into the solar market and unlock a pipeline of projects that has so far moved slowly.

  Sources told The Edge Malaysia that the government is exploring higher renewable energy targets for future data center projects powered off-grid, with a threshold as high as 50% under discussion for that category. No details have been published in an official document.

  The government has approved between 23 gigawatts (GW) and 23.5GW of data center capacity measured by maximum demand, according to data from the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (Miti). Of that, 27 facilities are expected to draw electricity off-grid, with a combined reported capacity of approximately 8GW. As of August, operational data centers had contracted 2.3GW in capacity demand. Total maximum data center demand is projected to exceed 8GW by 2030.

  If a 30% renewable energy rule is applied on a gigawatt-hour basis — measuring actual energy consumed over time rather than installed capacity — the math is demanding. Because solar generation is intermittent, a solar farm's peak capacity must be roughly 80% to 90% larger than the data center's power draw to deliver 30% of its annual energy consumption. A 100-megawatt (MW) data center would require approximately a 180-megawatt-peak (MWp) solar farm to meet that threshold. Applied to the 8GW demand projected for 2030, the rule could require as much as 14GWp of renewable energy capacity.

  ## Impact on CRESS

  The framework is expected to be the catalyst that the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme (CRESS) has been waiting for. CRESS allows renewable energy developers to sell electricity directly to large industrial customers, bypassing the standard utility tariff, but adoption has been limited because data centers — the scheme's primary target market — have been weighing the combined cost of solar energy plus fees to use the grid against simply drawing power from national utility Tenaga Nasional Bhd (Tenaga).

  A mandatory quota removes that choice. "Thirty per cent is a good number for the DCs. The requirement could be more after that," one source told The Edge.

  According to the latest publicly available data, CRESS has attracted 11 solar farm developers and eight end-consumers, representing more than 3GW in capacity. One project already under construction is a 100MW solar farm in Kedah, led by privately owned HLGY Energy Sdn Bhd in partnership with Singapore-headquartered Bridge Data Centres. Northern Solar Holdings Bhd has recently been awarded two CRESS projects totalling 189MW. Tenaga is separately reported to be exploring a 500MW solar supply arrangement with DayOne DCs.

  ## Tariff structure remains unsettled

  Despite the momentum, some developers are withholding judgment until the framework is published. "I won't celebrate yet, not until we see [the conditions] in an official document," one power generation executive told The Edge, citing recent shifts in grid usage charges.

  In September, the Ministry of Energy Transition and Water Transformation reduced the System Access Charge (SAC) — the fee CRESS participants pay to transmit solar electricity over Tenaga's grid — from 20 sen per kilowatt-hour (kWh) to 14 sen/kWh. The SAC is a key cost component that determines whether direct solar procurement is competitive with drawing grid power. However, the Energy Commission clarified in October that CRESS projects remain subject to an additional charge of 4 sen/kWh, partially offsetting the reduction.

  ## Eligibility questions

  The framework's precise definitions will determine which supply arrangements count toward the 30% quota. Industry players have raised several unresolved questions.

  One is whether CRESS itself qualifies as off-grid supply for data centers, or whether only dedicated self-consumption (Selco) solar installations — projects sited close to the consumption point and not routed through the grid — meet the off-grid definition. Selco arrangements are logistically more difficult to develop.

  A second question concerns the Green Electricity Tariff (GET), a product sold by Tenaga through the grid at a modest premium to the standard rate that lets buyers claim renewable energy attributes. GET supply of 6.6 terawatt-hours (TWh) has been fully subscribed, according to data from the Malaysia Green Attribute Tracking System (mGATS). If the GET quota were expanded and ruled eligible for the mandatory target, it could absorb much of the demand from the 2.3GW in contracted data center capacity already operational. One industry participant described that outcome as unlikely, stating: "The government's ultimate objective is new generation capacity. But nothing is official yet."

  ## Broader solar market

  The data center policy is one of several factors tightening the outlook for solar developers. Malaysia's electricity tariff has included a fuel surcharge since May, and official projections indicate the surcharge will stabilise at a higher level in coming months, potentially rising to between 6 and 8 sen/kWh. That increase makes solar economics more attractive relative to conventional grid power for all industrial users, not only data centers.

  CRESS is drawing interest beyond the data center sector. A 680MW joint development involving Gamuda Bhd, SD Guthrie Bhd and PETRONAS subsidiary Gentari Sdn Bhd is under way for a US-based technology company.

  Rooftop solar demand has also accelerated. Data from the Sustainable Energy Development Authority (Seda) shows more than 490MW in rooftop solar applications were submitted in the first ten months of 2026, with 307MW already operational.

  ## What comes next

  For utility-scale developers, the most immediate milestone is Malaysia's sixth Large-Scale Solar bidding round (LSS6), which concluded in late October with a total allocation of 2,650MW on offer. Winning bids are expected to be announced as early as January 2027.
body_zh: |
  馬來西亞能源轉型及水務轉型部長拿督阿瑪‧法迪拉‧尤索夫（Datuk Amar Fadillah Yusof）上月宣布，政府正研擬監管框架，要求資料中心至少30%電力必須來自再生能源。這項政策尚在最終定案階段，可能迫使全國部分最大電力消費業者進入太陽能市場，並為目前進展遲緩的太陽能建設管線打通障礙。

  《The Edge Malaysia》消息人士透露，政府正研議對未來採離網供電的新建資料中心訂定更高的再生能源目標，據悉討論中的門檻最高可達50%。目前官方尚未發布任何正式文件。

  根據投資、貿易及工業部（Miti）資料，政府已核准23至23.5吉瓦（GW）的資料中心容量（以最大需求計）。其中27座設施預計採離網取電，合計申報容量約8吉瓦。截至今年8月，已投入運營的資料中心已簽約2.3吉瓦容量需求。資料中心總最大需求預計到2030年將突破8吉瓦。

  若以千瓦時為基準——衡量實際消耗電量而非裝置容量——落實30%再生能源要求的計算相當嚴苛。由於太陽能發電具間歇性，太陽能農場的峰值容量必須比資料中心實際用電量大80%至90%，方能達成每年30%的供電比例。一座100百萬瓦（MW）的資料中心，約需一座180百萬瓦峰值（MWp）的太陽能農場方能達標。若將此規則套用至2030年預估的8吉瓦需求，所需再生能源裝置容量可能高達14吉瓦峰值（GWp）。

  ## 對CRESS的影響

  此框架預計將成為「企業再生能源供應計畫」（Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme，CRESS）期待已久的催化劑。CRESS允許再生能源開發商繞過標準電費，直接向大型工業用戶售電，但採用率有限——身為該計畫主要目標市場的資料中心業者，長期以來都在衡量太陽能電力成本加上輸電費用的總負擔，與直接向國家電力公司Tenaga Nasional Bhd（以下簡稱「Tenaga」）購電相比是否划算。

  強制配額消除了這個選擇空間。「30%對資料中心而言是個合理數字，之後要求可能還會更高，」一名消息人士告訴《The Edge》。

  根據最新公開資料，CRESS已吸引11家太陽能農場開發商及8家終端用戶，總容量逾3吉瓦。其中一個正在建設的項目，是位於吉打州的100百萬瓦太陽能農場，由私人企業HLGY Energy Sdn Bhd與總部位於新加坡的Bridge Data Centres合作開發。Northern Solar Holdings Bhd近期獲授兩個CRESS項目，合計189百萬瓦。Tenaga則另有報導指出，正在探討與DayOne DCs達成一項500百萬瓦的太陽能供電安排。

  ## 費率結構仍懸而未決

  儘管勢頭看好，部分開發商仍選擇靜觀其變，等待框架正式公布。「我還不想慶祝，要等到在官方文件裡看到（相關條件）才算，」一位發電業高管告訴《The Edge》，並提及近期電網使用費的變動。

  今年9月，能源轉型及水務轉型部將系統接入費（System Access Charge，SAC）——CRESS參與者透過Tenaga電網輸送太陽能電力所需繳交的費用——從每千瓦時20分降至14分。SAC是決定直採太陽能電力能否與電網電力競爭的關鍵成本項目。然而，能源委員會於10月澄清，CRESS項目仍須另繳每千瓦時4分的附加費，部分抵銷了先前的降費效果。

  ## 資格認定問題

  框架的精確定義將決定哪些供電安排可計入30%的配額，業界人士也提出了若干尚待厘清的問題。

  其一是CRESS本身是否符合「離網供電」的定義，還是只有專用自用（Selco）太陽能裝置——亦即緊鄰用電點設置、不經電網傳輸的項目——才符合離網定義。Selco安排在後勤上較難開發。

  其二是關於「綠色電力費率」（Green Electricity Tariff，GET）的問題。GET是Tenaga透過電網出售的產品，售價略高於標準費率，讓買方得以主張再生能源屬性。根據馬來西亞綠色屬性追蹤系統（mGATS）資料，GET供電量6.6兆瓦時（TWh）已全數售罄。若GET配額獲得擴大並被認定符合強制目標資格，可能將吸收現有已簽約2.3吉瓦容量的資料中心業者的大部分需求。一名業界人士認為此結果不太可能發生，並指出：「政府的最終目標是新增發電容量，但目前一切尚未正式定案。」

  ## 更廣泛的太陽能市場

  資料中心政策只是收緊太陽能開發商前景的諸多因素之一。馬來西亞電費自今年5月起加入燃料附加費，官方預測顯示附加費將在未來數月穩定於較高水準，可能升至每千瓦時6至8分，使太陽能的經濟效益相對傳統電網電力更具吸引力——受益對象不限於資料中心，而是涵蓋所有工業用戶。

  CRESS在資料中心以外的領域同樣備受矚目。由Gamuda Bhd、SD Guthrie Bhd與馬來西亞國家石油公司（PETRONAS）旗下子公司Gentari Sdn Bhd合作推動的一個680百萬瓦聯合開發案，正針對一家美國科技公司進行中。

  屋頂太陽能需求也明顯加速。永續能源發展局（Seda）資料顯示，2026年前十個月共提交逾490百萬瓦的屋頂太陽能申請，其中307百萬瓦已投入運營。

  ## 後續發展

  對於公用事業規模的開發商而言，最迫切的里程碑是馬來西亞第六輪大型太陽能競標（LSS6），競標已於10月底結束，總分配量為2,650百萬瓦。得標結果預計最早將於2027年1月公布。
