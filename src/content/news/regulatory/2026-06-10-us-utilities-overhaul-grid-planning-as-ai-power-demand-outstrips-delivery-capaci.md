body_en: |
  US utilities are restructuring financing, tariffs, and grid-planning processes to manage an AI-driven electricity demand wave that has produced far more announced projects than the grid can serve, according to utility filings, regulator warnings, and industry analysts.

  The disconnect is most visible in Ohio, where American Electric Power (AEP), one of the country's largest electric utilities, received more than 30 gigawatts (GW) of preliminary data center load requests — enough to power roughly 25 million average US homes. After implementing stricter financial requirements through its Data Center Tariff (DCT) process, AEP cut that figure to approximately 13 GW proceeding to formal load studies; ultimately, 5.6 GW advanced with signed Electric Service Agreements backed by financial commitments.

  That compression — from 30 GW to 5.6 GW — illustrates what Ihab Osman, an independent strategist specializing in data center and other mission-critical infrastructure, described as a structural shift in the market. "The market is moving from an 'announced demand' problem to a 'deliverable demand' problem," Osman said.

  Utilities are now sorting AI projects into a tiered credibility ladder: announced projects, formal load-study requests, signed service agreements, financed developments, active construction, and fully energized facilities. The widening gap between those tiers is forcing grid operators and regulators to rethink how infrastructure gets financed and built across Texas, Virginia, the Carolinas, Ohio, and other major data center corridors.

  ## From gradual growth to speculative gigawatts

  For decades, utilities built to gradual growth forecasts — population shifts, incremental industrial expansion — that unfolded slowly over years. Companies including OpenAI, Oracle, Crusoe, CoreWeave, and xAI are now pursuing campuses requiring hundreds of megawatts to potentially gigawatts each, collapsing the traditional planning timeline.

  Betsy Soehren Jones, a partner at West Monroe who focuses on AI, cybersecurity, and critical infrastructure risk across the utility sector, said the challenge has multiplied beyond AI alone. Utilities are simultaneously fielding demand growth from AI infrastructure, manufacturing, cryptocurrency mining, and Department of Defense modernization projects.

  "It's no longer, 'I've got a singular manufacturer coming into the state anymore,'" Jones said. "You've got lots of different variables, so it's got to be more portfolio-based."

  Quarterly filings from Duke Energy, AEP, and Exelon show companies restructuring procurement operations and customer agreements around surging AI-related electricity demand.

  ## Utilities reorganize supply chains and financing

  Duke Energy created procurement subsidiaries — known internally as "ProCos" — to secure transformers and other electrical equipment through specialized financing and leasing structures, as utilities confront worsening shortages and multiyear equipment lead times.

  Duke also extended a $10 billion master credit facility through 2031 and raised its five-year capital plan to $103 billion, citing rising demand from data centers and other large-load customers.

  AEP this month raised its own five-year capital plan to $78 billion and said it expects 63 GW of incremental contracted load by 2030, much of it tied to data centers.

  Wood Mackenzie projected the US data center electrical equipment market could grow from roughly $20 billion to $65 billion by 2030 as AI infrastructure drives demand for transformers, switchgear, and power-distribution systems.

  The Southeast has emerged as one of the fastest-growing AI infrastructure corridors because vertically integrated utilities there can often move generation and transmission projects faster than deregulated markets struggling with interconnection backlogs and fragmented planning systems.

  Analysis from Grid Strategies and Americans for a Clean Energy Grid (ACEG) found the United States may need roughly 5,000 miles of new high-capacity transmission lines annually through 2035 to maintain reliability and support rising electricity demand. Fewer than 1,000 miles were built in 2024. Utilities and regulators are also deploying grid-enhancing technologies to extract more capacity from existing infrastructure while transmission construction lags.

  ## Reliability risks prompt federal warning

  The North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC), the nonprofit body responsible for grid reliability standards across North America, this month issued a Level 3 "Essential Action" alert — its most urgent designation — warning that utilities and grid operators "generally did not have sufficient processes, procedures, or methods to address risks associated with computational loads," including AI training infrastructure and large data centers.

  NERC said rapidly changing computational loads could trigger voltage instability, oscillations, sudden load drops, and violent swings in power demand occurring within seconds — failure modes that traditional industrial-load planning frameworks were not designed to manage. The organization said it is developing a new "Computational Load Entity" category covering large facilities connected to the bulk power system.

  Working-group discussions within the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), which operates the Texas grid, have focused on how AI-oriented data centers, battery systems, and large electronic loads can create rapid power fluctuations and voltage instability. ERCOT stakeholders have also debated whether large AI facilities and on-site battery systems can reduce demand quickly enough to support grid stability during stressed operating conditions.

  ## Regulators and utilities rewrite the rules for large loads

  At the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), the federal agency overseeing interstate electricity markets and transmission, officials have opened proceedings examining how massive AI-related loads should connect to transmission systems and whether hyperscale customers should directly fund grid upgrades tied to their projects. The debates intensified after proposals involving co-located generation projects inside the PJM Interconnection footprint — the grid serving parts of the Mid-Atlantic and Midwest — raised questions about priority access to constrained power infrastructure.

  In Ohio, AEP proposed requiring large new data centers to commit to paying for at least 90 percent of the electricity capacity they request for a decade before the utility would build supporting infrastructure. "We need accurate plans and solid commitments from large data center customers so the right facilities are built at the right time," AEP Ohio President Marc Reitter said when the company filed the proposal.

  Utilities across multiple states are rolling out specialized large-load tariffs requiring hyperscale customers to post collateral and guarantee minimum electricity usage levels — a direct response to the risk that billions in grid investment could be stranded if AI development projects slow or consolidate.

  ## What the shift means for the industry

  The cumulative effect is a structural reset of how the US power sector contracts with its largest customers. Utilities are embedding financial and contractual guardrails into the planning process, demanding that developers prove commitment before grid infrastructure construction begins. The fear driving many of these decisions, according to the source reporting, is explicit: utilities could spend billions building for projects that never fully materialize.

  AEP's expectation of 63 GW of incremental contracted load by 2030 represents one of the most concrete near-term benchmarks for how much of the announced AI demand pipeline a major utility believes will prove durable.
body_zh: |
  美國各大電力公司正積極重整融資、費率及電網規劃流程，以應對AI驅動的電力需求浪潮。據電力公司申報文件、監管機構警告及業界分析師指出，這波浪潮催生的宣布開發案，遠遠超過電網實際能夠承載的上限。

  這個落差在俄亥俄州最為顯著。美國電力公司（American Electric Power，AEP）是全美規模最大的電力公司之一，該公司收到逾30吉瓦的初步資料中心負載申請——足以供應約2,500萬戶美國平均家庭用電。在透過「資料中心費率」（Data Center Tariff，DCT）流程實施更嚴格的財務要求後，AEP將這個數字削減至約13吉瓦，進入正式負載研究階段；最終，僅有5.6吉瓦附有已簽署電力服務協議及財務承諾的案件獲准推進。

  從30吉瓦壓縮至5.6吉瓦——這樣的縮減幅度，印證了專注於資料中心與其他關鍵任務基礎設施的獨立策略顧問伊哈布·奧斯曼（Ihab Osman）所描述的市場結構性轉變。「市場正從『宣布需求』問題轉向『可交付需求』問題，」奧斯曼說。

  電力公司如今將AI相關開發案按可信度排列成梯級：宣布的專案、正式負載研究申請、已簽署的服務協議、已取得融資的開發案、積極施工中的專案，以及已完全通電的設施。各梯級之間日益擴大的差距，正迫使電網業者與監管機構重新思考，德州、維吉尼亞州、南北卡羅來納州、俄亥俄州及其他主要資料中心走廊的基礎設施，究竟應如何融資與建設。

  ## 從緩步成長到投機性吉瓦需求

  數十年來，電力公司依據緩步成長的預測來規劃建設——人口遷移、工業逐步擴張——這些趨勢緩慢地在數年間展開。如今，包括OpenAI、甲骨文（Oracle）、Crusoe、CoreWeave及xAI在內的企業，正積極開發每座需要數百百萬瓦、甚至可能達數吉瓦規模的資料中心園區，傳統規劃時程因此被大幅壓縮。

  西門羅（West Monroe）合夥人貝琪·索倫·瓊斯（Betsy Soehren Jones）專注於電力產業的AI、網路安全與關鍵基礎設施風險。她表示，挑戰已不只是AI一個因素，電力公司同時面對來自AI基礎設施、製造業、加密貨幣挖礦以及美國國防部現代化專案的用電需求成長。

  「現在不再是『某家單一製造商要進駐某州』的情況了，」瓊斯說，「你面對的是許多不同的變數，因此必須用更全面的投資組合角度來綜合管理。」

  杜克能源（Duke Energy）、AEP及艾克斯朗（Exelon）的季度申報文件顯示，這些公司正圍繞著AI相關電力需求激增的趨勢，重整採購作業與客戶協議。

  ## 電力公司重組供應鏈與融資結構

  面對日益嚴峻的設備短缺與長達數年的交貨期，杜克能源成立了採購子公司——內部稱為「ProCos」——透過專屬融資與租賃架構，確保變壓器及其他電氣設備的供應。

  杜克能源同時將100億美元的主信用額度延長至2031年，並將五年資本計畫上調至1,030億美元，理由是來自資料中心及其他大型負載客戶的需求持續攀升。

  AEP本月將自身的五年資本計畫提高至780億美元，並表示預計到2030年前將增加63吉瓦的增量合約負載，其中大部分與資料中心有關。

  伍德麥肯錫（Wood Mackenzie）預測，隨著AI基礎設施推動對變壓器、開關設備及電力配電系統的需求，美國資料中心電氣設備市場可能從約200億美元，成長至2030年的650億美元。

  美國東南部已成為AI基礎設施成長最快速的走廊之一，原因在於當地垂直整合的電力公司，往往能以比解除管制市場更快的速度推動發電與輸電專案，而後者正深陷電網接入申請積壓與規劃系統碎片化的困境。

  電網策略公司（Grid Strategies）與美國清潔能源電網組織（Americans for a Clean Energy Grid，ACEG）的分析發現，美國到2035年前每年可能需要新建約5,000英里的高容量輸電線路，才能維持電網可靠性並因應電力需求成長。2024年實際完工的輸電線路不足1,000英里。在輸電建設進度落後的情況下，電力公司與監管機構也正積極部署電網強化技術，設法從現有基礎設施中挖掘更多容量。

  ## 可靠性風險引發聯邦警告

  北美電力可靠性公司（North American Electric Reliability Corporation，NERC）是負責北美電網可靠性標準的非營利機構。該機構本月發出第三級「必要行動」警報——其最高等級的緊急指定——警告電力公司與電網業者「普遍缺乏足夠的流程、程序或方法，以應對與計算負載相關的風險」，包括AI訓練基礎設施及大型資料中心。

  NERC指出，快速變化的計算負載可能引發電壓不穩定、震盪、突然的負載驟降，以及在數秒內發生的電力需求劇烈波動——這些失效模式是傳統工業負載規劃框架所無法因應的。NERC表示，正在研擬新的「計算負載實體」（Computational Load Entity）類別，涵蓋連接至主幹電力系統的大型設施。

  德州電力可靠性委員會（Electric Reliability Council of Texas，ERCOT）負責運營德州電網，其工作小組的討論焦點在於，以AI為導向的資料中心、電池儲能系統及大型電子負載，如何造成快速的電力波動與電壓不穩定。ERCOT的利益相關方也在討論，大型AI設施與現場電池系統能否在電網緊繃的運行條件下，迅速削減需求以支撐電網穩定性。

  ## 監管機構與電力公司重寫大型負載規則

  聯邦能源管制委員會（Federal Energy Regulatory Commission，FERC）是負責監管跨州電力市場與輸電的聯邦機構。委員會官員已啟動審查程序，探討大規模AI相關負載應如何接入輸電系統，以及超大規模客戶是否應直接出資支付與其開發案相關的電網升級費用。在PJM互聯電網（PJM Interconnection）範圍內——該電網服務中大西洋地區及中西部部分地區——涉及共置發電專案的提案引發外界質疑，究竟誰應優先使用受限的電力基礎設施，相關爭議也因此升溫。

  在俄亥俄州，AEP提出要求，大型新設資料中心在電力公司動工建設配套基礎設施之前，必須承諾在十年內支付所申請電力容量至少90%的費用。「我們需要大型資料中心客戶提供精確的計畫與明確的承諾，才能在正確的時間建設合適的設施，」AEP俄亥俄州總裁馬克·萊特（Marc Reitter）在公司提交該提案時表示。

  多州的電力公司正陸續推出針對大型負載的專屬費率，要求超大規模客戶繳交擔保品並保證最低用電量——這是對一旦AI開發專案放緩或整併、數十億美元電網投資恐淪為擱置資產的風險所做出的直接回應。

  ## 這場轉變對產業的意義

  上述種種加總，構成美國電力產業與其最大用戶之間合約關係的一次結構性重置。電力公司正將財務與合約的防護機制嵌入規劃流程，要求開發商在電網基礎設施動工之前，先行證明其投入承諾。據相關報導，驅動這些決策的隱憂是相當具體的：電力公司可能耗費數十億美元，為那些最終無法完全落地的開發案大興土木。

  AEP預計到2030年前實現63吉瓦的增量合約負載，代表了一家主要電力公司所能提出、最具體可信的近期基準，用以衡量宣布的AI需求管線中，究竟有多少能夠被證明是持久的。
