  ## Malaysia data centres at half capacity on power and water

  Data centres operating in Malaysia are consuming roughly half of the electricity and water capacity the government has authorised for them, Minister of Energy Transition and Water Transformation Datuk Seri Fadillah Yusof said in a written parliamentary reply on June 24, 2026.

  As of April 2026, data centres drew 1,102 megawatts (MW) of electricity against a maximum approved demand of 2,050 MW — 54% of the sanctioned ceiling. On water, operators reported consumption of 28.68 million litres per day (MLD) to the National Water Services Commission (known by its Malay acronym SPAN) as of January 2026, equivalent to 51.4% of the 55.83 MLD approved for operational facilities.

  ## Approval process and oversight

  Fadillah said the government routes all proposed data centre projects through the Data Centre Task Force (DCTF), an interministerial body that assesses whether developments are adequately planned and sustainable before granting approval. Both electricity and water are evaluated as part of that process.

  For water, state operators assess availability and coordinate with SPAN before submitting findings to the DCTF. SPAN has also issued guidelines covering planning, application, and operational stages for water supply at data centre developments, Fadillah said.

  For electricity, assessors examine whether the existing power supply system and grid network can accommodate a proposed project without reducing supply reliability for domestic, commercial, and industrial users. No approval is granted until those technical assessments are complete, the minister said.

  ## Why the gap matters

  The figures show that Malaysia has approved significantly more capacity than operators are currently drawing on. That headroom indicates either that facilities have been built out ahead of demand, that approved projects have not yet reached full operational load, or both. The minister did not elaborate on the reason for the gap.

  For industry planners and policymakers, the data provide an early indication that approvals are tracking ahead of actual consumption — a dynamic that regulators appear to be monitoring closely to ensure data centre growth does not crowd out existing electricity and water users.

  Fadillah said the government's measures are specifically designed to ensure the rapid expansion of data centres does not affect supplies for existing consumers, and that all assessments are completed before projects are authorised to proceed.

  ## 馬來西亞資料中心電力與用水量均僅達核准上限一半

  馬來西亞境內運營的資料中心，目前實際消耗的電力與用水，均約為政府核准上限的一半。能源轉型與水務改革部長拿督斯里法迪拉·尤索夫（Datuk Seri Fadillah Yusof）於2026年6月24日向國會提交書面答覆，公布了上述數據。

  截至2026年4月，馬國資料中心的電力需求為1,102百萬瓦（MW），而政府核准的最高用電上限為2,050 MW，實際使用量約佔核准上限的54%。在用水方面，業者截至2026年1月向國家水務委員會（馬來文縮寫：SPAN）申報的每日用水量為2,868萬公升，相當於已核准運營設施每日用水上限5,583萬公升的51.4%。

  ## 審查程序與監管機制

  法迪拉指出，政府已將所有資料中心開發案納入「資料中心任務小組」（DCTF）的跨部會審查流程，確保各案在規劃層面已臻完善且具備永續性，方才予以核准。電力與用水兩大面向均為評估的必要項目。

  在用水審查方面，州立水務業者須先評估供水可行性，並與SPAN協調後，再將結果提交DCTF。法迪拉表示，SPAN亦已就資料中心開發案的供水事宜，發布涵蓋規劃、申請及運營各階段的相關準則。

  在電力審查方面，評估人員須審核現有電力供應系統及電網，確認在容納新開發案的同時，不會降低住宅、商業及工業用戶的供電穩定性。部長強調，相關技術評估全數完成之前，任何案件均不得獲准推進。

  ## 供需落差的意涵

  上述數據顯示，馬來西亞政府核准的產能遠高於業者目前實際使用的水準。這一餘裕空間，可能反映設施已超前建置以因應未來需求、核准案件尚未達到滿載運營狀態，或兩者兼而有之。對於造成此落差的原因，部長並未進一步說明。

  對於產業規劃者與政策制定者而言，這份數據初步顯示核准量已領先實際消耗量——主管機關似乎正密切追蹤這一動態，以確保資料中心的快速擴張不會排擠既有電力與用水用戶的需求。

  法迪拉表示，政府所採行的各項措施，正是為了確保資料中心的快速擴張不影響現有消費者的供電與供水，並要求所有評估均須在專案獲准啟動之前全數完成。
