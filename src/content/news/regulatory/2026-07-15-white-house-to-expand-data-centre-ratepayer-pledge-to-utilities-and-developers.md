body_en: |
  The White House is preparing to expand the Ratepayer Protection Pledge, a voluntary commitment requiring large technology companies to cover the full cost of power infrastructure their data centres demand, to include electric utilities, data centre developers and state governors, according to Reuters. No additional signatories have been confirmed and the guest list for the expected expansion event has not been finalised.

  The pledge was signed by Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle and xAI at a White House ceremony on 4–5 March 2026, following an announcement by President Trump in his 24 February State of the Union address.

  ## What the pledge requires

  The pledge sets four core obligations. Signatories must build, buy or otherwise bring to market new generation resources sufficient to meet their own data centre electricity demand, paying the full cost of those resources. They must negotiate separate rate structures with utilities and state governments in each jurisdiction where they build, and pay those rates whether or not the power is actually consumed — a provision designed to prevent stranded-asset costs from falling on residential electricity customers. Companies also commit to local hiring and workforce development, and their power resources are expected to support grid stability during emergencies.

  ## Why it was created

  Data centre construction has accelerated sharply. January 2026 project starts reached a record $25.2 billion, according to construction data firm ConstructConnect. That surge has driven a corresponding rise in electricity demand, prompting state regulators, consumer advocates and lawmakers to warn that household ratepayers could end up subsidising grid capacity built primarily to serve hyperscale artificial intelligence facilities.

  ## State enforcement is ahead of federal action

  The pledge is voluntary and carries no federal penalty for non-compliance, but Virginia and Ohio have moved furthest toward making similar requirements binding.

  Virginia's State Corporation Commission approved a new rate class for Dominion Energy customers drawing 25 megawatts or more — roughly the continuous draw of a mid-to-large data centre building — effective January 2027. Those customers must sign 14-year contracts, pay at least 85% of transmission and distribution costs and 60% of generation costs, and post $1.5 million per megawatt in collateral.

  Ohio's Public Utilities Commission approved a comparable tariff for AEP Ohio customers, requiring large-load customers to pay at least 85% of their contracted capacity for up to 12 years regardless of actual electricity consumption. Customers that cancel or delay a project by more than 12 months become liable for the full construction cost.

  ## Congressional and public pressure is mounting

  A House Energy and Commerce Subcommittee hearing on 29 April 2026 examined several bills addressing data centre energy demand. In June, Representatives Kathy Castor and Gabe Evans introduced a proposed Ratepayer Protection Act. House Energy and Commerce Committee chairman Brett Guthrie has said he wants the pledge's terms put into statute.

  Public opinion is also shifting. A Marquette University Law School poll of Wisconsin voters found the share who believe data centre costs outweigh the benefits rose from 55% to 70% within six months.

  ## What this means for operators and developers

  For data centre operators and the connectivity providers that serve them, the pledge's expected expansion signals that cost allocation is becoming a formal precondition for new development in the United States, not a discretionary public relations commitment. Separate utility rate negotiations, longer-term power contracts with collateral requirements and growing state-level scrutiny are set to become standard features of site selection and permitting across more jurisdictions.

  The White House event intended to bring utilities, developers and governors into the pledge framework has not been scheduled with a confirmed date or finalised participant list.
body_zh: |
  根據路透社報導，白宮正準備擴大「用電戶保護承諾」（Ratepayer Protection Pledge）的適用範圍——這項自願性承諾要求大型科技公司承擔其資料中心所需全部電力基礎設施費用——預計將進一步納入電力公司、資料中心開發商及各州州長。目前尚未確認新加入的簽署方，預計擴大儀式的受邀名單也尚未敲定。

  該承諾由亞馬遜、Google、Meta、微軟、OpenAI、甲骨文（Oracle）及xAI，於2026年3月4日至5日在白宮舉行的儀式上簽署，起因於川普總統在2月24日國情咨文中的相關宣布。

  ## 承諾的核心義務

  該承諾設有四項核心義務。簽署方必須自行興建、購買或以其他方式將足以滿足其資料中心電力需求的新發電資源引入市場，並承擔這些資源的全部費用。他們須在每個設廠的轄區內，分別與電力公司及州政府協商獨立的費率結構，並無論實際用電量多寡均須支付這些費率——此條款旨在防止電力設施閒置產生的「擱淺資產」成本轉嫁至住宅用電戶。此外，各公司還承諾優先僱用在地人才並推動勞動力培育，其電力資源亦須在緊急情況下支援電網的穩定運作。

  ## 承諾設立的緣由

  資料中心建設近年急速加速。根據建設數據公司ConstructConnect的資料，2026年1月單月新開工專案金額創下252億美元的歷史紀錄。此一熱潮帶動電力需求同步攀升，促使州級監管機構、消費者倡議團體及立法者相繼警告，住宅用電戶恐將被迫補貼主要服務於超大規模人工智慧設施的電網容量建設。

  ## 州層級執法走在聯邦前面

  該承諾屬自願性質，聯邦層級並無違規罰則，但維吉尼亞州與俄亥俄州已走在最前面，著手將類似規定轉化為具法律約束力的規範。

  維吉尼亞州企業委員會批准為用電量達25百萬瓦（MW）以上的Dominion Energy用戶——大約相當於一棟中大型資料中心建築的持續用電量——設立新費率類別，自2027年1月起生效。這類用戶須簽訂14年合約、支付至少85%的輸配電費用及60%的發電費用，並繳納每百萬瓦150萬美元的保證金。

  俄亥俄州公用事業委員會也為AEP Ohio的用戶批准了類似費率，要求大用電量客戶在最長12年的合約期內，無論實際用電量如何，均須支付至少85%的簽約容量費用。取消或延誤專案超過12個月的用戶，將須承擔全部建設費用。

  ## 國會與民間壓力持續升高

  眾議院能源與商業小組委員會於2026年4月29日召開聽證會，審查多項涉及資料中心能源需求的法案。6月，眾議員凱西·卡斯特（Kathy Castor）與蓋布·埃文斯（Gabe Evans）共同提出「用電戶保護法案」（Ratepayer Protection Act）草案。眾議院能源與商業委員會主席布雷特·葛斯里（Brett Guthrie）表示，他希望將承諾條款正式入法。

  民間輿論也出現明顯轉向。馬凱特大學法學院針對威斯康辛州選民所做的民調顯示，認為資料中心帶來的成本大於效益的受訪者比例，在短短六個月內從55%上升至70%。

  ## 對業者與開發商的影響

  對於資料中心業者及其連接服務提供商而言，承諾範圍的預期擴大，意味著費用分攤正逐漸成為在美國推動新開發案的正式前提條件，而非自由裁量的公關承諾。分別與電力公司協商費率、附帶保證金要求的長期電力合約，以及日益嚴格的州級審查，將成為更多轄區在廠址選擇與許可申請過程中的標準要件。

  白宮原定舉辦的、旨在將電力公司、開發商與各州州長納入承諾框架的活動，目前尚未確定日期，受邀名單也尚未定案。
