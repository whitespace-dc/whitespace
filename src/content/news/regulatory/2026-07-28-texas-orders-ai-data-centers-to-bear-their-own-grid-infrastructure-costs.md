  ## Texas orders AI data centers to bear their own grid infrastructure costs

  Texas Gov. Greg Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) to require AI data center developers to pay their share of electric grid infrastructure costs and reduce the burden on residential customers, in a June 10 directive that has since produced a broad package of completed rulemakings, pending reforms, and legislative recommendations.

  The directive instructed PUCT to reduce residential transmission costs, require data centers to fund their own grid connections, and identify additional regulatory and legislative actions as large computational loads expand rapidly across the state. Abbott wrote that "the rapid scale of data center development requires oversight to ensure everyday Texans are not burdened with the costs of infrastructure driven by data center expansion."

  Last week, PUCT published a regulatory roadmap covering interconnection standards, transmission cost allocation, load forecasting, reliability programs, and large-load planning. The package represents the commission's formal response to Abbott's order.

  ## What the pending rulemaking would require

  A rulemaking still in progress would impose several new obligations on large-load customers — companies seeking to connect power-hungry facilities such as AI data centers to the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), the grid operator that manages electricity for most of the state.

  Under the proposed rules, large-load developers would be required to post financial security before entering ERCOT planning studies, disclose operational information to regulators, and pay direct interconnection costs. Developers would also begin paying transmission charges once grid capacity becomes available, even if their facilities have not yet started operating. PUCT said it expects to complete the rulemaking by December.

  PUCT Chairman Thomas Gleeson framed the changes as a balance between economic development and ratepayer protection. "We should welcome responsible economic development," Gleeson wrote, "but it must do so in a manner that prioritizes affordability, reliability and the interests of the residents who depend on the grid."

  ## ERCOT's new batch evaluation process

  The regulatory filing also highlights ERCOT's new Batch Zero process, which evaluates proposed loads of at least 75 megawatts — roughly the electricity demand of a mid-size city neighborhood — collectively rather than one at a time. The one-time study will determine available grid capacity by location and year through 2032, allocate transmission capacity among qualified projects, and identify upgrades needed for committed developments. ERCOT expects batch studies to become a permanent part of its planning process in 2027.

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, told Data Center Knowledge that Batch Zero represents a fundamental shift in how ERCOT handles large-load requests. Rather than treating each proposed project as an independent ask for capacity, the new process evaluates qualified projects together to better assess their combined impact on the transmission system.

  "At sufficient scale, they become system-planning assumptions," Osnato said, describing the logic behind the collective approach.

  Osnato raised a concern about the process, however: whether the eligibility criteria are strong enough to separate submitted megawatts from projects that are actually financeable, buildable, and durable. A developer clearing the financial security requirement does not guarantee a project gets built, he said. "A well-funded developer can still delay, downsize, relocate, sell the project, change its power strategy, or abandon the site."

  ## Completed actions on cost allocation and forecasting

  PUCT also pointed to actions already completed. Regulators have standardized requirements for utilities to incorporate new large loads into ERCOT demand forecasts, which the commission said improves forecast accuracy. Separate rules now prevent existing generation capacity from being redirected to serve new data centers, preserving supply already committed to current customers.

  PUCT's transmission cost review concluded that rapidly growing computational loads are driving major infrastructure investment that could raise electricity bills for residential customers and small businesses under existing policies. Regulators said they have begun revising cost-allocation rules so that large loads bear more of those costs directly.

  ## Reliability program and legislative requests

  PUCT and ERCOT are developing a reliability program under Senate Bill 6 that would compensate large electricity users for voluntarily reducing demand before anticipated grid emergencies. Under the program, large-load developers would also be required to disclose their ability to curtail operations or rely on backup generation during emergencies.

  The commission is asking the Texas Legislature to authorize direct ERCOT communication with large computational load customers, require those facilities to formally register with both ERCOT and PUCT, and extend the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act — a critical infrastructure security law — to large computational load owners.

  Abbott separately pledged to pursue legislation requiring new data centers to use water-efficient cooling, report annual electricity and water consumption, repeal existing sales tax exemptions and other incentives, and adopt setbacks and noise-reduction measures to limit community impacts.

  ## What this means for data center development in Texas

  The regulatory package signals that Texas, which recently surpassed Virginia as a leading data center market, intends to channel continued growth through a framework that distributes infrastructure costs to the developers generating the demand rather than spreading them across the residential rate base. The combination of financial security requirements, direct interconnection charges, pre-operational transmission fees, and collective capacity planning marks a departure from the more permissive interconnection environment that helped attract large computational loads to the state.

  PUCT's December target for completing the large-load rulemaking sets the near-term deadline by which developers seeking ERCOT interconnection will need to understand the full scope of new financial and disclosure obligations.

  ## 德州要求AI資料中心自行負擔電網基礎設施成本

  德州州長葛瑞格·艾伯特於6月10日發出指令，要求德州公用事業委員會（PUCT）強制AI資料中心開發商支付其應負擔的電網基礎設施費用，以減輕一般民眾的壓力。這項指令自發布以來，已催生出一整套已完成的法規制定、待審改革措施及立法建議。

  艾伯特在指令中要求PUCT降低住宅用電的輸電費用、要求資料中心自行出資建設電網連接設施，並在大型算力負載於全州快速擴張之際，找出其他應採取的監管與立法行動。他寫道：「資料中心發展規模之迅速，需要主管機關加強監督，確保德州一般民眾不必為資料中心擴張所帶動的基礎設施建設買單。」

  上週，PUCT發布了一份涵蓋併網標準、輸電成本分攤、負載預測、可靠性計畫及大型負載規劃的監管路線圖，正式回應艾伯特的命令。

  ## 待審法規將帶來哪些要求

  目前仍在推進中的一項法規制定程序，將對大型用電戶——即有意將耗電設施（如AI資料中心）接入德州電力可靠性委員會（ERCOT）電網的企業——課以數項新義務。ERCOT負責管理德州大部分地區的電力調度。

  根據擬議中的規定，大型負載開發商在進入ERCOT規劃研究程序之前，必須繳交財務擔保金、向主管機關揭露營運資訊，並支付直接併網費用。此外，一旦電網容量備妥，開發商即須開始繳納輸電費用，即便其設施尚未實際投入運營。PUCT表示，預計於12月前完成上述法規制定。

  PUCT主委湯瑪斯·葛里森將這些調整定位為促進經濟發展與保護用戶之間的平衡。他寫道：「我們應歡迎負責任的經濟發展，但這樣的發展必須以優先兼顧負擔能力、供電可靠性，以及依賴電網維持生活的居民利益為前提。」

  ## ERCOT的新批次評估程序

  此次提交的監管文件也突顯了ERCOT新推出的「零批次」（Batch Zero）程序。這項程序以集中評估方式處理規模至少達75百萬瓦（MW）的擬議負載——大致相當於一個中型城市社區的用電需求——而非逐案審查。這項一次性研究將評估各地點至2032年的可用電網容量、在合格專案之間分配輸電容量，並識別已承諾開發案所需的電網升級工程。ERCOT預計批次研究將於2027年成為其規劃流程的常設機制。

  Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾·奧斯納托向Data Center Knowledge表示，零批次程序代表ERCOT處理大型負載申請方式的根本性轉變。新程序不再將每個擬議專案視為各自獨立的容量申請，而是將合格專案放在一起評估，以更準確地衡量其對輸電系統的整體影響。

  「規模夠大時，這些需求就會成為系統規劃的基本假設，」奧斯納托如此描述集中評估方式背後的邏輯。

  不過，奧斯納托也對這項程序表達了一項顧慮：資格審核標準是否足夠嚴格，能夠將已申報的百萬瓦數，與真正具備融資能力、可實際興建且能持續運營的專案區分開來。他指出，通過財務擔保要求的開發商，並不保證其專案最終會動工。「資金充裕的開發商仍可能延遲施工、縮小規模、異地重建、出售專案、改變供電策略，或棄置基地。」

  ## 成本分攤與負載預測的已完成措施

  PUCT也指出了已完成的若干行動。監管機關已統一要求各電力公司將新增大型負載納入ERCOT需求預測，委員會表示此舉有助於提升預測準確度。另有獨立規定禁止將現有發電容量轉移用於服務新設資料中心，以保障已承諾供應給現有用戶的電力。

  PUCT的輸電成本審查結論指出，算力負載快速增長正在驅動大規模基礎設施投資，若現行政策不變，恐將推高住宅用戶與小型企業的電費。監管機關表示，已開始修訂成本分攤規則，使大型用電戶直接承擔更多相關費用。

  ## 可靠性計畫與立法請求

  PUCT與ERCOT正依據《參議院第6號法案》共同研擬一項可靠性計畫，將補償在電網緊急狀況預警前自願降低用電量的大型用電戶。依據該計畫，大型負載開發商也須揭露其在緊急情況下削減運營或啟用備用發電的能力。

  委員會同時向德州立法機構提出多項立法請求：授權ERCOT直接與大型算力負載用戶溝通、要求上述設施同時向ERCOT與PUCT進行正式登記，並將《孤星基礎設施保護法》（Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act）——一部關鍵基礎設施安全法律——的適用範圍擴及大型算力負載所有人。

  艾伯特也另行承諾推動立法，要求新設資料中心採用節水冷卻系統、每年申報用電量與用水量、廢除現行銷售稅豁免及其他獎勵措施，並遵守退縮距離及降噪要求，以減少對周邊社區的影響。

  ## 對德州資料中心發展的意涵

  這套監管方案釋出明確訊號：德州——近期已超越維吉尼亞州，躍升為主要資料中心市場——意在將持續湧入的成長動能，導入一套將基礎設施費用轉由需求製造者（即開發商）承擔、而非平攤至住宅費率基礎的監管框架之中。財務擔保要求、直接併網費用、預運營輸電費，以及集中容量規劃等措施相互結合，標誌著此前吸引大量算力負載進駐的寬鬆併網環境，正式走入歷史。

  PUCT訂下的12月法規完成期限，也為有意申請ERCOT併網的開發商劃定了近期截止日期——屆時，他們必須全面掌握所有新增財務義務與資訊揭露要求的完整範疇。
