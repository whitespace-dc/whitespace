body_en: |
  North Carolina has repealed the sales and use tax exemption on electricity purchased by qualifying data centers, ending a key operating cost subsidy while preserving tax breaks on equipment and capital investments, after Gov. Josh Stein signed the state's 2026 budget this week.

  Signing the budget, Stein cited the repeal as one of the measure's accomplishments, saying it "eliminates tax exemptions for data centers' electricity use." The change took effect upon budget passage.

  ## What the budget changes

  The budget removes the electricity exemption from North Carolina's data center sales and use tax program. Exemptions covering qualifying equipment and other eligible investments under existing statutes remain in place.

  According to the North Carolina General Assembly's Fiscal Research Division, eliminating the electricity exemption will increase General Fund revenue by $21.4 million in fiscal year 2026-27, rising to $28.6 million annually by fiscal year 2030-31.

  ## Industry reaction

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, said the repeal shifts the economic calculus for large AI data campuses, where electricity represents a substantial share of operating expenses, but he cautioned against overstating its weight in siting decisions.

  "Repealing the electricity sales tax exemption changes the economics at the margin, especially for very large AI campuses where power is not a minor operating expense," Osnato said. "But I would not view it as the primary siting variable."

  Osnato said developers continue to prioritize whether utilities can deliver power on schedule, whether transmission infrastructure can support growing loads, and whether long-term operating costs remain predictable. "For AI-scale data centers, the first-order questions remain: Can I get power? Can it be delivered on the timeline I need? Can the transmission system support the load? Can the interconnection process move fast enough? Can the operating cost remain durable over the life of the investment?"

  Dan Diorio, vice president of state policy at the Data Center Coalition, an industry group, said the preserved incentives still provide a foundation for future investment. "While the electricity sales tax exemption was an important part of the overall data center sales tax exemption program, we are happy that this budget provides certainty for data centers to continue investing in North Carolina," Diorio said. He noted that the industry intends to continue working with state and local leaders to support development and keep North Carolina competitive.

  ## A signal on state incentive policy

  Osnato framed the repeal as part of a broader policy shift in how states may approach AI infrastructure incentives going forward. He argued the change separates two incentives that have often been treated as one: encouraging the construction of data centers versus subsidizing the electricity they consume across decades of operation.

  "The tax change matters because it signals that states may be becoming less willing to subsidize open-ended electricity consumption while still supporting capital investment, equipment, construction, and local economic development," Osnato said. "For operators, the implication is simple: power availability, deliverability, and cost durability are becoming more important than headline incentive packages."

  ## Pending legislation

  The budget action coincides with ongoing legislative debate over broader policies governing AI-era electricity demand in North Carolina. Senate Bill 730, the proposed Ratepayer Protection Act, would require special utility service agreements for qualifying large-load customers and establish additional requirements for future data center development. The bill has not yet been enacted.

  Taken together, the enacted budget change and the pending legislation indicate that North Carolina is adjusting rather than abandoning its approach to AI infrastructure: continuing to incentivize capital investment while applying greater scrutiny to the long-term public costs associated with rising electricity demand.

  Data Center Knowledge noted it had requested comment from Duke Energy and Gov. Stein's office before publication and that the story would be updated if responses were received.
body_zh: |
  北卡羅來納州州長喬許・史坦（Josh Stein）本週簽署2026年州預算案，正式廢除合格資料中心購電所享有的銷售及使用稅豁免，終結這項關鍵的營運成本補貼，但同步保留設備採購與資本投資的相關稅務優惠。

  史坦在簽署預算案時，將此廢除措施列為重要施政成果之一，表示此舉「取消了資料中心電力使用的稅務豁免」。此項變更於預算案通過後即刻生效。

  ## 預算案的具體變更

  此次預算案將電力豁免從北卡羅來納州資料中心銷售及使用稅方案中移除，但現行法規中涵蓋合格設備及其他合規投資的豁免條款仍繼續有效。

  根據北卡羅來納州議會財政研究部門的估算，廢除電力豁免將使州普通基金在2026至2027財政年度增加2,140萬美元的收入，並於2030至2031財政年度逐步上升至每年2,860萬美元。

  ## 業界反應

  Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾・奧斯納托（Neil Osnato）表示，此項廢除措施改變了大型AI資料園區的經濟盤算——在這類設施中，電費佔營運支出的比重相當可觀——但他也提醒外界不應過度高估其對選址決策的影響力。

  「廢除電力銷售稅豁免在邊際上改變了經濟效益，尤其是對電費並非小額支出的超大型AI園區而言，」奧斯納托說，「但我不會把它視為選址的首要考量因素。」

  奧斯納托指出，開發商仍優先考慮公用電力業者能否如期供電、輸電基礎設施能否支撐日益成長的用電需求，以及長期營運成本是否可預期。「對於AI規模的資料中心而言，核心問題依舊是：我能取得電力嗎？能在所需時程內完成供電嗎？輸電系統能否承載這樣的負載？併網程序能否以足夠快的速度推進？投資存續期間的營運成本能否保持穩定？」

  資料中心聯盟（Data Center Coalition）州政策副總裁丹・迪奧里奧（Dan Diorio）表示，現行保留的獎勵措施仍為未來投資奠定基礎。「雖然電力銷售稅豁免是整體資料中心銷售稅優惠方案的重要組成部分，但我們很高興這份預算案為資料中心持續在北卡羅來納州投資提供了確定性，」迪奧里奧說。他並指出，業界將持續與州及地方領導人合作，推動產業發展並維持北卡羅來納州的競爭力。

  ## 州政府獎勵政策的風向轉變

  奧斯納托將這次廢除措施定位為各州未來處理AI基礎設施獎勵政策時更廣泛政策轉向的一環。他認為，這項變更將過去常被視為一體的兩種獎勵明確區分開來：鼓勵資料中心的興建，與補貼其長達數十年的電力消耗，本質上是兩回事。

  「此次稅制變更意義重大，因為它釋放出一個信號：各州或許正逐漸不願補貼無上限的電力消耗，但仍願意支持資本投資、設備採購、工程建設與地方經濟發展，」奧斯納托說。「對於業者而言，這意味著電力的可及性、可靠供應以及成本的長期穩定性，正變得比表面上的獎勵優惠更為重要。」

  ## 待審法案

  預算案的通過，與北卡羅來納州就AI時代電力需求制定更完整政策的立法辯論同步進行。第730號參議院法案，即擬議中的《電力用戶保護法》（Ratepayer Protection Act），將要求對合格大用電量客戶採用特別電力服務協議，並為未來的資料中心開發設立額外規範，但該法案目前尚未通過立法。

  綜觀已通過的預算變更與待審法案，可以看出北卡羅來納州正在調整而非放棄其AI基礎設施發展策略：繼續以稅務優惠鼓勵資本投資，同時對電力需求持續上升所帶來的長期公共成本採取更嚴格的審視態度。

  《資料中心知識》（Data Center Knowledge）表示，該媒體在發稿前曾向杜克能源（Duke Energy）及史坦州長辦公室請求置評，若收到回應將更新報導。
