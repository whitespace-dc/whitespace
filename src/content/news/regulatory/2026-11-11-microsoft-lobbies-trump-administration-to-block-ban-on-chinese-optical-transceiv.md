body_en: |
  Microsoft has been pressing the Trump administration to halt plans to bar U.S. imports of Chinese optical transceivers, components that connect servers within artificial intelligence data centers over fiber-optic cables, with the company and other technology firms warning that such a move could damage the U.S. advantage in the global AI race, three people familiar with the situation said.

  The lobbying effort, which has not been previously reported, centers on a proposal being developed by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), the U.S. telecommunications regulator. Reuters reported in August that the administration was pursuing a ban on new models of Chinese optical transceivers over concerns that the devices could be used by Beijing to steal data, install malware, or disrupt service at U.S. data centers.

  Microsoft was among a group of companies and trade groups that met with officials from the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) and the National Security Council (NSC) in September to flag supply chain challenges a ban would create, according to two of the sources. Microsoft declined to comment. The White House and the FCC did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

  The company has 35 data centers under construction in the United States and another 134 planned, according to Cleanview, a market intelligence company that tracks data center development.

  ## Why the industry says a ban would halt AI construction

  Optical transceivers allow data to travel over fiber-optic cables at the speed of light within data centers, linking servers, routers, and switches. Some data centers require millions of the devices.

  John Miller, general counsel of the Information Technology Industry Council (ITI), a tech industry lobbying group that has publicly urged the administration not to proceed with a ban, said the restriction could "arguably grind [the data centre roll-out] to a halt." Cloud companies have already purchased the AI chips required for their buildouts, Miller said, but those chips cannot function without the transceivers that connect them. "Those chips, without optical transceivers literally could just sit there waiting around un-utilised until you actually have the transceivers to essentially connect them together," he said.

  ITI sent a letter to the FCC on August 13 calling on the regulator to "refrain from adding optical transceivers" to a trade blacklist, citing "potential effects that supply disruptions could have on data centre deployment and US AI leadership."

  ## China's control of optical transceiver supply

  China accounts for approximately two-thirds of the global optical transceiver market, according to Counterpoint Research. Chinese firms Eoptolink and Zhongji Innolight, the latter of which is on the Pentagon's list of companies with ties to the Chinese military, dominate global supply and count major U.S. cloud providers among their customers.

  U.S.-based manufacturers Coherent and Lumentum produce competing technology but lack the scale to replace Chinese vendors, according to a report by the Foundation for American Innovation.

  The Chinese Embassy in Washington said Beijing "firmly opposes the US overstretching the concept of national security and abusing state power to unjustifiably suppress Chinese companies" and stated that "China will continue to firmly safeguard the lawful rights and interests of Chinese companies."

  ## A narrower threshold under consideration

  The administration may be weighing a targeted restriction rather than a broad ban. Morgan Stanley said in an October client note that officials are considering banning only Chinese optical transceivers capable of handling 3.2 terabits per second (Tbps) of data, a next-generation specification whose production is not expected to ramp until 2028 and whose widespread installation is not anticipated until 2029. Two of the sources confirmed the 3.2 Tbps threshold is under consideration.

  Congress may move more quickly on narrower grounds. In September, a bipartisan group of U.S. lawmakers introduced legislation to bar the federal government from equipping sensitive government systems with Chinese-made optical transceivers, a measure that falls short of a full import ban but signals continued legislative pressure on the issue.

  ## 微軟遊說川普政府阻止封禁中國光收發器

  據三名知情人士透露，微軟正積極向川普政府施壓，要求阻止美國禁止進口中國光收發器——這是一種透過光纖電纜連接人工智慧資料中心內各伺服器的關鍵元件。微軟及其他科技業者警告，此舉恐損及美國在全球AI競賽中的優勢地位。

  這場迄今尚未曝光的遊說行動，矛頭指向美國電信主管機關「聯邦通訊委員會」（FCC）正在研擬的一項提案。路透社曾於今年八月報導，川普政府正著手推動禁止新型中國光收發器進口，理由是擔憂這些裝置可能遭北京用於竊取資料、植入惡意程式，或破壞美國資料中心的正常運作。

  據其中兩名消息人士表示，微軟與多家企業及產業公會代表，已於九月間與白宮科技政策辦公室（OSTP）及國家安全委員會（NSC）官員召開會議，就禁令可能引發的供應鏈衝擊提出示警。對此，微軟拒絕發表評論；白宮與FCC則未即時回應媒體的置評請求。

  根據追蹤資料中心建設動態的市場情報公司Cleanview的資料，微軟目前在美國境內有三十五座資料中心正在興建，另有一百三十四座列入規劃。

  ## 業界為何認為禁令將使AI建設陷入停頓

  光收發器讓資料得以在資料中心內部透過光纖電纜以光速傳輸，串聯起各台伺服器、路由器與交換器。部分資料中心所需的光收發器數量高達數百萬個。

  科技產業遊說團體「資訊科技產業委員會」（ITI）法律總顧問約翰·米勒（John Miller）表示，相關限制「可說是會讓資料中心的建設部署完全停擺」。他指出，雲端業者已購入AI建設所需的AI晶片，但這些晶片少了光收發器的串聯就無法運作。「這些晶片，少了光收發器，說白了只能擺在那裡白白閒置，等到光收發器到位，才能真正把它們連接起來。」

  ITI已於八月十三日致函FCC，呼籲主管機關「暫勿將光收發器列入」貿易黑名單，理由是「供應中斷對資料中心部署及美國AI領導地位可能造成的潛在影響」。

  ## 中國掌控光收發器全球供應鏈

  根據Counterpoint Research的調查，中國約佔全球光收發器市場三分之二的份額。中國廠商億博光通（Eoptolink）與中際旭創（Zhongji Innolight）主導全球供應，其中中際旭創更列名五角大廈公布的「涉及中國軍方企業」名單，兩家公司的客戶均包含多家美國主要雲端服務業者。

  美國本土製造商Coherent與Lumentum雖有能力生產競爭性產品，但根據「美國創新基金會」（Foundation for American Innovation）發布的報告，兩者的產能規模不足以取代中國供應商。

  中國駐華盛頓大使館則表示，北京「堅決反對美方泛化國家安全概念、濫用國家權力，對中國企業進行無理打壓」，並強調「中方將繼續堅定維護中國企業的合法權益」。

  ## 當局考慮採取更精準的限制門檻

  川普政府或許正在評估採行針對性限制，而非全面封禁。摩根士丹利於十月間發布的客戶報告指出，官員正考慮僅禁止傳輸速率達每秒三點二兆位元（3.2 Tbps）的中國光收發器——這是一種次世代規格，預計要到二○二八年才能大規模量產，二○二九年才有望普及安裝。兩名消息人士確認，3.2 Tbps的門檻確實在討論之列。

  國會方面或許會在更窄的範疇上率先採取行動。今年九月，美國兩黨國會議員共同提出法案，擬禁止聯邦政府在敏感政府系統中安裝中國製光收發器。此舉雖未達全面禁止進口的程度，卻釋出了立法機構持續向這一議題施壓的明確訊號。
