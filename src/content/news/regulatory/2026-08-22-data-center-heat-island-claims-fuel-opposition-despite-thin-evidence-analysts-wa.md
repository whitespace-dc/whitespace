  ## Data centers face heat island scrutiny as unreviewed study fuels opposition

  An academic paper proposing that data centers raise surrounding land surface temperatures by an average of 2°C — and occasionally by as much as 9°C (16°F) — has generated significant media coverage and been seized upon by community opposition groups, even though it has not yet undergone peer review and its methodology has drawn sharp criticism from the Uptime Institute, a data center industry research and standards body.

  The paper, titled *The data heat island effect: quantifying the impact of AI data centers in a warming world*, was led by Professor Andrea Marinoni of the University of Cambridge (UK). It used a NASA global land surface temperature dataset spanning 2004 to 2024, with a spatial resolution of 500 meters (roughly 547 yards), to analyze locations surrounding 8,472 data centers built outside urban areas. The analysis attributed observed temperature increases to waste heat emitted by facility cooling systems.

  CNN described the findings as an "alarming" discovery. During a June 2026 heatwave in the United Kingdom, residents near Slough — a major European data center hub — complained that the area was generating hotspots exceeding 36°C (97°F). In the United States, Good Jobs First, a national policy resource center that tracks corporate subsidies and opposes data center developments, added the heat island effect to the list of community harms that operators should address through Local Benefits Agreements, which are negotiated mechanisms intended to neutralize local opposition.

  ## What the study actually measured

  The dataset underpinning the paper consists mostly of smaller facilities in the 10 to 20 megawatt (MW) range — roughly the output of a small power plant — that were built before the recent surge in artificial intelligence-driven demand. The paper describes them as "AI hyperscalers," a term typically applied to large cloud and AI facilities of several hundred megawatts, but according to Uptime Institute the characterization does not match the dataset.

  Uptime Institute also challenged the physical attribution. Facilities of 10 to 20 MW produce relatively little waste heat compared with the scale of warming the paper describes, and that heat disperses in a directional plume rather than radiating outward uniformly, making it an unlikely cause of the symmetric temperature increases the satellite data appears to show.

  ## Construction materials, not cooling systems, likely explain the findings

  Uptime Institute argued that the concrete roofs and car parks typical of data center campuses are sufficient to account for the temperature increases Marinoni et al. reported, without invoking waste heat at all. This is a well-established feature of the urban heat island effect: buildings and paved surfaces absorb solar energy and release it as heat, and cities can be up to 12°C (54°F) warmer than surrounding rural areas for this reason alone.

  Data centers are already subject to the same heat mitigation requirements applied to other buildings under local permitting regimes. These requirements typically include the creation of green spaces, use of construction materials and designs that limit solar gain, and measures to minimize air-conditioning demand. All jurisdictions that permit data center construction require environmental impact analysis; many demand computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations to model the dispersion of pollutants, noise, and heat around a proposed facility.

  ## A growing list of community objections

  The heat island controversy is the latest addition to a widening catalogue of local grievances associated with data center development, which already includes rising electricity prices, noise from cooling equipment, water consumption, and landscape change, as well as objections tied to greenhouse gas emissions. Uptime Institute said operators and developers should expect the heat island issue to surface in community engagement sessions and permitting hearings, and will be expected to demonstrate both monitoring programs and mitigation measures.

  The confusion between localized waste heat effects and global climate change has further complicated the conversation. According to Uptime Institute, some groups opposing data centers have conflated the two distinct phenomena in public advocacy.

  ## Future risk rises with facility size

  While Uptime Institute concluded that waste heat currently makes only a minimal contribution to local warming, it noted that the situation is likely to change. Average facility capacities are growing rapidly, and waste heat output will increase as facilities expand and site densities rise. On-site power generation using gas turbines and other thermal systems — a trend driven by grid constraints — will add further heat output as well as on-site emissions.

  Efficiency improvements will partially offset waste heat growth. Waste heat storage and reuse — capturing thermal output rather than venting it into the surrounding environment — are cited by Uptime Institute as the most effective technical mitigations for the longer term.

  The Global Urban Data Centres Pact, launched in June 2026 by a coalition of mayors representing 41 cities across six continents, raised waste heat reuse as a consideration operators should address.

  ## 資料中心遭熱島效應質疑，未審查研究成反對派推手

  一篇學術論文提出，資料中心使周邊地表溫度平均上升2°C、偶爾可高達9°C（約華氏16度），在引發大量媒體報導之後，已被社區反對團體積極援引。然而，這篇論文尚未通過同儕審查，其研究方法也遭資料中心業界研究與標準機構Uptime Institute強烈質疑。

  這篇論文題為《資料熱島效應：量化人工智慧資料中心在全球暖化時代的衝擊》，由英國劍橋大學教授安德烈亞·馬里諾尼（Andrea Marinoni）主導。研究採用NASA全球地表溫度資料集，涵蓋2004年至2024年、空間解析度為500公尺（約547碼），分析8,472座建於都市區域外的資料中心周邊環境，並將觀測到的溫升現象歸因於設施冷卻系統所排放的廢熱。

  CNN將這項研究結果形容為「令人警覺」的發現。2026年6月英國熱浪期間，斯勞（Slough）——歐洲主要資料中心樞紐之一——附近居民投訴，該地區出現逾36°C（華氏97度）的高溫熱點。在美國，追蹤企業補貼、反對資料中心開發的全國性政策資源中心「好工作優先」（Good Jobs First）已將熱島效應納入業者應透過「在地利益協議」加以回應的社區損害清單——這類協議是旨在化解在地反對聲浪的談判機制。

  ## 這份研究究竟測量了什麼

  該論文所採用的資料集，大多涵蓋容量在10至20百萬瓦（MW）之間的中小型設施——大致相當於一座小型電廠的輸出功率——均建於近期人工智慧需求浪潮興起之前。論文將其描述為「AI超大規模業者」，但這個術語通常指的是數百MW規模的大型雲端與AI設施；Uptime Institute指出，這樣的定性與資料集的實際狀況並不相符。

  Uptime Institute也對物理歸因提出質疑。10至20 MW的設施所產生的廢熱相對有限，與論文所描述的升溫幅度不成比例；廢熱散逸的方式是形成具方向性的羽狀流，而非向四周均勻輻射，因此不太可能造成衛星資料所呈現的對稱性升溫現象。

  ## 建材而非冷卻系統，才是升溫的可能成因

  Uptime Institute主張，資料中心園區典型的混凝土屋頂與停車場，就足以解釋馬里諾尼等人所記錄的溫升現象，完全無需援引廢熱排放。這是城市熱島效應中早已確立的現象：建築物與鋪面吸收太陽能後以熱能形式釋放，城市僅因這一因素便可比周邊農村地區高出多達12°C（華氏54度）。

  資料中心在當地許可制度下，本已與其他建築物適用相同的熱緩解規定。這些規定通常包括：設置綠化空間、採用能限制太陽熱增益的建材與設計，以及降低空調需求的措施。所有允許資料中心興建的司法管轄區均要求進行環境影響分析；許多地區更要求以計算流體力學（CFD）模擬，來模擬污染物、噪音與熱量在擬建設施周邊的擴散情形。

  ## 日益擴大的社區反對清單

  熱島效應爭議是資料中心開發引發在地反彈情緒持續升溫的最新一環，其他既有的爭議包括：電力費率上漲、冷卻設備噪音、耗水量、景觀改變，以及與溫室氣體排放相關的反對聲浪。Uptime Institute表示，業者與開發商應預期熱島議題將在社區說明會及許可聽證會上被提出，屆時將須展示監測計畫與緩解措施。

  局部廢熱效應與全球氣候變遷之間的概念混淆，更使討論愈加複雜。據Uptime Institute指出，部分反對資料中心的團體在公開倡議中，已將這兩種截然不同的現象混為一談。

  ## 設施規模擴大，未來風險隨之上升

  儘管Uptime Institute的結論是廢熱目前對在地暖化的貢獻微乎其微，但也指出這種情況可能隨時間改變。設施平均容量正快速增長，隨著設施規模擴張與園區密度提高，廢熱排放量也將隨之增加。受電網容量限制所驅動、使用燃氣渦輪機及其他熱力系統進行現地發電的趨勢，將進一步增加廢熱產出及現地排放量。

  效率提升將部分抵銷廢熱成長的趨勢。Uptime Institute認為，廢熱儲存與再利用——捕獲熱能輸出而非將其排入周邊環境——是長遠而言最有效的技術緩解手段。

  2026年6月，由六大洲41個城市市長組成的聯盟發起「全球城市資料中心公約」（Global Urban Data Centres Pact），明確將廢熱再利用列為業者應回應的議題。
