body_en: |
  Singapore has introduced a digital infrastructure bill that makes environmental sustainability a co-equal regulatory pillar alongside cybersecurity for data centre operators, according to a discussion featuring technology and legal experts published by The Straits Times.

  The legislation frames environmental sustainability as "another key consideration for data centres other than cyber security," according to the Straits Times' Green Pulse podcast, which featured Irene Tham, the publication's technology editor, and Rajesh Sreenivasan, Partner and Head of Technology, Media and Telecommunications at law firm Rajah & Tann Singapore.

  ## AI as a geopolitical force

  The bill is taking shape at a moment when data centre construction has moved beyond the technology sector into domestic politics and international relations. AI, once treated primarily as a software issue, is now shaping diplomatic and regulatory decisions globally, according to the podcast discussion.

  South-east Asia is pursuing its own data centre expansion plans against that backdrop, with the podcast examining what the trajectory of AI infrastructure investment means for the region specifically.

  ## Environmental pressure and regional risk

  Data centres are drawing protests worldwide over their environmental footprint, the podcast said, raising the question of whether South-east Asia could face similar opposition as it scales up capacity. Singapore's decision to anchor environmental requirements in legislation rather than leaving them as secondary guidelines reflects that pressure.

  The pace of that expansion also faces an external constraint. US restrictions on semiconductor exports and what the podcast described as the "Nvidia controversy" were examined as potential brakes on global data centre growth, with implications for how quickly the region can build out AI-supporting infrastructure.

  ## Data centres as infrastructure contributors

  The podcast also addressed whether data centres could become active contributors to water and renewable energy infrastructure development, rather than functioning solely as consumers of those resources — a framing that positions operators as participants in the broader infrastructure build-out the region requires.

  ## Significance

  By codifying environmental sustainability as a foundational requirement on par with cybersecurity, Singapore's digital infrastructure bill establishes a regulatory model that other South-east Asian governments could reference as they develop their own frameworks for governing data centre growth. How the bill defines and enforces its environmental requirements will determine whether that model proves replicable across a region with widely varying regulatory capacity.

  新加坡推出數位基礎設施法案，將環境永續列為資料中心業者的核心監管支柱，與資安地位並駕齊驅。此一消息來自《海峽時報》刊登的一場科技與法律專家對談。

  該立法明確將環境永續定位為「資料中心除資安以外的另一項關鍵考量」，根據《海峽時報》旗下環保播客節目「Green Pulse」的內容，節目邀請到該報科技編輯 Irene Tham，以及新加坡 Rajah & Tann 律師事務所科技、媒體與電信部門合夥人暨主任律師 Rajesh Sreenivasan 共同出席。

  ## AI 作為地緣政治力量

  該法案成形之際，資料中心建設已超越科技產業的範疇，進入國內政治與國際關係的領域。人工智慧曾一度被視為純粹的軟體議題，如今卻正在左右全球各地的外交與監管決策，播客討論中如此指出。

  東南亞各國正在上述背景下積極推進各自的資料中心擴張計畫，播客節目針對 AI 基礎設施投資走向對該地區的具體意義進行了深入探討。

  ## 環境壓力與區域風險

  播客指出，資料中心在全球各地因其環境足跡而引發抗議，進而提出東南亞在擴大容量的過程中是否可能面臨類似反對聲浪的問題。新加坡選擇將環境要求明文納入法律，而非將其列為次要準則，正是對這股壓力的回應。

  擴張的速度也面臨外部制約。播客就美國半導體出口限制及其所稱的「輝達爭議」進行了討論，將兩者視為可能拖慢全球資料中心成長的煞車，並分析其對該地區快速建置 AI 支援基礎設施能力的潛在影響。

  ## 資料中心作為基礎設施貢獻者

  播客也探討了資料中心能否從單純的資源消耗者，轉型為水資源與再生能源基礎設施發展的積極貢獻者——這樣的論述框架將業者重新定位為推動區域整體基礎設施建設不可或缺的參與者。

  ## 意義

  藉由將環境永續明文規定為與資安並列的基礎性要求，新加坡數位基礎設施法案為東南亞其他國家政府建立了一套可資參考的監管範本，供其制定各自的資料中心成長治理框架。該法案最終如何定義並執行其環境要求，將決定這套範本能否在監管能力參差不齊的整個區域中複製推廣。
