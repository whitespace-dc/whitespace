body_en: |
  Malaysia has mandated that new data centre projects approved by the country's Data Centre Task Force include at least 30% local content, with the requirement covering engineering, maintenance, network services, and technology provision, Deputy Digital Minister Datuk Wilson Ugak Kumbong told parliament on Wednesday.

  Wilson Ugak made the disclosure in the Dewan Rakyat, Malaysia's lower house of parliament, in response to questions about whether rapid data centre investment growth was generating commensurate benefits for domestic companies and workers.

  ## Compliance and enforcement

  The Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) monitors compliance through implementation reports that companies must submit every six months, Wilson Ugak said. Data centre operators are also encouraged to run local supplier development programmes to strengthen domestic industry capabilities and promote technology transfer.

  ## Scale of approvals

  The Data Centre Task Force (DCTF), jointly chaired by the Ministry of Digital and the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry, had approved 27 projects with a combined estimated investment value of RM225.97 billion as of October 2026. Across the broader industry, 117 data centre projects had been approved in Malaysia as of October, with approved investments in the data centre and cloud computing segments reaching RM95.8 billion in the first half of 2026.

  ## Jobs and wages

  DCTF-approved projects are expected to generate 4,053 jobs, of which 3,523 are classified as highly skilled, spanning electrical and mechanical engineering, networking, cybersecurity, and data centre operations and maintenance.

  Wilson Ugak said the sector's direct job count is lower than in manufacturing, but the positions are more specialised. Data centre development also creates indirect employment in construction, engineering, and support services, he said.

  Citing a Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) report for the third quarter of 2025, Wilson Ugak said entry-level industry salaries range from RM3,000 to RM7,000 per month, while senior positions pay between RM10,000 and RM30,000. Malaysia's median monthly salary stood at RM2,793 in 2024.

  ## Investment figures versus actual economic impact

  Wilson Ugak cautioned that announced investment figures do not translate directly into economic activity. The industry is capital-intensive, a large share of equipment is imported, and capital expenditure is realised in stages across a project's development timeline, he said. Announced investment values therefore cannot be directly equated with actual expenditure, job creation, or value added to the local economy in any given period.

  Data centre development and operations also contribute to government revenue through sales and service taxes, local authority assessment charges, and utility-related revenues, Wilson Ugak said.

  ## Infrastructure concerns

  A supplementary question in the same parliamentary sitting asked whether the government had conducted a cumulative impact assessment for a proposed 656.8-hectare industrial park at Bukit Kerayong, which is expected to house a data centre campus. Dr Halimah Ali (PN-Kapar) raised concerns about potential flooding, pressure on water and electricity supply, and added strain on roads and drainage, asking whether residents would bear social and environmental costs while investors captured economic benefits.

  Wilson Ugak said data centre applications are evaluated for water and electricity supply adequacy, infrastructure capacity, and energy efficiency before approval. Operators are also encouraged to adopt renewable energy sources and more efficient technologies, he said.

  ## Policy goals

  The government's stated objective extends beyond positioning Malaysia as a data centre location. Wilson Ugak said the priority is to ensure investments develop the country's artificial intelligence and cybersecurity ecosystem, in line with the AI Nation 2030 national aspiration, while producing highly skilled jobs, greater local company participation, and sustainable economic value.

  Direct corporate income tax contributions from data centre projects are expected to rise gradually as the investment incentive periods currently enjoyed by some operators expire, Wilson Ugak said.

  ## 馬來西亞規定新資料中心須達三成本地含量

  馬來西亞副數位部長拿督威爾遜·烏嘎·昆邦（Datuk Wilson Ugak Kumbong）週三在國會表示，凡經該國資料中心專責小組審批的新建資料中心專案，工程、維運、網路服務及技術供應等範疇均須達到至少30%的本地含量。

  威爾遜·烏嘎在馬來西亞下議院（Dewan Rakyat）答覆議員質詢時作出上述披露。議員關切的核心問題，在於資料中心投資的快速增長是否為本地企業與勞工帶來相應的實質效益。

  ## 合規與執法

  威爾遜·烏嘎表示，馬來西亞投資發展局（MIDA）透過業者每半年提交一次的執行報告，監督各方的合規情況。資料中心業者同時被鼓勵推動本地供應商培育計畫，以強化國內產業能力並促進技術移轉。

  ## 審批規模

  資料中心專責小組（DCTF）由數位部與投資、貿易及工業部共同主持。截至2026年10月，該小組已審批27個專案，合計估計投資額達2,259.7億馬幣。就整體產業而言，截至同年10月，馬來西亞已獲批准的資料中心專案共117個；2026年上半年，資料中心與雲端運算領域的核准投資金額已達958億馬幣。

  ## 就業與薪資

  DCTF審批的專案預計將創造4,053個就業機會，其中3,523個屬於高技能職位，涵蓋電機工程、機械工程、網路、資安，以及資料中心的營運與維護等專業領域。

  威爾遜·烏嘎表示，資料中心產業的直接就業人數雖不及製造業，但職位的專業程度更高。他並指出，資料中心的開發同時在建築、工程及支援服務等領域間接帶動就業。

  援引馬來西亞國家銀行（BNM）2025年第三季報告，威爾遜·烏嘎表示，該產業入門職位的月薪介於3,000至7,000馬幣之間，資深職位則介於10,000至30,000馬幣。馬來西亞2024年的每月薪資中位數為2,793馬幣。

  ## 投資數字與實際經濟效益的落差

  威爾遜·烏嘎提醒，宣布的投資金額並不等同於實際的經濟活動產出。他指出，資料中心產業屬於資本密集型，大量設備仰賴進口，且資本支出須隨專案開發時程分階段落實，因此公告的投資金額不能直接換算為特定期間內的實際支出、就業創造或對本地經濟的附加價值。

  威爾遜·烏嘎亦表示，資料中心的開發與營運透過銷售及服務稅、地方政府門牌稅及公用事業相關收入，對政府財政有所貢獻。

  ## 基礎建設疑慮

  同一次國會議程中，議員以補充提問方式詢問，政府是否已就武吉格拉揚（Bukit Kerayong）一座預計佔地656.8公頃、規劃供資料中心園區進駐的工業園區，進行過整體性的累積影響評估。民族聯盟卡巴（PN-Kapar）議員哈莉瑪·阿里博士（Dr Halimah Ali）對潛在的洪患風險、供水與供電壓力，以及道路與排水系統的額外負荷表達關切，並質疑居民是否將承擔社會與環境成本，而投資者卻獨享經濟利益。

  威爾遜·烏嘎回應表示，資料中心申請在審批前，均會就供水與供電的充裕程度、基礎建設容量及能源效率進行評估，業者同時被鼓勵採用再生能源及更具效率的技術。

  ## 政策目標

  政府明確表示，施政目標不僅止於將馬來西亞打造為資料中心選址地。威爾遜·烏嘎指出，政府的優先方向是確保相關投資能夠發展馬來西亞的人工智慧與資安生態系，呼應「AI國家2030」國家願景，同時創造高技能就業機會、提升本地企業的參與程度，並實現具有永續性的經濟價值。

  威爾遜·烏嘎表示，隨著部分業者目前享有的投資獎勵期限陸續屆滿，資料中心專案對企業所得稅的直接貢獻預計將逐步提升。
