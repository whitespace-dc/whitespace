body_en: |
  The U.S. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) is drafting rules that would prohibit new optical communication modules manufactured by Chinese companies from entering U.S. networks, according to Commercial Times, citing Pioneer Investment Weekly. The proposed ban, still in draft form, would strike the dominant Chinese suppliers of the modules that underpin AI data center operations, while benefiting U.S. manufacturers Lumentum and Coherent.

  ## What optical modules do and why the FCC is moving now

  Optical communication modules sit at each end of a fiber-optic link, converting electronic signals into light pulses for transmission and then converting them back to electrical signals. Inside AI data centers, tens of thousands of graphics processing units (GPUs) must coordinate at extremely low latency across server racks; fiber-optic links, with optical modules at each junction, carry that traffic.

  FCC officials and network security experts, the Commercial Times report says, are concerned that Chinese-controlled modules embedded in AI infrastructure could be used to insert malicious code into device firmware, intercept data, manipulate transmitted information, or be shut down by remote command during a geopolitical conflict — effectively disabling U.S. AI infrastructure from abroad.

  ## Scale of Chinese market dominance

  The commercial stakes behind the proposal are substantial. According to research firms LightCounting and Counterpoint, cited by the report, seven of the top ten optical module makers globally are Chinese companies. In the 800 Gigabit-per-second (800G) and 1.6 Terabit-per-second (1.6T) segments — the highest-speed modules required for training large AI language models — two Chinese companies, Innolight (中際旭創) and Eoptolink (新易盛), together account for more than 60 percent of global market share. The report states that the AI data centers of major U.S. cloud providers, including Amazon, Microsoft, and Google, are predominantly equipped with Chinese-made modules. The U.S. government, the report adds, is concerned that this level of dependence could give China leverage to counter U.S. semiconductor export restrictions.

  ## Why Western firms ceded the middle tier

  European and American companies did not lose this market because of a technology gap, the Commercial Times report explains. Over the past decade or more, companies such as Broadcom, Marvell, Coherent, and Lumentum concentrated on the highest-value parts of the supply chain: digital signal processor (DSP) chips and advanced laser chips — including electro-absorption modulated lasers (EMLs) and vertical-cavity surface-emitting lasers (VCSELs). They vacated the more labor-intensive and capital-intensive middle tier of optical assembly, packaging, and testing, leaving that work to Chinese and Taiwanese manufacturers with lower production costs.

  The report notes that optical module packaging and testing cannot yet be fully automated — particularly optical path alignment and high-frequency testing, which require large numbers of specialized engineers for fine-tuning and yield optimization. Chinese manufacturers hold a cost advantage in that engineering labor. The report also notes that massive domestic investment in 5G base stations and telecom infrastructure, driven by large orders from Huawei and ZTE, built the production capacity and manufacturing experience of Chinese domestic module makers such as Innolight and Accelink (光迅科技).

  ## The Huawei precedent

  The FCC's draft action follows an established pattern in U.S. telecommunications policy, according to the report. The U.S. government previously allowed Huawei and ZTE equipment into domestic telecom networks during the 4G and 5G build-out, then required carriers to remove it on national security grounds — a process that cost the U.S. government and industry billions of dollars and fell significantly behind schedule. To avoid repeating that experience with AI infrastructure the report describes as worth hundreds of billions of dollars, the FCC is moving to block Chinese optical modules at the point of new data center construction, before the technology becomes entrenched in the network.

  ## Supply chain implications

  If the ban is fully implemented, the Commercial Times report projects two distinct disruption periods. Near-term, the industry would face an order-migration stress point for 800G modules. The following period would bring a blockade at the 1.6T level among what the industry calls "non-red" suppliers — a term for non-Chinese supply chains. The central question the report raises is whether non-red suppliers can absorb the volume gap left by Innolight and Eoptolink; the answer, it argues, will determine whether AI server rack shipments face a scenario in which physical rack capacity exists but optical network connectivity does not.

  Lumentum and Coherent are identified by the report as the primary U.S. beneficiaries of any ban. Taiwanese manufacturers are also cited as potential recipients of redirected orders, given their existing position in optical module assembly and packaging.
body_zh: |
  美國聯邦通訊委員會（FCC）正起草規定，擬禁止中國廠商製造的新款光通訊模組進入美國網路。據《工商時報》引述先鋒投資週刊，上述禁令草案將重創AI資料中心核心供應鏈中最具主導地位的中國廠商，美國製造商Lumentum與Coherent則有望因此受惠。

  ## 光模組的功能與FCC出手的背景

  光通訊模組位於光纖連結的兩端，負責將電子訊號轉換為光脈衝送出，並於接收端將光脈衝還原為電訊號。AI資料中心內部，數以萬計的繪圖處理器（GPU）需在極低延遲下跨伺服器機架協同運作，每個節點間的光纖連結及其兩端的光模組，正是承載這些流量的關鍵基礎設施。

  據報導，FCC官員與網路安全專家憂慮，深埋AI基礎設施中、受中國管控的光模組，可能成為在設備韌體植入惡意程式碼的管道，也可能被用來攔截資料、竄改傳輸資訊，甚至在地緣政治衝突期間遭遠端指令強制關閉——實際上即從境外癱瘓美國AI基礎設施。

  ## 中國廠商的市場主導規模

  這項提案背後的商業利害關係十分龐大。報導引述LightCounting與Counterpoint等研究機構的數據指出，全球前十大光模組廠商中，有七家來自中國。在訓練大型AI語言模型所需的最高速規格——800 Gbps（800G）與1.6 Tbps（1.6T）——中際旭創（Innolight）與新易盛（Eoptolink）兩家中國廠商合計囊括全球逾六成市占。

  報導指出，亞馬遜、微軟、Google等美國主要雲端業者的AI資料中心，目前裝配的主要正是中國製光模組。美國政府擔憂，如此高度的依賴將讓中國握有籌碼，得以反制美方的半導體出口管制措施。

  ## 歐美廠商為何退出中間層

  歐美廠商並非因技術落差才退出這塊市場。報導解釋，過去十餘年間，Broadcom、Marvell、Coherent、Lumentum等業者將資源集中於供應鏈中附加價值最高的環節：數位訊號處理器（DSP）晶片，以及電吸收調製雷射（EML）、垂直共振腔面射型雷射（VCSEL）等高階雷射晶片。光模組的組裝、封裝與測試這一更耗人工與資本的中間層業務，這些業者選擇退出，將相關工作留給生產成本更具競爭力的中國與台灣廠商。

  報導亦指出，光模組的封裝與測試目前仍無法完全自動化——尤其是光路對準及高頻測試，需要大量專業工程師進行精密調校與良率優化，中國廠商在工程人力成本上具有明顯優勢。此外，源自華為與中興通訊（ZTE）大批訂單、驅動5G基地台與電信基礎設施建設的龐大需求，也為中際旭創、光迅科技（Accelink）等中國本土廠商積累了相當的產能規模與製造經驗。

  ## 從華為案例看前車之鑑

  據報導，FCC此次草擬行動延續了美國電信政策一貫的應對模式。美國政府過去在4G與5G建網期間，曾允許華為與中興設備進入國內電信網路，事後卻以國家安全為由要求業者全面拆除——這一過程耗費美國政府與業界數十億美元，且執行進度嚴重落後預期。為避免在報導所稱價值數千億美元的AI基礎設施上重蹈覆轍，FCC選擇在新建資料中心階段便封堵中國光模組，趕在相關技術深根固柢之前先行阻斷。

  ## 供應鏈衝擊展望

  若禁令全面落實，報導預估將出現兩段明顯的衝擊期。短期而言，產業將面臨800G模組訂單移轉的壓力節點；其後則將進入1.6T層級的「非紅色」（non-red，即非中國供應鏈）廠商封鎖期。報導提出的核心問題是：非紅色廠商能否填補中際旭創與新易盛退場後留下的供貨缺口？這個答案，將決定AI伺服器機架出貨是否淪入「機架已到位、光網路連線卻斷鏈」的窘境。

  Lumentum與Coherent被報導點名為禁令若落實後的主要美國受益廠商。台灣廠商同樣被列為潛在受惠者，鑑於台灣業者在光模組組裝與封裝領域原本即具備相當基礎，有望承接轉移而來的訂單。
