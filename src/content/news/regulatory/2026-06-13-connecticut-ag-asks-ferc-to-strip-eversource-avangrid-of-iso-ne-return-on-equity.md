body_en: |
  Connecticut's attorney general and three state regulatory agencies filed a complaint at the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) on June 11, 2026, asking the federal regulator to eliminate a 0.5% return on equity (ROE) adder that Eversource Energy subsidiary Connecticut Light & Power (CL&P) and Avangrid subsidiary The United Illuminating Co. (UI) collect on their transmission asset investments for belonging to ISO New England (ISO-NE), the regional grid operator for the six-state New England power market.

  The complaint was filed jointly by Connecticut Attorney General William Tong's office, the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection, the Connecticut Office of Consumer Counsel, and the Connecticut Public Utilities Regulatory Authority.

  ## What the adder is and why Connecticut says it no longer applies

  FERC allows transmission utilities that voluntarily join an independent system operator (ISO) or regional transmission organization (RTO) to collect an extra 0.5 percentage point on top of their base return on equity — a financial metric that determines how much profit a regulated utility earns on shareholder investment in transmission infrastructure. The incentive is intended to encourage utilities to join regional grid coordination bodies, which FERC has long viewed as improving efficiency and lowering costs.

  A 2025 Connecticut law now requires CL&P and UI to participate in ISO-NE. Because participation is mandatory under state law rather than voluntary, the complaint argues, the utilities no longer qualify for an adder that was designed to reward a choice they are no longer free to make.

  ## Financial impact on ratepayers

  In 2024, the RTO adder raised CL&P and UI transmission rates by $17 million across New England, with Connecticut ratepayers bearing approximately $4.5 million of that total, according to the complaint. Eversource said it expects that eliminating the adder would save an average residential customer roughly 9 cents per month.

  The complaint states that Connecticut already has some of the highest electricity rates in the country. As of January 2026, Connecticut ratepayers ranked seventh-highest among all U.S. states, with transmission charges accounting for about 15% of a typical residential customer's monthly bill, according to figures cited in the complaint.

  "Worse, the adder imposes a substantial, incremental charge on electric transmission rates that already represent some of the highest in the nation," the agencies and AG's office wrote in their filing.

  The agencies asked FERC to require refunds dating back to the date of the complaint.

  ## Prior precedent and comparable state actions

  Connecticut's complaint follows a legal and regulatory pattern that has developed in other states where participation in an RTO or ISO is required by law rather than chosen voluntarily. California and Ohio have previously enacted such mandates, and Maryland most recently joined that group, according to the complaint.

  A federal appeals court in January 2025 ordered FERC to strip Ohio utilities of the ROE adder on the same grounds. The Office of the Ohio Consumers' Counsel said at the time it expected the ruling to save American Electric Power customers $220 million through 2031, while FirstEnergy and Duke Energy customers would save $126 million and $99 million, respectively, over the same period.

  ## Utility and government response

  Eversource, in a statement attributed to spokesperson Sarah Paduano, said transmission investment has delivered significant savings to customers. "Our ongoing, strategic investments in the transmission system have helped to eliminate substantial congestion costs, providing customers with billions of dollars in savings over the past several years," Paduano said. Eversource confirmed it expects the adder's removal to reduce average residential bills by approximately 9 cents per month.

  Connecticut Gov. Ned Lamont, a Democrat, backed the filing. "Utilities with record profits should not receive bonus profits for doing something they are required to do by law," Lamont said in a press release. "This is another way we're addressing the issue of high electric bills in our state and ensuring utilities are not able to take advantage of ratepayers."

  Avangrid did not respond to requests for comment.

  ## What comes next

  FERC must now decide whether to accept the complaint. If the commission sides with the Connecticut agencies, CL&P and UI would lose the 0.5% adder and could be required to issue refunds to ratepayers from the June 11, 2026, filing date. The outcome would add to a growing body of FERC precedent — reinforced by the January 2025 federal appeals court ruling on Ohio — holding that mandatory RTO membership disqualifies a utility from collecting a benefit designed for voluntary participation.
body_zh: |
  康乃狄克州總檢察長辦公室與三個州監管機關於2026年6月11日聯合向聯邦能源監管委員會（FERC）提起申訴，要求這個聯邦監管機構取消Eversource Energy旗下子公司康乃狄克照明與電力公司（CL&P），以及Avangrid旗下子公司聯合照明公司（UI），因加入新英格蘭獨立系統營運商（ISO-NE）而在輸電資產投資上所收取的0.5%股東權益回報（ROE）加碼。ISO-NE是涵蓋新英格蘭六州電力市場的區域電網營運商。

  此次申訴由康乃狄克州總檢察長威廉·唐（William Tong）辦公室、康乃狄克州能源與環境保護部、康乃狄克州消費者諮詢辦公室，以及康乃狄克州公用事業監管局共同提出。

  ## 加碼機制為何，以及康乃狄克州為何認為不再適用

  FERC允許自願加入獨立系統營運商（ISO）或區域輸電組織（RTO）的輸電業者，在基本股東權益回報率之上額外收取0.5個百分點的加碼。股東權益回報率是衡量受監管電力公司從輸電基礎設施股東投資中獲取多少利潤的財務指標。此獎勵機制旨在鼓勵電力公司加入區域電網協調機構，FERC長期以來認為這有助於提升效率、降低成本。

  康乃狄克州2025年通過的一項法律，現已強制要求CL&P與UI加入ISO-NE。申訴方主張，由於加入已是州法律強制規定而非出於自願，兩家電力公司不再符合領取此加碼的資格——這項加碼原本是為了獎勵一個如今已不存在的自主選擇而設計的。

  ## 對用電戶的財務影響

  根據申訴文件，2024年RTO加碼使CL&P與UI在整個新英格蘭的輸電費率合計增加1,700萬美元，其中康乃狄克州用電戶承擔約450萬美元。Eversource表示，預計取消加碼後，一般住宅用戶每月可節省約9美分。

  申訴文件指出，康乃狄克州的電費已是全美最高之列。根據申訴引用的數據，截至2026年1月，康乃狄克州用電戶的電費在全美五十州中排名第七高，輸電費用約占一般住宅用戶每月帳單的15%。

  「更糟的是，這項加碼在已是全美最高之列的輸電費率上，額外課徵了一筆相當可觀的費用，」各機關與總檢察長辦公室在申訴文件中寫道。

  各機關要求FERC命令退還自申訴提出日起計算的溢收款項。

  ## 前例與其他州的類似行動

  康乃狄克州此次申訴，延續了其他州在法律強制加入RTO或ISO、而非出於自願的情形下所發展出的法律與監管模式。根據申訴文件，加州與俄亥俄州先前已立法強制要求，馬里蘭州則是最近加入這個行列的州。

  聯邦上訴法院於2025年1月基於相同理由，裁定FERC應取消俄亥俄州電力公司的ROE加碼。俄亥俄州消費者諮詢辦公室當時表示，預計該裁決將為美國電力公司（American Electric Power）客戶在2031年前節省2.2億美元，而FirstEnergy與杜克能源（Duke Energy）的客戶則分別可節省1.26億美元與9,900萬美元。

  ## 電力公司與政府的回應

  Eversource在發言人莎拉·帕杜亞諾（Sarah Paduano）的聲明中表示，輸電投資已為客戶帶來可觀節省。「我們持續、有策略地投資輸電系統，協助消除了大量的壅塞成本，過去幾年來為客戶節省了數十億美元，」帕杜亞諾說。Eversource確認，預計取消加碼後，一般住宅用戶每月帳單將減少約9美分。

  民主黨籍康乃狄克州長內德·拉蒙特（Ned Lamont）支持此次申訴。「獲利創紀錄的電力公司，不應因履行法律義務而再獲額外利潤，」拉蒙特在新聞稿中表示。「這是我們解決本州高電費問題、確保電力公司無法剝削用電戶的另一項行動。」

  Avangrid則未回應置評請求。

  ## 後續發展

  FERC現在必須決定是否受理此申訴。若委員會支持康乃狄克州各機關的立場，CL&P與UI將喪失0.5%的加碼，並可能被要求自2026年6月11日申訴提出日起退還溢收款項。此案結果將進一步鞏固一系列日益增多的FERC先例——2025年1月聯邦上訴法院針對俄亥俄州案的裁決已強化了這一立場——即強制性的RTO成員資格，使電力公司喪失領取為自願參與而設計之補貼的資格。
