body_en: |
  The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission on Friday largely approved a proposal by the Midcontinent Independent System Operator to impose stricter financial penalties on demand-side resources that fail to perform when called, with the new framework set to take effect June 1.

  The order targets Load Modifying Resources (LMRs) — demand response and behind-the-meter generation assets that receive capacity payments in exchange for committing to respond to MISO dispatch orders during grid emergencies. According to MISO, the new penalty structure caps a series of FERC-approved changes to the grid operator's LMR rules prompted by a string of enforcement cases involving its demand response program.

  ## Scale of demand-side participation

  LMRs carry significant weight in MISO's resource mix. In the grid operator's most recent planning resource auction, LMRs backed by demand response cleared 9.1 gigawatts of capacity, while those backed by behind-the-meter generation contributed 4.3 GW for the summer season. Combined, the two categories made up 9.4% of all planning resources in that season, according to MISO.

  ## Enforcement backdrop

  A pattern of market misconduct drove the rulemaking. FERC enforcement actions beginning in 2023 targeted multiple demand response participants in MISO:

  - Voltus agreed to pay a $10.9 million penalty and return $7.1 million in unjust profits to settle allegations it registered uncontracted and overstated demand response resources.
  - Big River Steel agreed to pay nearly $22 million to settle market gaming charges.
  - Northern Indiana Public Service Co. and Linde Inc. agreed to pay $66.7 million to settle separate market gaming allegations.
  - Ketchup Caddy and its owner were ordered to pay approximately $27 million for using fraudulent demand response resources to make offers in MISO's capacity market.

  FERC, citing MISO, said the existing LMR penalty structure did little to deter fraud and manipulative behavior because LMRs are called on infrequently relative to the capacity payments they receive, weakening financial disincentives against misconduct.

  ## What the new framework changes

  Under the approved rules, MISO will expand existing participation restrictions for underperforming LMRs, differentiate between partial and complete performance failures — which previously carried the same treatment — and add a new financial penalty calculated against auction clearing prices. FERC said the layering of multiple penalty components applying concurrently is justified because it incentivizes performance and compels replacement of missing capacity that MISO would otherwise expect to be available, increasing system reliability.

  FERC rejected arguments from protestors — Advanced Energy United, American Municipal Power, and the Michigan Public Power Agency — that the penalties were excessive. The commission said the structure is not "overly severe or unduly punitive" given its reliability rationale. However, FERC directed MISO to address an inconsistency in its proposed method for calculating two of the penalty types within 30 days.

  ## Commissioners flag monitoring obligation

  In a joint concurring statement, FERC commissioners Judy Chang and David Rosner endorsed the order but urged ongoing vigilance. The commissioners warned that if the penalty severity drove well-performing demand-side resources out of the market, that outcome would significantly increase the cost of meeting resource and energy adequacy. They called on MISO and market participants to monitor the effects of both the new penalties and previously approved LMR reforms, and urged MISO to continue improving its market rules to keep demand-side resources participating at the lowest possible cost.

  ## Industry reaction

  Rao Konidena, president of Rakon Energy, a consulting firm, said FERC "got this right," telling Utility Dive that MISO needs LMRs it can count on when the risk of load shedding is real, and that the commission was deliberate — including in sending MISO back to fix the tariff language inconsistency. Konidena nonetheless flagged a risk that stiffer penalties could displace well-performing resources alongside bad actors.

  Konidena also raised a structural concern outside the scope of FERC's order: demand-side aggregators depend on utilities and other load-serving entities to obtain node designations, and currently face no firm timelines for receiving them. He said barriers to market entry combined with steep exit-side penalties create conditions that could erode the demand response supply precisely when grid conditions make it most necessary.

  The June 1 effective date gives MISO and market participants roughly six months to adapt to the new penalty regime, with the 30-day tariff correction deadline falling well before that implementation date.
body_zh: |
  美國聯邦能源管理委員會（FERC）週五大致批准中大陸獨立系統運營商（MISO）的提案，對未能在被調度時履約的需量側資源施加更嚴格的財務罰則，新框架預計於6月1日正式生效。

  此命令針對的是「負載調節資源」（Load Modifying Resources，LMRs）——亦即需量反應資產及表後發電設備。這類資產可獲得容量費用，作為其承諾在電網緊急狀況時響應MISO調度指令的對價。MISO表示，新罰則結構為一系列FERC核准的LMR規則修訂畫下句點，這些修訂的起因是一連串涉及需量反應計畫的執法案件。

  ## 需量側資源的參與規模

  LMRs在MISO的資源組合中舉足輕重。在電網運營商最近一次規劃資源競標中，以需量反應為支撐的LMRs清算容量達9.1 GW（百萬瓩），而以表後發電為支撐的LMRs則為夏季貢獻了4.3 GW的容量。MISO指出，兩類資源合計占該季節全部規劃資源的9.4%。

  ## 執法背景

  一連串的市場不當行為推動了此次規則制定。FERC自2023年起對MISO多名需量反應參與者展開執法行動：

  - Voltus同意支付1,090萬美元罰款，並退還710萬美元不當獲利，以和解其登記未簽約且高估需量反應資源的指控。
  - Big River Steel同意支付近2,200萬美元，以和解市場操縱指控。
  - 北印第安納公共服務公司（Northern Indiana Public Service Co.）與林德公司（Linde Inc.）同意支付6,670萬美元，以和解各自面臨的市場操縱指控。
  - Ketchup Caddy及其所有人被命令支付約2,700萬美元，原因是利用詐欺性的需量反應資源在MISO容量市場中進行報價。

  FERC援引MISO的說法指出，現行LMR罰則結構對欺詐及操縱行為的嚇阻力不足——原因在於LMRs被調度的頻率，相對於其所獲容量費用而言偏低，使得違規的財務代價顯著削弱。

  ## 新框架的調整內容

  依據獲批規則，MISO將擴大對表現不佳之LMRs的現行參與限制、區分部分履約與完全未履約的處理方式（此前兩者一視同仁），並新增一項依據競標清算價格計算的財務罰款。FERC表示，多項罰則同時並行的疊加設計有其正當性，因為這樣既能激勵資源確實履約，也能迫使業者補足MISO原本預期可用、卻未出現的容量缺口，從而提升系統可靠性。

  FERC駁回了異議方的主張——包括先進能源聯合組織（Advanced Energy United）、美國市政電力（American Municipal Power）及密西根公共電力協會（Michigan Public Power Agency）——認為罰則過重。委員會表示，考量到其可靠性的立論基礎，此結構並非「過度嚴苛或不當懲罰性」。不過，FERC責令MISO在30天內解決其提案中兩種罰款計算方式的不一致問題。

  ## 委員呼籲持續監測

  FERC委員張茱迪（Judy Chang）與大衛．羅斯納（David Rosner）在聯合附議聲明中支持此命令，但呼籲持續保持警覺。兩位委員警告，若罰則力度過重，導致表現良好的需量側資源退出市場，將大幅推升滿足資源與能源充裕度的成本。他們呼籲MISO及市場參與者持續追蹤新罰則及先前已核准的LMR改革的實際影響，並敦促MISO繼續完善市場規則，讓需量側資源以最低成本持續參與市場。

  ## 業界反應

  Rakon Energy諮詢公司總裁饒．科尼迪納（Rao Konidena）表示，FERC「這次做對了」，他告訴《Utility Dive》：MISO需要的是在真正面臨甩負載風險時，可以仰賴的LMRs；而委員會此次的決策深思熟慮，包括責令MISO修正費率規章用語不一致之處在內。不過，科尼迪納也指出一項風險：更嚴苛的罰則可能在淘汰害群之馬的同時，也波及表現良好的資源。

  科尼迪納還提出了一項超出FERC此次命令範圍的結構性隱憂：需量側聚合業者必須仰賴電力公司及其他負荷服務實體，才能取得節點指定，而目前這個流程並無明確的時間期限。他表示，市場進入障礙與嚴苛的退出側罰則雙重夾擊，可能在電網狀況最需要需量反應的時候，反而削弱需量反應的供給。

  6月1日的生效日期讓MISO及市場參與者有約六個月的時間，適應新的罰則制度；而30天的費率修正期限則遠早於該實施日期到來。
