body_en: |
  A former chairman of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) — the federal agency that regulates interstate electricity transmission rates and wholesale power markets — is calling for three specific amendments to Senate permitting bill SB 5653, warning that its transmission provisions would override a century of state authority over utility infrastructure and could drive consumer electricity bills sharply higher.

  Mark Christie, who also served as chairman of the Virginia State Corporation Commission and now directs the William and Mary Law School Center for Energy Law and Policy, published the critique in Utility Dive. He described the bill as a genuine compromise — supporting its provisions to ensure large electricity customers pay the full grid costs they impose — but said three transmission clauses are incompatible with both consumer protection and established state-federal jurisdictional boundaries.

  ## The century-old division the bill would disrupt

  Under the Federal Power Act, Christie wrote, states have held authority over the siting and permitting of physical grid assets within their borders — including generation and transmission lines — for nearly a century. FERC holds authority over interstate transmission service rates and wholesale power sales. That division, Christie said, reflects a clear and logical allocation of responsibility.

  SB 5653 would give FERC the power to order a transmission line built anywhere in the country, without geographic constraint, regardless of a state's own permitting decision or the opposition of affected residents. Christie contrasted this with earlier federal backstop authority: the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act of 2021 granted FERC similar powers but confined them to specific geographic corridors designated by the U.S. Department of Energy, and only after DOE made an affirmative finding that the national interest required each corridor. SB 5653 contains no such geographic limitation, Christie wrote, calling it a "breathtaking expansion of federal power at the expense of a core state function."

  ## Three amendments Christie recommends

  **Replace the 'public interest' standard.** The bill currently allows FERC to order transmission construction based on a "public interest" standard, which Christie described as a blank check. He argued the standard is broad enough to support approval of virtually any proposed line, including lines built to serve hyperscale data center development — which he identified as a Trump administration priority — or lines justified by climate goals under a future administration. His recommended amendment would replace the general criteria with a requirement that FERC find the proposed line is the optimal solution to a proven reliability need, achievable at just and reasonable rates, compared to alternative solutions.

  **Protect state cost-allocation agreements.** FERC Order 1920 — a prior FERC rulemaking — guaranteed that states in a multi-state region could reach their own agreement on how to allocate the costs of regional transmission projects and submit that agreement to FERC. Christie recommended adding an explicit guarantee to SB 5653 that states in each region retain that right, and that FERC must presume any such state-negotiated agreement is just and reasonable.

  **Restore a one-year window before federal backstop proceedings.** The Senate bill would allow FERC to begin its backstop permitting proceeding simultaneously with a state's own proceeding. Christie said this effectively renders the state proceeding meaningless: states have no incentive to invest time and limited resources in a process that FERC is already duplicating in parallel, and any evidentiary record the state develops may not be available to FERC before the federal override occurs. When FERC implemented the 2021 law's backstop authority, Christie noted, the agency gave each affected state one full year to complete its proceeding before FERC would begin its own. He recommended the same one-year window be written into SB 5653.

  ## Consumer cost exposure

  Christie framed the consumer cost risk as independent of any view on state authority. Transmission costs have been among the fastest-growing components of residential electricity bills over the past decade, he wrote. Because SB 5653's "public interest" standard and lack of geographic limits would allow FERC to mandate transmission construction in support of broad policy goals, the bill as drafted could result in FERC ordering "literally trillions of dollars" of new transmission lines, with those costs flowing directly into consumers' monthly bills.

  ## Significance for the permitting debate

  Christie's analysis is significant because it comes from a former federal regulator who is not opposed to permitting reform — he explicitly endorsed provisions requiring large electricity consumers to pay their full grid costs. His critique targets the specific mechanism by which the bill expands FERC's siting authority beyond what prior Congresses have permitted, and offers amendments he said are narrow enough to preserve the bill's legitimate goals while restoring jurisdictional boundaries and consumer cost protections.

  Christie stated that with the three amendments in place, the bill can be substantially improved without requiring its broader permitting reform framework to be reopened.
body_zh: |
  前聯邦能源監管委員會（FERC）主席正呼籲對參院許可法案SB 5653提出三項具體修正，警告該法案的輸電條款將凌駕各州近百年來對公用事業基礎設施的管轄權，並可能大幅推高消費者的電費負擔。FERC是負責監管跨州電力輸送費率與批發電力市場的聯邦機構。

  馬克．克里斯蒂（Mark Christie）曾任維吉尼亞州公司事務委員會主席，目前擔任威廉與瑪麗法學院能源法律與政策中心主任，他在《Utility Dive》發表評論，將這項法案定性為一項誠意十足的妥協之作——他支持其中要求大型用電戶支付其所造成之全部電網成本的條款——但指出其中三項輸電條款，與消費者保護及既有的州聯邦管轄界線均不相符。

  ## 法案將打亂的百年分工

  克里斯蒂寫道，根據《聯邦電力法》，各州近百年來擁有轄區內實體電網資產的選址與許可權，涵蓋發電設施與輸電線路。FERC則擁有跨州輸電服務費率與批發電力銷售的管轄權。克里斯蒂表示，這樣的分工體現了清晰而合理的責任劃分。

  SB 5653將賦予FERC在全國任何地點下令興建輸電線路的權力，不受地理限制，無論該州自身的許可決定為何，也不論受影響居民是否表示反對。克里斯蒂將此與過去的聯邦後備授權作比較：2021年的《基礎設施投資與就業法》雖賦予FERC類似權力，但僅限於美國能源部（DOE）指定的特定地理走廊範圍，且只有在DOE就每條走廊正式認定符合國家利益後方可啟動。SB 5653不含任何此類地理限制，克里斯蒂稱此為「以犧牲州核心職能為代價的聯邦權力驚人擴張」。

  ## 克里斯蒂建議的三項修正

  **以更嚴格標準取代「公共利益」標準。** 現行法案允許FERC以「公共利益」標準下令興建輸電設施，克里斯蒂形容這等同於一張空白支票。他認為此標準過於寬泛，足以支持批准幾乎任何擬議路線，包括為超大規模資料中心開發所建設的線路——他指出此為川普政府的施政優先項目——或在未來政府以氣候目標為由核准的路線。他建議的修正案，將把這項泛泛標準替換為明確要求：FERC須認定擬議路線是解決已獲證實之可靠性需求的最優方案、可以合理費率實現，且相較於各替代方案更具優勢。

  **保護各州的成本分攤協議。** FERC第1920號命令——此前FERC的一項規則制定——保障多州地區內的各州可自行就區域輸電計畫的成本分攤方式達成協議，並向FERC提交。克里斯蒂建議在SB 5653中明確加入保障條款：各區域的各州保留這項權利，且FERC必須推定任何由各州協商達成的協議係公正合理的。

  **恢復聯邦後備程序啟動前的一年緩衝期。** 參院法案將允許FERC與各州的許可程序同步啟動後備審批程序。克里斯蒂表示，此舉實際上使州程序形同虛設：當FERC已在並行複製整個程序時，各州便失去投入時間與有限資源的動機，且各州建立的任何證據紀錄，可能在聯邦否決發生之前都無法為FERC所取用。克里斯蒂指出，當FERC落實2021年法律的後備授權時，該機構給予每個受影響的州整整一年時間完成其自身程序，之後FERC才會啟動自己的程序。他建議將同樣的一年緩衝期明確寫入SB 5653。

  ## 消費者面臨的成本風險

  克里斯蒂將消費者成本風險定位為獨立於州權立場之外的議題。他寫道，過去十年來，輸電成本在住宅電費帳單中是成長最快的組成之一。由於SB 5653的「公共利益」標準及缺乏地理限制，將允許FERC以廣泛政策目標為由強制建設輸電設施，現行版本的法案可能導致FERC下令興建「從字面意義上高達數兆美元」的新輸電線路，而這些成本將直接反映在消費者每月的電費帳單上。

  ## 對許可改革辯論的意義

  克里斯蒂的分析之所以意義重大，在於其出自一位並不反對許可改革的前聯邦監管官員之口——他明確支持要求大型用電戶支付全部電網成本的條款。他的批評針對的是法案擴張FERC選址管轄權、超越過去歷屆國會所允許範圍的具體機制，並提出他所稱足夠精準的修正建議，在保留法案正當目標的同時，重建管轄界限並強化消費者成本保護。

  克里斯蒂表示，只要納入這三項修正，法案便能在無需重啟其更廣泛許可改革框架的前提下，獲得實質性的改善。
