body_en: |
  Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim confirmed on Nov 4 that strict environmental conditions have been imposed on data center operators in the country, with all companies involved having accepted the requirements.

  "Yes, these are the conditions imposed... and they have accepted," Anwar told reporters after attending the Mantap Malaysia Bersama AI programme at Universiti Malaya. He was responding to questions about whether the government was doing enough to address environmental concerns related to data center development.

  ## Energy supply and existing industries

  Speaking separately on the same day, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Ghani said the government would ensure data center development does not compromise electricity supply to existing industrial users. Johari described data centers as important infrastructure for supporting emerging technologies, but said their growth must not disrupt the energy needs of sectors already operating in the country.

  ## Scale of approved development

  Malaysia has approved 117 data centers with a combined maximum energy demand of approximately 23,500 megawatts (MW) upon full completion, according to Johari. To put that figure in context, 23,500 MW represents a substantial share of national grid capacity and underscores why officials are focused on managing the sector's power draw. Of the 117 approved projects, 55 are already operational. The remaining facilities are at various stages of planning and construction.

  ## What this means for the sector

  The twin statements from the prime minister and the trade and industry minister signal that Malaysia's government is responding to public and industry pressure to formalize oversight of a rapidly expanding sector. The confirmation that operators have accepted the environmental conditions provides a public accountability marker, though the specific content of those conditions was not disclosed. The government's explicit pledge to protect existing industries from energy shortfalls indicates that grid capacity management will be a continued policy concern as the remaining 62 approved projects move toward completion.

  馬來西亞總理安華·依布拉欣（Anwar Ibrahim）於11月4日正式確認，政府已對全國資料中心業者設下嚴格環保條件，所有相關企業均已接受並同意遵守。

  安華出席馬來亞大學「Mantap Malaysia Bersama AI」計畫後，回應記者詢問政府是否採取足夠措施應對資料中心開發所引發的環境疑慮時表示：「是的，這些就是所設定的條件……而他們已接受。」

  ## 電力供應與現有產業保障

  同日，投資、貿易及工業部長佐哈里·加尼（Johari Ghani）另行表示，政府將確保資料中心的發展不會損及現有工業用電戶的電力供應。佐哈里形容資料中心是支撐新興科技的重要基礎設施，但強調其成長絕不能排擠國內既有運營產業的能源需求。

  ## 核准開發規模

  根據佐哈里透露，馬來西亞目前已核准117座資料中心，全部完工後合計最大用電需求約達23,500百萬瓦（MW）。就比例而言，23,500 MW占全國電網容量的相當大份額，這也正是官員高度重視管控該產業電力消耗的主因。117項核准案中，已有55座正式投入運營，其餘設施則處於規劃或施工的不同階段。

  ## 政策意涵

  總理與工業部長的雙雙公開表態，顯示馬來西亞政府正積極回應外界與業界壓力，致力將快速擴張的資料中心產業納入正式監管框架。業者已接受環保條件一事公諸於眾，形同一項公開的問責標記，惟相關條件的具體內容並未對外揭露。政府明確承諾保護既有產業免受電力短缺衝擊，意味著隨著其餘62項核准案陸續邁向完工，電網容量管理將持續成為施政關注的核心議題。
