body_en: |
  The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) on Thursday ordered the six largest U.S. regional grid operators to justify or rewrite the rules governing how large power users connect to the electricity grid, opening a sweeping market review driven by surging AI-related electricity demand.

  FERC issued show-cause orders to PJM Interconnection, the Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO), Southwest Power Pool (SPP), the California Independent System Operator (CAISO), ISO New England, and the New York Independent System Operator (NYISO). Together, those markets serve roughly 200 million Americans across more than 30 states and the District of Columbia and cover nearly two-thirds of electricity demand served under commission-jurisdictional rates.

  Each operator has 60 days to explain why its existing tariff — the set of rules governing how customers access and use the transmission grid — remains just and reasonable under federal law, or to propose reforms. FERC said a preliminary review found that current tariffs "appear to be unjust and unreasonable because they do not adequately address the challenges associated with the integration of large and co-located loads onto the transmission system."

  ## Resource adequacy reports due in 30 days

  Alongside the tariff review, FERC directed each grid operator and its transmission owners to submit a resource adequacy report within 30 days. The reports must detail how each operator plans to ensure sufficient generation — enough generating capacity to meet electricity demand reliably — is available to serve both existing customers and new large loads.

  FERC Chairman Laura Swett said the timeline is deliberate. "We're holding grid operators accountable with tight, ambitious deadlines because the stakes are high and the country demands urgency," she said during a briefing following the commission meeting.

  The resource adequacy requirement could prove as consequential as the tariff reforms themselves. While much of the debate around AI infrastructure has focused on interconnection queues — the backlog of projects seeking grid connection — and transmission capacity, the reports will compel grid operators to publicly explain how they intend to support projected demand growth.

  ## Five areas targeted for reform

  FERC identified five areas where regional tariffs may require revision:

  - Transmission service application and study processes, including the use of alternative transmission technologies
  - Cost-allocation mechanisms intended to prevent existing customers from paying for upgrades required by large new loads
  - Co-location arrangements and behind-the-meter generation
  - New transmission services tailored to flexible large loads
  - Processes for evaluating generating facilities serving electrically proximate or co-located large loads

  Co-location refers to arrangements in which a large power user such as a data center operates on or near the same site as a power plant, sometimes connecting to that generation directly rather than drawing power from the broader grid. Electrically proximate generation means power plants located close enough to a large load to serve it with limited use of the shared transmission network.

  The focus on cost allocation reflects one of the central tensions in the large-load debate: who pays for the grid infrastructure upgrades needed to serve data centers and other power-intensive facilities. Arushi Sharma Frank, senior associate (non-resident) with the Center for Strategic and International Studies' Energy Security and Climate Change Program, said in a post on X that FERC's approach is aimed at "guarding against cost misallocation," adding that "free riding on other customers isn't an option."

  ## Regional solutions, not a national framework

  FERC stopped short of imposing a single national standard, instead acknowledging regional differences and citing progress already made by PJM and Southwest Power Pool on large-load and co-location issues since October 2025. "There's no universal formula for landing this," Swett said. "Each region and every grid operator faces unique challenges and opportunities."

  PJM said it views the orders as a continuation of work already underway. "PJM has already begun this work under the Commission's Order on Co-Located Load, and PJM and stakeholders are actively advancing initiatives designed to support the efficient integration of new large loads, strengthen reliability, and facilitate the fair and transparent allocation of costs," Jeff Shields, PJM's senior manager of external communications, said. "We view the Commission's order as a chance to build on that momentum."

  Interested parties will have 30 days to respond after grid operators submit their filings.

  ## DOE proceeding and next steps

  The action builds on a Department of Energy (DOE) large-load interconnection proceeding launched last year amid concerns that existing planning and interconnection processes were not designed for gigawatt-scale data center campuses and other rapidly growing sources of electricity demand. FERC said the United States is experiencing unprecedented electricity demand driven in large part by data center growth, and the commission reviewed more than 3,500 pages of comments before issuing the orders.

  FERC said its goal is to accelerate large-load integration while protecting existing customers from cost shifting. The commission signaled that any reforms will apply prospectively, meaning they are not intended to disrupt large-load arrangements already underway or finalized before new tariff reforms are filed.

  Utilities, power producers, and hyperscalers — large cloud and AI companies operating at scale — are already pairing data centers with nearby gas, nuclear, renewable, and hybrid generation projects. FERC's focus on electrically proximate and co-located generation indicates regulators expect those arrangements to play a larger role as AI infrastructure expands.

  FERC left the broader DOE large-load docket open, signaling that additional regulatory action could follow the current proceeding.

  ## FERC命令六大電網業者60天內為大型用電接入規則提出說明或改革

  美國聯邦能源監管委員會（FERC）週四責令全美六大區域電網業者就大型用電者接入電網的相關規則提出合理說明或全面改革，正式展開一場由AI用電需求急速攀升所推動的大規模市場審查。

  FERC向PJM Interconnection、中大陸獨立系統運營商（MISO）、西南電力池（SPP）、加州獨立系統運營商（CAISO）、新英格蘭ISO，以及紐約獨立系統運營商（NYISO）分別發出說明命令。上述市場合計服務逾30個州及哥倫比亞特區約2億名美國民眾，所涵蓋的用電量接近FERC管轄費率下全美電力需求的三分之二。

  各業者須在60天內說明其現行費率規章——即規範用戶使用輸電網路之規則——為何依聯邦法律仍屬公正合理，或提出改革方案。FERC表示，初步審查發現現行費率規章「表面上並不公正合理，因為其未能充分因應大型負載及共址負載併入輸電系統所帶來的挑戰」。

  ## 資源充裕度報告須在30天內提交

  除費率規章審查外，FERC亦要求各電網業者及其輸電業者在30天內提交資源充裕度報告，詳述各業者如何確保備有充足的發電容量，以穩定供應現有用戶及新增大型負載的用電需求。

  FERC主席蘿拉·史薇特（Laura Swett）表示，此一時程設定出於深思熟慮。她在委員會會議後的簡報中說：「我們以嚴格、積極的期限要求電網業者負起責任，因為風險極高，而這個國家需要的是緊迫行動。」

  資源充裕度要求的影響力或許不亞於費率規章改革本身。過去圍繞AI基礎設施的討論多聚焦於併網候審隊伍——即等待接入電網之專案積壓——以及輸電容量，但這些報告將迫使電網業者公開說明如何因應預期中的需求成長。

  ## 五大改革重點

  FERC點出區域費率規章可能需要修訂的五個面向：

  - 輸電服務申請與研究流程，包括替代輸電技術的應用
  - 旨在防止現有用戶為新增大型負載所需升級買單的成本分攤機制
  - 共址安排與電表後端發電
  - 針對具彈性大型負載量身訂制的新型輸電服務
  - 評估服務於電氣鄰近或共址大型負載之發電設施的流程

  所謂「共址」，指的是資料中心等大型用電者在發電廠同一地點或鄰近地點運營，有時直接使用該發電設施供電，而非從共用電網取電。「電氣鄰近發電」則指位置足夠接近大型負載、可在僅少量使用共用輸電網路的情況下直接供電的發電廠。

  成本分攤問題之所以備受關注，在於其觸及大型負載爭議的核心矛盾：服務資料中心等高耗能設施所需的電網基礎設施升級費用，究竟應由誰來承擔。美國戰略與國際研究中心（CSIS）能源安全與氣候變遷計畫非駐館資深研究員阿魯西·夏瑪·法蘭克（Arushi Sharma Frank）在X平台發文指出，FERC此舉旨在「防範成本被錯誤分配」，並強調「讓其他用戶替你埋單這條路是行不通的」。

  ## 區域差異化方案，而非全國統一標準

  FERC並未強制推行單一全國標準，而是正視各區域差異，並肯定PJM及西南電力池自2025年10月以來在大型負載與共址議題上已取得的進展。史薇特主席表示：「沒有放諸四海皆準的公式。每個區域、每個電網業者都面臨獨特的挑戰與機遇。」

  PJM表示，將此次命令視為既有工作的延伸。PJM外部傳播資深經理傑夫·席爾茲（Jeff Shields）說：「PJM已依據委員會的共址負載命令展開相關工作，PJM與各利益關係人正積極推進各項措施，致力於有效整合新增大型負載、強化供電可靠性，並促進公平透明的成本分攤。我們將委員會此次命令視為在此一動能基礎上繼續深化的契機。」

  電網業者提交報告後，各方利益關係人將有30天時間提出回應。

  ## 能源部進程與後續步驟

  此次行動是在美國能源部（DOE）去年啟動的大型負載併網程序基礎上推進的。當時各界憂慮現行規劃與併網程序並非為吉瓦級資料中心園區及其他快速成長的用電來源所設計。FERC表示，美國正面臨前所未有的電力需求成長，資料中心擴張是主要驅動力之一，委員會在發布上述命令前已審閱逾3,500頁的意見書。

  FERC表示，其目標在於加速大型負載整合，同時保護現有用戶免受成本轉嫁之害。委員會亦表明，任何改革措施均將適用於未來，意即不打算干擾新費率規章提交前已在進行或已完成的大型負載安排。

  公用事業公司、發電業者以及大型雲端與AI公司等超大規模業者，已在積極推動將資料中心與附近的天然氣、核能、再生能源及複合式發電專案配對建設。FERC對電氣鄰近及共址發電的關注，顯示監管機構預期隨著AI基礎設施持續擴張，這類安排將扮演更重要的角色。

  FERC保留能源部大型負載案件的審理空間，暗示在當前程序結束後，後續仍可能採取進一步監管行動。
