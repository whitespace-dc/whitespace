body_en: |
  PJM Interconnection filed proposed rules with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) on Aug. 13 that would require new large data centers to either secure their own new power capacity, have their needs covered through a forthcoming backstop procurement, or accept potential curtailment during periods of supply shortage — ending the assumption that new large industrial loads automatically share in the grid's existing capacity reserves.

  ## The filings and their context

  The Aug. 13 filing introduced two linked instruments: the Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS) — previously known as Connect and Manage — and the Large Load Registry that would track customers subject to its terms. The filings followed PJM's July 31 submission of a proposed Reliability Backstop Procurement to FERC and the launch that same day of its FERC-approved Expedited Interconnection Track for new generation.

  Together, the package represents the most substantial revision to PJM's approach to large load growth since the grid operator launched its Critical Issue Fast Path process for large loads in 2025. The framework became substantially more concrete in late July and August 2026.

  ## The capacity shortfall behind the action

  Two consecutive capacity auctions falling short of PJM's full reliability requirement provided the immediate catalyst. PJM's July 2026 capacity auction for the 2028/2029 delivery year procured 138,318 megawatts (MW) of unforced capacity — a measure of how much power a resource can reliably deliver — through the centralized auction. Even after including Fixed Resource Requirement resources, the region came up 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement. The auction cleared at the FERC-approved price cap of $325 per MW-day. It was the second consecutive auction in which PJM failed to procure its full reliability requirement, something that had not occurred before these two auctions.

  The shortfall is occurring as demand growth has accelerated sharply. PJM's 2026 long-term forecast projects summer peak demand growing at an average annual rate of 3.6% over the next decade, compared with 0.3% in the comparable 2021 forecast. Summer peak demand is projected to rise by nearly 66 gigawatts (GW) over 10 years. Data centers are a major component of the large-load adjustments driving that change; PJM's resource-adequacy planning work says prospective large-load additions could reach as much as 70 GW by 2038.

  ## How IRAS would work

  Under the proposed IRAS, a new large load that does not bring its own new capacity — and is not covered by the Reliability Backstop Procurement — could still connect to the grid. PJM defines a Large Load as an end-use customer with cumulative peak demand of at least 50 MW at a single site behind one or more delivery points or points of interconnection within a one-mile radius.

  If PJM faces a capacity shortage, it would direct affected zones to reduce demand in proportion to their share of new Large Load not supported by new generation. States and electric distributors would determine which retail customers fulfill a zonal reduction before PJM calls on broader Pre-Emergency Load Management resources. PJM said only the megawatts required to address an actual real-time resource-adequacy shortage would be subject to reduction — making the mechanism more targeted than a blanket policy of disconnecting data centers first.

  The proposed IRAS treatment would apply beginning June 1, 2027, to new Large Loads that have not brought their own new capacity or otherwise secured supply.

  Beginning with the 2029/2030 capacity auction, PJM is also proposing to exclude incremental new Large Loads that have not brought their own new supply from the demand figure used to determine how much capacity PJM procures in future auctions. The change is intended to prevent existing consumers from bearing higher capacity costs created by new large loads that have not contracted for the supply needed to serve them.

  ## The Bring Your Own New Capacity path

  PJM frames the compliance alternative as Bring Your Own New Capacity, or BYONC. Under BYONC, a load-serving entity does not need to build generation adjacent to the data center; it can instead identify qualifying new supply through contractual arrangements that add accredited capacity to the PJM system. PJM has also launched a facilitated bilateral matchmaking process designed to connect large-load customers seeking long-term power with developers of new generation, storage, and demand-side resources.

  The practical consequence for developers is that power procurement moves earlier in the development cycle. A company planning a 500 MW or 1 GW campus may need to advance IT infrastructure, utility interconnection, and generation procurement in parallel rather than sequentially. Sites with credible capacity strategies would carry materially different reliability and commercial profiles from projects that rely solely on regional generation to catch up with load growth.

  ## Expedited interconnection for new generation

  On the supply side, FERC approved PJM's temporary Expedited Interconnection Track in June, and PJM began accepting applications on July 31. The program can advance as many as 10 qualifying generation projects per calendar year during 2026 and 2027. To qualify, a project must provide at least 250 MW of unforced capacity, have support from an appropriate state siting authority, and be capable of entering commercial operation within three years. PJM expects successful applicants to reach a Generation Interconnection Agreement within roughly 10 months.

  The mechanism is aimed at shovel-ready generation rather than speculative projects. FERC Commissioner David Rosner, in his concurrence on the Expedited Interconnection Track order, noted that faster interconnection timelines do not eliminate state permitting, siting, financing, equipment procurement, or construction delays — steps that state authorities, load-serving entities, and developers must still complete independently.

  ## The Reliability Backstop Procurement

  PJM's July 31 filing proposed a one-time backstop procurement aimed directly at filling the 6,831 MW gap identified in the 2028/2029 auction. Assuming regulatory approval, PJM plans a bidding window running from Sept. 30 through Oct. 21, with results expected by Dec. 2. PJM said it would reduce the procurement target to account for new supply secured through bilateral contracts, integrated resource plans, or qualifying demand-side arrangements. The backstop contracts could extend as long as 15 years, which PJM described as an unusually long commitment compared with the grid operator's normal capacity-market terms.

  ## What the framework means for data center development

  The IRAS framework shifts a portion of grid reliability risk explicitly onto new large loads that do not secure their own supply, rather than distributing that risk across all existing consumers through the regional capacity market. The choice PJM is presenting to developers is binary: invest in capacity procurement early in the development cycle, or accept defined interruptibility under shortage conditions.

  PJM's next procedural milestone on the backstop procurement is the opening of the bidding window on Sept. 30.
body_zh: |
  PJM Interconnection於8月13日向美國聯邦能源監管委員會（FERC）提交擬議法規，要求新大型資料中心自行取得新增電力容量、透過即將推出的後備採購機制加以涵蓋，或在電力供應短缺期間接受可能的限電——終結了新大型工業用電可自動共享電網既有容量儲備的慣例假設。

  ## 申報文件與背景脈絡

  8月13日的申報引入了兩項相互連結的機制：臨時資源充足服務（Interim Resource Adequacy Service，IRAS）——此前稱為「接入與管理」（Connect and Manage）——以及追蹤受其條款約束客戶的大型負載登記（Large Load Registry）。這些申報緊隨PJM於7月31日向FERC提交的擬議可靠度後備採購案，以及同日啟動的FERC核准快速併網通道（供新發電專案使用）。

  整套方案是PJM自2025年為大型負載啟動「重大議題快速處理程序」以來，針對大型負載成長管理最重大的一次修訂。整體框架於2026年7月底至8月間趨於具體成形。

  ## 促成行動的容量缺口

  連續兩次容量拍賣未達PJM完整可靠度需求，構成此次行動的直接導火線。PJM於2026年7月針對2028／2029年輸電年度舉行的容量拍賣，透過集中拍賣共取得138,318 MW的非強制容量——此為衡量電力資源可靠輸出能力的指標。即便納入固定資源需求（Fixed Resource Requirement）資源，整個地區仍短缺6,831 MW，未達可靠度要求。拍賣以FERC核准的每MW日325美元上限價格結清。這是PJM連續第二次在容量拍賣中未能取得完整可靠度所需容量，此前從未出現過連續兩次的情況。

  容量缺口浮現之際，需求成長已急遽加速。PJM 2026年長期預測顯示，未來十年夏季尖峰需求的年均成長率為3.6%，遠高於2021年同期預測的0.3%。夏季尖峰需求預計在十年內增加近66 GW。資料中心是帶動這一變化的大型負載調整的主要因素；PJM資源充足規劃工作指出，潛在大型負載增量至2038年可能多達70 GW。

  ## IRAS的運作方式

  根據擬議的IRAS，未自行帶入新容量、且未獲可靠度後備採購涵蓋的新大型負載，仍可接入電網。PJM將大型負載定義為：在一英里半徑範圍內一個或多個輸電點或互聯點後方，單一場址累計尖峰需求達50 MW以上的終端用電客戶。

  若PJM面臨容量短缺，將指示受影響區域按其未由新發電支撐的新大型負載比例，相應削減需求。在PJM動用更廣泛的緊急前負載管理（Pre-Emergency Load Management）資源之前，各州及電力配送業者將決定由哪些零售客戶承擔區域削減量。PJM表示，只有因應實際即時資源充足短缺所需的MW數量才會受到削減——使此機制比一律優先切斷資料中心的一刀切政策更具針對性。

  擬議的IRAS處理方式，將於2027年6月1日起適用於未自行帶入新容量或另行取得供電的新大型負載。

  從2029／2030年容量拍賣起，PJM也擬議將未自行帶入新供電的新增大型負載，排除於決定PJM未來拍賣採購容量的需求數字之外。此變更旨在防止現有消費者承擔由未簽訂所需供電合約的新大型負載所引發的更高容量成本。

  ## 「自備新容量」路徑

  PJM將合規替代方案定名為「自備新容量」（Bring Your Own New Capacity，BYONC）。在BYONC框架下，供電實體無需在資料中心旁自建發電設施，而可透過合約安排向PJM系統新增已取得認證的容量，藉此確認符合資格的新供電來源。PJM也已啟動一項協助配對的雙邊媒合程序，旨在將尋求長期電力的大型負載客戶，與新發電、儲能及需求端資源開發商連結起來。

  對開發商而言，實際影響在於電力採購必須提前至開發週期的更早階段。規劃500 MW或1 GW園區的企業，可能需要同步推進IT基礎設施、公用事業併網及發電採購，而非依序進行。具備可信容量策略的場址，在可靠度與商業條件上，將與純粹依賴區域發電追趕負載成長的專案呈現實質差異。

  ## 新發電的快速併網通道

  在供電端，FERC於6月核准了PJM的臨時快速併網通道，PJM於7月31日開始受理申請。該計畫在2026年及2027年每個曆年最多可推進10個符合資格的發電專案。符合資格的專案須提供至少250 MW的非強制容量、獲得相應州政府選址主管機關支持，並能在三年內投入商業運轉。PJM預計，成功申請者將在約10個月內簽訂發電併網協議。

  此機制針對的是具備立即動工條件的發電專案，而非投機性計畫。FERC委員David Rosner在快速併網通道命令的協同意見書中指出，加快併網時程並不能消除各州許可、選址、融資、設備採購或施工延誤等環節——這些步驟仍須由州政府主管機關、供電實體及開發商各自獨立完成。

  ## 可靠度後備採購

  PJM於7月31日提交的申報，擬議推出一次性後備採購，直接針對2028／2029年拍賣所發現的6,831 MW缺口。假設取得監管核准，PJM計畫於9月30日至10月21日開放競標，預計於12月2日前公布結果。PJM表示，將依據透過雙邊合約、綜合資源計畫或符合資格的需求端安排所取得的新供電，相應調降採購目標。後備合約期限最長可達15年——PJM描述這相較於電網業者正常容量市場條款而言，是異常長的承諾。

  ## 此框架對資料中心開發的意義

  IRAS框架明確將部分電網可靠度風險轉移至未自行取得供電的新大型負載，而非透過區域容量市場將風險分攤至所有既有消費者。PJM向開發商提出的選擇是二元的：在開發週期早期投入容量採購，或在短缺條件下接受明確的可中斷供電安排。

  PJM後備採購的下一個程序里程碑，是9月30日競標窗口的開放。
