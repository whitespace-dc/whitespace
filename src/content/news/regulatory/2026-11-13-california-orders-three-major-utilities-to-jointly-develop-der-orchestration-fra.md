body_en: |
  The California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) on Oct. 29 directed the state's three largest investor-owned utilities — Pacific Gas & Electric, Southern California Edison and San Diego Gas & Electric — to jointly develop a common framework for orchestrating distributed energy resources (DERs), which include customer-owned batteries, electric vehicles and other behind-the-meter assets. A formal proposal is due in October 2027.

  The order requires the framework to enable coordinated measurement, forecasting and scheduling of DER load and dispatch to meet location- and time-specific needs on California's distribution grid, the CPUC said. The commission listed dozens of mandatory design requirements, including open and interoperable communication protocols, data sharing, cybersecurity provisions, ratepayer protections, clear customer participation pathways, recognition of the locational and temporal value of DER output, and rules to prevent double compensation for DER owners.

  The CPUC order followed California's enactment of SB 913, a state law intended to significantly expand DERs' role in wholesale energy markets and resource adequacy planning. Industry advocates and leaders have said SB 913 could help address what they describe as California's utility affordability crisis, depending on how it is implemented by the CPUC and the California Independent System Operator (CAISO), the nonprofit that manages the state's bulk power grid.

  ## How the framework will be developed

  Rather than prescribing a finished design, the CPUC directed the utilities to develop their proposal through a working group process that will incorporate input from ratepayer representatives, energy service providers, community choice aggregators (CCAs) — local government entities that procure power on behalf of residents — and other stakeholders. The result is intended to establish a common set of rules and policies across California that utilities would implement over time, paced by technological readiness, the commission said.

  The working group is required to address a range of technical, market and customer-facing questions, including methodologies for assessing cost-effectiveness; how DERs become visible to distribution system operators who manage local grid segments; the role of DER aggregators, which bundle small assets into larger dispatchable blocks; technology ownership; flexible dispatch; and performance measurement and accountability.

  ## Flexible load planning as a grid management tool

  In a separate but related directive within the same order, the CPUC told utilities to evaluate how flexible electric loads — the ability to shift when devices such as EV chargers draw power, or when stationary batteries charge and discharge — can relieve grid constraints and support electrification. The commission said planning scenarios that more explicitly account for flexible demand will complement existing demand forecasts and help utilities assess how dynamic load shifts could affect the timing, size and necessity of grid infrastructure upgrades.

  CPUC Commissioner Christine Harada said in a statement that the order would help California utilities make better use of existing grid capacity, reduce interconnection delays for new customers and projects, and lower the cost of system upgrades. Commissioner Darcie Houck said the decision moves California toward a common framework for utilities to manage and forecast DERs and to create visibility for CAISO.

  ## What comes next

  The CPUC framed the order's goals as harnessing DERs to meet local electricity needs, support grid reliability, reduce or defer infrastructure investment, and advance California's electrification and decarbonization goals. The joint utility proposal, once submitted in October 2027, will mark the start of a longer implementation process, with deployment timed to technological readiness across the three utilities' service territories.
body_zh: |
  加州公用事業委員會（CPUC）於10月29日責令加州三大民營公用事業商——太平洋瓦斯電力公司（PG&E）、南加州愛迪生公司（SCE）及聖地牙哥瓦斯電力公司（SDG&E）——共同研擬一套協調分散式能源資源（DER）的統一框架。分散式能源資源涵蓋用戶自有電池、電動車及其他表後資產，正式提案須於2027年10月前完成。

  根據此命令，CPUC要求框架須能支援對加州配電網路在特定地點與特定時段的需求，就分散式能源資源的負載及調度進行協同量測、預測與排程。委員會列出數十項強制設計要求，包括：開放且具互通性的通訊協定、資料共享、網路安全措施、費率支付者保護機制、清晰的用戶參與途徑、承認分散式能源資源輸出的地點與時間價值，以及防止分散式能源資源業主重複獲償的相關規則。

  此命令的背景，是加州通過SB 913州法——該法律旨在大幅擴大分散式能源資源在批發能源市場及資源充足性規劃中的角色。業界倡導者與業者領袖表示，SB 913能否有助於緩解外界所稱的加州公用事業費率負擔危機，取決於CPUC與加州獨立系統運營商（CAISO）的落實方式；CAISO為負責管理加州大型輸電網路的非營利組織。

  ## 框架開發方式

  CPUC並未直接規定最終設計方案，而是責令三家公用事業商透過工作小組流程共同研擬提案，過程中將納入費率支付者代表、能源服務供應商、社區選擇聚合商（CCA）——即代表居民採購電力的地方政府實體——及其他利害關係人的意見。委員會表示，最終成果旨在確立一套跨全加州的統一規則與政策，各公用事業商將依據技術就緒程度，分階段逐步落實。

  工作小組須處理一系列技術、市場及面向用戶的議題，包括：評估成本效益的方法論；分散式能源資源如何對管理地方電網區段的配電系統運營商提高能見度；負責將小型資產整合為更大可調度區塊的分散式能源資源聚合商之角色；技術所有權；彈性調度；以及效能量測與責任歸屬機制。

  ## 彈性負載規劃作為電網管理工具

  在同一命令的另一相關指令中，CPUC要求公用事業商評估彈性電力負載的應用——亦即調整電動車充電樁等裝置的充電時段，或調節固定式電池充放電時機的能力——如何舒緩電網瓶頸並支援電氣化進程。委員會表示，更明確將彈性需求納入考量的規劃情境，將能補強現有需求預測，協助公用事業商評估動態負載調度對電網基礎設施升級時程、規模及必要性的影響。

  CPUC委員克里斯汀·原田（Christine Harada）在聲明中指出，此命令將協助加州公用事業商更有效運用現有電網容量、縮短新用戶及新案場的併網等待時間，並降低系統升級成本。委員達西·霍克（Darcie Houck）則表示，這項決定推動加州朝向建立公用事業管理與預測分散式能源資源的統一框架邁進，並為CAISO提供更清晰的可見度。

  ## 後續進展

  CPUC將此命令的目標定位為：善用分散式能源資源以滿足地方電力需求、支援電網可靠性、減少或推遲基礎設施投資，並推進加州的電氣化與去碳化目標。三家公用事業商的聯合提案一旦於2027年10月提交，將標誌著一段更長期落實流程的開始，部署進度將依三家公用事業商各自服務區域的技術就緒程度分階段推進。
