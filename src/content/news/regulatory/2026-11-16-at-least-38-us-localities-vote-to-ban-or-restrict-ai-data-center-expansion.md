body_en: |
  At least 38 local jurisdictions across the United States voted during the 2026 midterm elections to ban or strictly restrict AI data center construction, according to Taiwan's Commercial Times, as opposition to rising electricity costs transformed data center development into a ballot-box issue in states from California to Florida.

  The votes occurred across 13 states, which collectively saw at least 50 separate ballot measures related to data center development. The heaviest concentrations were in Ohio, California, Maine, Kansas, Illinois, and Florida, the publication reported.

  ## Electricity costs behind the backlash

  US residential electricity prices have risen more than 10 percent since 2024, with Ohio and other states seeing increases exceeding 20 percent, the Commercial Times reported. Residents have attributed the increases to utilities expanding generation capacity and upgrading grid infrastructure to meet the high power demands of data centers, with concern that those infrastructure costs are being passed through to household electricity bills.

  Nearly 90 percent of voters cited the cost of living as their primary voting consideration in this election cycle, the publication reported, describing AI data centers as an unexpected flashpoint linking energy consumption, electricity prices, and pressure on local environmental resources.

  ## What the ballot measures require

  The 38 jurisdictions that passed restrictive measures fall into four categories, as described by the Commercial Times:

  - **Power and size caps:** Ohio's Conneaut and Kansas's Ottawa both passed measures prohibiting data centers with peak load or total electricity demand exceeding 250 megawatts. Maine's North Berwick banned data center facilities exceeding 25,000 square feet.
  - **Development moratoriums:** Towns in Maine, including Wells and Peru, enacted 180-day freezes on permits and approvals. New Hampshire's Rockingham County passed a 12-month ban on accepting any data center permit applications.
  - **Outright construction bans:** California's Alhambra, in Los Angeles County, passed Measure DB with 82 percent of the vote, amending its land-use code to prohibit all data center construction within city limits. Florida's Edgewater and Ohio's Hubbard passed amendments prohibiting the approval, construction, or operation of new data centers within their borders.
  - **Advisory opposition:** Joliet Township in Illinois held an advisory referendum in which 79 percent of voters opposed city council approval of AI data center construction.

  ## State-level repercussions

  The backlash extended beyond local ballots to statewide races. In Texas, a major existing hub for data center investment, Democratic candidate Gina Hinojosa won the governorship on a platform that included a statewide pause on data center development, the Commercial Times reported.

  Democrats also recaptured both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections, which the publication said is expected to increase legislative scrutiny and oversight hearings of the AI sector. The Commercial Times noted, however, that the immediate determinants of data center build-out timelines are state-level electricity approvals and utility rate policies in states such as Texas, Georgia, Ohio, and Pennsylvania, governed by governors and local public utility commissions rather than Congress.

  ## How tech companies are adapting

  Microsoft, Amazon, and Google have been responding to grid interconnection approval delays of 24 to 36 months and local opposition through four broad strategies, according to the Commercial Times.

  **Secondary markets and overseas locations.** Companies are redirecting investment from primary data center hubs to smaller markets. The publication noted that secondary markets typically lack established deep-fiber backbone networks, requiring operators to spend hundreds of millions of dollars laying long-haul submarine or overland fiber to connect remote computing capacity back to core networks.

  **Bring Your Own Power (BYOP) and behind-the-meter microgrids.** The Commercial Times described this as the most immediately effective approach. More than 65 percent of data center operators are evaluating or using on-site power generation, typically through natural gas turbines or fuel cells, which the publication said can compress a five-year grid interconnection wait to more than one year. Natural gas generation conflicts with corporate net-zero carbon commitments, requires management of high-pressure gas pipelines and water-cooling infrastructure, and would necessitate additional investment in carbon capture and storage (CCS) technology to reduce emissions.

  **Distributed computing.** Spreading workloads across multiple facilities in different jurisdictions can reduce exposure to any single locality's approval threshold. The Commercial Times described this as the least efficient approach for AI model training, because it disrupts the physical proximity requirements of GPU compute clusters. The publication noted the industry is shifting measurement focus from Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) to Power Compute Effectiveness (PCE), which it described as measuring the ratio of energy converted into effective computing power, to better reflect efficiency losses in distributed architectures.

  **Flexible power agreements.** Signing agreements with local utilities to offer demand flexibility has helped operators manage delays and secure approvals.

  ## Industry implications

  The Commercial Times concluded that while ballot measures and regulatory restrictions will not halt US AI infrastructure buildout entirely, they have created material friction. The expected consequences include higher construction costs, delayed project completion schedules, and the need to reconfigure where investment is deployed.

  The election of a governor in Texas who supports a statewide data center development pause marks the most significant escalation of the opposition from local to state-level politics recorded in the election cycle, the publication indicated.
body_zh: |
  美國2026年期中選舉期間，全美至少38個地方行政區投票通過禁止或嚴格限制AI資料中心興建，據台灣《工商時報》報導，民眾對電費持續攀升的反彈，已將資料中心開發議題轉化為選票上的關鍵戰場，影響範圍從加州延伸至佛羅里達州。

  此次投票涉及13個州，合計出現至少50項與資料中心開發相關的公投議案。《工商時報》報導，提案最密集的地區集中在俄亥俄州、加州、緬因州、堪薩斯州、伊利諾州及佛羅里達州。

  ## 電費上漲引爆民怨

  《工商時報》報導，美國住宅電費自2024年以來已上漲逾10%，俄亥俄州等多個州的漲幅更超過20%。民眾普遍認為，電力公司為因應資料中心龐大的用電需求，大規模擴充發電容量、升級電網基礎設施，相關費用最終轉嫁至家庭電費帳單。

  《工商時報》指出，本屆選舉中近九成選民將生活成本列為首要投票考量，AI資料中心意外成為串聯能源消耗、電費價格與地方環境資源壓力的爭議焦點。

  ## 各項投票措施內容

  通過限制措施的38個行政區，依《工商時報》描述可歸納為四大類：

  - **電力與規模上限：** 俄亥俄州科諾特（Conneaut）與堪薩斯州渥太華（Ottawa）均通過措施，禁止峰值負載或總用電需求超過250百萬瓦（MW）的資料中心進駐。緬因州北伯里克（North Berwick）則禁止樓地板面積超過25,000平方英尺的資料中心設施。
  - **開發暫停令：** 緬因州威爾斯（Wells）及秘魯鎮（Peru）等地頒布180天許可暫停令，凍結相關申請與核准程序。新罕布夏州洛金漢郡（Rockingham County）則通過12個月禁令，全面停止受理任何資料中心許可申請。
  - **全面禁建：** 加州洛杉磯郡的阿罕布拉市（Alhambra）以82%得票率通過DB提案，修訂土地使用法規，全面禁止市內一切資料中心興建。佛羅里達州艾奇瓦特（Edgewater）及俄亥俄州哈伯德（Hubbard）亦通過修正案，禁止在轄區內核准、興建或營運任何新資料中心。
  - **諮詢性反對：** 伊利諾州喬利埃特鎮（Joliet Township）舉行諮詢性公投，79%的選民反對市議會核准AI資料中心興建案。

  ## 州層級的政治波及

  此波反彈效應不僅止於地方投票，更蔓延至州長選舉層面。《工商時報》報導，德克薩斯州本是資料中心投資的重鎮，民主黨候選人吉娜·伊諾霍薩（Gina Hinojosa）以包含全州資料中心開發暫停在內的政見，贏得該州州長職位。

  此外，民主黨亦在期中選舉中重新取得國會兩院多數席次，《工商時報》預期此將加強立法部門對AI產業的審查力道及聽證監督。不過，《工商時報》也指出，資料中心實際建設時程的直接決定因素，仍在於德克薩斯州、喬治亞州、俄亥俄州及賓夕法尼亞州等地的州級電力核准程序與公用事業費率政策，這些事務由各州州長及地方公用事業委員會主導，而非由國會決定。

  ## 科技業者的因應之道

  面對電網併網審批動輒延遲24至36個月及各地民間阻力，微軟、亞馬遜與谷歌正採取四大因應策略，《工商時報》報導如下。

  **次級市場與海外據點。** 業者正將投資從主要資料中心樞紐轉向規模較小的市場。《工商時報》指出，次級市場通常缺乏成熟的長途光纖骨幹網路，業者須投入數億美元鋪設長途海底電纜或陸上光纖，方能將遠端運算資源接回核心網路。

  **自備電源（BYOP）與後端微電網。** 《工商時報》將此列為目前最具立竿見影效果的策略。逾65%的資料中心業者正在評估或採用現地自發電方案，通常以天然氣渦輪或燃料電池為主，據報導可將原本長達五年的電網併網等待期壓縮至一年以上。然而，天然氣發電與企業的淨零碳排承諾相悖，同時需要管理高壓輸氣管線與水冷基礎設施，並須額外投資碳捕集與封存（CCS）技術以降低排放。

  **分散式運算。** 將工作負載分散至多個不同行政區的設施，可降低在單一地點遭遇核准瓶頸的風險。《工商時報》指出，此方式對AI模型訓練而言效率最低，因為它破壞了GPU運算叢集對實體鄰近性的基本需求。報導並提及，業界正逐漸將衡量指標從電力使用效率（PUE）轉向算力電力效率（PCE），後者衡量能源轉化為有效運算力的比率，更能反映分散式架構下的效率損耗。

  **彈性電力協議。** 與地方電力公司簽訂需量回應彈性協議，有助於業者管理審批延遲、爭取核准。

  ## 產業影響

  《工商時報》最終結論指出，儘管公投措施與法規限制不會完全阻斷美國AI基礎設施的建設腳步，但已造成實質阻力。預期後果包括建設成本上揚、專案完工時程延宕，以及投資布局須重新調配。

  報導指出，德克薩斯州選出支持全州資料中心開發暫停的州長一事，標誌著本屆選舉週期中，資料中心反對浪潮從地方層級升級至州層級最具指標意義的一刻。
