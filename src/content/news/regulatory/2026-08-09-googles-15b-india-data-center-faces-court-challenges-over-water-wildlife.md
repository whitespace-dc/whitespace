body_en: |
  Google's planned data center campus in Visakhapatnam, India is facing four separate legal challenges and escalating public opposition over its impact on water supplies and a nearby wildlife sanctuary, creating early hurdles for the U.S. tech giant's largest-ever investment in the country.

  The Andhra Pradesh High Court on Monday asked the state government to defend against a public interest litigation filed by activist group Jal Biradari (Water Community), which alleges the project will strain a nearby reservoir already serving a city that receives 70 million litres of water per day less than its population of 2.5 million requires. The court's next hearing is set for August 24.

  Google is building the $15 billion campus in partnership with Indian billionaire Gautam Adani's group. The southern state of Andhra Pradesh, governed by an ally of Prime Minister Narendra Modi, has described the investment as historic and transformational and has said the project is expected to create up to 188,000 jobs.

  ## Court cases and regulatory pressure

  Beyond the High Court proceeding, the Human Rights Forum has filed three additional cases at India's environmental court demanding a halt to construction. Those filings argue the state approved the project without properly assessing the impact of drawing supply from a rural drinking-water scheme, according to court documents.

  The state government denied that the project was fast-tracked without weighing environmental risks. In a statement to Reuters, it called activists' concerns "incorrect and misleading," while adding it is open to feedback. "Such protests are their democratic right. In case any of these claims are legitimate, government is committed to engage and provide factual clarifications and suitable redressal," the state said.

  ## Water supply strain

  Visakhapatnam receives 410 million litres of water per day from its reservoirs and rivers against a stated daily requirement of 480 million litres, and rationing of water supplies is common across the city.

  Green Visakha, a local non-profit, raised concern at a public gathering Sunday about the state's promise of "guaranteed" water supplies to the data center for 20 years given the existing shortfall. "Development should not be at the cost of livelihood of the people," said Raja Rama Mohan Roy, the group's founder, at the event, which Reuters attended. In recent weeks, activists and children have marched through Visakhapatnam carrying banners reading "We cannot drink DATA" and painting handcuffs on the Google logo, according to social media posts.

  The state said no water allocated for rural or residential use will be directed to the data centers, and the nearby reservoir will not be drawn upon. Google said in a statement to Reuters that it will implement "advanced air cooling to protect vital local water resources," a design approach that reduces dependence on water-based server cooling.

  ## Wildlife and noise concerns

  The Kambalakonda Wildlife Sanctuary, which is home to leopards and pangolins, sits 860 metres from the construction site. The High Court petition also alleges that heavy construction and associated noise will harm the sanctuary. The state said the site is farther from the sanctuary than the law requires. Google said it will implement sound-dampening measures to be "a quiet, unobtrusive neighbour."

  Work was visibly underway when Reuters visited the site Monday, with earthmovers operating on a hillside stripped to red earth and terraced into steps.

  ## Industry context and what comes next

  Data centers are drawing opposition worldwide over their consumption of water and electricity, with advocates arguing the impact is more pronounced in developing nations where baseline infrastructure is already under pressure. In the United States, opponents held 142 protests across 42 states in July over similar concerns, according to the report.

  For Google, the Visakhapatnam project is its single largest India investment. The Andhra Pradesh High Court is scheduled to hear the Jal Biradari petition again on August 24.

  Google 計畫在印度維沙卡帕特南市（Visakhapatnam）興建的資料中心園區，正面臨四起獨立法律訴訟及日益升溫的民眾反對聲浪，爭議焦點在於該計畫對當地水源供應及附近野生動物保護區的潛在衝擊，為這家美國科技巨擘在印度史上最大規模的投資案增添了重重阻礙。

  安德拉邦（Andhra Pradesh）高等法院本週一要求邦政府就民間公益訴訟進行答辯——訴訟方為倡議團體「賈爾比拉達里」（Jal Biradari，意為「水源社群」），該組織指控，此計畫將使附近一座水庫不堪重負；而這座水庫所服務的城市，每日用水量已較250萬市民的實際需求短缺7,000萬公升。法院定於8月24日進行下一次審理。

  Google 此次攜手印度億萬富翁高塔姆．阿達尼（Gautam Adani）旗下集團，共同開發這座斥資150億美元的園區。由印度總理納倫德拉．莫迪（Narendra Modi）盟友執政的安德拉邦，將這項投資形容為具有歷史意義、足以帶來深遠轉變的大事，並預估計畫最多可創造18萬8,000個就業機會。

  ## 法院訴訟與監管壓力

  除安德拉邦高等法院正在審理的案件外，人權論壇（Human Rights Forum）已向印度環境法院追加提出三起訴訟，要求叫停施工。依據法院文件，這三份訴狀主張，邦政府在批准此計畫時，未就從農村飲用水系統取水所造成的衝擊進行妥善評估。

  邦政府否認計畫係在未充分評估環境風險的情況下倉促核准。政府在回覆路透社的聲明中，將倡議人士的憂慮定調為「不正確且具誤導性」，同時表示願意接受各方意見。「此類抗議是民主社會的正當權利。若其中任何指控確有依據，政府承諾積極回應，提供真實說明與妥善的申訴管道，」邦政府如此表示。

  ## 水資源供應問題

  維沙卡帕特南市每日可從水庫及河川取得4億1,000萬公升的水，但市民每日實際需求高達4億8,000萬公升，分區限量配給供水在全市早已是司空見慣之事。

  當地非營利組織「綠色維沙卡」（Green Visakha）週日在一場公開集會上表達疑慮，指出在城市本已存在供水缺口的情況下，邦政府仍向資料中心承諾提供長達20年的「保障」供水，令人憂心。「發展不應以犧牲人民的生計為代價，」該組織創辦人拉賈．拉瑪．莫漢．羅伊（Raja Rama Mohan Roy）在路透社記者出席的集會上如此說道。近幾週來，倡議人士和兒童手持「我們不能喝 DATA（資料）」的標語橫幅，走上維沙卡帕特南街頭遊行，並在 Google 商標上塗繪手銬圖案，相關畫面已在社群媒體上廣泛流傳。

  對此，邦政府表示，原先分配給農村或住宅用途的水源，絕不會被轉供資料中心使用，附近水庫亦不在取水範疇之內。Google 則向路透社聲明，公司將採用「先進的氣冷技術，以保護當地珍貴的水資源」，此一設計方案可大幅降低對水冷式伺服器散熱系統的依賴。

  ## 野生動物與噪音疑慮

  甘巴拉孔達野生動物保護區（Kambalakonda Wildlife Sanctuary）距施工現場僅860公尺，區內棲息著豹與穿山甲等野生動物。安德拉邦高等法院的訴狀亦指出，大規模施工及相關噪音將對保護區造成危害。邦政府則表示，工地與保護區之間的距離符合相關法規的要求。Google 表示，公司將採取隔音措施，力求成為「安靜、不造成打擾的好鄰居」。

  路透社記者週一前往現場採訪時，施工作業正在持續進行中——挖土機在一座已被剷除植被、依山勢整平為紅土階梯地形的山坡上穿梭作業。

  ## 產業背景與後續發展

  全球各地的資料中心正因大量消耗水資源與電力而引發反對聲浪，倡議人士指出，在基礎設施本已捉襟見肘的開發中國家，此類衝擊尤為顯著。根據相關報告，僅在美國，7月間便有反對人士在42個州發起了142場抗議活動，訴求同樣與水資源和電力問題有關。

  對 Google 而言，維沙卡帕特南計畫是其在印度單筆金額最高的投資項目。安德拉邦高等法院預定於8月24日，就賈爾比拉達里提出的公益訴訟進行下一輪審理。
