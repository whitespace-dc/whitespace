body_en: |
  Virginia Gov. Abigail Spanberger on Monday proposed a six-year freeze on base residential electricity rates as the price for state regulatory approval of the $67 billion proposed merger between Dominion Energy and NextEra Energy — a combination the utilities say would create the largest regulated electric utility in the world.

  ## Governor's conditions

  Spanberger filed expert witness testimony with the State Corporation Commission (SCC), Virginia's utility regulator, calling for monthly bill credits of $29 for four years. That figure is more than triple the $10 per month the utilities originally offered. Her filing also calls for raising the debt share of Dominion's capital structure from 48% to 60%, a structural change intended to lower the company's allowed rate of return and reduce upward pressure on customer bills.

  The testimony was filed by Richard McCann, an environmental consultant with the firm M.Cubed. McCann called additionally for adopting a "probability of dispatch" cost allocation method — a formula for how power plant dispatch costs are divided among customers — along with improved disconnection policies and a range of energy efficiency and clean energy commitments. "Mandatory, Comprehensive Enforceability provisions must be imposed to hold the Joint Petitioners fully accountable for any violation of commitments, including the labor and workforce commitments," McCann wrote. "Unless these additional commitments are substantially addressed or alternative solutions are identified, the Commission should reject the joint petition."

  "Any proposed merger involving Virginia's primary state-regulated utility must benefit families, small businesses, and Virginia's workforce — not just the shareholders of these multi-billion-dollar corporations," Spanberger said in a statement.

  ## Widening opposition at the SCC

  Spanberger's filing arrived on the same day as separate submissions from Virginia's attorney general and SCC staff, both opposing the deal unless additional terms they propose are also adopted. Lt. Gov. Ghazala Hashmi had previously pushed back on the merger as well.

  The SCC is conducting a formal review of the merger and will weigh all filings through its established regulatory process.

  ## Background: merger origins and prior concessions

  Dominion and NextEra announced the proposed merger in May, stating it would facilitate a more cost-effective buildout of Virginia's electric grid to meet rising electricity demand from large-load data centers. The utilities initially proposed $10-a-month customer bill credits for two years. As opposition mounted, they extended the proposed credit period to four years and made additional assurances on clean energy, stating those commitments were in line with Virginia's decarbonization statute, the Virginia Clean Economy Act.

  ## Utilities' response

  Following Spanberger's announcement, Dominion and NextEra issued a joint statement: "As expected in a proceeding of this size and significance, today's testimony reflects a wide range of viewpoints and interests. We are reviewing the filings and will address the issues raised through the Commission's established process."

  ## What the filings mean for the proceeding

  Simultaneous opposition from the governor's office, the attorney general, and SCC staff represents a broad front of state-level resistance, and signals that the merger faces substantial conditions before any approval can occur. The governor's proposal — particularly the shift in Dominion's debt-to-equity ratio and a six-year rate freeze — would, if adopted, constrain the combined utility's financial structure and limit its ability to raise base rates for most of the next decade.

  Spanberger was scheduled to hold a media call Tuesday afternoon to discuss her proposal further, with the SCC proceeding ongoing.
body_zh: |
  維吉尼亞州長阿比蓋爾・史班伯格（Abigail Spanberger）週一提議，凍結住宅基本電費長達六年，作為州監管機構核准Dominion Energy與NextEra Energy合併案的代價。這筆高達670億美元的合併案，兩家電力公司聲稱將催生全球最大的受規管電力公司。

  ## 州長開出的條件

  史班伯格向維吉尼亞州企業委員會（State Corporation Commission，SCC）——州政府的電力監管機構——提交了專家證人證詞，要求連續四年每月向用戶提供29美元的電費抵免。這一金額超過兩家電力公司原先承諾每月10美元的三倍以上。她的申請文件同時要求將Dominion資本結構中的債務比例從48%提高至60%，此一結構性調整旨在壓低公司獲准的資本回報率，從而減少電費上漲的壓力。

  上述證詞由環境顧問公司M.Cubed的顧問理查德・麥肯（Richard McCann）代表州長提交。麥肯進一步主張採用「調度機率」（probability of dispatch）成本分攤方式——一套決定電廠調度成本如何在用戶之間分配的計算公式——並要求改善斷電政策，以及一系列能源效率與潔淨能源方面的承諾。麥肯在證詞中寫道：「必須施加強制性、全面性的可執行條款，讓合併申請雙方對任何違反承諾的行為——包括勞工與人力資源承諾——承擔完全責任。除非這些額外承諾獲得實質性回應，或找到替代方案，否則委員會應駁回聯合申請。」

  史班伯格在聲明中表示：「任何涉及維吉尼亞州主要州級受規管電力公司的合併提案，都必須讓家庭、小型企業和維吉尼亞勞工受益，而不只是讓這些數十億美元跨國企業的股東獲益。」

  ## SCC反對聲浪擴大

  史班伯格的申請文件與維吉尼亞州檢察長及SCC幕僚的獨立申請書同日遞交，後兩者均對此合併案持反對立場，除非相關單位提出的額外條款也一併獲得採納。副州長加扎拉・哈什米（Ghazala Hashmi）此前亦已公開反對這項合併案。

  SCC目前正依法對此合併案進行正式審查，將依既定監管程序審酌所有申請文件。

  ## 背景：合併案緣起與先前讓步

  Dominion與NextEra於今年五月宣布這項合併計畫，表示此舉將有助於更具成本效益地建設維吉尼亞州電網，以因應大型負載資料中心帶動的電力需求成長。兩家電力公司最初提議提供每月10美元的用戶電費抵免，為期兩年。隨著反對聲浪升高，他們將抵免期限延長至四年，並就潔淨能源作出額外保證，聲稱相關承諾符合維吉尼亞州去碳化法規《維吉尼亞潔淨能源經濟法》（Virginia Clean Economy Act）的要求。

  ## 電力公司的回應

  史班伯格宣布上述提案後，Dominion與NextEra隨即聯合發表聲明：「正如此規模與重要性的程序所預期，今日的證詞反映了廣泛的觀點與利益考量。我們正在審閱相關申請文件，並將透過委員會的既定程序處理所提出的各項議題。」

  ## 此次申請文件對審查程序的意義

  州長辦公室、檢察長及SCC幕僚同步提出反對，形成了廣泛的州層級阻力，並預示著合併案在獲批之前將面臨大量附加條件。倘若州長的提案——尤其是調整Dominion債股比例及六年電費凍結——獲得採納，將對合併後電力公司的財務結構形成約束，並限制其在未來近十年內調漲基本電費的能力。

  史班伯格預計於週二下午召開媒體電話會議，進一步說明其提案，SCC審查程序目前仍在進行中。
