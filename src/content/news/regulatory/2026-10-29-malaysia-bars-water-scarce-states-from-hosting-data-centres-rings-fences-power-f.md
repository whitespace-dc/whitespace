body_en: |
  Malaysia will deny data centre applications in states with insufficient water supply and has ring-fenced the sector's electricity allocation from the country's other industrial users, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani said Wednesday during his ministry's winding-up speech on Budget 2027 in the Dewan Rakyat, the lower house of parliament.

  The government has approved 117 data centres valued at RM385.7 billion (approximately US$88 billion) nationwide. Of those, 55 are operational, 19 are under construction, 16 have received approval but have not yet broken ground, and 27 are at various stages of implementation, with their electricity needs expected to be met through off-grid arrangements.

  If all 117 approved facilities are completed and become fully operational, electricity demand from data centres alone could reach between 23,000 megawatts (MW) and 23,500 MW, Johari said. One megawatt of capacity is roughly enough to power 800 to 1,000 average homes; 23,000 MW represents a substantial share of national generation capacity.

  ## New screening criteria

  Malaysia has no plans to impose a blanket moratorium on new data centre approvals, Johari said, but will evaluate each application against three criteria: water availability, electricity supply, and the financial strength of the investor. States that cannot demonstrate adequate water resources will not be permitted to host facilities regardless of available land, he said.

  The Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA), the government's principal investment promotion and approval agency, will coordinate with state governments to assess water requirements for each proposed project. Electricity availability will be evaluated in consultation with Tenaga Nasional Bhd (TNB), the national utility, traded on Bursa Malaysia under the ticker TENAGA.

  ## Electricity separated from other industries

  To protect TNB's reserve margin — the buffer of spare generation capacity that prevents blackouts — and to ensure continued supply to existing businesses, the government has formally separated data centres' electricity requirements from those of the 27 to 28 other industrial sectors that TNB serves, Johari said.

  "There has been misunderstanding in the country regarding data centres," Johari said, adding that the separation was necessary to ensure other industries continue to receive sufficient electricity to sustain their operations.

  Data centres connected to the TNB grid will be charged at market rates without government subsidies, with pricing determined by global market conditions. Operators will also be held to a take-or-pay structure: a facility that contracts for 300 MW but consumes only 100 MW must still pay for the unused 200 MW. The arrangement is intended to discourage over-contracting and to recover the cost of capacity reserved on the operator's behalf.

  ## Local content and gas redirection

  All data centre developments in Malaysia will be required to meet a minimum local content threshold of 30% for data centre-related work, Johari said.

  The rapid expansion of the sector could also create a new domestic market for Malaysia's natural gas resources, Johari said. Malaysia is the world's fifth-largest gas exporter and currently ships approximately 30 million tonnes of gas annually, largely under long-term agreements with overseas buyers. The growing electricity demand from data centres could allow state energy company PETRONAS to redirect some of that gas toward domestic power generation rather than export, Johari said, providing an alternative revenue channel while supporting both data centre operators and existing industries.

  ## What the measures mean

  The water and electricity screening rules mark a shift from volume-led data centre attraction toward capacity-constrained management of the sector. By conditioning approvals on demonstrated resource availability — and by charging market rates with mandatory capacity payments — Malaysian authorities are signalling that future growth will be rationed against physical infrastructure limits rather than approved on demand.

  Johari said the measures aim to ensure that data centre investments benefit Malaysia while taking into account the country's water and electricity resources.

  Electricity demand projections of up to 23,500 MW from the approved pipeline, combined with the potential reorientation of PETRONAS gas exports toward domestic generation, indicate that the sector's resource footprint will remain a central consideration for energy and industrial policy in the period ahead.
body_zh: |
  馬來西亞投資、貿易及工業部長拿督斯里佐哈里·阿都加尼（Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani）週三在下議院（Dewan Rakyat）就2027年財政預算案進行部門總結發言時表示，馬來西亞將拒絕水資源不足州屬的資料中心設立申請，並已將該產業的用電配額與國內其他工業用戶分開管理。

  政府已在全國批准117座資料中心，總值達3857億令吉（約合880億美元）。其中55座已投入運營，19座正在興建，16座已獲批准但尚未動工，另有27座處於不同實施階段，其用電需求預計將透過離網方式解決。

  佐哈里表示，若所有117座已獲批設施全部完工並正式投運，單是資料中心的用電需求便可能達到2萬3000至2萬3500百萬瓦（MW）。1 MW的容量大約可供800至1000個普通家庭用電；2萬3000 MW相當於全國發電容量的相當大比例。

  ## 新審核標準

  佐哈里表示，馬來西亞目前無意全面暫停新資料中心的審批，但將依據三項標準逐案評估：水資源供應、電力供應，以及投資方的財務實力。他說，無法證明擁有充足水資源的州屬，無論土地是否充裕，一律不得設立資料中心。

  馬來西亞投資發展局（MIDA）身為政府主要的投資促進與審批機構，將與各州政府協調，評估每個擬建項目的用水需求。電力供應方面，則將諮詢國家電力公司馬電力（Tenaga Nasional Bhd，TNB，馬交所股票代碼：TENAGA）進行評估。

  ## 電力供應與其他產業分開管理

  佐哈里表示，為保護馬電力的備用容量——即防止停電的備用發電緩衝——並確保現有企業持續獲得電力供應，政府已正式將資料中心的用電需求，與馬電力所服務的其他27至28個工業部門分開管理。

  佐哈里說：「國內對資料中心存在一些誤解。」他補充指出，此次分開管理是必要之舉，以確保其他產業能繼續獲得充足電力維持運營。

  接入馬電力電網的資料中心將按市場價格計費，不享有政府補貼，電價由全球市場行情決定。業者亦須遵守「用或付」（take-or-pay）機制：若某設施簽約購買300 MW電力，但實際僅消耗100 MW，仍須為未使用的200 MW付費。此安排旨在遏制過度簽約，並收回為業者預留容量的成本。

  ## 本地含量要求與天然氣重新調配

  佐哈里表示，馬來西亞所有資料中心開發項目，其資料中心相關工程的本地含量須達至少30%的最低門檻。

  佐哈里指出，資料中心產業的快速擴張也可能為馬來西亞的天然氣資源創造新的國內市場。馬來西亞是全球第五大天然氣出口國，目前每年出口約3000萬噸天然氣，主要依據與海外買家簽訂的長期協議進行。他表示，資料中心日益增長的用電需求，或將使國有能源公司國油（PETRONAS）得以將部分天然氣從出口轉向國內發電，在支持資料中心業者及現有產業的同時，也為國油開闢另一條收入管道。

  ## 措施的深遠意義

  水資源與電力篩查規定，標誌著馬來西亞的資料中心招商策略，從以量取勝轉向受制於基礎設施容量的管理模式。透過將審批條件與資源可用性掛鉤——並以市場價格收費、強制執行容量付款機制——馬來西亞當局正在發出訊號：未來的成長將依據實體基礎設施限制加以管控，而非按需審批。

  佐哈里表示，上述措施旨在確保資料中心投資惠及馬來西亞，同時兼顧國家的水資源與電力資源。

  已批准項目管線所預測高達2萬3500 MW的用電需求，加上國油天然氣出口或轉向國內發電的可能性，顯示資料中心產業的資源消耗，在未來一段時期內仍將是能源與工業政策的核心考量。
