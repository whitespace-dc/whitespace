body_en: |
  A newly released Oxford Economics study commissioned by Digital Prosperity Asia (DPA) warns that a shift toward more restrictive digital regulation could cut annual venture capital (VC) investment in Malaysia by approximately US$186 million. Economic modelling in the report projects that under a restrictive regulatory scenario, VC funding would decline 26% between 2026 and 2035, equivalent to around US$198 million (RM792 million, where RM denotes Malaysian ringgit) less per year. A more enabling regulatory approach, the study finds, could instead add around US$49.5 million (RM198 million) annually over the same period.

  The study examined how digital regulations affect compliance costs, talent allocation, innovation investment, and capital access across Malaysia's startup ecosystem. It found that while Malaysia currently takes a "broadly enabling" approach to digital regulation, balancing safeguards in higher-risk domains with openness across the broader digital economy, regulatory design choices over the coming decade carry significant implications for startup growth and investment.

  ## Compliance now a structural operating cost

  For most Malaysian startups, regulatory compliance has become an embedded operational cost rather than a one-time adjustment. Eighty-eight percent of startups surveyed reported operational constraints from digital regulations; 23% described the impact as major or severe.

  Compliance spending is now substantial across the sector: 81% of startups said digital regulations increase compliance-related costs. More than eight in ten startups allocate more than 5% of operating budgets to compliance, and 39% of those firms devote more than 15% of operating costs to meeting regulatory requirements.

  Sixty-eight percent of startups said they had already taken active steps in response to digital regulatory requirements, including building new compliance processes, shifting workloads to compliant cloud service providers, and engaging external legal and advisory services.

  ## Talent costs rise as R&D spending falls

  Compliance requirements are reshaping how startups deploy people and capital. Eighty-seven percent of startups said digital regulations have affected workforce costs or management, with 74% specifically reporting rising human capital costs tied to growing demand for expertise in compliance, cybersecurity, and data governance.

  Financial resources are also being redirected away from product development. Sixty-seven percent of startups said funds are being diverted from research and development (R&D) toward compliance, a trend corroborated by 64% of VCs and incubators surveyed. Fifty-seven percent of startups reported delays in product development or longer time-to-market as a result, while 59% of VCs said they observed a slowdown in innovation momentum across their portfolios.

  ## Regulatory uncertainty weighs on capital raising

  Digital regulations are influencing capital allocation at the investor level. Sixty-three percent of startups said digital regulations increase market uncertainty and make raising capital more difficult. Seventy-three percent of VCs said regulations heighten uncertainty around investment returns.

  Expectations of tighter regulation also dampen investment sentiment. Under a more restrictive regulatory scenario, the share of startups expecting increased investment falls from 47% to 27%, according to the study.

  ## What the economic modelling shows

  Oxford Economics modelled three regulatory scenarios for the 2026 to 2035 period. The restrictive scenario projects a 26% decline in VC funding, equivalent to around US$198 million (RM792 million) less per year. The enabling scenario projects a 6% increase, adding around US$49.5 million (RM198 million) annually.

  "The study highlights how regulations influence decisions across Malaysia's startup ecosystem," said Henry Worthington, managing director of Economic Consulting at Oxford Economics. "Startups face immediate pressures as they navigate compliance across a broad range of digital regulations. Meeting these requirements often demands specialised talent and changes to operating models, diverting resources from innovation and growth. The implication is not that safeguards should be weakened, but that regulatory design, proportionality and predictability will be critical to sustaining Malaysia's startup momentum."

  ## Policy implications

  Koh Liang Wei of DPA said Malaysia's relatively enabling regulatory approach is an important asset for startups but argued that as rules on data, cybersecurity, and artificial intelligence (AI) continue to evolve, preserving that balance must be the priority.

  "For SMEs [small and medium enterprises] and startups, clear, coherent and consultative regulation is not just a policy preference," Koh said. "It shapes whether limited resources go into compliance or into hiring, product development and regional expansion."

  The study does not call for weakening regulatory safeguards. Its central finding is that design, proportionality, and predictability in regulation will determine whether Malaysia's startup ecosystem sees VC inflows contract by roughly US$198 million per year or expand by US$49.5 million per year over the next decade. DPA said it intends to support dialogue between policymakers and the startup ecosystem to ensure that regulation safeguards trust while enabling growth.
body_zh: |
  牛津經濟研究院（Oxford Economics）一份最新受託研究報告發出警告，指出若馬來西亞數位法規走向更為嚴格的限制性方向，該國每年創業投資（VC）金額將減少約1.86億美元。報告中的經濟模型推估，在管制趨嚴的情境下，2026年至2035年間創投資金將下滑26%，相當於每年減少約1.98億美元（馬幣7.92億令吉）。若法規走向更具開放性的賦能方向，同一期間每年則可望增加約4,950萬美元（馬幣1.98億令吉）。

  這份研究由「數位繁榮亞洲」（Digital Prosperity Asia，DPA）委託執行，探討數位法規如何影響馬來西亞新創生態系的合規成本、人才配置、創新投入與資金取得。研究發現，馬來西亞目前採取「整體賦能」的數位監管取向，在較高風險領域設置必要防護的同時，對整體數位經濟保持開放態度；但未來十年的法規設計走向，將對新創成長與投資環境帶來舉足輕重的影響。

  ## 合規成本已成企業固定營運支出

  對多數馬來西亞新創企業而言，法規合規已不再是一次性的調適成本，而是深植於日常營運的固定支出。受訪新創企業中，88%表示數位法規已對營運造成限制，其中23%更形容影響程度為「重大」或「嚴重」。

  合規支出已在各業者間形成可觀負擔：81%的新創企業表示數位法規推高了合規相關成本。逾八成新創企業將超過5%的營運預算用於合規，其中39%的企業更將逾15%的營運成本投入於因應法規要求。

  68%的新創企業表示已主動採取因應措施，包括建立新的合規流程、將工作負載移轉至符合規定的雲端服務供應商，以及委聘外部法律與顧問服務。

  ## 人才成本攀升，研發投入遭到排擠

  合規要求正在改變新創企業配置人力與資金的方式。87%的新創企業表示數位法規已影響其人力成本或人員管理，其中74%具體指出，由於對合規、資安及資料治理專業人才的需求持續增加，人力資本成本隨之上升。

  財務資源同樣正從產品開發移轉至合規用途。67%的新創企業表示資金正從研發（R&D）移轉至合規，這一趨勢獲得64%受訪創投業者及育成機構的印證。57%的新創企業表示產品開發因此延誤、上市時程拉長，而59%的創投業者則表示觀察到投資組合中的創新動能出現放緩跡象。

  ## 法規不確定性壓制募資動能

  數位法規正在影響投資人層面的資金配置決策。63%的新創企業表示數位法規加劇市場不確定性，使募資更加困難；73%的創投業者則表示法規提高了投資報酬的不確定性。

  對法規趨嚴的預期同樣壓抑了投資意願。根據這份研究，在管制趨嚴的情境下，預期投資金額將增加的新創企業比例從47%驟降至27%。

  ## 經濟模型推算結果

  牛津經濟研究院針對2026年至2035年間建立了三種法規情境模型。在管制趨嚴的情境下，創投資金預計下滑26%，相當於每年減少約1.98億美元（馬幣7.92億令吉）；在賦能開放的情境下，則預計增加6%，每年可額外帶來約4,950萬美元（馬幣1.98億令吉）。

  牛津經濟研究院經濟顧問業務執行董事亨利·沃辛頓（Henry Worthington）表示：「這份研究揭示了法規如何影響馬來西亞新創生態系各個層面的決策。新創企業在因應各類數位法規的合規要求時，承受著即時的壓力；滿足這些要求往往需要專業人才，並對營運模式進行調整，進而排擠創新與成長所需的資源。這並不意味著應削弱既有的保護機制，而是說明法規的設計方式、比例原則與可預測性，將是維繫馬來西亞新創動能的關鍵所在。」

  ## 政策意涵

  DPA的許良威（Koh Liang Wei）表示，馬來西亞相對開放的監管取向是新創生態系的重要資產，但隨著資料、資安及人工智慧（AI）相關法規持續演進，維護這一平衡必須列為首要之務。

  許良威說：「對中小企業（SME）與新創企業而言，清晰、一致且具諮詢性質的法規，絕不僅是政策偏好的問題。它直接決定有限資源究竟流向合規支出，還是流向人員招募、產品開發與區域擴張。」

  這份研究並未呼籲削弱監管保護機制。其核心結論在於：法規的設計方式、比例原則與可預測性，將決定馬來西亞新創生態系在未來十年間，究竟是每年創投資金縮水約1.98億美元，還是年增4,950萬美元。DPA表示，將致力推動政策制定者與新創生態系之間的對話，確保法規在保障信任的同時，也能持續促進成長。
