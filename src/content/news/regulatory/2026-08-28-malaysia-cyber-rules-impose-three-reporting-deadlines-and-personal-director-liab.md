body_en: |
  Malaysian companies face overlapping cyber-incident reporting deadlines of one hour, six hours, and 72 hours running concurrently to three different regulators after a single breach event, while four separate laws each expose board directors to personal criminal or civil liability, according to analysis published by Digital News Asia.

  The analysis, written by a practitioner with 25 years of incident response experience, maps how a breach event that may involve personal data at a financial institution subject to the Cyber Security Act 2024 simultaneously triggers obligations across the central bank, the national cybersecurity agency, and the data protection regulator — none of which waits for forensic confirmation.

  ## Three regulators, three clocks

  The shortest deadline belongs to Bank Negara Malaysia, the central bank. Under its Risk Management in Technology (RMiT) framework — a set of requirements designed to strengthen technology risk management at financial institutions — regulated entities must notify the bank of a significant incident within one hour.

  The Cyber Security Act 2024, which came into force on 26 August 2024 and marks its second year this week, sets a six-hour window for entities designated as National Critical Information Infrastructure (NCII). NCII designation covers 11 sectors: banking and finance, healthcare, energy, water, transport, agriculture, defence, government, and information, communication and digital. Section 23 of the Act engages where an incident has occurred or might have — confirmation of a breach is not required to start the clock.

  The amended Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) gives companies 72 hours to notify the Personal Data Protection Commissioner where personal data is involved. Affected individuals must be notified within seven days. A supplemental report goes to the National Cyber Security Agency (NACSA) at day 14. Late notifications require a written explanation with supporting evidence, and that explanation enters the regulatory record.

  ## Personal liability under four laws

  Reporting deadlines are not the full extent of director exposure. Four legal instruments each carry provisions that can name directors personally.

  Section 58 of the Cyber Security Act 2024 allows officers to be charged as though they personally committed an offence. The available defence requires showing no knowledge of or consent to the offence, and that reasonable precautions were taken in advance.

  The amended PDPA's Section 133 reaches individual decision-makers, not the organisation alone. The analysis notes that appointing the mandatory Data Protection Officer does not extinguish that personal liability; what matters is a registered officer, a maintained breach register, and a notification procedure that has been tested rather than filed.

  Sector regulator overlays add a third layer. Bank Negara's RMiT is described as the sharpest version, with the Securities Commission and the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) running their own equivalents. These carry legal force against directors and officers directly.

  The Companies Act 2016 provides a floor obligation that no delegation can absorb: reasonable care, skill, and diligence applied to any foreseeable risk. The analysis argues that after two years of the Cyber Security Act and frequent breach disclosures, cyber risk is no longer reasonably unforeseeable. Each of the four defences rests on documentation created before an incident; where records are silent, no defence exists.

  ## Board AI literacy low in the most closely supervised sector

  Between December 2025 and March 2026, Accenture Malaysia and FIDE Forum surveyed approximately 100 board directors at Malaysian banks, insurers, and takaful operators on AI readiness. Roughly one-third responded — a response rate the authors themselves identified as a finding.

  Of those who replied, 78% rated their understanding of AI as merely aware. Nine per cent described themselves as fluent. No respondents claimed expertise.

  The survey found that only 26% of financial institution boards regularly discuss responsible AI, and 4% have metrics in place to track AI governance progress. Some 43% of financial institutions manage AI risk using controls designed for other risk types — controls that assume systems behave predictably, which the analysis notes AI systems often do not.

  AI development among Malaysian financial institutions is heavily internal: 65% build their AI technologies in-house, compared with 9% across the Asia-Pacific region and 6% globally. The analysis notes that infrastructure flaws in internally built systems propagate to all applications constructed on top of them.

  Despite low board literacy, the financial-sector data indicates large-scale AI adoption could increase the combined pre-tax profit of Malaysia's six largest banks by approximately US$1.8 billion (RM7.1 billion) over three years.

  ## Obligations travel through supply chains

  NCII designation of a customer organisation brings audit clauses and compliance requirements to suppliers and logistics providers through contract, regardless of whether those downstream companies are themselves designated. The analysis cites a logistics firm serving a port as an example of an entity that inherits NCII-standard obligations on the day a contract is signed.

  The same dynamic is expected to apply to the AI governance framework under development. In March 2026, the MY-AI Standards were published, making more than 80 international AI standards publicly available. In July 2026, the National AI Office completed a public consultation on Malaysia's first AI Governance Bill. The draft Bill distinguishes between entities that create AI systems and those that deploy them, with some companies qualifying as both. The analysis notes that the assumption that AI liability sits solely with the vendor is becoming less tenable as the regulatory framework develops.

  Malaysia's first AI Governance Bill is expected to be finalised by the end of 2026.
body_zh: |
  馬來西亞企業一旦發生資安事件，必須在同一起事件發生後，同時應對一小時、六小時及七十二小時三個並行的通報期限，分別向三個不同的主管機關呈報；此外，尚有四部法律各自賦予相關機關追究董事個人刑事或民事責任的權力。上述分析由《數位新聞亞洲》（Digital News Asia）刊載。

  執筆者擁有二十五年資安事件處理經驗，其分析揭示了一旦事發機構為受《2024年網路安全法》（Cyber Security Act 2024）規範的金融機構，且事件涉及個人資料，單一起資安事件將如何同步觸發馬來西亞國家銀行、國家網路安全局（NACSA）以及個人資料保護主管機關的通報義務——三個機關無一會等待鑑識調查完成才開始計時。

  ## 三個主管機關、三條時鐘

  最短的通報期限屬於馬來西亞國家銀行（Bank Negara Malaysia）。在其《科技風險管理》（Risk Management in Technology，RMiT）框架下——這是一套旨在強化金融機構科技風險管理的規範要求——受監管機構必須在重大事件發生後一小時內通知國家銀行。

  《2024年網路安全法》於2024年8月26日正式生效，本週適逢施行屆滿兩週年。對於被列為「國家關鍵資訊基礎設施」（NCII）的實體，該法設有六小時的通報窗口。NCII涵蓋十一個領域：銀行及金融、醫療衛生、能源、水資源、交通運輸、農業、國防、政府機關，以及資訊、通訊與數位產業。該法第二十三條規定，只要事件已發生或可能已發生，時鐘即告啟動，無需等待外洩事實獲得正式確認。

  修訂後的《個人資料保護法》（Personal Data Protection Act，PDPA）規定，若事件涉及個人資料，企業須在七十二小時內通知個人資料保護專員。受影響的當事人須在七天內獲得通知。至第十四天，企業需向國家網路安全局（NACSA）提交補充報告。若逾期通報，企業必須提交書面說明及佐證文件，且該說明將進入主管機關的正式紀錄存查。

  ## 四部法律下的個人責任

  通報期限並非董事面臨的全部法律風險。四部法律各自設有條款，可點名追究董事個人責任。

  《2024年網路安全法》第五十八條允許主管機關對相關主管人員提出指控，視同其本人親自觸犯該罪行。若要援引免責抗辯，須證明當事人對該違法行為毫不知情且未予同意，且事前已採取合理的預防措施。

  修訂後《個人資料保護法》第一百三十三條的責任對象，不僅限於企業本身，更直接指向個別決策者。分析指出，指定法定的個人資料保護主任（Data Protection Officer，DPO）並不能消除董事的個人責任；真正重要的，是須有登記在冊的主管人員、持續維護的外洩事件紀錄，以及一套曾實際演練而非僅供存檔的通報程序。

  行業主管機關的疊加規範構成第三層約束。馬來西亞國家銀行的RMiT被描述為其中最嚴格的版本，證券委員會（Securities Commission）及馬來西亞通訊及多媒體委員會（MCMC）各自也有類似規範，且對董事及高層主管具有直接法律效力。

  《2016年公司法》設立了一條任何授權行為都無法免除的基本義務：針對任何可預見風險，均應盡合理的注意、技能與勤勉義務。分析認為，在《2024年網路安全法》施行兩年、資安外洩事件頻繁曝光的今天，網路風險已不再是合理可謂「無從預見」的事項。上述四項抗辯均仰賴於事件發生前所建立的文件紀錄；若紀錄付之闕如，則無任何抗辯空間可言。

  ## 監管最嚴格的行業，董事對AI的認識卻最薄弱

  2025年12月至2026年3月間，埃森哲馬來西亞（Accenture Malaysia）與FIDE Forum針對約一百名任職於馬來西亞銀行、保險業者及伊斯蘭互助保險（takaful）業者的董事，進行了一項AI準備度調查。約三分之一的受邀者回覆——調查報告的作者本身即將此回覆率視為一項值得關注的調查發現。

  在回覆的董事中，78%表示自己對AI的理解程度僅止於「有所認知」。9%自評為「熟練」。沒有任何受訪者自稱具備專業水準。

  調查發現，僅有26%的金融機構董事會定期討論負責任的AI議題，4%已建立指標以追蹤AI治理進展。約43%的金融機構以針對其他風險類型所設計的管控措施來管理AI風險——這類管控措施預設系統行為具有可預測性，但分析指出，AI系統往往並不如此。

  馬來西亞金融機構的AI開發高度仰賴內部自建：65%的機構自行研發AI技術，相較之下，亞太地區此一比例為9%，全球則為6%。分析指出，內部自建系統若存在基礎架構缺陷，這些缺陷將擴散至所有建構於其上的應用程式。

  儘管董事會對AI的認知程度偏低，金融業的數據顯示，大規模導入AI可為馬來西亞六大銀行在三年內合計增加約18億美元（約71億令吉）的稅前利潤。

  ## 合規義務沿供應鏈延伸

  若某家企業的客戶被列為NCII，相關的稽核條款與合規要求便會透過合約傳遞給其供應商及物流服務商，無論這些下游企業本身是否具有NCII資格。分析以一家為港口提供服務的物流公司為例，指出這類企業在簽署合約的當天，便繼承了相當於NCII標準的合規義務。

  同樣的連帶效應預計也將適用於正在制定中的AI治理框架。2026年3月，馬來西亞發布《MY-AI標準》（MY-AI Standards），公開提供逾八十項國際AI標準。2026年7月，馬來西亞國家AI辦公室（National AI Office）完成了馬來西亞首部《AI治理法案》（AI Governance Bill）草案的公眾諮詢程序。草案對AI系統的開發者與部署者加以區分，部分企業同時兼具兩種身份。分析指出，「AI責任完全由供應商承擔」的假設，隨著監管框架的逐步完善，正日益難以成立。

  馬來西亞首部《AI治理法案》預計於2026年底完成定案。
