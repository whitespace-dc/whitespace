  ## Permanent bans and new mandates mark a shift in regulatory approach

  State and local governments across the United States are moving beyond temporary development freezes toward permanent restrictions and financial mandates on data centers, as regulators and utilities face mounting pressure over electricity costs and grid capacity. The actions, which include the nation's first voter-approved citywide ban and a new state-level cost-allocation framework, signal a structural change in how communities are responding to large-scale power demand growth tied to AI infrastructure.

  Monterey Park, California voters recently passed a permanent citywide ban on data center construction, making it the first voter-mandated permanent block on the industry in the country. The referendum was driven by local opposition to rate hikes and resource consumption, according to Data Center Knowledge.

  In New Jersey, state officials unveiled a framework that would require mega-scale data centers — those among the largest category of power consumers — to source their own clean energy generation and directly fund the electrical grid upgrades their facilities require, shielding residents from those infrastructure costs.

  Maine lawmakers advanced legislation earlier this year to temporarily halt new data center projects above 20 megawatts (MW) — enough capacity to power tens of thousands of homes — to evaluate impacts on grid reliability and electricity costs. That proposal was subsequently vetoed, but similar restrictions, reviews, or local approval measures are under consideration in other states, Data Center Knowledge reported.

  ## The supply-demand gap driving the backlash

  The regulatory escalation reflects a genuine capacity problem. Electricity demand in the United States is projected to grow by 15 to 20 percent over the next decade, driven by AI infrastructure, manufacturing growth, and broader electrification of transportation and heating, according to Data Center Knowledge. Generation and transmission are not keeping pace.

  Transmission lines can take years to approve and build. New generation additions are facing delays caused by supply chain constraints, permitting challenges, and interconnection bottlenecks — the queue of projects waiting to connect to the grid. The result is a system already under pressure absorbing successive waves of large, concentrated load.

  ## The flexibility argument

  Policy discussions have largely treated data centers as fixed loads — facilities that draw a stable, predictable amount of power regardless of conditions on the grid. That assumption is outdated, Data Center Knowledge argued, citing the operational capabilities of modern facilities.

  Data center operators can increasingly coordinate computing workloads, participate in demand response programs (agreements to reduce consumption during periods of grid stress in exchange for financial incentives), use onsite generation, and adjust portions of demand in response to grid signals without disrupting critical operations. Not every workload can be curtailed, but the characterization of all data center activity as inflexible does not reflect current capabilities, according to the publication.

  The regulatory backlash appears to be accelerating industry movement toward that flexibility. An OBM survey cited by Data Center Knowledge found that 64 percent of energy professionals say data center growth is already speeding up flexible load management initiatives that had previously been part of longer-term planning efforts.

  ## Why moratoriums fall short

  Temporary development pauses and outright bans ease political pressure but do not address the underlying grid management problem, according to Data Center Knowledge. Restricting new load does not modernize transmission infrastructure and does not improve coordination between utilities and large energy users. In many cases, it delays the reckoning rather than resolving it.

  A more effective path would require policymakers to create frameworks that encourage flexible load management, earlier coordination between developers and utilities, and greater transparency about how large facilities will operate during periods of grid stress, the publication argued. For developers, energy strategy would need to function as a core operational requirement rather than a consideration addressed after site selection.

  The industry also needs to move beyond older curtailment models that rely on blunt, all-or-nothing demand reductions. Grid conditions change too quickly for manual intervention alone to be effective, according to Data Center Knowledge, which said modern load management depends on automation, real-time visibility, and systems capable of continuously adjusting operations as conditions evolve.

  ## What comes next

  The localized bans and mandates are, in aggregate, a signal about what the next phase of data center development will require. Projects that can demonstrate operational flexibility may face fewer regulatory and community barriers than those built around fixed demand profiles, Data Center Knowledge reported. The 64 percent of energy professionals who say flexible load management is already accelerating suggests that structural shift is underway, regardless of whether individual moratoriums or bans hold.

  ## 永久禁令與新授權標誌監管方式的轉變

  美國各州與地方政府正逐步超越臨時開發凍結措施，轉而對資料中心推動永久性限制與財務授權，因為監管機構和電力公司在電費成本與電網容量方面承受著日益沉重的壓力。相關行動包括全美首個經選民公投通過的全市永久禁令，以及新的州級成本分攤框架，顯示社區應對人工智慧基礎設施所帶動之大規模用電需求成長的方式，已出現結構性轉變。

  加州蒙特雷公園市選民近日通過全市永久禁止資料中心興建的公投提案，成為全美首個由選民公投授權、永久封鎖該產業進駐的城市。據《Data Center Knowledge》報導，此次公投源於當地居民對電費上漲及資源消耗的強烈反對。

  紐澤西州官員則公布了一套新框架，要求超大規模資料中心——即用電量屬最大類別的設施——自行取得潔淨能源發電，並直接出資承擔其設施所需的電網升級費用，以保護一般居民免於負擔這些基礎設施成本。

  緬因州立法機關今年稍早推動立法，擬暫時禁止裝機容量超過20百萬瓦（MW）——足以供應數萬戶家庭用電——的新資料中心計畫，以評估對電網可靠性與電費的衝擊。該提案隨後遭到否決，但據《Data Center Knowledge》報導，其他州仍在研議類似的限制措施、審查機制或地方審批規定。

  ## 供需缺口引發強烈反彈

  監管層面的升級，反映了真實存在的容量問題。據《Data Center Knowledge》報導，受人工智慧基礎設施、製造業成長，以及交通運輸與暖氣的全面電氣化帶動，美國電力需求預計將在未來十年內增長15%至20%，但發電與輸電的建設速度跟不上需求的腳步。

  輸電線路的審批與建設往往需要數年時間。新增發電設施則面臨供應鏈受限、許可審批困難，以及電網互聯瓶頸——即等待接入電網之計畫大量積壓排隊——所造成的延誤。其結果是，一個已處於壓力之下的電力系統，必須持續吸收一波又一波大規模且高度集中的新增負載。

  ## 彈性負載的論點

  政策討論長期以來將資料中心視為固定負載——無論電網狀況如何，均會穩定且可預測地消耗固定電量的設施。《Data Center Knowledge》援引現代設施的實際運作能力指出，這一假設早已過時。

  資料中心業者越來越能夠統籌協調運算工作負載、參與需求回應計畫（即同意在電網緊張時期減少用電、以換取財務誘因的協議）、使用現場自備發電設備，並根據電網訊號調整部分需求，且不影響關鍵業務運作。並非所有工作負載都能被削減，但將所有資料中心活動一概視為不可調度，並不符合現有的實際能力，該媒體如此指出。

  法規層面的強烈反彈，似乎正在加速業界朝向彈性方向邁進。《Data Center Knowledge》援引OBM的一項調查顯示，64%的能源業專業人士表示，資料中心的成長已在加速推動彈性負載管理措施，而這些措施此前原本屬於較長期的規劃工作。

  ## 為何暫停令治標不治本

  據《Data Center Knowledge》分析，臨時性的開發暫停與全面禁令或許能緩解政治壓力，卻無法解決電網管理的根本問題。限制新增負載並不能使輸電基礎設施現代化，也無助於改善電力公司與大型用電戶之間的協調。在許多情況下，這只是推遲了問題的爆發，而非真正解決問題。

  該媒體認為，更有效的路徑需要政策制定者建立框架，鼓勵彈性負載管理、促進開發商與電力公司提早協調，並提升大型設施在電網緊張期間運作方式的透明度。對開發商而言，能源策略必須成為核心營運要素，而非選址完成後才列入考量的附帶事項。

  業界也需要跳脫依賴粗放式、全有全無需求削減的舊有調度模型。《Data Center Knowledge》指出，電網狀況變化太快，單靠人工介入已不足以有效應對；現代負載管理有賴於自動化、即時可視性，以及能夠隨狀況持續調整運作的系統。

  ## 未來展望

  綜觀各地陸續出現的禁令與授權措施，整體而言傳遞出一個訊號，揭示了資料中心開發進入下一階段所必須具備的條件。據《Data Center Knowledge》報導，能夠展現運營彈性的計畫，在監管與社區層面所遭遇的阻力，或將少於那些以固定需求模式建置的設施。64%的能源業專業人士表示彈性負載管理已在加速推進，顯示這一結構性轉變正在發生，無論個別的暫停令或禁令最終能否持續下去。
