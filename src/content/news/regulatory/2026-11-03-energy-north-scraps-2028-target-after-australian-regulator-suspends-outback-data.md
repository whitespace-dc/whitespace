body_en: |
  The developer of one of Australia's largest planned data centers has scrapped its target start date after the Northern Territory's environment regulator suspended its own review process, dealing a setback to the territory's effort to attract AI-driven data center investment.

  Energy North, a startup developing a 1-gigawatt gas and solar-powered data center campus on a cattle ranch 683 kilometers (424 miles) from Darwin, the nearest city, had filed a "first power" target date of June 28, 2028, with regulators four months ago. The company told Reuters it is no longer working to that date.

  "The June 2028 date in our referral was an indicative schedule at the time of lodgement, and we're no longer working to it," vice president of project development Daniel Hunter said in an email.

  ## What the regulator did

  The Northern Territory's Environment Protection Agency (EPA) triggered the delay in an October 7 ruling in which it suspended the standard seven-week window it has to set environmental assessment terms — the conditions a developer must address before a project can proceed. The EPA said it would instead seek advice from the Australian federal government, which it said is "developing policy expectations for data centres and AI infrastructure developers." The agency gave no new timeframe for completing that consultation.

  In the same ruling, the EPA flagged 10 potential adverse impacts from the project, ranging from strain on water systems to disruption of Aboriginal sacred sites. It said it had a "low level of confidence in the effectiveness of proposed measures to manage potential significant impact."

  Hunter said the company was "unfazed" and would confirm a revised timeline once the EPA issues the terms for a formal environmental impact statement — a document required to identify and address potential adverse effects. He declined to identify the project's finance backers.

  ## Scale of the proposed project

  Project Ares, as Energy North calls the development, would span 191 square kilometers (74 square miles) and include a gas turbine system, a solar farm, an airstrip, and accommodation for 4,300 construction workers. At 1 gigawatt of capacity — enough to power roughly 750,000 average homes — it is four times the size of a data center expansion in Melbourne that developer NextDC delayed this month over energy supply concerns, and 10 times the size of a project withdrawn by Goodman in Sydney after resident opposition, according to the Reuters report.

  ## Northern Territory's pitch to investors

  The Northern Territory has sought to position itself as a fast-track destination for data center capital by offering characteristics that constrained urban markets cannot: 260,000 residents spread across an area roughly twice the size of Texas, abundant solar irradiance described as comparable to the Sahara, and access to the newly opened Beetaloo Basin shale gas reserve for backup power generation.

  Northern Territory business minister Robyn Cahill has said her government is in talks with 12 data center-related projects. Cahill did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment on the Energy North delay.

  ## Feasibility questions across the territory

  Energy North is not alone in proposing large-scale remote data center infrastructure in the Northern Territory. Beetaloo Energy has proposed a data center near Darwin twice the size of Project Ares, at approximately 2 gigawatts. Based on Beetaloo Energy's own estimate of needing 200 terajoules of gas per gigawatt per day, that project would consume up to 400 terajoules of gas daily — ten times the volume currently flowing from the Beetaloo Basin's only producing project, Tamboran's Shenandoah South facility. Beetaloo Energy has said it aims to start producing a small volume of gas by year-end.

  Together, just those two projects would total 3 gigawatts of proposed data center capacity, approximately 10 times peak demand on the entire territory's electricity grid. Both companies have said their energy supply would ultimately combine renewables with their own gas-fired generation, bypassing the public grid entirely. Details of the other 10 territory projects have not been disclosed publicly.

  ## Regulatory and community barriers

  Adam Beck, an urban planner who has written environmental impact statements for infrastructure projects and advises governments, told Reuters that outdated planning frameworks were not designed for proposals of this type. "Our antiquated planning frameworks weren't designed for proposals like this," Beck said. "Tearing up timelines is kind of standard process, but I'm not going to discount the lack of social license. There is a certain heightened level of concern by the community, and that's playing out on real projects."

  Aboriginal land rights add another layer of complexity. A quarter of the Northern Territory's population is Indigenous. The Northern Land Council, an Aboriginal representative body, noted in a submission to an ongoing federal inquiry that Aboriginal consultation requires travel, interpreters, and multiple visits, and that expediting the process "does not produce prompt consent."

  The Northern Territory Cattlemen's Association, which represents landholders in the pastoral sector that shares the territory's land base with proposed data center sites, said it expected development to proceed with "effective regulation, properly enforced, that safeguards pastoral operations and the land, water and environment both industries depend on," according to CEO Romy Carey.

  Rolf Gerritsen, a former policy head in the territory's chief minister's office who now teaches at Charles Darwin University, said the territory government has the power to override stakeholder objections — except when a complaint escalates to federal or High Court proceedings. "If you've got increased opposition groups ... whatever the development proposal is, it does tend to tie it up," he said.

  ## What it means for Australia's AI infrastructure ambitions

  The Energy North delay illustrates that remote siting does not insulate large-scale AI infrastructure projects from regulatory friction. McKinsey has forecast $7 trillion in global investment in what it terms "AI factories" by 2030. Australia has sought a share of that capital, and the Northern Territory has specifically marketed its land and energy advantages as differentiators. The EPA's decision to pause and seek federal policy guidance suggests that the regulatory architecture for evaluating projects of this scale and type has not yet been established, leaving developers without a clear path to approval timelines.

  Energy North said it will set a new construction schedule after the EPA completes its environmental terms-setting process, for which no deadline has been given.
body_zh: |
  澳洲規模最大的計畫中資料中心之一的開發商，已放棄原訂開工日期——北領地環保機構宣告暫停審查程序，令這片土地爭取AI驅動資料中心投資的努力蒙上陰影。

  Energy North是一家新創公司，計畫在距最近城市達爾文683公里（424英里）的一座牧場上，興建一座1吉瓦的天然氣與太陽能混合供電資料中心園區。該公司四個月前向監管機構申報「首次供電」目標日期為2028年6月28日，但如今已向路透社表示不再以此日期為目標。

  「我們申報書中的2028年6月日期，是申請當時的預估時程，目前我們已不再以此為目標，」專案開發副總裁丹尼爾·亨特（Daniel Hunter）在一封電子郵件中表示。

  ## 監管機構的決定

  北領地環境保護署（EPA）在10月7日的一項裁定中，暫停了其須在七週內設定環境評估條件的標準期限，進而引發了上述延誤。環境評估條件是開發商在專案獲准推進前必須處理的各項要求。環保署表示，將轉而向澳洲聯邦政府尋求建議，並指出聯邦政府正「針對資料中心及AI基礎設施開發商制訂政策方向」。環保署未說明完成諮詢的新期限。

  在同一裁定中，環保署點名了該專案的10項潛在不利影響，涵蓋水資源系統的承壓，到擾動原住民聖地等。環保署表示，對「擬議措施管控潛在重大影響之成效，信心程度偏低」。

  亨特表示，公司對此「處之泰然」，待環保署發出正式環境影響說明書的審查條件後，將確認修訂後的時程。環境影響說明書是一份用於識別並處理潛在不利影響的必要文件。他拒絕透露專案的財務支持方。

  ## 計畫規模

  Energy North將此開發案命名為「阿瑞斯計畫」（Project Ares），預計涵蓋191平方公里（74平方英里），包含燃氣渦輪系統、太陽能農場、機場跑道，以及可容納4,300名建設工人的住宿設施。1吉瓦的容量足以供應約75萬戶一般家庭用電。據路透社報導，這是開發商NextDC本月因能源供應疑慮而延期的墨爾本資料中心擴建案規模的四倍，也是古德曼（Goodman）因居民反對而撤案的雪梨專案規模的十倍。

  ## 北領地的招商策略

  北領地積極將自身定位為資料中心投資的快速審批目的地，標榜其受限於市區的市場所無法提供的條件：約26萬名居民分布在面積約德克薩斯州兩倍的廣袤土地上，太陽能輻射量堪比撒哈拉沙漠，加上甫開放的畢達盧盆地（Beetaloo Basin）頁岩氣儲量可作為備用發電來源。

  北領地商業部長羅賓·卡希爾（Robyn Cahill）表示，其政府正與12個資料中心相關專案進行洽談。卡希爾未回應路透社就Energy North延期一事的置評請求。

  ## 整個領地的可行性疑問

  Energy North並非唯一在北領地提出大規模偏遠資料中心基礎設施計畫的業者。Beetaloo Energy提出了一座緊鄰達爾文、容量約達2吉瓦的資料中心計畫，規模約為阿瑞斯計畫的兩倍。根據Beetaloo Energy自身的估算，每吉瓦每日須消耗200兆焦耳天然氣，意味著該專案每日最多將耗用400兆焦耳天然氣——是目前畢達盧盆地唯一投產專案、坦博倫（Tamboran）公司謝南多厄南場（Shenandoah South）現有產量的十倍。Beetaloo Energy表示，目標是在年底前開始小規模產氣。

  僅此兩項專案合計，擬議資料中心容量即達3吉瓦，約為整個北領地電網峰值需求的十倍。兩家公司均表示，其能源供應最終將結合再生能源與自建燃氣發電，完全繞開公共電網。其餘10個北領地專案的細節目前尚未公開。

  ## 監管與社區障礙

  曾為基礎設施專案撰寫環境影響說明書、現任政府顧問的城市規劃師亞當·貝克（Adam Beck）向路透社表示，過時的規劃框架根本不是為此類提案而設計的。「我們陳舊的規劃框架，從來就不是為了這類提案而生的，」貝克說。「時程的撕毀某種程度上已是標準流程，但我不會低估社會認可缺失的影響。社區確實存在相當程度的高度顧慮，而這正在真實專案中逐一發酵。」

  原住民土地權益更添一層複雜性。北領地四分之一的人口為原住民。代表原住民的北地土地委員會（Northern Land Council）在一項持續進行的聯邦調查書面意見中指出，原住民諮詢工作需要安排出行交通、口譯員及多次訪問，倉促推進這一程序「並不會換來迅速的同意」。

  代表領地土地上牧業部門地主的北領地牧業協會（Northern Territory Cattlemen's Association）透過執行長蘿咪·凱瑞（Romy Carey）表示，期望開發能在「有效監管、切實執行，保障牧場營運及兩大產業共同依賴的土地、水資源與環境」的前提下推進。

  曾任北領地首席部長辦公室政策主任、現任查爾斯達爾文大學教師的羅夫·赫里森（Rolf Gerritsen）表示，領地政府有權凌駕利害關係人的反對意見——除非投訴升至聯邦法院或最高法院的層級。「一旦反對團體增加……無論是什麼開發提案，往往都會被纏住，」他說。

  ## 對澳洲AI基礎設施雄圖的意義

  Energy North的延期說明，偏遠選址並不能讓大型AI基礎設施專案免於監管阻力。麥肯錫（McKinsey）預測，到2030年全球將在其所稱的「AI工廠」上投入7兆美元。澳洲一直積極爭取這波資本的份額，北領地更以土地與能源優勢作為差異化賣點大力行銷。環保署決定暫停審查並向聯邦政府尋求政策指引，顯示針對此類規模與類型專案的評估監管架構尚未建立，讓開發商無從掌握明確的審批時程。

  Energy North表示，待環保署完成環境條件設定程序後，將訂定新的施工時程——而目前該程序尚未訂下任何截止期限。
