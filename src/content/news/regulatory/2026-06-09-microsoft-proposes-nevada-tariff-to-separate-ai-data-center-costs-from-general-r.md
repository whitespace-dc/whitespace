body_en: |
  Microsoft filed a proposed Ratepayer Protection Tariff with the Public Utilities Commission of Nevada (PUCN) in May, seeking to establish a state-level framework for allocating the grid infrastructure costs triggered by AI-driven data center load growth without increasing bills for residential and small-business customers.

  The proposal, described by the company as among the most detailed utility-rate filings to date focused on separating AI infrastructure costs from the general rate base, would require large-load customers — such as hyperscale data center operators — to pay directly for the grid assets built specifically to serve them.

  ## How the cost split would work

  Under the proposed structure, NV Energy, the state's dominant utility, would identify and track substations, generation capacity, and transmission facilities needed to serve each large-load customer on a customer-specific asset schedule. Those assets would be divided into two categories.

  The first is a Customer Contributed Share: the portion of infrastructure attributable specifically to the large-load customer, which that customer would fund either through an upfront capital contribution or ongoing facility payments. The second is a System Benefit Share: any portion of those same assets found to provide broader benefits to the overall grid, which could be considered for inclusion in the utility's rate base — subject to commission approval — and therefore spread across all ratepayers.

  Customer-funded assets could later be reclassified into the rate base if the commission determines system-wide benefit, according to the filing. A public accounting system would track all such assets from the planning stage through operation.

  To prevent customers from walking away from infrastructure built on their behalf, the proposal retains a final exit charge payable if a large-load customer departs before completing its contractual payments. The filing also requires customers to enter into an electric service agreement with a defined contract demand and a structured load ramp schedule.

  ## Bring Your Own Power and expedited approvals

  The proposal includes a "Bring Your Own Power" provision that would allow large-load customers to procure generation from third-party providers. Accredited capacity from those arrangements would be incorporated into NV Energy's planning assumptions, which Microsoft said could reduce the risk of overbuilding generation and improve transparency for the utility.

  An expedited review pathway is also proposed: projects fully funded upfront by developers, with no request for system-benefit cost recovery, could receive fast-tracked PUCN approval within 60 days.

  The framework departs from traditional "take-or-pay" commodity arrangements, under which utilities supply power at a fixed rate regardless of how much is consumed, by instead tying cost responsibility directly to the infrastructure a specific customer requires.

  ## Context and immediate stakes for Nevada

  The filing is framed as part of Microsoft's Community-First AI Infrastructure initiative and aligns with a federal ratepayer protection pledge the company signed in March, according to the utility's filing documents.

  Microsoft noted in the filing that it has already acquired property for a new data center within NV Energy's service territory. The company also suggested the tariff could help offset early costs associated with Nevada's Greenlink transmission project, a large-scale grid expansion already underway in the state.

  The Nevada proceeding is being conducted under PUCN Docket No. 20-08014, a docket opened in August 2020 following capacity constraints during a major heat wave. That docket has since served as a standing forum for resource adequacy, grid planning, and ratepayer-protection proposals.

  The commission is expected to move the matter through evaluation, scenario modeling, and a public comment period before deciding whether to adopt a Ratepayer Protection Tariff framework.

  ## Industry reaction

  Jordan Weiszhaar, senior manager for datacenter energy strategy at Microsoft, described the filing as a first step in converting the principle of large customers paying their own way into a practical, enforceable model, in a post on LinkedIn.

  Travis Kavulla, head of policy at Base Power, wrote on X that the tariff appears to mitigate cost-shifting concerns while achieving faster grid access for large customers — a combination that has been difficult to achieve under existing interconnection and rate structures.

  ## What it means for the industry

  If adopted, the Nevada framework could provide a replicable model for other states and utilities navigating the same tension: accommodating the accelerating electricity demand of AI infrastructure buildouts without socializing the cost of grid upgrades onto customers who derive no direct benefit. The proposal's combination of direct cost assignment, optional third-party power procurement, and an expedited approval track for self-funded projects represents a more structured approach than the line-extension rules most utilities currently apply to large industrial customers.

  The PUCN has not yet set a timeline for a final decision on the tariff proposal.

  ## 微軟向內華達州提案電費保護關稅　AI資料中心成本與一般費率基礎切割

  微軟五月向內華達州公用事業委員會（Public Utilities Commission of Nevada，PUCN）提交一份「用電費率保護關稅」提案，旨在建立一套州層級的框架，用以分配因AI驅動之資料中心負載成長所引發的電網基礎建設成本，同時不讓住宅用戶與中小企業的電費帳單因此增加。

  微軟將這份提案形容為迄今為止在電費費率申報方面最為詳盡的文件之一，核心目的在於將AI基礎建設費用與一般費率基礎切割。依據此提案，大型用電客戶——例如超大規模資料中心業者——必須直接支付專為服務其所建置的電網資產費用。

  ## 成本分攤的運作方式

  在這套提案架構下，內華達州主要電力公用事業公司NV Energy將針對每位大型用電客戶，逐一識別並追蹤為其服務所需的變電站、發電容量與輸電設施，並製成各客戶專屬的資產清單。這些資產將劃分為兩大類別。

  第一類為「客戶出資份額」（Customer Contributed Share）：屬於特定大型用電客戶專用的基礎建設部分，由該客戶以預付資本的方式或持續繳納設施費用來承擔。第二類為「系統效益份額」（System Benefit Share）：同一批資產中，被認定能為整體電網帶來更廣泛效益的部分，可在委員會核准的前提下，納入公用事業的費率基礎，進而攤分給所有用電戶。

  根據申報文件，若委員會日後認定某些由客戶出資的資產具有全系統效益，可重新將其歸類至費率基礎。公開的資產追蹤系統將從規劃階段起，全程記錄所有相關資產直至正式運轉。

  為防止客戶在電網基礎建設完工後棄約離去，提案保留了「最終退出費用」機制：若大型用電客戶在完成合約付款義務前提前離開，須繳納此項費用。申報文件也要求客戶與NV Energy簽訂供電服務協議，明確約定合約需量及分階段增載時程。

  ## 自備電源與快速審查機制

  提案納入「自備電源」（Bring Your Own Power）條款，允許大型用電客戶向第三方業者自行採購發電容量。這類安排取得認可的容量，將被納入NV Energy的電力規劃假設當中。微軟表示，此舉有助於降低過度建置發電設施的風險，並提升對公用事業而言的透明度。

  提案同時設計了快速審查途徑：若開發商願意全額預付資金，且不申請系統效益費用回收，相關案件可在六十天內獲得PUCN的快速核准。

  這套框架有別於傳統「照單全收」（take-or-pay）的商品供應安排——後者不論實際用量多寡，均以固定費率供電——轉而將費用責任直接連結至特定客戶所實際需要的基礎建設。

  ## 背景脈絡與內華達州的當前處境

  根據申報文件，此次提案是微軟「社區優先AI基礎建設」（Community-First AI Infrastructure）倡議的一環，並與微軟今年三月簽署的一項聯邦層級用電費率保護承諾相呼應。

  微軟在申報文件中指出，公司已在NV Energy服務範圍內取得新資料中心的用地。微軟也建議，這套關稅架構或許有助於抵銷內華達州「綠鏈」（Greenlink）輸電計畫的前期成本——這是一項目前已在進行中的大規模電網擴張工程。

  此次內華達州審查程序依PUCN第20-08014號案卷進行。該案卷開設於二○二○年八月，起因是一場重大熱浪期間暴露出電力容量不足的問題，此後便持續作為資源充裕度、電網規劃及費率保護提案的常設論壇。

  委員會預計將推動此案進入評估、情境模擬及公眾意見徵詢等程序，再決定是否採納「用電費率保護關稅」框架。

  ## 業界反應

  微軟資料中心能源策略資深經理喬丹．魏斯哈（Jordan Weiszhaar）在LinkedIn上表示，這份申報文件是將「大型客戶自付其費」原則轉化為實際可執行模型的第一步。

  Base Power政策主管特拉維斯．卡烏拉（Travis Kavulla）則在X平台上寫道，這份關稅提案似乎能同時緩解成本轉嫁的疑慮，又加快大型客戶取得電網接入的速度——這一兩全組合在現行的電網併網規定與費率結構下向來難以實現。

  ## 對產業的意義

  若此框架獲得採納，內華達州的做法或許能為其他州與電力公用事業公司提供可複製的範本，協助它們解決同樣的難題：在承接AI基礎建設快速增長的用電需求之餘，避免將電網升級的成本社會化，強加於對此毫無直接受益的一般用電戶身上。這份提案結合了直接費用歸屬、可選擇的第三方電力採購，以及針對自籌資金案件的快速核准機制，相較於目前多數電力公用事業沿用的「線路延伸規定」，提供了更為結構化的處理方式。

  目前PUCN尚未公布對此關稅提案作出最終裁決的時間表。
