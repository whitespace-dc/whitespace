body_en: |
  Japan's Fair Trade Commission (JFTC) has closed its investigation into Broadcom's bundled licensing of VMware software products, announcing it found insufficient facts to support a finding of violation under the country's antimonopoly law.

  The JFTC said it ended the review because "facts sufficient to determine a violation of the provisions of the law were not found." The commission had opened the investigation in 2024, examining whether Broadcom's post-acquisition restructuring of VMware's product lineup constituted illegal tying — bundled selling that forces customers to take products they do not want — or an abuse of superior bargaining position, both prohibited under Japan's Act on Prohibition of Private Monopolization and Maintenance of Fair Trade.

  ## What triggered the investigation

  Broadcom completed its acquisition of VMware in 2023. In the following year, it consolidated VMware's previously separate software lines — which had covered server virtualization, storage virtualization, and cloud infrastructure, among other categories — into bundled subscription packages. Cloud vendors that had licensed only specific VMware components were required to subscribe to the broader bundles, paying for software they had not previously used. The shift amounted to a substantial effective price increase for affected customers. Fujitsu was among the Japanese cloud vendors named as having faced this impact.

  ## How the JFTC reached its conclusion

  According to reporting by the Nikkei, the JFTC weighed the competitive disadvantages sustained by rivals and the relative bargaining power between Broadcom and its trading partners before arriving at its conclusion. The commission did not detail the specific factors that fell short of the threshold for a violation finding.

  ## Broadcom's commitment during the probe

  Although the investigation ended without a violation ruling, the JFTC disclosed that Broadcom made a voluntary offer during the review period. The company committed that, in cases where future changes to contract terms would cause significant disadvantage to cloud vendors, it would provide sufficient advance notice and would carry out sufficient and sincere negotiations before implementing such changes.

  ## Significance

  The outcome stops short of enforcement action against Broadcom but establishes a record of regulatory scrutiny in Japan. The voluntary commitment Broadcom provided — on notice periods and negotiation obligations — gives Japanese cloud vendors a stated expectation they can point to in future disputes. The JFTC stated it will continue to monitor competitive conditions in digital markets from the standpoint of promoting fair and free competition.
body_zh: |
  日本公正取引委員會（JFTC）宣布終止對博通（Broadcom）將VMware軟體產品捆綁授權一事的調查，指出未發現足以認定違反日本獨占禁止法的事實。

  JFTC表示，由於「未發現足以認定違反法律規定的充分事實」，決定終止審查。委員會於2024年展開此項調查，審查博通收購VMware後對產品陣容進行重組的行為，是否構成非法搭售——即強迫客戶購買其不需要之產品的捆綁銷售——或濫用優勢談判地位，上述兩種行為均受日本《禁止私的獨占及確保公正交易法》所禁止。

  ## 調查起因

  博通於2023年完成對VMware的收購。翌年，博通將VMware原本各自獨立的軟體產品線——涵蓋伺服器虛擬化、儲存虛擬化及雲端基礎架構等類別——整併為捆綁訂閱套餐。原先僅授權特定VMware元件的雲端業者，被要求訂閱更大範圍的套餐，須為過去從未使用的軟體付費。此一轉變對受影響客戶而言，實質上等同於大幅漲價。富士通（Fujitsu）是被點名受到此衝擊的日本雲端業者之一。

  ## JFTC如何得出結論

  根據《日本經濟新聞》的報導，JFTC在得出結論前，審酌了競爭對手所承受的競爭劣勢，以及博通與其交易夥伴之間的相對談判力。委員會並未詳細說明哪些具體因素未達認定違法的門檻。

  ## 調查期間博通的承諾

  儘管調查以未認定違法作結，JFTC仍披露博通在審查期間主動提出自願承諾。博通承諾，若未來合約條款的變更將對雲端業者造成重大不利，將提供充分的事前告知，並在實施前進行充分且誠意的協商。

  ## 意義

  此次結果雖未對博通採取任何執法行動，但確立了日本監管機關對此案進行審查的紀錄。博通所提出的自願承諾——涵蓋告知期限與協商義務——為日本雲端業者提供了一項明確的期待依據，可在未來發生爭議時加以援引。JFTC表示，將持續以促進公平自由競爭為出發點，監測數位市場的競爭狀況。
