body_en: |
  The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), which administers export licensing, issued an interim final rule on November 16 that extends advanced AI chip controls to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil and bars restricted Chinese entities from remotely accessing significant computing capacity held in overseas data centers. The rule took effect November 19.

  ## Scope of the new rules

  Companies importing advanced AI chips into the five newly covered countries now require US export licenses. BIS also established a threshold for overseas computing access: data centers that provide Chinese, Macanese, or otherwise restricted customers with more than 12 million units of total processing performance (TPP), a measure of computing throughput, must obtain prior US authorization, according to Commercial Times. That threshold is roughly equivalent to 760 Nvidia H100 chips or 330 B200 chips.

  The controls extend to indirect access arrangements, including cases where Chinese entities reach overseas computing capacity through subsidiaries or by contracting third parties to train artificial intelligence models on their behalf, Commercial Times reported.

  Unlike a proposed rule, an interim final rule is enforceable from its effective date. BIS is accepting public comments until January 19, 2027, and may revise the rule's provisions afterward. Existing data centers that provide significant computing capacity to restricted entities are subject to a 90-day transition period under specified conditions, Digital News Asia reported.

  The rule also adds oversight of advanced semiconductor manufacturing and packaging activities, meaning semiconductor companies that rely on overseas fabrication or packaging partners may face new licensing requirements even if they do not operate data centers.

  ## MegaSpeed investigation

  The rule's release coincided with the public disclosure of a US-Singapore investigation into MegaSpeed International Pte Ltd, a Singapore-registered company under investigation for suspected diversion of advanced Nvidia chips to China.

  MegaSpeed was originally incorporated in Singapore in 2015 as 7Road International, the overseas arm of a Chinese video game developer, Digital News Asia reported, citing the Associated Press. The company adopted the MegaSpeed name in March 2023 when its shares were transferred to Huang Le Limited, a British Virgin Islands company associated with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le. Singapore corporate records cited by The Business Times, as reported by Digital News Asia, show that Huang and another director resigned from MegaSpeed's board by November 10, 2023, when ownership passed to a Singapore-registered entity controlled by businessman Tan Yong Pong, also known as James Tan.

  MegaSpeed said in a statement in November 2026 that Huang left the company effective August 30, 2025, and no longer holds any position with it. The company did not clarify what role Huang held between her reported 2023 director resignation and her August 2025 departure.

  In a joint statement on November 16, the US Departments of Commerce and Justice confirmed that American authorities began investigating MegaSpeed in 2024 for possible export control violations. Investigators requested records from Nvidia in May 2025; Nvidia placed the company on its no-sell list the following month. The Justice Department later assumed responsibility for the criminal investigation. MegaSpeed has denied wrongdoing and says it complies with applicable export controls; the investigation remains open.

  ## Malaysia's regulatory response

  The MegaSpeed investigation extended to Malaysia through the company's local affiliate, Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd, which operates as a tenant at Malaysian data centers, bringing its own servers and AI chips into the country.

  In a November 13 statement, Malaysia's Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) said it is working with the Royal Malaysian Customs Department to determine whether chips imported by Speedmatrix were subsequently re-exported and whether any such transactions complied with Malaysian law. MITI said prior ministry visits to these facilities were routine monitoring and did not constitute regulatory clearance, and that it had never cleared MegaSpeed or Speedmatrix of potential wrongdoing.

  MITI noted that while importing AI chips into Malaysia does not currently require a permit, companies seeking to export, re-export, transship, or transit high-performance US AI chips from or through Malaysia have required approval under the country's Strategic Trade Act 2010 since July 2025. The ministry said it is cooperating with Singaporean and US authorities and is considering additional safeguards. "Malaysia welcomes investment in data centres and technology, but will not allow itself to be used to get around export controls," MITI said.

  ## Impact on semiconductor and equipment supply chains

  For Malaysian semiconductor design company SkyeChip Bhd, the immediate task is determining whether any part of its supply chain falls within the new licensing requirements. The Penang-based company disclosed in a Bursa Malaysia filing that its silicon product revenue of US$10.38 million (RM42.8 million) in the first half of its fiscal year ending March 31, 2027, representing 41.2% of group revenue, came entirely from customers in North America and Northeast Asia, excluding China, and that it does not operate data centers. Based on a preliminary assessment, SkyeChip said it does not expect the regulations to have a material impact on FY2027 revenue, though it is seeking advice from US export control lawyers and reviewing foundry and packaging supply chain implications.

  For Taiwan's supply chain, the exposure is broader. Server and equipment makers must now verify destination, end-user identity, and intended computing use at the order and procurement stage, adding compliance costs, Commercial Times reported. Power supply, cooling, and printed circuit board (PCB) manufacturers face indirect exposure through changes to rack-level shipment schedules: if customers change delivery locations or adjust equipment configurations, downstream procurement, inventory, and payment timelines could shift.

  Some banks are already incorporating export control clauses into data center loan negotiations, treating end-tenant eligibility as a credit condition, according to Commercial Times. If customers cannot obtain the required US licenses, procurement and equipment delivery timelines could be affected across the hardware stack.

  ## Capex commitments provide near-term support

  Analysts cited by Commercial Times said the near-term effect of the new controls is concentrated on hardware procurement timing rather than a broad weakening of AI demand. Amazon and Alphabet, the parent of Google, have both indicated they will increase capital expenditure in 2027, and Meta raised the lower bound of its 2026 investment guidance, providing continued support for global AI hardware demand.

  Analysts said a more significant supply chain impact would require US-based cloud providers to simultaneously cut capital spending, delay equipment acceptance, and slow supplier payments — a scenario that had not materialized as of the rule's publication date.

  BIS is accepting public comments on the interim final rule through January 19, 2027, after which the agency may revise its provisions.
body_zh: |
  美國商務部工業暨安全局（BIS）負責出口許可業務，於11月16日發布臨時最終規則，將先進AI晶片管制擴及馬來西亞、新加坡、泰國、印尼及巴西五國，同時禁止受限中國實體遠端存取境外資料中心的大規模算力。規則自11月19日起正式生效。

  ## 新規範疇

  向上述五國進口先進AI晶片的企業，現須取得美國出口許可。BIS亦就境外運算存取設定了門檻：依據《工商時報》報導，若資料中心向中國大陸、澳門或其他受限客戶提供的總處理效能（TPP）超過1,200萬單位——此為衡量運算吞吐量的指標——則必須事先取得美國授權。該門檻大致相當於760張輝達H100或330張B200晶片的算力。

  管制範圍亦涵蓋間接存取安排，包括中國實體透過子公司，或委託第三方代為訓練人工智慧模型，藉此取得境外算力的情況，《工商時報》報導。

  與擬議規則不同，臨時最終規則自生效日起即具有法律效力。BIS接受公眾意見至2027年1月19日，之後可能對規則內容進行修訂。依《Digital News Asia》報導，現行已向受限實體提供大量算力的資料中心，在符合特定條件的情況下，享有90天的過渡期。

  此規則亦新增對先進半導體製造及封裝活動的監管，意即仰賴境外晶圓廠或封裝合作夥伴的半導體企業，即使本身不經營資料中心，亦可能面臨新的許可要求。

  ## MegaSpeed調查案

  規則發布的同時，美國與新加坡聯合公開了一項調查——調查對象為新加坡註冊公司MegaSpeed International Pte Ltd，該公司涉嫌將先進輝達晶片轉售至中國。

  據《Digital News Asia》引述美聯社報導，MegaSpeed最初於2015年以「7Road International」之名在新加坡註冊成立，為一家中國網路遊戲開發商的海外子公司。2023年3月，該公司股份轉讓給與中國女商人Alice Huang（又名黃樂）相關的英屬維京群島公司Huang Le Limited，並更名為MegaSpeed。據《Digital News Asia》引述《商業時報》援引的新加坡企業登記資料，黃樂與另一名董事最遲於2023年11月10日辭去MegaSpeed董事職務，公司所有權隨後轉由新加坡籍商人Tan Yong Pong（又名James Tan）所控制的一家新加坡註冊實體持有。

  MegaSpeed於2026年11月發表聲明稱，黃樂自2025年8月30日起離開公司，現已不再擔任任何職務。惟公司並未說明，黃樂在其據報2023年辭去董事職務至2025年8月正式離職期間，究竟擔任何種職位。

  美國商務部與司法部於11月16日發表聯合聲明，確認美國當局已於2024年開始調查MegaSpeed是否違反出口管制規定。調查人員於2025年5月要求輝達提供相關記錄；輝達隨即於次月將該公司列入禁售名單。司法部其後接手負責刑事調查。MegaSpeed否認有任何違規行為，並表示遵守適用的出口管制規定；目前調查仍在進行中。

  ## 馬來西亞的監管因應

  MegaSpeed調查案透過其當地關聯企業Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd延伸至馬來西亞。Speedmatrix以租戶身份進駐馬來西亞資料中心，自行攜入伺服器及AI晶片。

  馬來西亞投資、貿易及工業部（MITI）於11月13日發表聲明，表示正與皇家馬來西亞關稅局合作，調查Speedmatrix進口的晶片是否遭再出口，以及相關交易是否符合馬來西亞法律。MITI表示，部門此前對相關設施進行的實地訪查屬例行監察，不代表法規層面的許可，亦從未對MegaSpeed或Speedmatrix的潛在違規行為予以背書。

  MITI指出，目前進口AI晶片至馬來西亞毋須許可，但自2025年7月起，企業若欲從馬來西亞出口、再出口、轉運或過境高效能美製AI晶片，依據《2010年戰略貿易法》須事先獲得批准。MITI表示正與新加坡及美國當局合作，並考慮採取額外防護措施。「馬來西亞歡迎資料中心與科技領域的投資，但絕不允許本國成為規避出口管制的跳板，」MITI表示。

  ## 對半導體及設備供應鏈的衝擊

  對馬來西亞半導體設計公司SkyeChip Bhd而言，當務之急是確認其供應鏈是否有任何環節落入新許可要求的規範之內。這家總部位於檳城的公司在馬來西亞交易所（Bursa Malaysia）的申報文件中揭露，在截至2027年3月31日的本財年上半年，矽產品營收為1,038萬美元（約4,280萬令吉），占集團總營收的41.2%，客戶全數來自北美及東北亞（不含中國大陸），且公司本身不經營資料中心。SkyeChip表示，根據初步評估，預計此次法規不會對2027財年營收造成重大影響，但仍在徵詢美國出口管制律師的意見，並審視晶圓廠及封裝供應鏈的相關影響。

  對台灣供應鏈而言，潛在衝擊層面更廣。《工商時報》報導，伺服器及設備製造商現須在下單及採購階段核實目的地、終端用戶身份及預定運算用途，將增加合規成本。電源供應器、散熱及印刷電路板（PCB）廠商則面臨間接衝擊——若客戶更改交貨地點或調整設備配置，機架層級的出貨排程可能連帶影響下游採購、庫存及付款時程。

  部分銀行已在資料中心貸款談判中納入出口管制條款，將終端租戶的資格視為授信條件，《工商時報》報導。若客戶無法取得所需的美國許可，整個硬體堆疊的採購及設備交付時程均可能受到影響。

  ## 資本支出承諾提供近期支撐

  《工商時報》引述分析師指出，新管制措施的短期影響集中於硬體採購時程，而非AI需求的全面走弱。亞馬遜與Google母公司Alphabet均表示將於2027年增加資本支出，Meta亦上調了2026年投資指引的下限，為全球AI硬體需求持續提供支撐。

  分析師表示，若要形成更顯著的供應鏈衝擊，須同時出現美國雲端業者大幅削減資本支出、延後設備驗收，以及放緩對供應商付款等情況——截至規則發布之日，上述情境尚未成真。

  BIS接受對此臨時最終規則的公眾意見至2027年1月19日，之後機構可能對相關規定進行修訂。
