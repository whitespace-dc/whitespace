body_en: |
  Vietnam's Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT) has proposed reclassifying data centers under the country's manufacturing electricity tariff, moving them out of the business-rate category that currently applies to facilities serving outside customers.

  The ministry said it is seeking public comment on draft amendments to Circular 60/2025/TT-BCT, prepared under a shortened procedure to address difficulties in applying electricity prices to data centers. The draft would add data center operations to the groups covered by manufacturing rates under Article 7 of the circular, and would amend Article 11 to remove data centers from the category of centralized data processing, storage, and management facilities billed at business rates.

  ## The billing problem the proposal addresses

  Under the rules currently in force, the tariff a data center pays depends on who it serves. Facilities that provide services to other organizations or individuals pay the business electricity price. Those that serve only the operator's own business are billed according to the purpose stated in their existing power purchase agreement. Where a single facility serves both internal operations and external customers, the buyer and seller must agree on how to split consumption between the two rate categories.

  That split has proven difficult to negotiate in practice. Data center power infrastructure serves internal and external workloads simultaneously, and some operators and utilities have been unable to reach agreement on the allocation. The resulting disputes have put continuity of power supply at risk, according to the MOIT.

  The Ministry of Science and Technology raised the issue in April, proposing a single tariff for all data center services regardless of whether they serve the operator or outside customers. It argued that a data center's technical systems directly create and maintain its capacity to deliver services, making them functionally equivalent to manufacturing. Under that logic, offices and customer service points within a data center campus would remain on the business tariff while operational infrastructure would move to the manufacturing rate.

  ## Regulatory rationale

  The MOIT said the proposed change would establish a uniform legal basis for pricing and prevent power utilities and enterprises from applying the rules inconsistently. It also cited stable and continuous power supply to data centers as a goal of the amendment.

  ## Market context

  Vietnam's data center market is projected to grow from 310 megawatts of capacity in 2025 to 580 megawatts by 2031, according to market research firm Mordor Intelligence. The firm attributed the expected expansion to enterprise spending on digital transformation, data localization requirements, and the removal of foreign-ownership caps, with construction activity concentrated in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City.

  The draft amendments remain open for comment before any final rule takes effect.
body_zh: |
  越南工業貿易部（MOIT）提案將資料中心重新歸類於製造業電費率，使其脫離現行對外服務設施所適用的商業電費類別。

  工業貿易部表示，正就第60/2025/TT-BCT號通函修正草案公開徵詢各界意見。此次修正採簡化程序進行，旨在解決資料中心電費計算的實務困難。草案擬於通函第7條製造業費率所涵蓋的類別中，新增資料中心業務；並修訂第11條，將資料中心從按商業費率計費的集中式資料處理、儲存及管理設施類別中移除。

  ## 此提案所欲解決的電費問題

  依現行規定，資料中心所適用的電費率取決於其服務對象。凡向外部組織或個人提供服務的設施，須繳納商業電費；僅服務營運商自身業務者，則依其現有購電協議所載明的用途計費。若同一設施同時服務內部業務與外部客戶，則買賣雙方須協商如何將用電量分攤至兩種費率類別。

  然而，此一分攤機制在實務上窒礙難行。資料中心的電力基礎設施須同時承載內部與外部工作負載，部分業者與電力公司始終無法就用電分配比例達成共識，衍生爭議。工業貿易部指出，此類糾紛已對供電的持續穩定造成威脅。

  科學技術部於4月提出這項問題，建議不論資料中心服務的是業者自身還是外部客戶，一律適用單一費率。該部認為，資料中心的技術系統直接創造並維持其提供服務的能力，在功能上與製造業無異。依此邏輯，資料中心園區內的辦公室及客服區域仍維持商業費率，而營運基礎設施則改適用製造業費率。

  ## 法規層面的考量

  工業貿易部表示，此次提案修正將建立統一的電費定價法律基礎，避免電力公司與企業各自解讀、適用標準不一的情形。工業貿易部亦將確保資料中心穩定且不中斷的電力供應，列為本次修正的目標之一。

  ## 市場背景

  根據市場研究機構Mordor Intelligence的預測，越南資料中心市場裝置容量將從2025年的310百萬瓦（MW）成長至2031年的580百萬瓦。該機構將此一擴張趨勢歸因於企業數位轉型投資、資料在地化法規要求，以及外資持股上限的解除，建設活動主要集中於河內與胡志明市。

  上述修正草案目前仍在公開徵詢意見階段，尚未正式生效。
