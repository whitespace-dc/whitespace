  ## Singapore revises sustainable finance taxonomy to account for water stress at Asian data centres

  The Singapore Sustainable Finance Association (SSFA), backed by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), released the second edition of the Singapore-Asia Taxonomy for Sustainable Finance on 11 November 2026 at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, revising how data centres outside Singapore are assessed for green and transition financing. The key change allows operators in water-stressed locations to demonstrate compliance using a combined efficiency formula rather than independent thresholds for electricity and water consumption.

  The revision covers four sectors: energy, maritime, iron and steel, and data centres. The taxonomy, first introduced in December 2023, provides financial institutions with a classification framework — not a mandatory operating standard — for determining whether economic activities qualify as Green or are undergoing a credible transition toward lower environmental impact.

  ## The efficiency trade-off the revision addresses

  Data centre sustainability assessments under the taxonomy use two measures. Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) is the ratio of total facility electricity consumption to the energy used by IT equipment; a PUE of 1.0 would mean all electricity goes directly to computing, with none lost to cooling or other overhead. Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) measures water consumption in cubic metres per megawatt-hour of IT energy used.

  The tension between these two measures is structural: cooling systems that minimise water use — such as air-cooled or dry-cooled systems — typically consume more electricity than evaporative or water-cooled alternatives. Operators in water-scarce regions who choose water-conserving cooling face a penalty under frameworks that treat the two metrics independently.

  The SSFA said the revised criteria were designed to avoid disadvantaging facilities that prioritise water conservation.

  ## How the new thresholds work

  Under the revised taxonomy, data centres outside Singapore seeking Green classification must achieve an average annual PUE no greater than 1.40 minus 0.0545 multiplied by WUE, with WUE capped at 2.2 cubic metres per megawatt-hour. For existing facilities seeking Amber classification — indicating a credible transition rather than current best practice — the formula is PUE no greater than 1.45 minus 0.04545 multiplied by WUE, with WUE capped at 2.3.

  The formula means that a facility with a WUE of 0.2 can qualify as Green with a PUE of up to 1.389, while a facility using the maximum permitted water allocation of 2.2 must achieve a PUE of 1.280. The sliding scale gives operators using water-saving cooling systems more headroom on electricity efficiency while maintaining binding limits on both measures.

  For data centres located within Singapore, the criteria remain aligned with the Building and Construction Authority-IMDA Green Mark framework, which is not affected by the revision.

  ## Why water stress is a material issue across Asia

  According to the ASEAN Guide for Sustainable Data Centre Development, published in December 2025, 63% of Asian data centres are located in water-stressed areas. The statistic, cited by the SSFA in its taxonomy documentation, provides the regional backdrop for the revision: a financing framework that assessed water and electricity efficiency in isolation could systematically disadvantage the majority of Asian data centre markets.

  The taxonomy does not impose operating requirements on existing facilities; its effect is on how lenders and investors classify projects when structuring green bonds, green loans, or transition finance instruments.

  ## Financing guidance also expanded

  Alongside the revised taxonomy, the SSFA published an addendum to its Guidance for Leveraging the Singapore-Asia Taxonomy in Green and Transition Financing, originally released in July 2025. The November 2026 addendum expands guidance on activities that support green and transition projects, including manufacturing, distribution, installation, and professional services. It also clarifies how equity investments, alongside bonds and loans, may fall within the taxonomy's scope, subject to safeguards on the use of proceeds and demonstrated environmental benefits.

  Abigail Ng, chief sustainability officer at MAS, said the updated taxonomy would give financial institutions and companies greater clarity and confidence to finance green and transition activities in Asia. The SSFA did not quantify how much additional data centre investment the revision could generate, noting that financial impact will depend on how lenders apply the criteria and whether individual projects satisfy the relevant requirements.

  ## Singapore's domestic data centre policy context

  The taxonomy revision sits alongside a series of domestic regulatory and planning measures Singapore has introduced for data centres.

  Singapore's parliament passed the Digital Infrastructure Bill in October 2026, establishing a statutory framework for mandatory energy-efficiency standards. Under the legislation, operators of facilities with a critical IT load of at least 3 megawatts — roughly the scale at which a facility begins to draw significant grid power — will require licences and must meet PUE requirements. Detailed standards are to be set through regulations and codes of practice, with implementation expected to begin in 2027.

  During the parliamentary debate, senior minister of state for digital development and information Tan Kiat How said Singapore's data centres consumed approximately 6.5 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2024, equivalent to slightly more than 10% of national electricity consumption.

  Singapore's Green Data Centre Roadmap, launched by the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) in May 2024, sets an ambition for data centres to achieve a PUE of 1.3 or lower within ten years. Separately, national water agency PUB has introduced a WUE target of two cubic metres per megawatt-hour or lower for new and existing data centres; that target is not itself a mandatory limit under the Digital Infrastructure Bill, though the framework permits water-efficiency requirements to be introduced through future regulations.

  In August 2026, the Economic Development Board and IMDA provisionally allocated 200 megawatts of additional data centre capacity — 50 megawatts each — to Digital Realty, Equinix, Keppel Data Centres, and ST Telemedia Global Data Centres under the second Data Centre Call for Application. Each selected operator committed to sourcing more than 50% of its new capacity's energy requirements from green sources, exceeding the exercise's stated minimum.

  ## What the revision means for developers and financiers

  The practical significance of the taxonomy update is that regional data centre developers in water-stressed markets now have a clearer path to qualifying projects for green or transition financing without being required to minimise water consumption at the expense of electricity efficiency. Financial institutions using the taxonomy as a reference framework will be able to apply the combined PUE-WUE formula when underwriting or structuring transactions.

  The SSFA's next step, as indicated by the addendum, is to extend the guidance to a wider range of activities connected to green infrastructure, including supply chain and professional services roles that do not operate data centres directly but contribute to their development.

  ## 新加坡修訂永續金融分類標準　納入亞洲資料中心水資源壓力考量

  由新加坡金融管理局（MAS）支持的新加坡永續金融協會（SSFA），於2026年11月11日在土耳其安塔利亞舉行的COP31大會上，發布第二版《新加坡—亞洲永續金融分類標準》，修訂了對新加坡境外資料中心進行綠色及轉型融資評估的方式。此次修訂的核心變化，是允許位於水資源緊張地區的業者，透過綜合效率公式來證明合規，而非分別設定電力與用水消耗的獨立門檻。

  此次修訂涵蓋四個產業：能源、航運、鋼鐵，以及資料中心。該分類標準於2023年12月首次推出，為金融機構提供一套分類框架——而非強制性的營運規範——用以判斷特定經濟活動是否符合「綠色」認定，或是否正朝降低環境衝擊的方向進行可信賴的轉型。

  ## 此次修訂所解決的效率取捨問題

  在該分類標準下，資料中心的永續性評估採用兩項指標。電力使用效率（PUE）是設施總用電量與IT設備用電量的比值；若PUE為1.0，代表所有電力直接用於運算，無任何耗損於冷卻或其他輔助設備。用水使用效率（WUE）則衡量每百萬瓦時IT能耗所消耗的水量，單位為立方公尺每百萬瓦時（m³/MWh）。

  這兩項指標之間存在結構性矛盾：採用氣冷或乾冷等節水冷卻系統的設施，其用電量通常高於採用蒸發冷卻或水冷系統的設施。在水資源匱乏地區，選擇節水冷卻方式的業者，在獨立評估兩項指標的框架下將處於不利地位。

  SSFA表示，修訂後的標準旨在避免讓優先考量節水的設施遭受不公平的懲罰。

  ## 新門檻的運作方式

  依據修訂後的分類標準，新加坡境外尋求「綠色」認定的資料中心，年平均PUE須不超過1.40減去0.0545乘以WUE，且WUE上限為每百萬瓦時2.2立方公尺。對於尋求「琥珀色」認定的現有設施——即代表可信賴的轉型過程，而非當前最佳實踐——公式為PUE不超過1.45減去0.04545乘以WUE，WUE上限為每百萬瓦時2.3立方公尺。

  依此公式，WUE為0.2的設施，可在PUE最高達1.389的情況下取得「綠色」認定；而使用最高許可用水量（WUE為2.2）的設施，則須達到PUE 1.280。此滑動式標準為採用節水冷卻系統的業者提供了更大的用電效率空間，同時維持對兩項指標的約束上限。

  位於新加坡境內的資料中心，其標準仍與建設局（BCA）暨資通訊媒體發展局（IMDA）綠色標誌（Green Mark）框架保持一致，不受此次修訂影響。

  ## 為何水資源壓力在亞洲是重要議題

  根據2025年12月發布的《東協永續資料中心發展指南》，63%的亞洲資料中心位於水資源緊張地區。SSFA在其分類標準文件中引用了這項數據，作為此次修訂的區域背景：若融資框架獨立評估用水與用電效率，可能系統性地使亞洲大多數資料中心市場處於不利地位。

  該分類標準並不對現有設施施加營運要求；其影響在於，當貸款方與投資方在架構綠色債券、綠色貸款或轉型融資工具時，如何對項目進行分類。

  ## 融資指引亦同步擴充

  除修訂後的分類標準外，SSFA也發布了《善用新加坡—亞洲分類標準進行綠色與轉型融資指引》的附錄，原版指引於2025年7月發布。2026年11月的附錄擴充了對支持綠色及轉型項目相關活動的指引，涵蓋製造、配銷、安裝及專業服務等範疇。附錄同時釐清，在符合資金用途保障措施及可驗證環境效益的前提下，股權投資與債券、貸款一樣，均可納入分類標準的適用範圍。

  新加坡金融管理局首席永續長Abigail Ng表示，更新後的分類標準將為金融機構和企業提供更清晰的方向與信心，以支持亞洲的綠色及轉型活動融資。SSFA並未量化此次修訂可能帶動的額外資料中心投資規模，僅指出財務影響將取決於貸款方如何應用相關標準，以及個別項目是否符合相應要求。

  ## 新加坡國內資料中心政策背景

  此次分類標準修訂，是新加坡針對資料中心所推出的一系列國內監管與規劃措施的一部分。

  新加坡國會於2026年10月通過《數位基礎設施法》，建立了強制性能源效率標準的法定框架。依據該法，IT關鍵負載達至少3百萬瓦（MW）的設施業者——大約是設施開始大量消耗電網電力的規模——須取得執照並符合PUE要求。詳細標準將透過法規及實務守則制定，預計於2027年開始實施。

  在國會辯論期間，數位發展及資訊部政務次長陳傑豪（Tan Kiat How）表示，新加坡資料中心於2024年的用電量約為6.5太瓦時（TWh），相當於全國用電量的略超10%。

  由資通訊媒體發展局（IMDA）於2024年5月推出的《綠色資料中心路線圖》，設定了資料中心在十年內達到PUE 1.3或更低的目標。另外，國家水務局（PUB）為新舊資料中心引入了每百萬瓦時2立方公尺或以下的WUE目標；該目標本身並非《數位基礎設施法》下的強制性限制，但該框架允許透過未來法規引入用水效率要求。

  2026年8月，經濟發展局與IMDA在第二輪「資料中心申請計畫」下，暫時向Digital Realty、Equinix、吉寶資料中心（Keppel Data Centres）及ST Telemedia全球資料中心（ST Telemedia Global Data Centres）各分配50百萬瓦，合計200百萬瓦的額外資料中心容量。每家獲選業者均承諾，新容量所需能源的50%以上將來自綠色能源，超出本次計畫所設定的最低要求。

  ## 此次修訂對開發商與融資方的意義

  此次分類標準更新的實際意義在於，位於水資源緊張市場的區域資料中心開發商，現在有了更清晰的路徑，可在不被要求犧牲電力效率的前提下，為項目取得綠色或轉型融資資格。以該分類標準作為參考框架的金融機構，將能在承銷或架構交易時採用PUE-WUE綜合公式。

  SSFA的下一步，如附錄所示，是將指引擴展至更廣泛的綠色基礎設施相關活動，包括供應鏈及專業服務角色——這些角色本身雖不直接營運資料中心，但對資料中心的開發有所貢獻。
