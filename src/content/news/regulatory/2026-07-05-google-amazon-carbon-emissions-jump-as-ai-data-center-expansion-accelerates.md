body_en: |
  Google's annual carbon emissions rose 25% year-over-year and Amazon's climbed 16%, the two companies disclosed in sustainability reports released this week, as artificial intelligence (AI) data center expansion pushes both further from their stated net-zero targets.

  Neither company directly attributed the increases to AI in its filings. Analysis cited by TechCrunch points to data center buildout as the dominant driver for both, and concludes that both companies face serious, costly business adjustments to remain on track for net-zero commitments.

  ## Scope 3 emissions point to data centers

  The clearest signal lies in Scope 3 emissions — the accounting category covering indirect pollution generated outside a company's direct operations, including the manufacture of equipment it procures and the energy its customers consume. Google's Scope 3 emissions rose by 2.1 million metric tons last year, double its 2019 baseline, the report showed.

  Because most of Google's consumer hardware is small and low-power, the analysis identifies the company's data center division as the primary source of that growth, driven by procurement of graphics processing units (GPUs) — the specialized chips used to run AI workloads.

  Amazon's Scope 3 emissions grew at an even faster rate than Google's, fueled by physical construction of warehouses and cloud computing facilities. In 2025, Amazon added more data center capacity globally than any other company, including more than 1.2 gigawatts (GW) of new power capacity in the fourth quarter alone.

  ## Renewable energy offset breaks down

  For years, technology companies managed their carbon footprints by purchasing renewable energy, effectively neutralizing emissions tied to office and server electricity use. AI has disrupted that approach because next-generation data centers require stable, large-scale power supply continuously — a demand that intermittent renewable sources cannot reliably meet alone.

  That constraint has pushed companies including Google to invest in natural gas-fired generation to maintain uninterrupted power for AI computing, according to TechCrunch's analysis.

  ## Supply chain adds further pressure

  The emissions challenge extends into the upstream manufacturing supply chain. Semiconductor factories in Asia that produce the AI chips both companies depend on operate largely on electricity grids heavily reliant on fossil fuels. The production of memory chips and GPUs also involves specialty chemicals that release greenhouse gases with atmospheric warming potential thousands of times greater than carbon dioxide (CO2), the reports show.

  ## Path to net zero

  To stay on their net-zero timelines, both Amazon and Google would need to aggressively scale renewable energy procurement, invest in low-carbon cement and steel for data center construction, and purchase millions of tons of carbon removal credits on global markets, the TechCrunch analysis concluded.
body_zh: |
  Google年度碳排放年增25%，Amazon則攀升16%，兩家公司本週在各自的永續報告中揭露這項數據。隨著人工智慧（AI）資料中心持續擴張，兩間科技巨頭距離原訂的淨零目標愈來愈遠。

  兩家公司在申報文件中均未直接將排放增加歸因於AI。TechCrunch引述的分析指出，資料中心的大規模建設才是兩者碳排成長的主要驅動力，並得出結論：若要守住淨零承諾，兩家公司都需要進行代價高昂的重大業務調整。

  ## 第三範疇排放直指資料中心

  最明顯的訊號來自第三範疇（Scope 3）排放——這個會計類別涵蓋企業直接營運以外所產生的間接污染，包括所採購設備的製造過程，以及客戶使用產品或服務所消耗的能源。報告顯示，Google去年的第三範疇排放增加了210萬公噸，已達2019年基準值的兩倍。

  由於Google旗下大多數消費性硬體體積小、功耗低，上述分析認定，該公司資料中心部門才是排放成長的主要來源，而成長動能則來自大量採購繪圖處理器（GPU）——即用於執行AI運算工作負載的專用晶片。

  Amazon的第三範疇排放成長速度甚至比Google更快，主要由倉儲設施與雲端運算機房的實體興建所帶動。2025年，Amazon在全球新增的資料中心容量超越所有競爭對手，光是第四季便新增逾1.2吉瓦（GW）的電力容量。

  ## 再生能源抵銷機制失靈

  多年來，科技公司藉由購買再生能源憑證來管控碳足跡，有效抵銷辦公室與伺服器用電所產生的排放。然而，AI的崛起打亂了這套做法——新一代資料中心需要持續、穩定的大規模電力供應，而間歇性的再生能源來源單獨依賴時往往無法可靠滿足這樣的需求。

  根據TechCrunch的分析，這項限制已促使包括Google在內的企業轉而投資天然氣發電，以確保AI運算所需的不間斷電力供應。

  ## 供應鏈帶來額外壓力

  碳排放挑戰更延伸至上游製造供應鏈。兩家公司所倚賴的AI晶片，主要由亞洲的半導體工廠生產，而這些工廠所使用的電網至今仍高度依賴化石燃料。報告亦顯示，記憶體晶片與GPU的生產過程中會使用多種特殊化學物質，這些物質所釋放的溫室氣體，其大氣暖化潛勢是二氧化碳（CO₂）的數千倍之多。

  ## 邁向淨零的路徑

  TechCrunch的分析最終指出，Amazon與Google若要守住各自的淨零時程，就必須大力擴大再生能源採購規模、投入低碳水泥與低碳鋼材用於資料中心建設，並在全球市場購買數百萬噸的碳移除信用額度。
