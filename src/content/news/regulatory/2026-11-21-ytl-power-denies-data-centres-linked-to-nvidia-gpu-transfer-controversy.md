body_en: |
  YTL Power International Bhd has denied that its data centres in Johor, Malaysia were involved in routing Nvidia Corp graphics processing units (GPUs) to Chinese customers, saying reports linking the group to the controversy are "speculative and unsubstantiated" and "completely untrue."

  The Kuala Lumpur-listed company issued the denial in a formal statement, pushing back against allegations that have surfaced around Malaysia's role in potential circumvention of US export controls on advanced semiconductors.

  ## What YTL Power says its data centres do

  YTL Power said its data centre operations provide co-location services — meaning they supply physical space, electrical power, cooling infrastructure, and network connectivity — to multiple global customers, including US hyperscalers, which is an industry term for large-scale cloud computing providers such as Amazon, Google, and Microsoft. The company said those arrangements comply with both Malaysian law and US trade controls.

  The group added that it has worked closely with Malaysia's Investment, Trade and Industry Ministry (Miti) on its data centre developments and has met all applicable reporting and import requirements.

  ## US export rule

  YTL Power addressed a specific US regulatory measure: the interim final rule issued by the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), the US Commerce Department agency that administers export controls. The rule, titled "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors," targets importers of GPUs and providers of computing services to customers in Country Group D:5, a US export-control designation that includes China.

  YTL Power said this rule does not affect its co-location services, characterising its business model as distinct from the activities the regulation covers.

  ## Significance for Malaysia's data centre sector

  The denial is significant because Malaysia has emerged as a major destination for data centre investment, partly due to its proximity to Southeast Asian markets and its relatively stable power supply. Allegations linking Malaysian data centre operators to GPU diversion schemes could affect regulatory scrutiny of the sector and investor confidence.

  YTL Power said it remains confident that demand for co-location data centre infrastructure in Malaysia will continue to grow, citing strong global demand including from the United States.

  楊忠禮電力國際有限公司（YTL Power International Bhd）否認其位於馬來西亞柔佛的資料中心，涉及將輝達（Nvidia Corp）繪圖處理器（GPU）轉手予中國客戶一事，聲明指有關報導「純屬臆測且毫無根據」，更是「完全不實」。

  這家在吉隆坡掛牌上市的企業發布正式聲明，強力駁斥外界就馬來西亞在規避美國先進半導體出口管制一事中所扮演角色的相關指控。

  ## 楊忠禮電力的資料中心業務說明

  楊忠禮電力表示，其資料中心業務提供的是主機代管服務——亦即提供實體空間、電力供應、冷卻基礎設施及網路連接——服務對象涵蓋多家全球客戶，其中包括美國超大規模雲端業者。所謂「超大規模業者」，係業界對亞馬遜、Google、微軟等大型雲端運算服務提供商的統稱。楊忠禮電力表示，上述所有合作安排均符合馬來西亞法律及美國出口管制規定。

  楊忠禮集團補充，在資料中心建設方面，已與馬來西亞投資、貿易與工業部（MITI）密切配合，並已履行所有適用的申報及進口要求。

  ## 美國出口管制規定

  楊忠禮電力亦就一項特定美國法規作出回應。該規定係由美國商務部旗下負責管理出口管制的工業及安全局（BIS）所頒布的暫行最終規則，規則名稱為「處理對先進半導體的遠端存取」，主要針對GPU進口商，以及向被列入美國出口管制「D:5國家群組」之客戶提供運算服務的業者；中國即屬於此一管制類別。

  楊忠禮電力表示，上述規定並不適用於其主機代管服務，強調自身的商業模式有別於該法規所規範的業務活動。

  ## 對馬來西亞資料中心產業的意義

  此次聲明意義重大。近年來，馬來西亞已成為資料中心投資的主要目的地，部分原因在於其鄰近東南亞市場，加上電力供應相對穩定。一旦有指控將馬來西亞資料中心業者與GPU轉移計畫掛勾，可能引發監管機構加強審查整個產業，並動搖投資者信心。

  楊忠禮電力表示，對於馬來西亞主機代管資料中心基礎設施的需求持續成長依然充滿信心，並援引包括來自美國在內的強勁全球需求作為支撐依據。
