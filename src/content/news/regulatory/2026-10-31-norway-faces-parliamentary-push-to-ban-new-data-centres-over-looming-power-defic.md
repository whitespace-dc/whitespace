body_en: |
  Norway is facing a political reckoning over data centre growth after the national energy regulator published forecasts Thursday showing the sector could consume 25 terawatt hours (TWh) of electricity annually by 2035, enough to eliminate the country's current power surplus and potentially push it into deficit. The far-left Red Party announced it will call an emergency parliamentary debate next week and propose a ban on new data centre construction.

  The Norwegian Water Resources and Energy Directorate (NVE), the country's energy regulator, said data centres now account for between 3 and 4 TWh of annual demand, or roughly 2.5 percent of Norway's total electricity consumption. The sector barely existed three to four years ago, NVE head Kjetil Lund told Reuters. The 25 TWh projection for 2035 is based solely on grid connections already approved — meaning facilities already in the pipeline, not future applicants.

  Norway's power system is dominated by hydropower generation and currently carries a surplus of approximately 15 TWh. At the 25 TWh consumption level the NVE projects, that surplus would be consumed entirely by data centres alone, leaving no buffer for other demand growth. Transmission system operator Statnett has issued a similar warning recently, the NVE report noted.

  ## Political response

  The Red Party's planned motion has drawn expressions of support from other parties, though it remains unclear whether a majority exists to pass a formal ban. The minority Labour Party-led government stopped short of endorsing a moratorium. Energy minister Terje Aasland told Reuters the government is already developing both short-term and long-term measures and is focused on "ensuring the right kind of data centre establishments in Norway, those that are strategically important at a national level."

  ## Economic case under scrutiny

  Critics of the sector argue that its economic contribution is disproportionately small relative to the power it consumes. A study commissioned by the Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance found that data centres generated approximately 3.2 billion crowns (about $345 million) in direct economic value in 2025 and employed the equivalent of around 600 full-time positions. Critics also contend the industry is largely foreign-owned and will drive up electricity prices for Norwegian households and businesses.

  ## What comes next

  Norway joins a growing list of countries where rapid data centre expansion is encountering regulatory and political resistance. The Red Party's motion is scheduled for debate in parliament next week, and the Labour-led government's parallel policy review means regulatory tightening is likely regardless of whether a formal ban passes.

  挪威正面臨一場因資料中心快速擴張引發的政治風暴。國家能源主管機關週四公布預測報告，顯示資料中心產業至2035年的年用電量可能高達25兆瓦時（TWh），足以抹去挪威目前約15兆瓦時的電力盈餘，甚至可能使國家陷入電力短缺。極左派的紅黨（Red Party）隨即宣布，將於下週提請國會緊急辯論，並提案禁止興建新的資料中心。

  挪威水資源與能源局（NVE）——即該國的能源主管機關——表示，資料中心目前每年消耗電力約3至4兆瓦時，占全國總用電量的2.5%左右。NVE局長克耶蒂爾．倫德（Kjetil Lund）告訴路透社，這個產業在三、四年前幾乎還不存在。值得注意的是，2035年25兆瓦時的預測數字，僅根據已獲批准的電網連接申請計算——換言之，這只涵蓋目前已在規劃中的設施，尚不包括未來的新申請案。

  挪威的電力系統以水力發電為主，目前維持約15兆瓦時的電力盈餘。若資料中心用電量如NVE所預測般達到25兆瓦時，這些盈餘將被資料中心單一產業全數吸收，其他需求的成長將毫無緩衝空間。NVE報告亦指出，電力傳輸系統業者Statnett近期也發出了類似警告。

  ## 政治回應

  紅黨的提案已獲多個政黨表態支持，但目前尚不清楚國會是否已有足夠多數票可通過正式禁令。以少數黨工黨（Labour Party）為首的執政政府則未明確背書禁建令。能源部長泰爾耶．奧斯蘭（Terje Aasland）告訴路透社，政府已在研擬短期及長期配套措施，目標是「確保在挪威設立正確類型的資料中心——那些對國家具有戰略重要性的設施」。

  ## 經濟效益受到質疑

  批評者認為，資料中心產業的經濟貢獻與其龐大的用電量相比，明顯不成比例。挪威數位化與公共治理部委託的一份研究顯示，資料中心在2025年所創造的直接經濟產值約為32億克朗（約合3億4,500萬美元），相當職位僅約600個全職工作。批評者亦指出，該產業大多由外國資本持有，並將推高挪威一般家庭與企業的電費。

  ## 後續發展

  挪威加入了一個日益壯大的行列——越來越多國家正面臨資料中心急速擴張所帶來的法規與政治阻力。紅黨的提案預計下週在國會進行辯論，加上工黨領導政府同步展開的政策檢討，無論正式禁令最終是否通過，法規趨嚴幾乎已是不可避免的走向。
