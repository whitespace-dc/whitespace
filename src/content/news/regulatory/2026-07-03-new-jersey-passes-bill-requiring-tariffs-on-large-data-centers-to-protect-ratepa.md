body_en: |
  New Jersey lawmakers on June 30, 2026, passed a bill requiring the state's Board of Public Utilities (BPU) to establish tariff standards for data centers of at least 50 megawatts (MW), a threshold equal to roughly the output of a small power plant, as the state seeks to prevent ordinary electricity customers from bearing the grid costs that accompany rapid growth in AI-driven computing demand. The bill now heads to the desk of Democratic Gov. Mikie Sherrill.

  The legislation directs the BPU — New Jersey's utility regulator — to develop tariff structures specifically applicable to large data centers, both new and existing. The bill's sponsor, Assemblyman David Bailey Jr. (D), told Utility Dive that Sherrill's office was involved in drafting the latest version and said he is optimistic she will sign it. Sherrill has identified energy affordability as a central policy priority.

  ## A revised bill after a predecessor was pocket-vetoed

  This is not the first attempt to enact such a framework in New Jersey. Former Gov. Phil Murphy effectively killed a similar bill earlier in 2025 by allowing his term to expire without signing it — a maneuver known as a pocket veto. Bailey said the new version is more protective than that prior legislation on two key dimensions: it applies to existing data center facilities in addition to new ones, and it lowers the eligibility threshold from 100 MW to 50 MW, capturing a broader share of large-load customers.

  Bailey said the utilities subject to the law offered little resistance. "We really did not get much, if any, pushback from the utilities," he told Utility Dive. "They've been receptive to the process."

  ## Key provisions

  The bill charges the BPU with determining which grid costs are attributable specifically to large data center customers rather than the broader ratepayer base. To prevent fragmentation — where a single operator might split a facility across multiple parcels to fall below the 50 MW threshold — the bill includes an aggregation rule: the BPU must treat facilities under common ownership or control, located on the same or contiguous sites, or sharing substantial physical, operational, or interconnection infrastructure as a single large data center for the purpose of applying the threshold.

  Among the bill's operational requirements:

  - Data centers must participate in demand-response and energy efficiency programs.
  - They must make binding flexibility commitments and report flexibility performance to the BPU.
  - During grid emergencies, large data center customers would be subject to curtailment before residential customers.
  - Prospective data centers must demonstrate their project is unique and not duplicative of existing capacity.
  - New applicants must provide financial guarantees that they will take or pay for at least 85% of requested service over a minimum 10-year period.

  The bill also directs the BPU to prioritize grid interconnection for data centers that make binding commitments to bring their own clean generation or energy storage, and grants the board authority to impose additional protections it deems necessary — including transmission security agreements and stipulations related to generation, distribution, or substation facilities.

  ## Ratepayer protection framework

  Dawone Robinson, a managing director at the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC), said the bill charts a "balanced path forward for data center growth" and argued its framework "will deliver cost savings to households faster and more effectively." Robinson also cited its relevance to an ongoing debate at PJM Interconnection — the grid operator that manages electricity transmission across New Jersey and much of the mid-Atlantic and Midwest — over whether large loads should be required to bring their own generating capacity. Robinson said the bill "ensures any 'bring your own capacity' framework adopted by PJM will support New Jersey's climate targets."

  ## Significance and next steps

  The legislation represents one of the more detailed state-level attempts to assign grid costs directly to large data center operators rather than spreading them across all ratepayers — a model Bailey said he hopes other states will adopt as AI infrastructure buildout strains electric grids nationally. Whether Sherrill signs the bill will determine whether New Jersey becomes the first state to enact this type of framework, or whether the effort stalls again as it did under her predecessor. Bailey told Utility Dive that Sherrill's office was involved in shaping the current version of the bill.

  ## 紐澤西立法要求大型資料中心繳交電網費用，保障一般用電戶權益

  紐澤西州議會於2026年6月30日通過一項法案，要求州公用事業委員會（Board of Public Utilities，BPU）針對50百萬瓦（MW）以上的大型資料中心建立費率標準。50 MW約相當於一座小型發電廠的輸出功率。此舉旨在防止一般用電戶承擔AI運算需求急速擴張所帶來的電網建設成本。法案現已送交民主黨籍州長Mikie Sherrill。

  該法案指示BPU——紐澤西州的公用事業監管機構——專門針對大型資料中心（無論新舊設施）制定費率架構。法案主要提案人、州眾議員David Bailey Jr.（民主黨）向《Utility Dive》表示，Sherrill州長辦公室參與了最新版本的草擬過程，他對州長最終簽署法案持樂觀態度。Sherrill一向將電力費用的負擔能力列為施政核心優先項目。

  ## 前案遭口袋否決，修訂版捲土重來

  這並非紐澤西州首次嘗試建立此類監管框架。前任州長Phil Murphy在2025年初，以任期屆滿卻未簽署的方式，實質上否決了一項類似法案——此舉在美國政治上稱為「口袋否決」（pocket veto）。Bailey表示，新版法案在兩個關鍵面向上比前案更具保護力：第一，適用範圍從僅限新設施擴大至涵蓋現有設施；第二，資格門檻從100 MW下調至50 MW，納入更多大用電量客戶。

  Bailey表示，受法案規範的公用事業業者幾乎未提出反對意見。他向《Utility Dive》說：「我們幾乎沒有收到公用事業業者的任何阻力，他們對整個過程相當配合。」

  ## 法案主要條款

  法案責成BPU釐清哪些電網費用應歸因於大型資料中心客戶，而非由全體用電戶共同承擔。為防止業者藉由將設施分拆至多個地塊以規避50 MW門檻，法案設有「合併計算」規定：BPU必須將同一所有權或控制權下、位於相同或毗鄰地點、或共用大量實體、營運及併網基礎設施的設施，視為單一大型資料中心，統一適用門檻規定。

  法案的具體營運要求包括：

  - 資料中心必須參與需量反應及能源效率計畫。
  - 業者須作出具法律約束力的電力調度彈性承諾，並向BPU報告執行成效。
  - 電網緊急狀況期間，大型資料中心客戶的用電削減順序排在住宅用電戶之前。
  - 申請新設的資料中心必須證明其計畫具備唯一性，不與現有容量重複。
  - 新申請者須提供財務擔保，承諾在至少10年期間內承擔或給付所申請服務容量的至少85%。

  此外，法案指示BPU優先為承諾自備清潔發電或儲能設施的資料中心辦理電網併接；並授予BPU視情況採取額外保護措施的權力，包括要求簽訂輸電安全協議，以及就發電、配電或變電站設施訂定相關規定。

  ## 保護一般用電戶的費率框架

  自然資源保護委員會（Natural Resources Defense Council，NRDC）董事總經理Dawone Robinson表示，這項法案為資料中心的發展規劃了一條「兼顧各方的前進路徑」，並主張其框架「將能更快速、更有效地為家庭帶來電費節省效益」。Robinson也提及法案與PJM Interconnection當前論辯的關聯性。PJM是管理紐澤西州及美國中大西洋地區與中西部大部分地區電力輸送的電網運營商，目前正討論是否應要求大用電量客戶自備發電容量。Robinson表示，這項法案「確保PJM未來採行的任何『自備容量』框架，都能支持紐澤西州的氣候目標」。

  ## 意義與後續動向

  這項立法代表美國各州迄今最詳盡的嘗試之一，直接要求大型資料中心業者承擔電網費用，而非將成本分攤至所有用電戶。Bailey表示，他希望其他州也能效法此一模式，因為全國各地的電網正因AI基礎設施大規模擴建而承受壓力。Sherrill最終是否簽署法案，將決定紐澤西州能否成為全美第一個實施此類費率框架的州，抑或此案將如前任州長任內一般再度受阻。Bailey向《Utility Dive》指出，Sherrill州長辦公室參與了現行版本法案的制定過程。
