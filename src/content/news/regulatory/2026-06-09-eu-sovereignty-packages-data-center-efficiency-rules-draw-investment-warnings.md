body_en: |
  The European Commission last week launched the European Technological Sovereignty Package, a set of legislative proposals designed to strengthen the bloc's position in artificial intelligence and chip manufacturing — but the package's data center efficiency requirements are already drawing warnings from operators that heavy-handed regulation could undermine the very investment it is meant to attract.

  The package includes three main legislative proposals: a Chips Act 2.0, a Cloud and AI Development Act, and a Strategic Roadmap for Digitalization and AI in Energy. Of those, the Strategic Roadmap carries the most direct consequences for the data center sector. Under the roadmap, the European Commission has adopted a rating scheme for data centers covering energy and water efficiency, clean energy use, waste heat reuse, and compliance with minimum EU energy performance standards. The scheme was adopted in 2026; the first labels are scheduled for 2027, with minimum performance standards also taking effect that year. Fourteen EU industry associations across the energy and data center sectors signed a declaration of intent aligned with the roadmap.

  Announcing the package, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said: "We cannot afford to depend on others for the technologies that keep our hospitals running, our energy grids stable, and our services secure. Europe has the talent, the research excellence, the industrial base and the Single Market. Together, we must turn these strengths into technological sovereignty."

  ## Industry concern over prescriptive rules

  The declaration signing coincided with Datacloud Global Congress 2026, held in Cannes, France, where data center operators, investors, and suppliers debated the package's likely effects. Michael Winterson, secretary general of the European Data Centre Association (EUDCA) — one of the declaration's signatories — said the sector's core objection is not to transparency requirements but to regulation that goes further and specifies how operators must achieve efficiency targets.

  Winterson said the existing European Energy Directive (EED), which was introduced in 2012 and requires data centers to report operational metrics including power usage effectiveness (PUE) and water usage effectiveness (WUE), demonstrates the distinction. "The name is kind of silly, because we will achieve no greater energy efficiency than what we're already achieving," he said. "So, the EED's benefit is not that it's creating efficiency; it's creating transparency, an honest mechanism to report on what you're doing. That's its power."

  His concern centers on how member states implement EU-level directives. Winterson cited Germany's version of the EED as a cautionary example. "It was gold-plated and started to prescribe, and this puts an impact on the cost of data centers, which drives away investment," he said. "Legislation that prescribes too much will actually drive investment away and into another market."

  The EUDCA and other operators had previously attempted to head off tighter regulation by developing voluntary commitments through the Climate Neutral Data Centre Pact. Winterson acknowledged that effort did not succeed. "Guess what? We failed," he said at the Datacloud panel. "Regulations add complexity and cost to your operations, which ultimately means you have to pass it on to your customers, which means they will take that project somewhere else."

  ## The case for data collection

  Not all voices on the Datacloud panel were opposed to the reporting framework. Emma Fryer, an EUDCA board member and director of public policy Europe at colocation provider CyrusOne, said mature data collection could serve a defensive purpose for the industry. "I do think that as that data collection matures, we will have really useful information that will help us refute a lot of misinformation we hear about the industry," she said, citing water use as an example. "If we know by country how much water we use, and it happens to be a tenth of what golf courses use, that is in fact handy to know, as you can immediately quash those silly headlines that we see in the press."

  Freyer also argued that the regulatory framework does not adequately reflect the operational differences among data center types. "You have different agents, different business models, you've got colocation sites, and we don't have the freedom to operate that some other data center types do when it comes to energy efficiency measures," she said, calling for clearer distinctions between enterprise, colocation, and hyperscale facilities.

  ## Workload differentiation rejected

  One proposal raised at the Datacloud panel — that efficiency regulations should differentiate based on the social value of the workloads a data center runs, for instance distinguishing AI-driven medical research from entertainment streaming — drew a firm rejection from industry figures. "There is no 'good' workload or 'bad' workload," said Damir Spoljaric, founder and managing partner of investment group Gi21. "For me, it is too dangerous, I would keep it as it is." Winterson added: "Whose right is it to regulate these activities in a liberal democracy?"

  ## What comes next

  The tension the package highlights is structural. Europe has been widely viewed as over-reliant on US cloud and technology suppliers, a dependency that the emergence of large-language-model development by US hyperscalers has deepened. The Datacloud event drew presentations from Meta and Microsoft, among other US AI companies. The sovereignty package is a direct response to that competitive gap.

  Yet the industry's position, articulated at Cannes, is that regulatory costs imposed in pursuit of efficiency and transparency could accelerate the shift of AI and digital investment to markets with lighter requirements, producing the opposite of the intended effect.

  Before the measures in the European Technological Sovereignty Package take effect, the proposals will go through further negotiations within the EU and among member states, as well as additional consultation with industry. The full package and Strategic Roadmap for Digitalization and AI in the Energy Sector are available on the European Commission website.

  歐盟委員會上週正式推出「歐洲科技主權套案」（European Technological Sovereignty Package），這套立法提案旨在強化歐盟在人工智慧與晶片製造領域的競爭地位。然而，套案中針對資料中心效率的相關規定，已引發業者警告：過重的監管負擔，恐將侵蝕套案本身所試圖吸引的投資動能。

  該套案包含三項主要立法提案：《晶片法案2.0》（Chips Act 2.0）、《雲端與人工智慧發展法》（Cloud and AI Development Act），以及《數位化與能源人工智慧戰略路線圖》（Strategic Roadmap for Digitalization and AI in Energy）。其中，《戰略路線圖》對資料中心產業的直接影響最為深遠。根據路線圖，歐盟委員會已採行一套資料中心評等機制，涵蓋能源與用水效率、潔淨能源使用、廢熱再利用，以及符合歐盟最低能源效能標準等面向。該機制於2026年正式採行，首批標章預計於2027年發出，最低效能標準亦同步生效。橫跨能源與資料中心產業的十四個歐盟行業協會，已聯署一份與路線圖方向一致的意向聲明。

  在宣布該套案時，歐盟委員會主席烏蘇拉·馮德萊恩（Ursula von der Leyen）表示：「我們不能繼續依賴他人，去維繫那些讓醫院正常運轉、讓能源電網穩定運行、讓各項服務安全可靠的關鍵技術。歐洲擁有人才、研究卓越性、工業基礎，以及單一市場。我們必須攜手將這些優勢轉化為科技主權。」

  ## 業界對過細規定表示憂慮

  意向聲明的聯署，恰逢2026年在法國坎城舉辦的「Datacloud全球大會」（Datacloud Global Congress 2026），與會的資料中心業者、投資人及供應商就此套案的可能影響展開辯論。歐洲資料中心協會（EUDCA）秘書長麥可·溫特森（Michael Winterson）——同時也是意向聲明的聯署方之一——表示，業界的核心異議並非針對透明度要求本身，而在於監管若進一步規定業者應「如何」達成效率目標，便已越界。

  溫特森以現行的《歐洲能源指令》（EED）為例，說明兩者的差異。該指令自2012年實施，要求資料中心申報包含電能使用效率（PUE）與用水效率（WUE）在內的各項營運指標。他說：「坦白說，這個名稱有點名不副實，因為這項法規並不會讓我們比現在更有效率。EED的真正價值，不在於創造效率，而在於創造透明度——一套如實申報現況的誠信機制。這才是它的力量所在。」

  他的憂慮，聚焦在各成員國落實歐盟層級指令的方式。溫特森以德國版EED為警示案例。他說：「德國將規定鍍金加料，開始細細規定該怎麼做，這對資料中心的成本造成衝擊，進而驅趕了投資。監管規定若開得太細，反而會把投資逼往其他市場。」

  EUDCA及其他業者此前曾試圖透過「氣候中和資料中心公約」（Climate Neutral Data Centre Pact）推動自願性承諾，以防堵更嚴格的法規出現。溫特森坦承那次努力並未成功。他在Datacloud座談會上直言：「結果如何？我們失敗了。法規會為你的營運增添複雜性與成本，最終你必然得把這些成本轉嫁給客戶，而客戶就會把業務移往別處。」

  ## 支持資料蒐集的聲音

  並非所有Datacloud座談的與會者都反對申報框架。EUDCA董事會成員、共置服務供應商CyrusOne歐洲公共政策總監艾瑪·費爾（Emma Fryer）指出，完善的資料蒐集機制可為產業提供防禦性武器。她說：「我確實認為，隨著資料蒐集日趨成熟，我們將能取得真正有價值的資訊，用來反駁外界對產業的諸多誤解。」她以用水問題為例：「如果我們能掌握各國用水量的資料，而實際上我們的用水量只有高爾夫球場的十分之一，那這個數字就非常有力——可以立刻戳破媒體那些荒謬的標題。」

  費爾也主張，現行法規框架並未充分反映不同類型資料中心之間的營運差異。她說：「資料中心有不同的參與者、不同的商業模式。以共置業者為例，在能源效率措施上，我們並沒有其他類型資料中心所擁有的那種操作自由。」她呼籲監管機構應對企業自建、共置與超大規模設施作出更清楚的區分。

  ## 以工作負載性質差異化規定的提議遭否決

  座談會上有人提議，效率法規應依資料中心所運行之工作負載的社會價值加以差異化——例如，對AI驅動的醫療研究與串流娛樂設定不同標準。這項建議遭到業界人士的強烈駁斥。投資集團Gi21創辦人暨執行合夥人達米爾·斯波爾賈里奇（Damir Spoljaric）明確表示：「沒有所謂『好』的工作負載或『壞』的工作負載。對我來說，這樣做太危險了，我寧願維持現狀。」溫特森也補充：「在一個自由民主社會裡，究竟是誰有權監管這些活動？」

  ## 後續發展

  這套套案所凸顯的矛盾，具有結構性的本質。歐洲長期以來被視為過度依賴美國雲端與技術供應商，而美國超大規模業者相繼投入大型語言模型開發，更加深了這種依賴關係。本屆Datacloud大會上，Meta、微軟等美國AI企業均有專題演講。主權套案，正是歐洲對這一競爭落差的直接回應。

  然而，在坎城大會上，業界清楚表達了另一種立場：以追求效率與透明度為名所加諸的監管成本，可能反而加速人工智慧與數位投資向法規較寬鬆的市場轉移，產生與初衷背道而馳的效果。

  在「歐洲科技主權套案」各項措施正式生效之前，相關提案仍須歷經歐盟內部及各成員國之間的進一步談判，以及與業界的額外諮商。完整的套案內容及《數位化與能源人工智慧戰略路線圖》，已在歐盟委員會官方網站公開。
