body_en: |
  Community resistance stalled or blocked 48 U.S. data center projects worth $156 billion in 2025, and ended another 20 in the first three months of 2026, according to research cited by the US Green Building Council of Texas, which is calling on the industry to make community benefits agreements — negotiated, legally enforceable contracts between developers and host communities — a standard element of project planning.

  The figures come from Miquel Vila, a researcher at 10a Labs, and are cited in an August 9, 2026 analysis by Jim Broughton, leader of the US Green Building Council of Texas Data Center Group, published by Data Center Dynamics. Broughton argues that local opposition has shifted from a fringe reaction to "a structural barrier to development" in many parts of the United States. He quotes Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates, who warned that "a public already suspicious of AI does not need to be told its fears are imaginary."

  ## Texas as a development test case

  Texas now ranks second only to Virginia for new data center proposals and is projected to become the leading U.S. and global data center market by 2030, according to Broughton, drawing developers through limited land-use restrictions, low-cost large land tracts, abundant power, and pro-business policies. As of the date of the analysis, 149 documented projects — plus an unspecified number of unreported proposals — are already in early planning and design in the state.

  A large share of those proposals target rural communities that have never previously hosted industrial-scale facilities. Broughton writes that many local governments in Texas have never evaluated a data center proposal before and lack the technical expertise, staff, and regulatory tools to assess impacts on water systems, transmission infrastructure, air quality, emergency services, and neighborhood character. Developers, meanwhile, typically arrive expecting rapid approvals and predictable timelines — a mismatch in expectations that Broughton says makes early alignment essential.

  ## What a community benefits agreement covers

  A community benefits agreement, or CBA, is a negotiated, enforceable partnership between a developer and a host community, Broughton writes — not a public-relations gesture or a list of voluntary commitments. The agreement defines mutual obligations, establishes transparency requirements, and specifies how impacts will be managed over the life of a project.

  Broughton identifies ten areas a comprehensive CBA should address:

  - **Community infrastructure and local investment**: Commitments to invest in local infrastructure, education, and workforce programs, and to coordinate with utilities on infrastructure planning.
  - **Water stewardship and aquifer protection**: Sustainable water operations including use of recycled or non-potable water and deployment of advanced cooling systems to reduce consumption.
  - **Energy, grid reliability, and clean power**: Developer funding of power system improvements and coordination with utilities to protect grid reliability and limit cost increases for residential ratepayers.
  - **Environmental impact, land use, and sustainability**: Carbon-reduction targets, renewable energy use, advanced cooling technologies, dark-sky lighting, noise mitigation, stormwater runoff controls, and air-pollution controls for any on-site fossil fuel generation.
  - **Workforce development and local hiring**: Requirements for local hiring, workforce training pipelines, data center academies, and partnerships with public schools and community colleges.
  - **Construction standards and community protections**: Mitigation of noise, dust, traffic, construction waste, and road degradation during the build phase.
  - **Transparency and ongoing community engagement**: Regular public reporting on water and energy use, employment commitments, and community impacts, and responsiveness to community concerns.
  - **Infrastructure protections for host communities**: Utility coordination, reduction of road and public-service impacts, and participation in emergency planning.
  - **Tax base and local economic contributions**: Defined tax commitments and abatements, local procurement expectations, and long-term economic contributions.
  - **Long-term community relationship standards**: Obligations that extend beyond construction into long-term planning and community partnerships.

  ## Microsoft's San Antonio model

  Broughton holds up Microsoft's "Community-First AI Infrastructure" program — first implemented in San Antonio — as what he describes as "the most advanced model in the United States today" and "the premier example of how a data center operator can build long-lasting partnerships with host communities." He presents its structure as the current benchmark for what a modern CBA can and should include.

  ## Industry implications

  The volume of stalled and cancelled U.S. projects — $156 billion in a single year — establishes that community opposition carries direct financial consequences for the sector, according to the figures Vila provided and Broughton cited. Broughton argues that a well-structured CBA gives developers the predictability and reduced project risk they seek from local approvals, while giving communities enforceable assurances that their interests will be protected.

  With Texas projected to become the world's largest data center market by 2030 and 149-plus documented projects already in early planning stages across the state, the coming years will determine whether voluntary adoption of community benefit frameworks reduces the rate of project failure driven by local opposition.
body_zh: |
  根據美國德州綠建築委員會（US Green Building Council of Texas）引用的研究數據，2025年全美共有48項資料中心專案因社區阻力而停滯或遭到封鎖，涉及金額高達1,560億美元；2026年首季再有20項宣告終止。德州綠建築委員會因此呼籲業界將「社區利益協議」（Community Benefits Agreement，CBA）——即開發商與地主社區之間經協商、具法律約束力的合約——納入專案規劃的標準流程。

  上述數字出自10a Labs研究員米蓋爾·比拉（Miquel Vila）的研究成果，由美國德州綠建築委員會資料中心工作小組召集人吉姆·布羅頓（Jim Broughton）於2026年8月9日發表的分析報告中引用，刊登於《資料中心動態》（Data Center Dynamics）。布羅頓主張，地方反對聲浪已從過去的邊緣現象，演變為美國許多地區「結構性的開發障礙」。他引用微軟共同創辦人比爾·蓋茲（Bill Gates）的警語：「一個已對AI充滿疑慮的社會大眾，不需要再被告知他們的擔憂是杞人憂天。」

  ## 德州：資料中心開發的試驗場

  根據布羅頓的分析，德州目前新資料中心提案數量僅次於維吉尼亞州，排名全美第二，預計在2030年前躍升為美國乃至全球最大的資料中心市場。德州之所以吸引開發商進駐，在於其土地使用限制少、大面積土地取得成本低、電力供應充裕，以及友善的商業政策環境。截至分析發布之日，德州已有149個有據可查的專案正處於早期規劃與設計階段，另有若干尚未公開的提案亦在其中。

  這批提案有相當大比例瞄準從未接待過工業規模設施的農村社區。布羅頓指出，德州許多地方政府從未評估過資料中心提案，缺乏技術專業、人力配置及法規工具，難以衡量相關設施對水資源系統、輸電基礎設施、空氣品質、緊急救援服務及社區特色的衝擊。另一方面，開發商通常抱持著快速取得核可、掌握可預期時程的期待前來——布羅頓認為，這種期望上的落差，正使得早期的溝通協調顯得格外重要。

  ## 社區利益協議的涵蓋範疇

  布羅頓寫道，社區利益協議（CBA）是開發商與地主社區之間經協商、可強制執行的夥伴關係——不是公關姿態，也不是一份自願承諾清單。協議明定雙方的相互義務、建立資訊透明機制，並規範專案存續期間各項衝擊的管理方式。

  布羅頓認為，一份完整的CBA應涵蓋以下十大領域：

  - **社區基礎建設與在地投資**：承諾投資當地基礎設施、教育及勞動力培育計畫，並與公用事業單位協調基礎設施規劃。
  - **水資源管理與含水層保護**：落實永續用水作業，包括使用再生水或非飲用水，以及導入先進冷卻系統以降低耗水量。
  - **能源、電網可靠性與潔淨電力**：由開發商出資改善電力系統，並與公用事業單位協調，以維護電網穩定、限制住宅用電用戶的電費漲幅。
  - **環境衝擊、土地使用與永續發展**：設定碳排放減量目標、使用再生能源、採用先進冷卻技術、暗天照明設計、噪音緩解措施、雨水逕流管制，以及廠區內化石燃料發電設備的空污防制。
  - **勞動力培育與在地雇用**：要求優先雇用當地人才，建立勞動力培訓管道、資料中心學院，並與公立學校及社區大學建立合作關係。
  - **施工標準與社區保護**：在施工階段緩解噪音、粉塵、交通壅塞、建築廢棄物及道路損耗等問題。
  - **資訊透明與持續的社區溝通**：定期公開水資源與能源使用情形、就業承諾及社區衝擊，並即時回應社區關切事項。
  - **對地主社區的基礎建設保護**：協調公用事業單位、降低對道路及公共服務的衝擊，並參與緊急應變規劃。
  - **稅基貢獻與在地經濟效益**：明訂稅務承諾與減免條件、在地採購期望，以及長期的經濟貢獻。
  - **長期社區關係標準**：義務延伸至施工完成之後，涵蓋長期規劃與社區夥伴關係的持續維繫。

  ## 微軟聖安東尼奧模式

  布羅頓以微軟的「社區優先AI基礎設施」（Community-First AI Infrastructure）計畫為最佳典範——該計畫首先在德州聖安東尼奧（San Antonio）付諸實施——稱其為「目前美國最先進的模式」，也是「資料中心業者如何與地主社區建立長久夥伴關係的首要典範」。他以此架構作為現代CBA應有樣貌的當前基準。

  ## 產業影響

  比拉提供、布羅頓引用的數據顯示：單一年度內被擱置與取消的美國資料中心專案金額高達1,560億美元，足以印證社區反對聲浪對業界帶來的直接財務代價。布羅頓主張，一份結構完善的CBA，既能給予開發商在地方核可程序中所追求的可預期性與較低的專案風險，同時也讓社區獲得具強制力的保障，確保其利益受到切實保護。

  隨著德州預計在2030年前成為全球最大的資料中心市場，加上全州已有149個以上記錄在案的專案進入早期規劃階段，未來數年將是關鍵的驗證期：業界自願採行社區利益框架，能否有效降低因地方阻力導致的專案失敗率，答案即將揭曉。
