body_en: |
  Australia's energy minister Chris Bowen said on Wednesday that data centres powered exclusively by gas will not be permitted to register under proposed national standards, placing the federal government on a collision course with the Northern Territory and Queensland, both of which have championed gas-fired power to attract large-scale technology investment.

  "If a data centre (is) proposed to only use gas, it will not meet the minimum national standards, and it won't be allowed to register," Bowen said in response to a Reuters question following a National Press Club address in Canberra, at which he also announced a new rooftop solar scheme.

  ## The policy and what it requires

  The proposed federal rules require new data centres to build new renewable energy sources directly — rather than simply drawing electricity from the existing grid — and envisage the issuance of renewable energy certificates to compliant facilities. Bowen indicated gas would be permitted in a "firming" role: backing up intermittent renewable supply when output falls short. He said gas used in that capacity should remain mostly dormant. "I think that's pretty sensible," he said.

  The distinction matters because data centres are among the most power-hungry facilities in modern infrastructure. A continuous, reliable electricity supply is a core operational requirement, and operators in regions with limited renewable buildout have historically looked to gas as that firm backup or primary source.

  ## The Northern Territory project at risk

  The policy intervention has immediate consequences for Beetaloo Digital, which last month received a grant of 185 hectares (457 acres) of land near Darwin, the Northern Territory capital, to develop a 2-gigawatt (GW) data centre. A gigawatt is roughly the output of a large coal or nuclear power station — enough to power hundreds of thousands of homes.

  Beetaloo Digital's parent company, Beetaloo Energy, plans to supply the facility using shale gas it is developing in the Beetaloo sub-Basin, a formation in the Northern Territory. The company estimated that generating 1 GW of power would require 200 terajoules (TJ) of gas per day — a volume that dwarfs, by comparison, the 84 TJ per day expected from Shell's coal seam gas development in Queensland, announced the same day.

  Beetaloo has said its data centre project could attract up to A$40 billion (approximately US$28.17 billion) in private investment, based on an exchange rate of A$1.4198 to the US dollar.

  A Beetaloo spokesperson said the project "has the potential to complement the Darwin Energy Hub's planned large-scale solar generation and battery storage by providing firm generation capacity," but declined to comment directly on Bowen's statements and did not specify what proportion of the facility's power would come from gas versus renewables.

  ## State-federal tension

  Both Queensland and the Northern Territory have publicly backed gas-fired power as a foundation for data centre development, positioning their gas resources as a competitive advantage in attracting technology investment. The federal standards, if enacted as described, would override those state-level strategies for any operator seeking national registration.

  The tension reflects a broader structural challenge: Australia's most gas-rich jurisdictions are also those promoting the largest data centre projects, while the federal government is tightening the emissions guardrails under which those projects would operate.

  ## Emissions context

  The Climate Change Authority has already flagged in published reports that Australia may fall short of its 2035 target of a 62% to 70% reduction in carbon emissions relative to 2005 levels — and that assessment does not account for large new energy-intensive projects such as the Beetaloo facility.

  ## What comes next

  Bowen's comments establish the federal government's position but the policy has not yet been formally enacted; the standards remain in proposed form. Beetaloo Digital has not disclosed the planned split between gas and renewable generation at its Darwin site, leaving open the question of whether the project could be restructured to meet the federal threshold. The Northern Territory government has not commented publicly on whether it will seek an accommodation with Canberra or contest the proposed rules.

  澳洲能源部長克里斯·鮑文（Chris Bowen）週三表示，完全以天然氣為電力來源的資料中心，將不得依據擬議中的全國標準進行登記，此舉使聯邦政府與北領地及昆士蘭州陷入正面衝突——這兩個地區均力推以天然氣發電吸引大規模科技投資。

  「若一座資料中心擬議中僅使用天然氣，將無法符合全國最低標準，也將不被允許登記，」鮑文在坎培拉國家新聞俱樂部發表演講後，回應路透社提問時如此表示。他當天同時宣布了一項新的屋頂太陽能計畫。

  ## 政策內容與要求

  擬議中的聯邦規定要求新建資料中心直接建置新的再生能源設施，而非單純從現有電網取電，並規劃向符合標準的設施核發再生能源憑證。鮑文指出，天然氣可在「調峰補充」的角色中獲准使用：於再生能源間歇性輸出不足時提供備援。他表示，在此情況下使用的天然氣，大多數時候應處於備而不用的狀態。「我認為這相當合理，」他說。

  這項區別至關重要，因為資料中心是現代基礎設施中用電最為密集的設施之一。穩定可靠的電力供應是核心營運需求，在再生能源建設尚不充足的地區，業者歷來仰賴天然氣作為穩定備援或主要能源。

  ## 北領地開發案面臨風險

  這項政策干預對Beetaloo Digital造成立即衝擊。該公司上個月獲得北領地首府達爾文（Darwin）附近185公頃（457英畝）的土地，用以開發一座2吉瓦（GW）的資料中心。1吉瓦的輸出量相當於一座大型燃煤或核能電廠——足以供應數十萬戶家庭用電。

  Beetaloo Digital的母公司Beetaloo Energy計畫利用其正在北領地Beetaloo次盆地（Beetaloo sub-Basin）開採的頁岩氣，為該設施供電。該公司估計，每生產1吉瓦的電力，每日需耗用200兆焦耳（TJ）的天然氣——這一數量遠超過殼牌（Shell）同日宣布、位於昆士蘭的煤層氣開發案每日預計產出的84兆焦耳。

  Beetaloo表示，其資料中心計畫可吸引最多400億澳幣（約合281.7億美元，以1美元兌1.4198澳幣匯率換算）的民間投資。

  Beetaloo發言人表示，該計畫「有潛力透過提供穩定發電容量，補充達爾文能源樞紐規劃中的大規模太陽能發電與電池儲能設施」，但拒絕直接回應鮑文的聲明，也未說明該設施天然氣與再生能源發電各自的占比。

  ## 州府與聯邦的緊張態勢

  昆士蘭州與北領地均公開支持以天然氣發電作為資料中心開發的基礎，將各自的天然氣資源定位為吸引科技投資的競爭優勢。聯邦標準若依目前描述付諸實施，將凌駕於上述州級策略之上，適用於所有尋求全國登記的業者。

  此一緊張局勢反映出一項更深層的結構性挑戰：澳洲天然氣資源最豐富的地區，恰恰也是推動最大規模資料中心計畫的地區，而聯邦政府正在收緊這些計畫所須遵守的碳排放門檻。

  ## 碳排放背景

  氣候變遷局在已發布的報告中指出，澳洲可能無法達成2035年的目標——即相較2005年水準，將碳排放量削減62%至70%——而該評估尚未將Beetaloo設施等大型新增高耗能計畫納入考量。

  ## 後續發展

  鮑文的表態確立了聯邦政府的立場，但相關政策尚未正式實施，標準仍處於擬議階段。Beetaloo Digital尚未公布其達爾文基地天然氣與再生能源發電的計畫配比，外界仍不清楚該計畫能否調整架構以符合聯邦門檻。北領地政府亦尚未公開表示，是否將與坎培拉尋求妥協，或對擬議中的規定提出異議。
