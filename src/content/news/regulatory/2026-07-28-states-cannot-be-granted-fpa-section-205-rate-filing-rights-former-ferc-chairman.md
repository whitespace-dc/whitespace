body_en: |
  Former Federal Energy Regulatory Commission Chairman Joseph Kelliher has argued that proposals advanced in the ongoing PJM Interconnection governance proceeding before FERC — calling for PJM and its transmission owners to make Federal Power Act Section 205 filings at state direction — are inconsistent with the Federal Power Act, a conclusion grounded in a 1984 federal court ruling that Kelliher says has not lost force.

  Kelliher, now principal of Joseph Kelliher Consulting and a former executive vice president of federal regulatory affairs for NextEra Energy, published the analysis in response to recommendations filed by the Organization of PJM States, known as OPSI, the body representing state utility regulators within PJM's territory.

  ## What OPSI Is Proposing

  OPSI has recommended, as part of its comments in the PJM governance proceeding, that both PJM — a regional transmission organization — and PJM public utility transmission owners be required to make tariff filings under Section 205 of the Federal Power Act at OPSI's behest. The organization's stated rationale is that "PJM's governance structure and stakeholder process were developed for a different era."

  ## Section 205 Versus Section 206

  The Federal Power Act establishes two separate tracks for changing a public utility's tariff, a framework Kelliher attributes to then-Judge Stephen Breyer's reasoning in *Massachusetts Dept. of Public Utilities v. FERC*. Section 205 allows a public utility — and only a public utility — to propose tariff changes for FERC review. Section 206 authorizes FERC to modify existing tariffs either on its own initiative or following a complaint, but places the burden on the Commission or the complainant to demonstrate that existing practices are unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential.

  OPSI is not a public utility, making Section 205 unavailable to it directly. Its proposals seek to work around that limitation by compelling public utilities to file on OPSI's behalf.

  ## The 1984 Precedent

  In *Massachusetts Dept. of Public Utilities v. FERC*, the Massachusetts DPU ordered Western Massachusetts Electric Co., a FERC-jurisdictional public utility, to make a Section 205 filing to change a rate-setting practice the state found objectionable. The utility complied, but FERC rejected the filing, holding that Section 205 "governs changes that the utility itself proposes, not those that a state regulator requires it to propose." As Breyer summarized FERC's position: "If Massachusetts objects to the rule, says FERC, it should file a complaint in accordance with [Section 206]." The court agreed.

  The court further noted that accepting Massachusetts' argument would allow a state to do "what FERC itself cannot, namely, to change an interstate rate practice that FERC has not found unreasonable." Kelliher writes that outcome is precisely what the OPSI recommendations seek.

  ## Why Consent or a FERC Order Would Not Cure the Problem

  Even if PJM and its transmission owners were to voluntarily agree to share their Section 205 filing rights with the states, Kelliher argues, FERC should still reject resulting filings under the *Mass. DPU* precedent. A request that FERC compel such sharing would also fail on two independent grounds: courts have repeatedly held that FERC has no power to force public utilities to make Section 205 filings, and FERC cannot delegate to states more power than the Commission itself holds. Kelliher states that FERC has no authority to delegate any of its Federal Power Act authority to states.

  ## Section 206 Is the Available Mechanism

  OPSI complained more than 20 times in its comments that it lacks "a formal means of placing state-developed proposals before FERC in a form the Commission can accept." Kelliher contests that characterization: Section 206 provides exactly that mechanism. The critical difference is that Section 206 casts state regulators as complainants and assigns them the burden of demonstrating that existing practices are unjust or unreasonable — a burden the OPSI recommendations are designed to avoid.

  "I understand states are offended at being classified as complainants," Kelliher writes, "but that is their only status under the Federal Power Act when it comes to tariff changes."

  ## The Atlantic City Electric Dispute

  OPSI cited the *Atlantic City Electric* decision to support its position, pointing to language stating that "utilities may choose to voluntarily give up, by contract, some of their rate-filing freedom under section 205." Kelliher disputes that reading on two grounds.

  First, the sharing of Section 205 rights discussed in *Atlantic City Electric* involved the allocation of those rights between PJM and its public utility members — not between public utilities and state regulators — in a context where one public utility was operating the assets of others. Second, Kelliher argues the quoted language refers only to a utility relinquishing its right to make unilateral rate changes during a fixed-rate contract, not to sharing filing rights with state complainants.

  The central holding of *Atlantic City Electric*, Kelliher writes, cuts against OPSI: "the power to initiate rate changes [under Section 205] rests with the utility and cannot be appropriated by FERC" — a principle that applies whether FERC appropriates those rights for itself or for state complainants. The court concluded in what Kelliher describes as "harsh language" that FERC in that case had "eliminated the very thing that the statute was designed to protect — the ability of the utility owner to 'set the rates it will charge prospective customers, and change them at will,' subject to review by the Commission."

  ## Broader Frustration and Its Proper Forum

  OPSI has expressed frustration that retail regulators cannot control wholesale electricity costs that flow through to retail consumers. Kelliher argues that grievance is properly directed at the Commerce Clause, the Supremacy Clause, and Supreme Court precedent spanning 100 years — including the filed rate doctrine — rather than at the PJM tariff or operating agreement.

  ## What Is at Stake

  Kelliher argues that implementing the OPSI recommendations would strip PJM and its public utility transmission owner members of their ability to "set the rates it will charge" and "change them at will," requiring them instead to file Section 205 tariff changes they actively oppose and believe to be unjust and unreasonable.

  Some parties in the proceeding have advanced proposals that go further than OPSI, seeking to grant states direct Section 205 filing rights rather than the ability to require PJM or its transmission owners to make filings on their behalf. The FERC proceeding on PJM governance, in which these proposals have been filed, remains open.
body_zh: |
  前聯邦能源管理委員會（FERC）主席約瑟夫．凱利赫（Joseph Kelliher）日前發表分析報告，主張目前FERC審理中的PJM互聯治理程序所涉及的若干提案——要求PJM及其輸電業者依各州指示提出《聯邦電力法》第205條費率申報——與《聯邦電力法》相牴觸。凱利赫的結論根植於1984年一項聯邦法院裁決，他強調該判決至今未失其效力。

  凱利赫現為凱利赫顧問公司（Joseph Kelliher Consulting）負責人，曾任NextEra Energy聯邦法規事務執行副總裁。他發表這份分析，係針對PJM州代表組織（Organization of PJM States，OPSI）所提建議作出回應。OPSI是代表PJM轄區各州公用事業監管機構的組織。

  ## OPSI的提案內容

  OPSI在PJM治理程序的意見書中建議，PJM——作為區域輸電組織——以及PJM公共事業輸電業者，均應依OPSI的要求，依《聯邦電力法》第205條向FERC提出費率申報。OPSI提出的說法是：「PJM的治理架構與利害關係人程序，是為不同時代所設計的。」

  ## 第205條與第206條的區別

  《聯邦電力法》為改變公用事業費率建立了兩套獨立程序，凱利赫將這一架構歸因於時任法官史蒂芬．布雷爾（Stephen Breyer）在《麻薩諸塞州公用事業局訴FERC》案（*Massachusetts Dept. of Public Utilities v. FERC*）中的論理。第205條允許公用事業——且僅限公用事業本身——就費率變更提出申報，供FERC審查。第206條則授權FERC得依職權或依投訴修改現行費率，但前提是由委員會或投訴方舉證，證明現行做法不公正、不合理，或存在不當歧視或偏袒。

  OPSI並非公用事業，因此無法直接適用第205條。其提案試圖透過強制公用事業代為申報的方式，繞過這一限制。

  ## 1984年的判例

  在《麻薩諸塞州公用事業局訴FERC》案中，麻薩諸塞州公用事業局（Massachusetts DPU）命令西麻薩諸塞州電力公司（Western Massachusetts Electric Co.）——一家受FERC管轄的公用事業——依第205條提出申報，以變更該州認為有問題的費率制定慣例。該公用事業照辦，然而FERC拒絕受理，認定第205條「規範的是公用事業自身提出的費率變更，而非州監管機構要求其提出的申報」。布雷爾法官對FERC立場的概括如下：「若麻薩諸塞州對此規則有異議，FERC表示，應依第206條提出投訴。」法院贊同此立場。

  法院進一步指出，若接受麻薩諸塞州的論點，則等同允許該州做「連FERC本身也無法做到的事，即變更FERC尚未認定為不合理的州際費率慣例」。凱利赫指出，OPSI建議所追求的，正是這樣的結果。

  ## 為何同意或FERC命令都無法解決問題

  凱利赫主張，即便PJM及其輸電業者自願同意與各州共享第205條申報權，FERC依據《麻薩諸塞州公用事業局》判例，仍應拒絕受理相關申報。此外，若有當事方要求FERC強制推行此種共享安排，亦將因兩項獨立理由而告失敗：其一，法院已一再裁定FERC無權強制公用事業提出第205條申報；其二，FERC不得向各州授予超出委員會本身所擁有的權力。凱利赫表示，FERC無權將其任何《聯邦電力法》職權授予各州行使。

  ## 第206條是現行可用的機制

  OPSI在其意見書中超過20次抱怨，稱其缺乏「正式途徑，以FERC可接受的形式將州政府提案呈交委員會審查」。凱利赫對此提出異議：第206條恰恰提供了這一機制。關鍵差異在於，第206條將州監管機構定位為投訴方，並由其承擔舉證責任，須證明現行做法不公正或不合理——而OPSI的建議正是為了規避這一舉證負擔。

  「我理解各州對被歸類為投訴方感到不滿，」凱利赫寫道，「但依據《聯邦電力法》，在費率變更事項上，這是它們唯一的身份定位。」

  ## 大西洋城電力公司案的爭議

  OPSI援引《大西洋城電力公司》（*Atlantic City Electric*）案判決支持其立場，引用其中「公用事業可選擇透過合約，自願放棄其部分第205條費率申報自由」的表述。凱利赫從兩個角度對此解讀提出質疑。

  其一，《大西洋城電力公司》案所涉及的第205條申報權共享，係PJM與其公用事業成員之間的權利分配問題——而非公用事業與州監管機構之間的問題——且背景為一家公用事業代管其他業者資產的情形。其二，凱利赫主張，上述引文僅係指公用事業在固定費率合約期間放棄單方面調整費率的權利，而非與州投訴方共享申報權。

  凱利赫指出，《大西洋城電力公司》案的核心裁決，恰恰與OPSI的立場相悖：「依第205條啟動費率變更的權力，歸屬於公用事業，FERC不得加以侵占」——此原則無論FERC是替自身還是替州投訴方挪用該權力，均同樣適用。法院以凱利赫所形容的「嚴厲措辭」裁定，FERC在該案中「消除了本法條所欲保護的核心——公用事業業主有權自行『制定向潛在客戶收取的費率，並可隨意調整』，並接受委員會審查的能力」。

  ## 更深層的不滿及其應循途徑

  OPSI表達了零售監管機構在管控躉售電力成本方面的挫折感——這些成本最終流轉至零售消費者身上。凱利赫認為，這一申訴應指向「商業條款」（Commerce Clause）、「最高法條款」（Supremacy Clause）以及最高法院逾百年的判例（包括備案費率原則），而非指向PJM費率表或營運協議。

  ## 爭議的根本所在

  凱利赫主張，若落實OPSI的建議，將剝奪PJM及其公用事業輸電業者成員「自行制定費率」及「隨意調整費率」的能力，取而代之的是迫使他們提出自身所反對、且認為不公正、不合理的第205條費率申報。

  本次程序中，部分當事方提出了比OPSI更為激進的方案，主張直接賦予各州第205條申報權，而非僅要求PJM或其輸電業者代各州提出申報。此一FERC PJM治理程序目前仍在進行中。
