  ## Overwhelming opposition to Western Downs Digital Park

  A data centre project in regional Queensland listing Anthropic as its anchor tenant received 595 objections out of 598 public submissions, with just two in support and one neutral, according to a regulatory filing published by developer Zerra DC.

  The Western Downs Digital Park, located approximately 250 kilometres west of Brisbane on farmland, is planned at a computing load of 1.44 gigawatts — a measure of the continuous electrical power draw that would make it one of the largest data centres in Australia. The filing, lodged by Zerra DC, acknowledged that "a significant number of submissions raise reasonable matters regarding water, power, noise, roads, housing and the scale of the project."

  About one-third of the nearly 600 submissions came from people living within 200 kilometres of the site. Objectors most frequently cited concerns about water consumption, electricity demand and its effect on prices, noise, road damage from construction traffic, and pressure on local housing supply.

  Anthropoc declined to comment. A Zerra spokesperson said the company took the submissions seriously, adding that they "reinforce that people want a greater say in how the project develops." The developer said it was expanding community engagement and planned to report changes demanded by the public.

  ## Timing tied to Anthropic name

  The volume of objections accelerated sharply after Anthropic was publicly named as the project's tenant. Before September 15, when the developer disclosed Anthropic's involvement, the project had received five objections over the course of a month, according to a log of public comments. The remaining 590 or more submissions arrived between that date and November 11, when the local government closed the submission window.

  Public submissions in Australian planning processes are not binding on decision-makers, but they form part of the formal record and can influence conditions attached to any approval or trigger further review.

  ## Broader pattern in Australia

  The opposition at Western Downs follows similar disputes elsewhere in Australia. Goodman Group scrapped a data centre planned for Sydney worth approximately A$1.2 billion (around S$1.03 billion) in September after a public backlash. NextDC delayed a project in Melbourne amid resident concerns about energy use, a development that triggered a sell-down of the company's shares.

  The filing described the response as reflecting a broader "souring relationship between communities and the so-called AI factories being built at pace around the world" during a construction boom that consultancy McKinsey has valued at US$7 trillion (S$9.03 trillion) by 2030.

  ## IPO context

  The publication of the Australian filing coincided with Anthropic's stock market debut on Wall Street. The company priced its initial public offering on November 18 in a deal that valued it at approximately US$1.5 trillion, according to Reuters.

  ## 西唐斯數位園區遭壓倒性反對

  昆士蘭州偏遠地區一座以Anthropic為主力租戶的資料中心計畫，在598份公眾陳情中收到595份反對意見，僅2份表示支持、1份態度中立——以上數據來自開發商Zerra DC提交的法規文件。

  西唐斯數位園區（Western Downs Digital Park）位於布里斯本西方約250公里的農地上，計畫運算負載為1.44吉瓦，以持續電力需求衡量，建成後將是澳洲規模最大的資料中心之一。Zerra DC在提交的文件中坦承，「大量陳情就水資源、電力、噪音、道路、住房及計畫規模提出合理疑慮。」

  近600份陳情中，約三分之一來自居住在基地200公里範圍內的民眾。反對者最常提及的顧慮包括：用水量、電力需求及其對電價的衝擊、噪音、施工車輛對道路的損害，以及對當地住房供給的壓力。

  Anthropic拒絕回應媒體詢問。Zerra發言人表示，公司認真看待各項陳情，並補充說這些意見「再次印證民眾希望對計畫的發展有更多發言權」。該開發商表示，將擴大社區溝通，並計畫公布因應公眾要求所做的修改。

  ## 反對聲浪因Anthropic曝光而驟增

  反對陳情的數量，在Anthropic身份公開後急遽攀升。根據公眾意見記錄，9月15日之前——即開發商披露Anthropic參與計畫之前——整整一個月僅收到5份反對意見。其餘590份以上的陳情，則在該日期至11月11日（地方政府截止收件日）之間接連湧入。

  在澳洲規劃程序中，公眾陳情對決策者並無約束力，但會納入正式紀錄，並可能影響核准所附帶的條件，或觸發進一步審查。

  ## 澳洲各地相似爭議

  西唐斯的反對聲浪，呼應了澳洲其他地方的類似紛爭。商業地產集團Goodman Group於9月放棄了一座原本計畫在雪梨興建、價值約12億澳幣（約10.3億新加坡幣）的資料中心，起因正是民眾的強烈反彈。NextDC則有一個墨爾本計畫因居民對能耗的疑慮而延宕，此一發展亦引發該公司股價遭到拋售。

  提交的文件將這波民意反彈形容為，反映出全球「社區與所謂人工智慧工廠之間日趨惡化的關係」——這波建設熱潮正在世界各地急速推進，諮詢公司麥肯錫估計，相關投資規模到2030年將達7兆美元（約9.03兆新加坡幣）。

  ## IPO背景

  澳洲這份文件的公開，恰與Anthropic在華爾街掛牌上市的時間相互輝映。據路透社報導，該公司於11月18日完成首次公開募股（IPO）定價，本次交易對其估值約為1.5兆美元。
