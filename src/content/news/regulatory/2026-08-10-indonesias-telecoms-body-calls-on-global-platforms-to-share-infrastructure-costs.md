  ## Indonesia's telecom lobby calls on global platforms to share digital infrastructure costs

  Indonesia's leading telecommunications trade group formally called on the government to develop policies requiring global digital platforms to contribute to national infrastructure investment, arguing that domestic telecoms bear a disproportionate share of network costs while foreign over-the-top (OTT) services — companies that deliver content or applications over existing telecom networks without owning that infrastructure — capture the economic benefits.

  Sarwoto Atmosutarno, chairman of MASTEL (Masyarakat Telematika Indonesia, the Indonesian Telematics Society), made the call on August 6, 2026, at the Digital Transformation Indonesia Conference and Expo (DTI-CX) 2026. The event was attended by Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs, the head of the National Cyber and Encryption Agency (BSSN, Badan Siber dan Sandi Negara), and the Director General of Digital Ecosystems at the Ministry of Communication and Digital.

  "We have mostly seen the benefits of the presence of global digital platforms, from digital economic growth, efficiency, to various service innovations," Sarwoto said. "But at the same time, digital infrastructure investment needs continue to increase and most are still borne by the telecommunications industry."

  ## Shared responsibility for infrastructure

  MASTEL argued that digital infrastructure development can no longer be assigned to a single sector. Sarwoto said global digital platforms have benefited from networks built and maintained by national providers, and should therefore help sustain their continued development. As connectivity demand grows, the group said, contributions from all participants in the digital ecosystem must become proportional to ensure network expansion can continue.

  ## Fair share framework

  MASTEL expressed support for a "fair share" model — a mechanism under which global platforms would make financial contributions toward infrastructure maintained by national providers. Sarwoto endorsed positions attributed to Indonesia's Minister of Communication and Digital, who at the DEAL 2026 event on June 23, 2026, emphasized the importance of retaining digital economic value within Indonesia, including through a fair share arrangement between global platforms and domestic infrastructure providers.

  Sarwoto described the fair share concept as "a strategic step to ensure digital economic growth goes hand in hand with strengthening national capacity."

  ## Political alignment

  MASTEL said support for reform of digital platform governance has grown across both the executive and legislative branches. According to Sarwoto, several ministries and agencies now share a common position on the need for such a policy, including the Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs, the Ministry of Finance, and the Ministry of National Development Planning (Bappenas).

  In the legislature, Sarwoto said Commissions I, VI, and XI of the House of Representatives (DPR RI) have aligned with the position. He framed this convergence as an opening for the Ministry of Communication and Digital to act.

  "This is the right momentum for the Ministry of Communication and Digital to present policies that encourage global digital platform contributions through fair collaboration with the telecommunications industry," Sarwoto said. "That way, national digital infrastructure development can proceed sustainably and provide greater benefits for Indonesia."

  ## What comes next

  The call represents a formal escalation of the infrastructure cost-sharing argument by Indonesia's telecoms sector, backed by stated alignment across multiple government ministries and parliamentary committees. MASTEL identified the Ministry of Communication and Digital as the body responsible for translating that alignment into concrete policy, with Sarwoto describing the current political environment as a timely moment to act.

  ## 印尼電信業公會呼籲全球平台共同承擔數位基礎建設成本

  印尼最具代表性的電信業公會正式呼籲政府制定政策，要求全球數位平台參與國家基礎建設投資。該公會指出，本土電信業者承擔了不成比例的大部分網路建設成本，而境外的OTT（Over-The-Top）業者——即透過現有電信網路提供內容或應用服務、但本身並不擁有該基礎建設的業者——卻坐享其經濟利益。

  MASTEL（Masyarakat Telematika Indonesia，印尼電信暨資訊協會）主席薩爾沃托·阿特莫蘇塔諾（Sarwoto Atmosutarno）於2026年8月6日在「2026年印尼數位轉型大會暨博覽會」（DTI-CX 2026）上提出上述呼籲。出席本次活動的貴賓包括印尼統籌經濟部部長、國家網路與密碼總署（BSSN，Badan Siber dan Sandi Negara）署長，以及通訊與數位部數位生態系統司司長。

  薩爾沃托說：「長期以來，我們大多看到全球數位平台帶來的好處，從數位經濟成長、效率提升，到各式各樣的服務創新。但與此同時，數位基礎建設的投資需求持續攀升，而大部分費用仍由電信業者獨力承擔。」

  ## 基礎建設共同責任

  MASTEL主張，數位基礎建設的發展不能再由單一產業獨自承擔。薩爾沃托指出，全球數位平台長期受惠於本土業者所建置與維護的網路，理應協助支持其持續發展。該公會表示，隨著連網需求不斷成長，數位生態系中所有參與者的貢獻都必須與其所獲利益相稱，如此才能確保網路擴建工作得以延續。

  ## 公平分擔機制

  MASTEL表態支持「公平分擔」（fair share）模式——這是一種讓全球平台對本土業者所維護的基礎建設提供財務貢獻的機制。薩爾沃托認同印尼通訊與數位部部長的立場，後者曾於2026年6月23日的DEAL 2026活動上強調，印尼必須留住數位經濟價值，途徑之一便是在全球平台與本土基礎建設提供者之間建立公平分擔機制。

  薩爾沃托將公平分擔概念形容為「確保數位經濟成長與強化國家能量並行不悖的戰略步驟」。

  ## 政治力量匯聚

  MASTEL表示，行政與立法兩端對數位平台治理改革的支持均持續升溫。據薩爾沃托指出，統籌經濟部、財政部及國家發展規劃部（Bappenas）等多個部會與機關，目前已就制定相關政策的必要性達成共識。

  在立法機關方面，薩爾沃托表示印尼眾議院（DPR RI）第一、第六及第十一委員會均已與上述立場接軌。他將這股政治力量的匯聚，視為通訊與數位部採取行動的有利契機。

  薩爾沃托說：「現在正是通訊與數位部提出政策的最佳時機，透過與電信業的公平合作，促使全球數位平台做出貢獻。如此一來，國家數位基礎建設的發展才能永續推進，為印尼帶來更大的利益。」

  ## 後續展望

  這次呼籲代表印尼電信產業在基礎建設費用分擔議題上正式升級施壓，並獲得多個政府部會及國會委員會公開表態支持。MASTEL明確點名通訊與數位部，要求其將這股政治共識轉化為具體政策；薩爾沃托更表示，當前的政治環境正是採取行動的最佳時機。
