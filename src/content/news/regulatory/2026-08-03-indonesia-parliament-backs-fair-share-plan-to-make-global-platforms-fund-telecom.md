body_en: |
  Indonesia's House of Representatives Committee on Communications and Digital Affairs formally endorsed the government's plan to require global digital platforms to share the cost of national telecommunications infrastructure on July 29, 2026, adding legislative backing to a policy drive the Ministry of Communication and Digital has been advancing since at least June.

  Sukamta, deputy chairman of Commission I (Komisi I) of the House of Representatives (DPR RI) — the committee with oversight of the digital sector — announced the endorsement in a statement, saying the panel supports the Ministry of Communication and Digital (Kementerian Komunikasi dan Digital, or Komdigi) "in implementing a fair share scheme between global digital platforms and national telecommunications providers to realize equitable national digital infrastructure as the foundation for digital economic sovereignty."

  ## Why the scheme is being pursued

  The stated rationale is an asymmetry in how infrastructure costs and economic benefits are distributed. National telecommunications operators carry the full capital investment burden of building and maintaining Indonesia's networks, while global digital platform companies derive substantial economic benefit from that infrastructure without contributing to its costs, according to Sukamta.

  He described fair share as a mechanism to "create a more balanced distribution of contributions within the national digital ecosystem" and said it is necessary to strengthen the sustainability of digital infrastructure investment in Indonesia.

  ## The government's position

  Communications and Digital Minister Meutya Hafid set out the policy logic at the DEAL 2026 forum in Jakarta on June 23, 2026. She argued that Indonesia's digital economy figures represent real national strength only if the value they represent can be retained domestically.

  "Large digital economy figures only become a strength if we can retain value domestically. This is what we are trying to do — demanding a fairer share of the digital economic value we create between global platforms and national digital infrastructure. There must be a fairer distribution or fair share with global companies," Meutya said.

  The minister also said that strengthening digital infrastructure is the primary foundation for spreading the economic benefits of digitalization more widely across society and for improving national competitiveness.

  ## Connectivity gaps as a policy target

  Sukamta said the committee expects a fair share arrangement to accelerate infrastructure deployment in regions that remain underserved. He cited "3T" areas — the government's designation for regions classified as underdeveloped (tertinggal), frontier (terdepan), or outermost (terluar) — as well as blank-spot locations in peri-urban and rural areas that lack coverage despite their proximity to city centers.

  He called for greater government-private sector cooperation as "one of the keys to expanding more inclusive digital access," and also emphasized improving digital literacy as a condition for ensuring that network expansion delivers its full benefit to the population.

  ## What comes next

  Formal backing from Commission I marks a significant step in the policy process: Komdigi now has explicit parliamentary support for applying fair share obligations to global platforms operating in Indonesia. The government has framed the scheme as the financial foundation for a broader goal of digital economic sovereignty — retaining domestically the value generated by Indonesia's digital economy rather than allowing it to flow to global platform companies.

  Meutya said the government intends digital infrastructure development to be a shared responsibility, with global platforms that benefit from Indonesia's digital ecosystem making contributions proportional to those benefits, rather than leaving network investment costs to national operators alone.
body_zh: |
  印尼眾議院通訊與數位事務委員會於2026年7月29日正式背書政府方案，要求全球數位平台分擔國家電信基礎設施成本，為通訊與數位部（Kementerian Komunikasi dan Digital，簡稱Komdigi）至少自今年6月起積極推動的政策增添了立法支持。

  眾議院第一委員會（DPR RI Komisi I）副主席蘇坎塔（Sukamta）發表聲明，宣布上述背書立場，表示委員會支持通訊與數位部「在全球數位平台與國家電信業者之間落實公平分擔機制，以實現公平的國家數位基礎設施，奠定數位經濟主權的根基。」

  ## 推動此方案的緣由

  方案的立論核心在於基礎設施成本與經濟利益的分配嚴重不對稱。根據蘇坎塔的說法，國內電信業者承擔了建設與維護印尼網路的全部資本投資負擔，而全球數位平台公司卻藉助這套基礎設施獲取可觀的經濟利益，卻不需分擔任何成本。

  他將公平分擔形容為「在國家數位生態系中創造更均衡貢獻分配」的機制，並表示這對強化印尼數位基礎設施投資的永續性不可或缺。

  ## 政府立場

  通訊與數位部長穆蒂亞·哈菲德（Meutya Hafid）於2026年6月23日在雅加達舉辦的DEAL 2026論壇上，詳細闡述了此政策背後的邏輯。她主張，唯有印尼數位經濟所創造的價值能夠留存於國內，亮眼的數位經濟數字才能真正轉化為國家實力。

  「數位經濟的數字要能成為真正的優勢，前提是我們能夠將這些價值留在國內。這正是我們努力的方向——要求全球平台與國家數位基礎設施之間，對我們所創造的數位經濟價值進行更公平的分配。我們與全球公司之間必須有更公平的分配，也就是公平分擔，」穆蒂亞說。

  部長也表示，強化數位基礎設施是讓數位化的經濟利益更廣泛惠及社會各階層、進而提升國家競爭力的首要根基。

  ## 縮短數位落差作為政策目標

  蘇坎塔表示，委員會期待公平分擔安排能加速在仍未獲服務的偏遠地區推展基礎設施建設。他特別提及政府所稱的「3T地區」——即被歸類為落後（tertinggal）、邊境（terdepan）或外島（terluar）的地區——以及儘管鄰近城市中心、卻仍存在覆蓋缺口的近郊與農村空白地帶。

  他呼籲深化政府與民間的合作，認為這是「擴大更具包容性數位近用的關鍵之一」，並強調提升數位素養是確保網路擴展能讓全體民眾充分受益的必要條件。

  ## 後續動向

  第一委員會的正式背書是政策推進過程中的重要里程碑：Komdigi如今獲得國會明確支持，得以對在印尼營運的全球平台適用公平分擔義務。政府將此方案定位為實現更宏觀目標——數位經濟主權——的財務基礎，核心精神在於讓印尼數位經濟所創造的價值留存於國內，而非持續流向全球平台公司。

  穆蒂亞表示，政府希望數位基礎設施的發展成為各方共同承擔的責任，在印尼數位生態系中獲益的全球平台，應按其所享利益的比例貢獻心力，而非讓網路投資成本全數落在國內業者身上。
