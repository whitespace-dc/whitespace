body_en: |
  Imperial Valley Computer Manufacturing (IVCM) has filed suit against the Imperial Irrigation District (IID) after the utility denied the company's request for roughly 260 million gallons of Colorado River water per year — approximately 750,000 gallons per day — to cool a planned 330-megawatt artificial intelligence data center in Imperial County, California. The case raises questions that extend well beyond a single project: whether agricultural water rights can be lawfully, physically, and environmentally redirected to industry under existing service rules and regional water-rights frameworks.

  IID rejected the request on May 1. IVCM had argued that its plan to lease and retire 160 acres of farmland that historically consumed roughly the same volume of water should entitle the proposed 950,000-square-foot campus to that conserved supply.

  ## The agricultural-to-industrial water transfer argument

  IVCM's case rests on the premise that retiring irrigated farmland frees up an equivalent volume of Colorado River water for a new industrial use on the same acreage. The company contends it has secured that farmland and that the water should follow the land out of agricultural production and into data center cooling.

  IID has rejected that framing, at least under its current policies. The utility's May 1 denial sets up a legal fight over whether the conservation of agricultural water through land retirement automatically creates a transferable industrial water supply, or whether additional legal and physical steps are required.

  ## What the Lithium Valley water plan assumes

  The lawsuit lands as Imperial County advances its proposed Lithium Valley Specific Plan, a regional development framework intended to attract lithium processing, advanced manufacturing, logistics, and data center operators to the area. A newly released Water Supply Assessment (WSA) accompanying the plan concludes that the region can support decades of new industrial development by shifting Colorado River water from retired farmland to industrial uses rather than by identifying a new water source.

  Today, roughly 149,000 acre-feet of Colorado River water — one acre-foot equals about 326,000 gallons, enough to serve roughly two average U.S. households for a year — is delivered annually to agriculture within the Lithium Valley planning area, according to the WSA. The assessment projects that industrial development would require about 63,243 acre-feet per year after 20 years and 77,776 acre-feet per year after 30 years, remaining below current agricultural demand if sufficient farmland is retired in the interim.

  Under the proposed Water Phasing Policy described in the WSA, developers relying on reduced agricultural demand would have to demonstrate that enough farmland has been retired to offset a project's water use, or provide an alternative supply. The WSA also assumes industrial facilities will recycle increasing shares of water over time, starting at roughly 20% and rising to 80% by 2055.

  ## The gap between county projections and IID's current policy

  The WSA explicitly acknowledges that the county's long-range industrial vision cannot be achieved under IID's existing Interim Water Supply Policy. That policy currently allocates 18,620 acre-feet per year for new non-agricultural water users. The county projects that more than three times that volume will be needed within two decades, and the WSA concludes that changes to IID policy would be required to accommodate full buildout of the Lithium Valley plan.

  That gap between what county planners envision and what the utility's current rules permit is the structural tension underlying the IVCM lawsuit.

  ## Utilities face coupled infrastructure obligations

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, said the case illustrates a challenge utilities increasingly face as large AI campuses arrive with simultaneous demands on multiple critical resources.

  "A data center is not a 330 MW electric load with a separate water issue," Osnato told Data Center Knowledge. "It is one coupled infrastructure obligation, and utilities will get into trouble when they evaluate those dependencies in isolation."

  Osnato said utilities must evaluate electricity, water, wastewater, and other infrastructure together rather than as separate approval tracks. He added that retiring farmland may reduce water consumption but does not automatically establish that the conserved water can be legally, physically, and environmentally redirected to support a large industrial project — the precise question at the center of the IVCM suit.

  When county-level long-range plans and utility-level service rules are not aligned, Osnato noted, the result can be litigation, development delays, or public opposition.

  ## What the case means for Lithium Valley development

  The proposed IVCM campus would require about 880 acre-feet of water annually, a small fraction of the planning area's projected industrial demand over the coming decades. But the lawsuit could function as an early-stage test of whether Colorado River water can be redirected from agriculture to support the broader industrial buildout envisioned for Lithium Valley.

  If IID ultimately declines to authorize such transfers — whether under its current policies or revised ones — that position could shape future proposals from lithium processing plants, advanced manufacturing facilities, and AI data centers across the region, each of which the county's plan assumes will rely on the same farmland-retirement mechanism.

  To support the infrastructure required for that long-term growth, Imperial County's Lithium Valley plan proposes constructing a regional backbone water conveyance system, 12 water storage facilities, and a special district to build and manage the new infrastructure.
body_zh: |
  帝國谷電腦製造公司（Imperial Valley Computer Manufacturing，IVCM）已就帝國灌溉區（Imperial Irrigation District，IID）拒絕其用水申請一事提起訴訟。IID拒絕為IVCM規劃在加州帝國縣興建的一座330MW人工智慧資料中心，每年供應約2.6億加侖科羅拉多河水——即每日約75萬加侖——作為冷卻之用。本案所引發的問題遠超單一個案的範疇：在現行服務規定及區域水權架構下，農業用水權是否可合法、實體且符合環境要求地轉供工業用途？

  IID於5月1日駁回這項申請。IVCM原主張，其計畫租用並退耕160英畝、歷史上耗水量大致相當的農地，此舉應使計畫中占地95萬平方英尺的園區有資格取得這批節省下來的水源。

  ## 農業轉工業用水的法律主張

  ICVM的訴訟立場建立在一項前提上：退出耕作的灌溉農地，可釋出等量的科羅拉多河水，供同一地塊上的新工業用途使用。該公司主張，其已取得該農地的租用與退耕權利，相關用水應隨土地從農業生產轉入資料中心冷卻系統。

  IID則至少在現行政策下拒絕接受這套說法。該電力公用事業局5月1日的否決決定，使雙方在法律層面正面交鋒——退耕農地所節省的農業用水，是否自動形成可移轉的工業用水供給？抑或需要額外的法律程序與實體配套措施？

  ## 鋰谷用水計畫的基本假設

  本案提起之際，帝國縣正積極推動「鋰谷特定計畫」（Lithium Valley Specific Plan）——一套旨在吸引鋰礦加工、先進製造、物流及資料中心業者進駐的區域開發框架。隨之新公布的《水源供給評估報告》（Water Supply Assessment，WSA）指出，該地區可藉由將科羅拉多河水從退耕農地轉供工業，而非另尋新水源，來支撐數十年的工業開發需求。

  根據WSA，鋰谷規劃區內目前每年約有14萬9,000英畝英尺的科羅拉多河水輸送至農業用途——1英畝英尺約等於32萬6,000加侖，足夠供應美國約兩戶普通家庭使用一年。評估報告預測，工業開發在20年後每年需耗水約6萬3,243英畝英尺，30年後則增至7萬7,776英畝英尺，只要在此期間有足夠農地退耕，仍低於目前的農業用水總量。

  WSA所描述的「用水分期政策」（Water Phasing Policy）草案規定，仰賴農業需求降低的開發商，必須證明已有足夠農地退耕以抵消其用水量，或提出替代水源。WSA亦預設工業設施將逐步提高循環用水比例，初期約達20%，至2055年時將升至80%。

  ## 縣府長期規劃與IID現行政策之間的落差

  WSA明確指出，縣府的長期工業願景在IID現行「臨時供水政策」（Interim Water Supply Policy）下根本無法實現。該政策目前僅為新增非農業用水戶每年分配1萬8,620英畝英尺。縣府預計二十年內所需用水量將超過這一數字的三倍，WSA亦結論認為，鋰谷計畫若要全面建置，IID政策勢必需要調整。

  縣府規劃者的願景與電力公用事業現行規定之間的這道鴻溝，正是IVCM訴訟案背後的結構性矛盾所在。

  ## 公用事業面臨基礎設施整合挑戰

  Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾·奧斯納托（Neil Osnato）表示，本案正說明了公用事業在大型AI園區同時對多種關鍵資源提出需求時，所日益面臨的挑戰。

  「一座資料中心不是一個330MW的電力負載加上一個獨立的供水問題，」奧斯納托告訴《Data Center Knowledge》，「它是一整套相互耦合的基礎設施義務，公用事業若將這些依賴關係割裂評估，遲早會出問題。」

  奧斯納托指出，公用事業必須將電力、供水、廢水及其他基礎設施統合考量，而非分開審核。他並補充，農地退耕或許可以減少用水消耗，但並不自動確立所節省的水資源可在法律、實體及環境層面合法轉供大型工業項目使用——而這正是IVCM訴訟案的核心爭點。

  奧斯納托亦指出，當縣級長期規劃與公用事業服務規定未能接軌時，往往導致訴訟纏身、開發延宕或公眾反對。

  ## 本案對鋰谷開發的意義

  ICVM擬建園區每年約需880英畝英尺用水，僅占規劃區域未來數十年工業需水預測總量的一小部分。然而，本案訴訟或將成為一項早期試驗，檢驗科羅拉多河水能否從農業用途轉供鋰谷更廣泛工業建設所需。

  若IID最終——無論依據現行政策或修訂後的政策——拒絕授權此類轉讓，其立場將對整個地區鋰礦加工廠、先進製造設施及AI資料中心的後續申請方案產生深遠影響，因為縣府計畫假設上述各類設施均將仰賴同一套農地退耕供水機制。

  為支撐長期發展所需的基礎設施，帝國縣鋰谷計畫擬興建一套區域骨幹輸水系統、12座蓄水設施，並設立一個專責特別行政區來建設和管理這些新基礎設施。
