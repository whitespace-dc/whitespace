body_en: |
  Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed an executive order establishing what has been described as the strictest data center regulations in the United States, requiring facility operators to secure community support, self-supply their electricity, and progressively increase clean energy use — a shift analysts say could restructure the economics of artificial intelligence infrastructure nationwide.

  The order bars data center developers from using non-disclosure agreements in community dealings and requires them to sign legally binding consent orders committing to grid and community standards before projects can proceed. Most consequentially, operators may no longer transfer their large electricity loads to the public grid; they must arrange their own power supply, bear the associated costs, and raise the share of clean energy in that supply over time. Additional requirements cover transparency, local hiring, and community benefit provisions.

  ## A widening regulatory front

  Pennsylvania is not acting in isolation. New York State has already imposed a moratorium on new large-scale data center construction. In Texas, Governor Greg Abbott has ordered audits of all data center projects seeking grid interconnection — a measure that Jefferies analysts described as a potential "chilling signal" for the power industry. The cluster of state-level actions reflects a backlash driven by data centers' heavy electricity consumption, which residents and local officials in multiple states attribute to rising power prices.

  According to Commercial Times, citing Wall Street financial reporting, Wall Street analysts have begun incorporating community resistance to data centers into market forecasting models. Bank of America strategists noted that if Republicans maintain their position after the November midterm elections, markets could receive a positive stimulus. Conversely, if Democrats capture the Senate and win the Texas governor's race, uncertainty over AI-related policy could intensify — with analysts suggesting the pressure could contribute to a decline of more than 10 percent in U.S. equities the following year. AI infrastructure, the analysis found, has become directly tied to ballot outcomes, energy prices, and local residents' interests, not only to corporate capital expenditure.

  Henrietta Treyz, co-founder of investment advisory and consulting firm Veda Partners, said that leading enterprises in the AI sector will face significant risks as a result of the regulatory trend.

  ## Who benefits as self-supply becomes the standard

  Pennsylvania's self-supply requirement — and the broader "bring your own generation," or BYOG, direction that regulators in multiple states appear to be moving toward — changes which companies stand to gain from data center growth.

  Morgan Stanley has analyzed that as pressure on data centers to self-supply electricity increases, companies with distributed power generation capabilities, such as GE Vernova and Bloom Energy, may benefit. Utilities that had expected to grow revenue by serving large data center electricity loads may instead face uncertainty, as that demand no longer necessarily flows through the public grid.

  Previously, rapid data center construction drove demand for power generation equipment, grid infrastructure, transformers, and related hardware across the traditional utility sector. The BYOG model redirects that spending toward distributed generation providers.

  ## Implications for the AI supply chain

  For companies building and supplying AI infrastructure, the new regulatory environment means the cost structure of data center expansion is changing. Beyond graphics processing units — the chips that run AI workloads — AI servers, networking equipment, and advanced chip packaging, the ability to deliver stable and cost-effective on-site power is becoming a prerequisite for project viability, according to the Commercial Times analysis.

  Pennsylvania's order represents the most detailed articulation yet of what states may require before approving new large-scale AI infrastructure, and Texas's ongoing grid interconnection audit is expected to set a further precedent for how states manage AI infrastructure demand against the stability of public electricity systems.
body_zh: |
  賓州州長喬希・夏皮羅（Josh Shapiro）日前簽署行政命令，建立起被各界分析師形容為全美最嚴格的資料中心監管框架，要求業者在正式動工前取得社區支持、自行籌備電力供應，並逐步提高使用清潔能源的比例。分析師指出，這項政策轉向可能重塑全美人工智慧基礎建設的整體經濟結構。

  根據該行政命令，資料中心開發商在與社區往來時，不得再援引保密協議（NDA）封鎖資訊，且必須在動工前簽署具法律效力的同意書，承諾遵守電網與社區標準。其中影響最深遠的一條規定是：業者不得再將龐大的用電需求轉嫁給公共電網，必須自行安排電力來源、自行承擔相關費用，並隨時間推移持續提高自備電力中清潔能源的占比。此外，命令也對資訊透明度、在地僱用以及社區利益條款訂定了相應要求。

  ## 監管壓力正在多個州蔓延

  賓州此舉並非孤例。紐約州已對新建大型資料中心實施暫停令；德州州長格雷格・阿博特（Greg Abbott）則下令對所有申請接入電網的資料中心專案展開審計——傑富瑞（Jefferies）分析師將此舉形容為對電力產業可能釋出的「寒蟬效應」信號。這一連串州層級的行動，反映出各界對資料中心高耗電問題的強力反彈：多州居民與地方官員紛紛將電費上漲的矛頭，直指資料中心猛爆式的用電需求。

  工商時報援引華爾街財務報告指出，華爾街分析師已開始將社區對資料中心的抵制情緒納入市場預測模型。美國銀行策略師指出，若共和黨在11月期中選舉後維持現有席次，市場可能受到正面刺激；反之，若民主黨拿下參議院並贏得德州州長選舉，AI相關政策的不確定性恐將加劇——分析師認為，這股壓力可能導致美國股市在翌年重挫逾10%。該分析的結論是，AI基礎建設已不再只是企業資本支出的問題，而是與選舉結果、能源價格及在地居民的切身利益緊密相連。

  投資諮詢與顧問公司Veda Partners共同創辦人亨麗埃塔・特雷茲（Henrietta Treyz）表示，受此波監管趨勢影響，AI產業的龍頭企業將面臨重大風險。

  ## 自供電成為主流後　誰將是最大受益者

  賓州的自供電要求，以及多州監管機構似乎正共同朝向的「自帶電源」（Bring Your Own Generation，BYOG）方向，將改變資料中心成長浪潮中的利益分配格局。

  摩根士丹利（Morgan Stanley）分析指出，隨著資料中心被迫自行供電的壓力升高，具備分散式電力生產能力的企業——例如GE Vernova與Bloom Energy——可能因此受惠。過去預期靠服務大型資料中心用電需求來擴大營收的傳統電力公司，則可能面臨前景不確定的困境，因為這些電力需求未必會繼續流經公共電網。

  過去，資料中心的快速興建帶動了傳統電力產業對發電設備、電網基礎設施、變壓器及相關硬體的整體需求；BYOG模式的出現，將把這部分支出重新導向分散式供電業者。

  ## 對AI供應鏈的深遠影響

  對於建設與供應AI基礎建設的企業而言，全新的監管環境意味著資料中心擴建的成本結構正在改變。工商時報分析指出，除了驅動AI運算工作負載的圖形處理器（GPU）之外，AI伺服器、網路設備以及先進晶片封裝，能否穩定且具成本效益地在現地提供充足電力，正逐漸成為專案能否順利推進的先決條件。

  賓州此次行政命令，是迄今各州對於批准大型AI基礎建設所提出條件的最詳盡闡述；而德州正在進行中的電網接入審計，預計將為各州如何在AI基礎建設需求與公共電力系統穩定性之間取得平衡，樹立新的判例。
