body_en: |
  The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit on June 26 denied all challenges to a 2024 Environmental Protection Agency rule that set a stricter national limit on fine particulate matter — commonly called soot — from power plants, vehicle exhaust, oil refineries, and other industrial sources, leaving the standard intact while a separate enforcement suit proceeds in a California federal court.

  ## What the court decided

  The challenge arrived from two directions simultaneously. Republican attorneys general from 24 states and a coalition of industry organizations filed suit in March 2024, arguing the EPA under President Joe Biden had exceeded its statutory authority in promulgating the rule and that it had been impermissibly shaped by "environmental justice" considerations. In November 2025, the EPA under the Trump administration added its own motion, seeking to vacate the rule on the grounds that the Biden-era agency had acted beyond its authority and had failed to consider costs when it initiated the review that led to the revision.

  The court rejected both lines of attack in a single ruling. "Because these arguments lack merit, we deny the petitions for review and the motion for vacatur," the June 26 decision states.

  ## The standard at issue

  The 2024 EPA rule lowered the National Ambient Air Quality Standard — the federally mandated ceiling for pollution levels — for fine particulate matter, particles 2.5 micrometers or smaller in diameter, from 12 micrograms per cubic meter of air to 9 micrograms per cubic meter, with compliance required by 2032. Those particles, abbreviated PM2.5, enter the lungs from vehicle exhaust, power plant emissions, factory output, and similar sources.

  The Clean Air Act directs the EPA to set such air quality standards and to designate areas of the country that fall out of compliance. Under the law, communities that fail to meet a revised standard must develop plans to reduce local pollution. A Harvard Law School Environmental and Energy Law Program brief cited in the case states that when a standard is tightened, "states and local regions must ensure that the sources of pollution in their jurisdiction decrease their emissions, so that the region can meet the new, more stringent national standard."

  ## Enforcement fight continues

  Despite the ruling, the 2024 standard is not currently being implemented. The EPA said in an emailed statement that it is reviewing the decision, leaving open the question of whether the agency will move to enforce the new limit.

  A separate lawsuit filed in April in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California is pressing the agency to act. That case was brought by a coalition of 10 states, the District of Columbia, Harris County, Texas, and New York City, which sued the EPA for failing to put the 2024 standard into effect. That case remains pending.

  ## Significance

  The D.C. Circuit's ruling strips away the legal basis on which the Trump EPA and state challengers sought to undo the standard, foreclosing at least the arguments raised in this case. For power generators, refineries, and other industrial operators, the ruling means the stricter 9-microgram limit retains its legal force, though practical compliance obligations hinge on whether and when the EPA moves to designate non-attainment areas and require state implementation plans.

  Vijay Limaye, a climate and health scientist at the Natural Resources Defense Council, said in a statement that the decision "removes any remaining doubt: the science has long been clear, and now the law is too. The EPA must stop stalling and deliver the clean air the Clean Air Act requires."

  The California district court case, which seeks to compel the EPA to implement the standard, remains the primary pending forum for that question.
body_zh: |
  美國哥倫比亞特區聯邦巡迴上訴法院於6月26日駁回所有針對美國環境保護署（EPA）2024年法規的挑戰。該法規為發電廠、汽車廢氣、煉油廠及其他工業排放源訂定了更嚴格的細懸浮微粒（俗稱「煤灰」或「細粉塵」）全國濃度上限，現行標準因此維持原狀，而另一起要求強制執行的訴訟則繼續在加州聯邦法院進行。

  ## 法院裁決內容

  這次挑戰同時從兩個方向發起。來自24個州的共和黨籍檢察長，以及一批產業組織組成的聯盟，於2024年3月提起訴訟，主張拜登政府時期的EPA在制定該法規時已逾越法定授權，且法規制定過程不當受到「環境正義」考量的影響。2025年11月，川普政府領導下的EPA亦提出自身動議，要求廢止該法規，理由是拜登時期的EPA不僅行動逾越職權，在啟動導致本次修訂的審查程序時，亦未將成本因素納入考量。

  法院以單一裁決駁回上述兩條攻防路線。法院於6月26日的判決中寫道：「由於這些論點均無足採，我們駁回各方的覆核請求及廢止動議。」

  ## 爭議標準的內容

  EPA於2024年頒布的法規，將《清潔空氣法》所規定的全國環境空氣品質標準（National Ambient Air Quality Standard）——即聯邦法律對污染濃度所設定的上限——中細懸浮微粒的標準，從每立方公尺12微克調降至9微克，要求相關地區須於2032年前達標。所謂細懸浮微粒，係指直徑在2.5微米以下的粒子，縮寫為PM2.5，來源包括汽車廢氣、發電廠排放、工廠生產及類似污染源，進入人體後可深入肺部。

  《清潔空氣法》授權EPA訂定上述空氣品質標準，並指定全國各地未達標的區域。依據該法，凡未能符合修訂後標準的社區，必須制定計畫以降低當地污染。本案所引用的哈佛大學法學院環境與能源法律計畫簡報指出，每當標準趨嚴，「各州及地方區域必須確保其轄區內的污染源降低排放量，使該地區能夠達到更嚴格的新國家標準。」

  ## 執行之爭仍在持續

  儘管法院已作出裁決，2024年的新標準目前仍未付諸實施。EPA在一份電子郵件聲明中表示，正在審查此次判決，外界對於該機構是否將著手執行新限值，仍存有疑問。

  另一起於今年4月在加州北區聯邦地區法院提起的訴訟，正持續向EPA施壓，要求其採取行動。提起該案的，是一個由10個州、哥倫比亞特區、德州哈里斯郡及紐約市組成的聯盟，他們以EPA未能落實2024年標準為由提告。該案目前仍在審理中。

  ## 裁決的意義

  哥倫比亞特區巡迴上訴法院的裁決，剝奪了川普政府EPA及各州挑戰者試圖推翻該標準所依賴的法律基礎，至少封堵了本案中所提出的各項論點。對於發電業者、煉油廠及其他工業營運商而言，此次裁決意味著每立方公尺9微克的更嚴格上限在法律上依然有效，然而實際的合規義務，則取決於EPA是否及何時著手指定未達標區域、要求各州提交實施計畫。

  自然資源保護委員會（NRDC）氣候與健康科學家維傑·利馬耶（Vijay Limaye）在一份聲明中表示，此次裁決「消除了所有剩餘的疑慮：科學早有定論，如今法律亦然。EPA必須停止拖延，落實《清潔空氣法》所要求的乾淨空氣。」

  目前，尋求強制EPA執行該標準的加州聯邦地區法院案件，仍是解決此一問題的主要訴訟戰場。
