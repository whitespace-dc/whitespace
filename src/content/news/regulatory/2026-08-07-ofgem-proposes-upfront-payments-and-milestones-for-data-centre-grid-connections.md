body_en: |
  UK energy regulator Ofgem is considering rules that would require data centre developers to pay upfront and meet project milestones to retain their place in the grid connection queue, a move aimed at clearing speculative applications from networks that are already operating under strain.

  The proposal comes as demand for grid connections rises across the UK and Europe, driven simultaneously by the expansion of data centres and the buildout of renewable energy projects. Policymakers and network operators have been grappling with long queues as developers compete for limited transmission and distribution capacity.

  ## The reform and its mechanics

  Under Ofgem's proposed approach, developers would bear greater financial and procedural responsibility for demonstrating that their projects are advancing. Supporters of stricter rules argue the change could reduce queue congestion caused by applicants who reserve network capacity without clear plans to construct and operate facilities.

  The reform has moved well beyond network engineering into questions of industrial policy, energy costs, and competition in artificial intelligence infrastructure, according to the sources. Data centres require large, often geographically concentrated power supplies, and delays in securing a grid connection can determine where projects are built and how they are powered.

  ## The behind-the-meter risk

  Olivier Darmouni, associate professor of finance at HEC Paris, said the outcome of the reform would hinge on whether the new rules succeed in removing projects that are unlikely to proceed, or whether they simply raise the cost of getting connected without shortening the queue.

  "Interconnection delays have major consequences: they push many tech companies to build 'behind the meter' (BTM), off-grid, personal power plants in their data centre backyards," Darmouni said. "These plants, however, tend to be inefficient fossil fuel plants, since renewables are intermittent and batteries are expensive."

  Behind-the-meter generation means a facility produces some or all of its own electricity on or near the site, bypassing the normal grid connection process. The arrangement can offer developers speed and operational control, but the sources note it frequently involves fossil-fuel generation when cleaner options are not consistently available.

  Darmouni described two possible scenarios. In the better outcome, the reform displaces enough speculative queue entries to accelerate connections for viable projects, reducing reliance on behind-the-meter plants and the associated emissions and fossil fuel imports. In the worse outcome, the reform raises interconnection costs without materially shortening the queue, making behind-the-meter generation more financially attractive still.

  ## Structural pressure on network rules

  Across western electricity markets, network operators have faced a volume of applications that existing queue rules were not built to handle, the sources say. Regulators are now attempting to distinguish between developers with credible, funded projects and those holding capacity reservations without clear construction timelines.

  Darmouni said the strain reflects a structural shift in power markets as new sources of both supply and demand arrive at the same time.

  "With the growth of renewables and data centres, we've witnessed across the western world a massive increase in interconnection requests," he said. "The rules and institutions in place were not designed for the growth and complexity associated with these new technologies. Interconnection delays are a key driver of future energy bills and energy investments, which largely determine the climate impact and competitiveness of European AI."

  ## Cost, competition, and market concentration

  A separate question raised by the proposal is whether higher upfront connection charges could fund network upgrades that benefit a wider range of users. Large technology companies have strong incentives to secure electricity for data centres, and some arguments hold they should bear more of the infrastructure expansion cost.

  Darmouni acknowledged a public interest rationale but warned the approach could entrench the position of the largest operators.

  "In principle, charging a higher cost for interconnection can have some benefits for society at large. Tech firms have a very high willingness to pay for data centres and energy — charging them more to pay for grid infrastructure that would benefit the public in the long run seems like a win-win," he said. "The downside, however, in addition to some tech firms choosing to go off-grid, is that only big tech firms might be able to afford interconnection, making it more difficult for new actors to challenge the existing behemoths. Down the road, this might only reinforce the concentration of AI power in the hands of a few giant firms."

  ## Regional price effects

  Darmouni recently studied 420 planned data centre projects in the United States and found that their aggregate effect on electricity prices would be limited at a national level but considerably larger in specific regions. The finding underlines how concentrated power demand can produce uneven effects across local markets, a dynamic that UK policymakers must weigh when designing rules that apply nationally but land locally.

  ## What is at stake

  For UK energy policy, the reform presents a narrow set of objectives that can pull in conflicting directions: accelerating grid access for legitimate projects, limiting the incentive to build fossil-fuel generation outside the regulated network, and preventing grid access from becoming a privilege available only to the largest technology companies. Whether Ofgem's proposed milestones and upfront payments achieve the first goal without worsening the second and third will depend on the specific design of the rules and whether they reduce queue volume or simply shift its cost structure.
body_zh: |
  英國能源監管機構Ofgem正考慮制定新規，要求資料中心開發商預先付款，並須達成專案里程碑，方能保住電網連線申請的排隊位置。此舉旨在清除已在超載電網上占據容量的投機性申請。

  此提案出爐之際，英國與歐洲各地對電網連線的需求正持續攀升，驅動力同時來自兩端：資料中心的快速擴張，以及再生能源專案的大規模建設。面對有限的輸配電容量，開發商相互競逐，政策制定者與電網業者對漫長的排隊現象已疲於應對。

  ## 改革內容與運作機制

  依照Ofgem提出的方案，開發商須承擔更高的財務與程序責任，以證明其專案正持續推進。支持嚴格規定的人士認為，此舉有助減少因申請方占位卻無明確興建計畫所造成的排隊壅塞。

  據消息人士指出，這項改革早已超越電網工程的範疇，深入觸及工業政策、能源成本，以及人工智慧基礎設施的競爭等深層議題。資料中心需要大規模、且往往在地理上高度集中的電力供應，而能否順利取得電網連線，往往直接左右著專案建置地點與供電方式的抉擇。

  ## 自建電廠的隱患

  巴黎高等商學院（HEC Paris）財務學副教授奧利維耶·達爾穆尼（Olivier Darmouni）表示，改革成效的關鍵在於：新規究竟能否成功淘汰不可能推進的專案，還是僅僅推高了接電成本，卻縮短不了排隊長龍。

  「電網連線的延誤會帶來重大後果：許多科技公司被迫在資料中心旁邊自建『表後』（BTM）離網私人電廠，」達爾穆尼說。「然而這些電廠往往是效率低落的化石燃料電廠，因為再生能源具有間歇性，而電池的建置成本相當高昂。」

  所謂「表後發電」（behind-the-meter generation），是指設施在廠區內或附近自行發電，部分或全部繞過正常的電網連線程序。此安排可讓開發商加快進度並掌控運營，但消息人士指出，當清潔能源無法穩定供應時，通常仍須仰賴化石燃料發電。

  達爾穆尼描述了兩種可能情境。在較佳情況下，改革能淘汰足夠多的投機性排隊申請，加快可行專案的連線速度，從而減少對表後電廠的依賴及其伴隨的碳排放與化石燃料進口壓力。在較差情況下，改革只是推高了連線成本，卻未能實質縮短排隊，反而讓表後發電在財務上更具吸引力。

  ## 電網規則面臨的結構性壓力

  消息人士表示，在西方各大電力市場，電網業者收到的申請量早已超出現行排隊規則的設計承載上限。監管機構如今正努力區分兩類申請者：一類是擁有可信計畫且資金到位的開發商，另一類則只是占位卻未附明確興建時程的投機者。

  達爾穆尼表示，這股壓力反映出電力市場正在經歷結構性轉變——新的供給與需求來源幾乎同步湧現。

  「隨著再生能源與資料中心的成長，我們見證了西方世界電網連線申請的爆炸性增長，」他說。「現行的規則與制度根本不是為這些新技術所帶來的成長規模與複雜程度而設計的。電網連線延誤是左右未來能源帳單與能源投資的關鍵因素，而這在很大程度上決定了歐洲人工智慧的氣候影響力與競爭力。」

  ## 成本、競爭與市場集中

  這項提案衍生出另一個問題：較高的預付連線費用，是否有助於資助電網升級，進而嘉惠更廣泛的使用者？大型科技公司有強烈誘因為資料中心取得穩定用電，部分論點主張它們應當承擔更大比例的基礎設施擴充成本。

  達爾穆尼認可此舉具有公共利益的正當性，但同時警告此做法可能鞏固最大型業者的既有優勢地位。

  「原則上，提高電網連線費用對整體社會確實有一定好處。科技公司對資料中心和能源的支付意願極高——要求它們多付費來建設長遠有益於公眾的電網基礎設施，乍看是雙贏，」他說。「但不利之處在於，除了部分科技公司可能選擇離網自建，還有一個風險是：可能只有大型科技公司才負擔得起電網連線費用，讓新進業者更難撼動現有巨頭的地位。長此以往，人工智慧的主導權恐怕只會更加集中在少數幾家大公司手中。」

  ## 區域電價衝擊

  達爾穆尼近期研究了美國420個規劃中的資料中心專案，發現其整體效應對全國電價的衝擊相對有限，但在特定區域的影響則相當顯著。這一發現突顯出高度集中的電力需求如何在地方市場造成不均等的衝擊——而英國政策制定者在設計全國一致適用的規則時，必須充分衡量這些規則落地後因地而異的實際效應。

  ## 核心議題

  對英國能源政策而言，這項改革面臨一組可能相互拉扯的目標：加快合法專案取得電網連線的速度、降低在監管電網以外興建化石燃料電廠的誘因，以及防止電網連線資格淪為大型科技公司的專屬特權。Ofgem提出的里程碑機制與預付費規定，能否在達成首項目標的同時，不讓後兩項問題雪上加霜，將取決於規則的具體設計，以及這些規則是否真能縮減排隊申請量，而非僅僅改變其成本結構。
