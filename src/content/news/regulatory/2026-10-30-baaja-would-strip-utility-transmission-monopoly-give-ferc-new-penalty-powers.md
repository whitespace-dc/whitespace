body_en: |
  ## What the bill does

  Bipartisan permitting reform legislation moving through the U.S. Senate would expand federal authority over electric transmission buildout by stripping incumbent utilities of their automatic right to construct new regional lines and giving the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) — the independent agency that regulates interstate electricity markets — new tools to penalize utilities that fail to comply with grid upgrade orders.

  The Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, known as BAAJA, was introduced in the Senate on Sept. 30. A cloture vote — a procedural step to end debate and advance the bill — is scheduled for Nov. 9, according to the Bipartisan Policy Center.

  Rep. Josh Harder, D-Calif., chair of the Build America Caucus and a supporter of the legislation, said FERC will have "a much bigger hammer to swing" at obstacles to transmission buildout if regulators determine insufficient progress is being made, in an interview with Utility Dive.

  ## Core provisions

  The bill eliminates the federal right of first refusal currently held by incumbent utilities — the mechanism that has historically given existing grid operators an automatic claim to build new regional transmission lines before competitors can bid. BAAJA would require FERC to issue a rule mandating that each transmission planning region file a consolidated interconnection and regional transmission planning process.

  FERC would also gain the authority to require public utilities to make cost-effective grid upgrades and to set a compliance timeline, with penalties for utilities that miss it. Those penalties could include a reduction in a utility's return on equity — a key financial metric that determines how much profit a regulated utility is permitted to earn on its infrastructure investments.

  The legislation further directs FERC to establish rate treatments for regional and interregional transmission planning and cost allocation, and to require tariffs that spread those costs to customers who benefit.

  Harder said the cost-sharing provisions are the most consequential part of the bill. "I think the cost-sharing elements are by far probably the most important part of this bill, and my hope is that can unlock a lot more collaboration as opposed to what we have now, which is kind of folks pointing the finger at each other," he said. He noted that no new interregional transmission has been built at the FERC level in a substantial period: "That's unacceptable, and it's just not doing the job to lower prices for folks."

  ## A missing 'forcing function'

  Chirag Lala, vice president of research and chief economist at the nonprofit Center for Public Enterprise and co-author of a recent paper proposing a federally coordinated macrogrid, wrote in an October brief that BAAJA "gets transmission right" but identified a gap: the interregional planning mechanism has no "forcing function" if regions fail to submit a planning process. Under the current text, a region that does not comply receives only a six-month extension, after which the outcome is unspecified.

  Advait Arun, senior associate for energy finance at the Center for Public Enterprise and co-author of the macrogrid paper, told Utility Dive in an email that a forcing function should be added, and that the bill could be strengthened by expanding federal transmission finance programs. He pointed specifically to the U.S. Department of Energy's Transmission Facilitation Program, a $2.5 billion revolving fund that provides loans and other financial support for transmission projects.

  Arun also flagged the bill's "federal support restriction," which currently bars the DOE's Office of Energy Dominance Financing — formerly the Loan Programs Office — from financing projects that have already received another form of non-tax-credit federal commitment. He said that restriction could limit the program's utility.

  On the House side, Arun said the Build More Agenda — a House bipartisan package announced Oct. 5 that Harder said was developed alongside the Senate bill — raises a fundamental concern: "One worry I have here is that there is no money in any of these proposals. Ultimately, a lot of the private investment that these suggestions aim to catalyze through targeted streamlining of regulations will still be at the mercy of a temperamental macroeconomic picture."

  Harder acknowledged the fiscal environment as a constraint. "I think that a lot of policymakers on both sides of the aisle are going to be looking for pro-growth policies that don't demand enormous new spending," he said, citing "a lack of fiscal headroom that we're going to have at a national level over the next couple of years."

  ## Clean firm power and the House agenda

  The House Build More Agenda addresses clean firm generation — electricity sources that can produce power reliably on demand, unlike weather-dependent renewables — by proposing expanded support for programs such as DOE's Utah Frontier Observatory for Research in Geothermal Energy, and by streamlining Nuclear Regulatory Commission requirements. One proposal would allow nuclear plant developers to use commercial-grade materials in non-safety structures where they would be equally safe as specialized nuclear-grade materials.

  Harder said he would like to see a follow-on to the Accelerating Deployment of Versatile, Advanced Nuclear for Clean Energy Act of 2024 that would codify changes the Trump administration has made at the NRC, shifting from what he characterized as a zero-risk regulatory posture to a risk-based one. "You can't have any regulatory agency that doesn't want to build anything at all," he said.

  ## Outlook and opposition

  Harder described BAAJA as a "pretty delicate negotiation that's gone on for over two years" and cautioned against expecting major amendments. He did acknowledge that tribal organizations have opposed changes the bill makes to the National Historic Preservation Act, and said those provisions might be clarified, but added that "the vast majority of this bill, I think, will probably remain as-is."

  The Senate cloture vote is set for Nov. 9.

  ## 法案內容

  一項正在美國參議院推進的跨黨派許可改革立法，擬透過取消現有電力公司自動享有的新區域輸電線路建設優先權，並授予聯邦能源管理委員會（FERC）——負責監管跨州電力市場的獨立機構——新的懲處工具，對未能遵守電網升級命令的公用事業予以懲罰，進而大幅擴大聯邦對輸電建設的管轄權。

  《跨黨派美國可負擔性與就業法案》（Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act，簡稱BAAJA）於9月30日在參議院正式提出。根據跨黨派政策中心的資訊，終結辯論表決——即終止辯論、推進法案審議的程序性步驟——預定於11月9日舉行。

  加州民主黨眾議員、「建設美國核心小組」主席暨該法案支持者Josh Harder在接受Utility Dive採訪時表示，若主管機關認定輸電建設進度不足，FERC將擁有「更大的槌子」來打擊阻礙建設的障礙。

  ## 核心條款

  該法案取消了現有電力公司目前持有的聯邦優先建設權——這一機制歷來賦予現有電網營運商在競爭者得以競標之前，自動取得新區域輸電線路建設權的優先地位。BAAJA將要求FERC發布規則，強制每個輸電規劃區提交整合的互聯及區域輸電規劃流程。

  FERC同時將獲得授權，可要求公用電力公司進行具成本效益的電網升級，並設定合規時間表，對逾期未達標的公用事業祭出罰則。這些罰則可包括降低電力公司的股本回報率——這是決定受管制公用事業在基礎建設投資上獲准賺取多少利潤的關鍵財務指標。

  法案進一步指示FERC針對區域及跨區域輸電規劃與成本分攤建立費率處理機制，並要求訂定關稅，將相關費用分攤至受益的用戶身上。

  Harder表示，成本分攤條款是整部法案中最具關鍵性的部分。他說：「我認為成本分攤條款遠遠可能是這部法案最重要的部分，我希望這能解鎖更多合作空間，而不是像現在這樣，大家都在互相推諉。」他指出，在FERC層級，實質上已有相當長一段時間未曾建設任何新的跨區域輸電線路：「這是無法接受的，根本無法完成降低民眾電費的任務。」

  ## 缺乏「強制執行機制」

  非營利機構「公共企業中心」研究副總裁兼首席經濟學家Chirag Lala，同時也是近期一篇提議建立聯邦統籌宏觀電網論文的共同作者，在10月份的一份簡報中寫道，BAAJA「在輸電議題上做對了方向」，但也指出一項缺口：若各區域未能提交規劃流程，跨區域規劃機制缺乏相應的「強制執行機制」。根據現行文本，未合規的區域僅獲得六個月的延期，之後的處置結果並無明確規範。

  公共企業中心能源金融高級研究員暨前述宏觀電網論文共同作者Advait Arun在電子郵件中向Utility Dive表示，法案應加入強制執行機制，並指出可透過擴大聯邦輸電融資計畫來強化法案效力。他特別點名美國能源部的「輸電促進計畫」——一個規模達25億美元的循環基金，為輸電專案提供貸款及其他財務支持。

  Arun也特別提出法案中的「聯邦支持限制條款」，該條款目前禁止能源部「能源主導辦公室融資部門」（前身為貸款計畫辦公室）為已獲得其他形式非稅收抵免聯邦承諾的專案提供融資。他表示，這項限制可能削弱該計畫的實用性。

  就眾議院部分，Arun表示「建設更多議程」——10月5日宣布的眾議院跨黨派方案，Harder表示其與參議院法案係同步發展——引發了一個根本性的疑慮：「我的一個擔憂是，這些提案中都沒有任何資金。歸根究柢，這些建議希望透過有針對性的法規鬆綁來催化的大量私人投資，仍將受制於變幻莫測的整體經濟環境。」

  Harder承認財政環境是一項制約因素。他表示：「我認為兩黨許多政策制定者都會在尋求不需要龐大新支出的促進成長政策，」並引述「未來幾年我們在國家層級將面臨的財政空間不足」作為背景因素。

  ## 清潔穩定電力與眾議院議程

  眾議院「建設更多議程」透過提議擴大對能源部猶他州地熱能源研究前沿觀測站等計畫的支持，以及簡化核能管制委員會（NRC）相關要求，來回應清潔穩定發電的議題——所謂清潔穩定電力，係指不同於天氣依賴型再生能源、能夠可靠按需供電的電力來源。其中一項提案將允許核電廠開發商在非安全結構中使用商業級材料，前提是其安全性與專用核級材料相當。

  Harder表示，他希望看到2024年《加速部署多用途先進核電清潔能源法》的後續立法，將川普政府在NRC所推動的改變予以法典化，從他所描述的「零風險」監管立場轉向以風險為基礎的監管立場。他說：「任何監管機構都不能完全不想批准任何建設。」

  ## 展望與反對聲浪

  Harder將BAAJA形容為「歷時超過兩年的相當微妙談判過程」，並告誡外界不要期待出現重大修正。他確實承認，部落組織反對法案對《國家歷史保護法》所做的修改，並表示相關條款或許會予以釐清，但補充說：「這部法案的絕大多數內容，我認為可能將維持原樣。」

  參議院終結辯論表決定於11月9日舉行。
