body_en: |
  Taiwan's Keelung District Prosecutors' Office expanded its investigation into alleged illegal exports of AI servers to China on June 29, conducting a second wave of searches targeting the Taiwan branch of Super Micro Computer (Supermicro), technology distributor Qingyun Technology, and Internet Data Center (IDC) operator Shifang Telecommunications, while summoning multiple executives and staff for questioning.

  ## Second wave broadens from individuals to supply chain

  The investigation's first wave, launched in May, targeted 12 locations connected to three individuals suspected of facilitating illegal shipments and focused on the direct export chain. The June 29 action extended scrutiny to upstream supply chain actors and peripheral service providers, spanning system manufacturing, channel sales, logistics, and data center colocation.

  Investigators are examining whether participants used false customs declarations or misreported final cargo destinations to ship AI servers equipped with Nvidia high-end graphics processing units (GPUs) to China, Hong Kong, and Macau — regions subject to U.S. export controls on advanced computing hardware. The probe is proceeding along lines of document forgery, breach of fiduciary duty, and violations of trade law, according to iThome. The suspected method involves routing equipment through third countries and misreporting shipment flows to evade export restrictions.

  ## Qingyun Technology stock hits limit-down

  Qingyun Technology issued a material disclosure stating it was cooperating fully with prosecutors and that its operations, finances, and business remained unaffected. Despite that statement, the company's shares fell to the daily limit-down threshold, the maximum allowable single-day decline under Taiwan exchange rules, after news of the search became public.

  ## Shifang says it only provided colocation services

  Shifang Telecommunications, a major IDC provider, issued two statements clarifying its role. The company said it was cooperating with an investigation into a customer's alleged illegal export of AI equipment, but that Shifang was neither the equipment owner nor a participant in any equipment transaction. The company said it provides data center hosting and colocation services only and has no legal authority under applicable law to inspect, intervene in, or dispose of customer equipment housed in its facilities. Shifang said operations and finances remain normal and that it would cooperate with regulators if customers were found to have acted illegally.

  ## U.S. Justice Department charged Supermicro co-founder in March

  The Taiwan investigation follows a U.S. federal case filed in March 2026. The U.S. Department of Justice charged Supermicro co-founder Wally Liaw and two others with allegedly using third-party companies and fraudulent transaction arrangements, beginning in 2022, to route AI servers equipped with Nvidia high-end GPUs to China in violation of U.S. export controls. The DOJ valued the transactions at approximately $2.5 billion.

  The U.S. case alleged that the defendants falsified end-user information, used offshore companies to place equipment orders, and altered stated shipment destinations to circumvent export restrictions.

  ## What the expansion means

  The widening of the Taiwan investigation to encompass the full supply chain — from manufacturer to colocation host — indicates prosecutors are working to trace the complete path of equipment from procurement through final delivery. The case now involves parallel enforcement proceedings in two jurisdictions, with U.S. federal charges already filed and Taiwan's prosecutorial sweep continuing to expand.

  The Keelung District Prosecutors' Office has not announced whether further searches or charges are forthcoming.

  ## 第二波搜索從個人延伸至供應鏈全體

  基隆地方檢察署於6月29日擴大偵辦涉嫌非法出口AI伺服器至中國一案，對超微電腦（Supermicro）台灣分公司、科技通路商勤誠科技，以及網際網路資料中心（IDC）業者世方電信展開第二波搜索，並傳喚多名主管與員工到案說明。

  偵查工作的第一波行動於五月啟動，鎖定三名涉嫌協助非法出貨的個人，共搜索12處地點，聚焦於直接出口鏈的清查。6月29日的行動則將偵查觸角延伸至上游供應鏈業者及周邊服務提供商，涵蓋系統製造、通路銷售、物流，以及資料中心代管等環節。

  檢察官正在調查相關業者是否以偽造報關文件或虛報貨物最終目的地等手法，將搭載輝達高階繪圖處理器（GPU）的AI伺服器出口至受美國出口管制的中國、香港及澳門地區。據《iThome》報導，本案係依偽造文書、背信及違反貿易法等罪嫌進行偵查，涉嫌手法包括透過第三國繞道轉運，並虛報貨物流向以規避出口限制。

  ## 勤誠科技股價跌停

  勤誠科技發布重大訊息，表示已全力配合檢察機關調查，公司營運、財務及業務均未受影響。然而，搜索消息曝光後，該公司股價隨即跌停，觸及台灣證券交易所規定的單日最大跌幅下限。

  ## 世方電信強調僅提供機房代管服務

  主要IDC業者世方電信連發兩份聲明，說明自身在本案中的角色。該公司表示，目前配合檢察機關調查的是某客戶涉嫌非法出口AI設備一事，世方電信並非該設備的所有權人，亦未參與任何設備交易。世方強調，公司業務範疇僅限於提供資料中心主機代管與機房租用服務，依據相關法規，公司對存放於機房內的客戶設備並無權限加以稽查、介入或處置。世方電信表示，公司營運與財務狀況一切正常，若客戶確有違法情事，公司將全力配合主管機關處理。

  ## 美國司法部三月已對超微共同創辦人提起訴訟

  台灣的調查行動，源於美國聯邦政府今年3月提起的訴訟案。美國司法部於2026年3月，對超微電腦共同創辦人廖文藝（Wally Liaw）及另外兩人提出起訴，指控三人自2022年起，藉由第三方公司及虛偽交易安排，將搭載輝達高階GPU的AI伺服器非法出口至中國，違反美國出口管制規定。美國司法部估算，相關交易總金額約達25億美元。

  美方起訴書指控，被告偽造最終用戶資訊、利用境外公司下單採購設備，並竄改貨物申報目的地，以規避出口管制。

  ## 偵查範圍擴大的意義

  台灣此次調查行動將偵查範圍拓展至整條供應鏈——從製造商到機房代管業者——顯示檢察官正試圖追查設備從採購到最終交付的完整流向。本案目前已形成跨兩個司法管轄區的並行執法態勢：美國聯邦起訴已正式提出，台灣的偵查掃蕩行動亦持續擴大。

  基隆地方檢察署目前尚未對外宣布是否將進行進一步搜索或提起訴訟。
