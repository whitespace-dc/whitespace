body_en: |
  The U.S. Department of Energy on June 4, 2026, ordered the Orlando Utilities Commission (OUC), a municipal utility, to suspend plans to place its 465-MW coal-fired Stanton Unit 1 in cold shutdown and continue operating the unit through September 1. The department framed the directive as necessary to address what it characterized as an energy emergency in Florida, citing in part the state's projected inability to keep pace with load growth from incoming data centers.

  OUC spokesman Mike Codichini confirmed the utility will comply, saying in an email that it will delay the planned cold shutdown of Stanton Unit 1. The utility did not respond to questions about estimated compliance costs or the status of the unit's coal stockpiles.

  ## Basis for the emergency order

  The order was issued under Section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act, the same legal authority DOE has used for a string of similar directives affecting six other power plants, five of them coal-fired. DOE said it has argued in court that the emergencies these orders are designed to address do not need to be imminent.

  As primary evidence of the Florida emergency, DOE cited the North American Electric Reliability Corp.'s (NERC) most recent long-term reliability report. NERC is the nonprofit organization responsible for assessing grid reliability across North America. The report classified Florida as being at "normal risk" for long-term energy adequacy — not a high-alert designation — but noted that projections for resource and transmission growth in the state lag what would be needed to support new data centers and other large loads, according to DOE's order.

  DOE Secretary Chris Wright stated in the order: "The conditions resulting from the combination of increasing demand and [electricity] shortage will continue on in the near term and are also likely to continue in subsequent years. This could lead to the loss of power to homes and local businesses in the areas affected by curtailments or power outages, presenting a risk to public health, and safety."

  DOE also pointed to Winter Storm Fern, an event in which exceptionally cold weather covered large sections of the U.S., as a demonstration of OUC's resource adequacy vulnerabilities. The Orlando utility requested and received two prior Section 202(c) emergency orders to preserve reliability during that storm, according to the department.

  ## Broader policy context

  The order on Stanton Unit 1 was issued the same day the Trump administration announced plans to spend $850 million to support coal-fired power plants and the broader coal sector. DOE has so far reissued each of its 90-day emergency orders before the previous ones expire, extending the effective operating period for affected plants beyond the initial 90-day windows.

  DOE directed OUC to file with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) any tariff revisions necessary to allow the utility to recover the costs of complying with the order. FERC is the federal agency that regulates interstate electricity markets and transmission.

  ## OUC's transition plans

  The emergency order runs counter to OUC's own resource planning trajectory. The utility had been preparing since 2020 to convert the Stanton plant's two coal-fired units to burn natural gas. In 2021, OUC purchased the roughly 475-MW Osceola peaking power plant — a facility designed for rapid dispatch to meet peak demand, unlike the slower-ramping Stanton Unit 1 — which gave the utility the flexibility to cold-store rather than convert Stanton Unit 1, according to an annual site plan OUC filed with the Florida Public Service Commission in April 2026.

  In that filing, OUC told state regulators it has adequate generating capacity to satisfy forecast reserve margin requirements through 2035, accounting for existing resources and planned power purchase agreements under its base-case load forecast. OUC plans to convert Stanton Unit 2, also a 465-MW coal unit, to natural gas by the end of 2027.

  OUC's resource planning is tied to a net-zero carbon emissions target by 2050, with interim goals of 50% carbon emissions reductions by 2030 and 75% reductions by 2040, both measured against 2005 levels.

  ## Unit output has declined

  Stanton Unit 1's actual generation has been falling. The unit produced 296,856 megawatt-hours (MWh) in the first quarter of 2026, a 32% drop from 436,796 MWh in the same period in 2025, according to the most recent data from the U.S. Energy Information Administration. One megawatt-hour is enough electricity to power roughly 1,000 average homes for one hour.

  OUC serves approximately 288,000 electric and water customers and also supplies wholesale power to nearby cities.

  ## What the order means for utility planning

  The Stanton Unit 1 directive illustrates the tension between utilities' long-term decarbonization plans and the federal government's use of emergency authority to keep coal capacity online. OUC's own planning documents project adequate supply through 2035 without Stanton Unit 1, yet the federal order requires the unit to remain available through at least September 1, 2026, with the possibility of renewal based on the pattern DOE has established with earlier orders.

  OUC's planned gas conversion of Stanton Unit 2 is scheduled to be complete by the end of 2027.
body_zh: |
  美國能源部（DOE）於2026年6月4日命令奧蘭多公用事業委員會（OUC）——一家市立電業——暫停將旗下465MW燃煤史坦頓一號（Stanton Unit 1）機組轉入冷停機封存的計畫，並要求繼續運轉至9月1日。能源部將這項指令定性為應對佛羅里達州能源緊急狀況的必要措施，部分原因在於該州被預測將無法跟上新進資料中心所帶來的負載成長。

  OUC發言人麥克·科迪基尼（Mike Codichini）透過電子郵件確認，該機構將遵從命令，延後史坦頓一號的冷停機封存計畫。OUC未回應有關預估合規成本及機組煤炭庫存現狀的相關詢問。

  ## 緊急命令的依據

  這項命令依據《聯邦電力法》第202(c)條發布，能源部對其他六座電廠（其中五座為燃煤電廠）所發布的類似指令，援引的正是同一法律授權。能源部表示，已在法庭上主張，這些命令所針對的緊急狀況無需迫在眉睫即可成立。

  作為佛羅里達州緊急狀況的主要佐證，能源部援引了北美電力可靠性公司（NERC）最新發布的長期可靠性報告。NERC是負責評估北美電網可靠性的非營利組織。根據能源部命令所述，該報告將佛羅里達州的長期能源充裕度列為「正常風險」等級——並非高度警戒——但指出該州資源與輸電成長的預測進度，落後於支撐新進資料中心及其他大型負載所需的水準。

  能源部長克里斯·萊特（Chris Wright）在命令中表示：「需求持續攀升與電力短缺相互交疊所造成的局面，短期內將持續存在，未來數年內同樣可能延續。這可能導致受限電或停電衝擊地區的住家與在地商家失去供電，對公共健康與安全構成威脅。」

  能源部同時援引「冬季暴風蕨」（Winter Storm Fern）——一場席捲美國大片區域的極寒天氣事件——以說明OUC在資源充裕度方面的脆弱性。根據能源部說明，奧蘭多這家公用事業單位在那場暴風雪期間曾兩度申請並獲核第202(c)條緊急命令，以維持電力供應可靠性。

  ## 更廣泛的政策背景

  史坦頓一號的命令，與川普政府宣布斥資8億5,000萬美元支持燃煤電廠及整體煤炭產業的計畫同日發布。迄今為止，能源部在每道90天緊急命令到期前均予以續發，使受影響電廠的實際運轉期限超出最初90天的時間窗口。

  能源部指示OUC向聯邦能源管理委員會（FERC）提交必要的費率修訂，以便該公用事業單位能夠收回遵從命令所產生的成本。FERC是負責監管跨州電力市場與輸電業務的聯邦機構。

  ## OUC的轉型計畫

  這項緊急命令與OUC自身的資源規劃方向背道而馳。該機構自2020年起便開始籌備將史坦頓廠兩座燃煤機組轉換為天然氣發電。2021年，OUC收購了裝機容量約475MW的奧西奧拉調峰電廠（Osceola peaking power plant）——這是一座專為快速調度以應付尖峰需求而設計的設施，不同於啟停速度較慢的史坦頓一號。根據OUC於2026年4月向佛羅里達州公用事業服務委員會提交的年度廠址規劃，這次收購使OUC得以選擇將史坦頓一號採冷停機封存而非直接轉換，從而保有更大的調度彈性。

  在該份申報文件中，OUC向州監管機關表示，依據現有資源及基本負載預測情境下的購電協議計畫，其發電容量充裕，足以滿足2035年前的預測備用容量需求。OUC計畫於2027年底前完成史坦頓二號（Stanton Unit 2，同為465MW燃煤機組）的天然氣轉換工程。

  OUC的資源規劃與2050年達成碳淨零排放的目標相連結，並設有中程里程碑：相較於2005年的排放水準，分別於2030年減碳50%、2040年減碳75%。

  ## 機組發電量持續下滑

  史坦頓一號的實際發電量已呈現下滑趨勢。根據美國能源資訊署（EIA）最新數據，該機組2026年第一季發電量為29萬6,856百萬瓦小時（MWh），較2025年同期的43萬6,796 MWh下跌32%。一百萬瓦小時的電力，足以供約1,000戶一般家庭用電一小時。

  OUC約服務28萬8,000名電力及自來水用戶，並向鄰近城市提供躉售電力。

  ## 這項命令對電業規劃的意義

  史坦頓一號的命令，凸顯了電業長期去碳化計畫與聯邦政府援用緊急授權強行維持燃煤產能之間的深層矛盾。OUC自身的規劃文件預測，即便沒有史坦頓一號，2035年前的電力供應仍屬充裕；然而聯邦命令要求該機組至少維持運轉至2026年9月1日，且依能源部在先前命令中確立的慣例，仍有可能再度展延。

  OUC原訂史坦頓二號的天然氣轉換工程，計畫於2027年底完工。
