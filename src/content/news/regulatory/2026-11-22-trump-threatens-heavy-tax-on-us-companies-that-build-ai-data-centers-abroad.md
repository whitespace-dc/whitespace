body_en: |
  President Donald Trump on November 20 threatened to impose a "tremendous tax" on US technology companies that build artificial intelligence data centers in other countries, pledging to keep AI infrastructure on American soil even as domestic opposition to new data centers contributed to midterm election losses for Republicans.

  Trump made the announcement at a White House briefing, shortly after returning from a summit in Asia attended by the United States, China, and Russia. He told reporters he had not consulted his own officials before going public with the plan. "I don't know if this is a big announcement. I haven't even told my people this. But I'm announcing it now to the people here," he said, according to Kompas.com citing CNBC.

  Companies could still build abroad, Trump said, but would pay a steep price. "They can do it, but we will charge them a very big tax."

  ## The rationale: AI competition with China

  Trump framed the domestic data center push as essential to maintaining American leadership in artificial intelligence. He said the United States leads China in developing what he called "super intelligence," but warned that growing political resistance to data center construction at home could allow China to close that gap. He drew a direct parallel with semiconductor manufacturing, arguing that permitting chip production to move offshore had caused "tremendous damage" to the United States and said he did not want AI infrastructure to follow the same path.

  Data centers are central to the AI industry because they supply the computing power required to train and run increasingly complex AI systems, Commercial Times reported.

  ## Companies exposed

  The threat would reach across a wide swath of the US technology sector. Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, and Oracle have all built data centers outside the United States, according to Kompas.com. Major US data center developers including Equinix and Digital Realty also build and operate facilities in other countries, Commercial Times reported.

  International expansion has been a standard growth strategy for large-scale cloud computing providers. Building facilities in countries where customers operate allows companies to reduce latency and, in some cases, satisfy local data-residency regulations that require infrastructure to be sited within a given jurisdiction.

  ## Chip export controls

  Trump's data center remarks came four days after a parallel move on hardware. The administration on November 16 issued new rules restricting remote access to advanced US computing chips and servers — the latest step in a sustained effort to prevent China from using American chips to train its most capable AI models, The Straits Times reported.

  The Commerce Department earlier in the week also restricted exports of advanced AI chips to several Southeast Asian countries, Commercial Times reported. That action targeted a documented workaround: Chinese companies have continued to access restricted chips by renting computing capacity at data centers in countries that can legally import the hardware.

  ## Domestic backlash and midterm consequences

  Trump's push to build more data centers in the United States runs against sharp and growing public opposition. Anti-data center sentiment was a significant factor in the midterm elections, in which Democrats took control of Congress, The Straits Times reported. The midterm cycle produced a wave of Democratic candidates who campaigned on pledges to constrain the energy-hungry facilities.

  An Associated Press voter poll found that about three-quarters of American voters in the midterm election opposed new data centers in their own communities, Commercial Times reported. The same survey found most Americans were "extremely" or "very" concerned about data centers' effects on local electricity prices or water supplies.

  Trump had addressed community resistance before the midterms as well, urging residents to extract concessions from developers rather than block projects outright. "You ought to take advantage of the data centers. Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but don't shut them off," he said at the November 20 briefing, repeating a position he had staked out earlier in the campaign, according to The Straits Times.

  ## Industry concessions

  Facing the changed political environment, major technology companies have promised to alter their approach. Google and Meta officials told the Associated Press that they are working to build trust by increasing transparency with communities where they seek to build, Commercial Times and The Straits Times reported. Companies are also pledging to reduce environmental impact, offering assurances that data centers will lift local property values, and promising to seek fewer taxpayer subsidies and disclose more about their operations.

  Industry officials maintain that cloud computing services, not AI alone, remain the primary use case for US data centers, and that AI represents a net economic benefit despite skepticism from many communities, Commercial Times reported.

  ## Significance

  Trump's announcement, made without prior coordination with administration officials, leaves the specific legal mechanism and scope of any such tax undefined. The statement nonetheless signals that the White House intends to use fiscal or trade tools to enforce domestic siting of AI infrastructure — a posture that could complicate international data center planning for US companies and add pressure on a sector already navigating new chip export rules. With Democrats now controlling Congress and expected to pursue a tougher regulatory posture toward AI companies and data center developers, the industry faces simultaneous pressure from federal and local levels, The Straits Times reported.
body_zh: |
  美國總統唐納·川普於11月20日揚言，將對在境外興建人工智慧資料中心的美國科技企業課徵「重稅」，誓言保留AI基礎設施在美國本土，儘管國內民眾對新建資料中心的反彈，已被視為共和黨在期中選舉失利的重要因素之一。

  川普在白宮記者會上發表這項聲明，時間點恰在他從一場由美、中、俄三國出席的亞洲峰會返美後不久。他告訴媒體，在公開這項計畫之前，他甚至未知會自己的官員。他說：「我不知道這算不算重大聲明，我連自己人都還沒告訴，但我現在就宣布給在場的各位聽。」這段話由Kompas.com引述自CNBC的報導。

  川普表示，企業仍然可以赴海外建設，但必須付出高昂代價。「他們可以去做，但我們會向他們課徵非常高的稅。」

  ## 理由：與中國的AI競爭

  川普將推動資料中心在國內落地，定位為維持美國在人工智慧領域領導地位的關鍵所在。他表示，美國在發展他所稱的「超級智慧」方面領先中國，但警告國內日益高漲的反資料中心情緒，可能讓中國得以縮短差距。他直接類比半導體製造的前車之鑑，指出允許晶片生產移往海外已對美國造成「巨大傷害」，並強調不希望AI基礎設施重蹈覆轍。

  《工商時報》報導指出，資料中心是AI產業的核心，因為它們提供訓練和執行日益複雜的AI系統所需的運算能力。

  ## 受影響企業

  這項威脅的矛頭，將指向廣大的美國科技業。根據Kompas.com的引述，Alphabet、亞馬遜、微軟、Meta以及甲骨文（Oracle），均在美國境外設有資料中心。《工商時報》亦報導，美國主要資料中心開發商，包括Equinix與Digital Realty，同樣在其他國家興建並營運設施。

  國際擴張向來是大型雲端運算服務提供商的標準成長策略。在客戶所在地建置設施，可讓企業降低網路延遲，並在部分情況下，滿足當地對資料必須存放於特定管轄區內的法規要求。

  ## 晶片出口管制

  川普有關資料中心的言論，是在相關硬體政策出爐四天後提出的。美國政府於11月16日頒布新規，限制遠端存取美國先進運算晶片與伺服器——這是阻止中國利用美製晶片訓練其最強大AI模型這一持續努力的最新一步，《海峽時報》如此報導。

  《工商時報》報導，商務部在同一週稍早也限制了先進AI晶片對東南亞多個國家的出口。這項措施針對的是一個已有記錄的規避手法：中國企業持續透過在合法可進口相關硬體的國家之資料中心租用運算資源，藉此取得受限制的晶片。

  ## 國內反彈與期中選舉後果

  川普力推在美國境內興建更多資料中心，卻面臨強烈且不斷升溫的公眾反對聲浪。《海峽時報》報導，反資料中心情緒是期中選舉的重大因素之一——民主黨在該次選舉中奪回國會控制權。此次期中選舉出現一波以承諾限制這類高耗能設施為訴求的民主黨候選人浪潮。

  《工商時報》報導，美聯社的選民民調顯示，約四分之三的美國選民反對在自己的社區新建資料中心。同一份調查也發現，多數美國民眾對資料中心影響當地電費或水資源供應的問題，感到「極度」或「非常」憂慮。

  川普此前在期中選舉前也處理過社區的抵制情緒，他當時敦促居民向開發商爭取讓步，而非直接封殺計畫。他在11月20日的記者會上再度重申這一立場，說道：「你們應該善用資料中心的優勢。讓他們付錢給你們，讓他們付錢給你們的社區，但不要把他們趕走。」《海峽時報》引述，這是他在選戰期間便已表達的立場。

  ## 業界讓步

  面對已然改變的政治環境，主要科技企業紛紛承諾調整做法。《工商時報》與《海峽時報》報導，Google與Meta的官員向美聯社表示，他們正努力透過提高透明度，與有意興建設施的社區建立互信。各企業也承諾降低環境衝擊，向外界保證資料中心將帶動當地房產價值提升，並承諾減少申請納稅人補貼、公開揭露更多營運資訊。

  《工商時報》報導，業界人士堅持認為，雲端運算服務——而非單純的AI應用——仍是美國資料中心的主要使用情境，且儘管許多社區持懷疑態度，AI整體上仍為經濟帶來淨效益。

  ## 影響與意義

  川普這項未經幕僚事先協調便公開的聲明，使任何此類稅收的具體法律機制與適用範圍仍懸而未決。儘管如此，這項聲明已清楚傳遞出白宮意圖運用財政或貿易工具、強制AI基礎設施在國內落地的政策態勢——此一立場，可能使美國企業的跨國資料中心規劃趨於複雜，並對一個已在因應新晶片出口規定的產業形成額外壓力。隨著民主黨如今掌控國會，預料將對AI企業與資料中心開發商採取更強硬的監管立場，整個產業正同時承受聯邦與地方兩個層面的壓力，《海峽時報》如此報導。
