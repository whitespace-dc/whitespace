body_en: |
  QTS Data Centers and Compass Datacenters have both withdrawn from the Prince William Digital Gateway, a proposed 2,100-acre data center corridor in Northern Virginia, after state courts voided its rezoning approvals over procedural failures. QTS terminated the project and withdrew its petitions before the Supreme Court of Virginia on July 2, 2026; Compass had withdrawn in April, leaving no active developer in the corridor. The project's collapse came less than two weeks before New York Gov. Kathy Hochul issued Executive Order 62 on July 14, establishing what the state describes as the nation's first statewide moratorium on new hyperscale data center permits.

  The two actions are unrelated in cause, but together they illustrate an accelerating shift in the regulatory and political environment surrounding large-scale data center development.

  ## The collapse of the Digital Gateway

  The corridor originated with an effort by landowners along Pageland Lane, near Gainesville, Virginia, to assemble more than 200 properties near existing transmission and fiber infrastructure. In November 2022, Prince William County approved a comprehensive plan amendment allowing as much as 27 million square feet of data center development on the site. County estimates projected nearly $25 billion in investment and up to $400 million in annual tax revenue.

  Compass sought to rezone approximately 825 acres; QTS proposed a campus of roughly 800 acres. By April 2023, the two companies together were pursuing a combined 23 million square feet of development over approximately a decade. The corridor's location adjacent to Manassas National Battlefield Park, Conway Robinson State Forest and rural neighborhoods made the proposal contentious from the outset. The debate encompassed transmission expansion, water demand, equipment noise, backup generation, land conversion and the visual impact of large industrial buildings near the battlefield.

  Both companies responded to local concerns with commitments covering forest preservation, wildlife corridors, cultural-resource protections, stormwater and water management, road improvements and noise mitigation. The Board of County Supervisors nonetheless approved the rezonings by only a 4-3 vote in December 2023, following an extended public hearing. Both the county Planning Commission and the county's professional planning staff had recommended denial.

  Legal challenges followed. Prince William Circuit Court Judge Kimberly A. Irving voided all three rezoning ordinances in August 2025, finding that the county had not published required newspaper notices at proper intervals, had not made the proposed ordinances available for public inspection as required and had held the public hearing too soon after the final notice appeared. The Court of Appeals of Virginia affirmed that ruling on March 31, 2026.

  The appellate decision did not address the merits of data center development or the county's economic development objectives. It turned on whether the county had followed procedural requirements under state and local law necessary for its approvals to be valid. The three ordinances were void from the moment of their adoption.

  The Board of County Supervisors voted unanimously to stop pursuing further appeals. Compass withdrew its application on April 29. QTS filed petitions with the Supreme Court of Virginia but terminated the project and withdrew those petitions on July 2, the company said, after "careful consideration." QTS added that Virginia remains an important market for the company.

  ## New York's Executive Order 62

  On July 14, 2026, Gov. Hochul issued Executive Order 62, temporarily holding in abeyance incomplete state environmental permit applications for data centers capable of drawing at least 50 megawatts (MW) of power. The state said it would use the pause to prepare a Generic Environmental Impact Statement and to develop new policies on grid-cost allocation and community investment.

  The order did not stem from the Digital Gateway's collapse and involves different legal and regulatory issues. But it reflects the same underlying shift the Virginia episode illustrates: state governments are increasingly evaluating large data centers as major industrial and energy infrastructure, subjecting their environmental effects, grid costs, community benefits and procedural approvals to a level of scrutiny that was not routinely applied when such projects were treated as commercial real estate.

  ## What the cases mean for development

  The Digital Gateway did not collapse because demand for data center capacity in Northern Virginia fell, because financing failed or because the county's economic development priorities changed. It collapsed because a procedural deficiency in the county's own approval process could not survive judicial review — years after the rezoning votes, and after substantial planning, community engagement and entitlement work had already been completed.

  The corridor's scale intensified that exposure. A project encompassing dozens of anticipated buildings, major substations, new transmission infrastructure and potential regional environmental effects carries a higher political and legal profile than a single discrete facility, and a correspondingly higher cost when any element of the development process fails.

  The cases together signal that identifying land and power is no longer sufficient to underpin a viable large-scale data center project. The development stack now must include procedural accuracy throughout the entitlement process, political support durable enough to survive a multi-year development cycle and an administrative record capable of withstanding legal challenge through the courts.

  ## QTS撤出維吉尼亞數位走廊，紐約同步暫停超大型資料中心許可

  QTS資料中心與Compass Datacenters雙雙撤出位於北維吉尼亞州的「普林斯威廉數位走廊」（Prince William Digital Gateway）開發案。這條預定廊道佔地2,100英畝，最終因州法院認定土地重新分區程序存在瑕疵而宣告無效。QTS於2026年7月2日終止專案並撤回向維吉尼亞州最高法院提出的請願；Compass則早在4月便已退出，廊道內自此再無任何活躍的開發商。這項計畫的瓦解，距紐約州長凱西·郝楚爾（Kathy Hochul）於7月14日簽署第62號行政命令不到兩週——該命令確立了全美首例針對超大型資料中心新建許可的全州暫停令。

  這兩起事件起因各異，但合在一起清楚呈現出大型資料中心開發所面臨的法規與政治環境正在加速轉變。

  ## 數位走廊的崩局始末

  此一廊道計畫的起源，是維吉尼亞州蓋恩斯維爾（Gainesville）附近沛格蘭路（Pageland Lane）沿線的地主群，試圖在既有輸電與光纖基礎設施附近整合逾200筆土地。2022年11月，普林斯威廉郡批准綜合計畫修正案，允許在該地點興建最多2,700萬平方英尺的資料中心設施，郡府預估此案將引進近250億美元投資，並帶來每年高達4億美元的稅收。

  Compass申請約825英畝土地的重新分區，QTS則規劃興建約800英畝的園區。至2023年4月，兩家公司合計推進約2,300萬平方英尺、開發期長達約十年的聯合計畫。然而，廊道緊鄰馬納薩斯國家戰場公園（Manassas National Battlefield Park）、康威羅賓森州立森林（Conway Robinson State Forest）及農村社區，從一開始便爭議不斷。爭論焦點涵蓋輸電擴容、用水需求、設備噪音、備用發電、土地轉用，以及大型工業建築對戰場景觀的視覺衝擊。

  兩家公司為回應地方疑慮，作出一系列承諾，涵蓋森林保護、野生動物廊道、文化資源保護、雨水與水資源管理、道路改善及噪音防制。郡議會仍以4票對3票的些微差距，在2023年12月歷經冗長的公聽會後通過重新分區案。值得注意的是，郡規劃委員會及郡府專業規劃人員此前均建議否決此案。

  法律挑戰隨即而來。2025年8月，普林斯威廉巡迴法院法官金伯莉·歐文（Kimberly A. Irving）宣告三項重新分區條例全數無效，理由是郡府未依規定間隔刊登報紙公告、未依法供民眾查閱擬議條例，且在最後一次公告刊出後過短的時間內便舉行公聽會。2026年3月31日，維吉尼亞州上訴法院維持原判。

  上訴審判決並未觸及資料中心開發的實質利弊，亦未評論郡府的經濟發展目標，而是聚焦於郡府是否遵循州法及地方法規所要求的程序，以確保其核准效力。三項條例自通過之日起即告無效。

  郡議會隨後一致投票決定不再尋求進一步上訴。Compass於4月29日撤回申請；QTS雖向維吉尼亞州最高法院提出請願，但在「審慎考量後」，於7月2日終止計畫並撤回請願。QTS表示，維吉尼亞州對公司而言仍是重要市場。

  ## 紐約州第62號行政命令

  2026年7月14日，郝楚爾州長簽署第62號行政命令，暫時擱置用電量達至少50百萬瓦（MW）的資料中心尚未完成的州級環境許可申請。州府表示，將利用這段暫停期間編製「通用環境影響說明書」（Generic Environmental Impact Statement），並研擬電網成本分攤及社區投資的新政策方向。

  此一行政命令與數位走廊案的崩局無關，涉及的法律與法規議題也截然不同。但它所反映的，正是維吉尼亞州案例所揭示的同一深層轉變：各州政府正日益將大型資料中心視為重大工業與能源基礎設施，並以前所未有的力度審視其環境影響、電網成本、社區效益及程序核准——而在此之前，這類專案往往僅被當作一般商業不動產處理。

  ## 兩案對產業發展的啟示

  普林斯威廉數位走廊的失敗，並非因為北維吉尼亞州的資料中心市場需求下滑、融資告吹，或郡府的經濟發展優先順序改變，而是因為郡府自身核准程序中的一項程序瑕疵，在歷經多年後仍無法通過司法審查——儘管此時大量規劃、社區溝通及土地開發許可作業早已完成。

  廊道的龐大規模更加劇了這種脆弱性。一個涵蓋數十棟預定建築、大型變電站、新輸電基礎設施，且可能對區域環境造成影響的計畫，其政治與法律能見度遠高於單一獨立設施，一旦開發流程的任一環節出現問題，付出的代價也相應更為沉重。

  綜觀兩案，其共同訊號再清晰不過：光是找到土地與電力，已不足以支撐一個可行的大型資料中心開發計畫。如今，開發商必須在整個土地開發許可流程中確保程序無誤，必須擁有足以撐過多年開發週期的政治支持，並必須建立一套能夠承受司法挑戰的行政紀錄。

  QTS重申，維吉尼亞州對公司而言仍是重要市場。
