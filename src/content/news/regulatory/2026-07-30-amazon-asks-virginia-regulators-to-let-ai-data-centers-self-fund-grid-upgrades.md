body_en: |
  Amazon has urged Virginia's utility regulator to let hyperscale data center operators voluntarily pay for transmission infrastructure serving their campuses, putting a new question before the state commission that decides how grid expansion costs are assigned across all customers.

  The proposal surfaced this month during hearings before the Virginia State Corporation Commission (SCC) as part of Dominion Energy Virginia's annual Rider T1 proceeding — the mechanism through which Dominion recovers transmission costs from its customers. Amazon argued that large electricity users should be allowed to finance transmission facilities built specifically for their projects through contributions in aid of construction (CIAC), a funding model already used for generator interconnections and some distribution projects.

  ## The case for customer-funded transmission

  Amazon witness Cameron Brooks, president of consulting firm E9 Insight, testified that voluntary transmission CIAC would let hyperscale customers "put their own capital at risk" while reducing costs for other ratepayers by shifting stranded-asset risk — the financial exposure if expensive infrastructure ends up underused — to the customers generating new demand.

  "The question of whether to allow data centers to put their own capital at risk, to pay their own way, there's really no reason why the Commission ... can't take some immediate action on that," Brooks testified, according to transcripts reviewed by Data Center Knowledge.

  ## Dominion's objection

  Dominion Energy Virginia countered that the Rider T1 proceeding is the wrong venue. The utility argued that transmission CIAC raises broader questions involving the PJM Interconnection (PJM), the regional transmission organization, Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) jurisdiction, and retail rate design — and that those questions merit a separate proceeding.

  Applying CIAC principles to transmission is more complicated than for generator interconnections because high-voltage transmission facilities often serve multiple customers and regional reliability needs under PJM's FERC-approved planning process, making it difficult to treat them as single-customer assets.

  ## Complexity at the regional level

  Johannes Pfeifenberger, a principal at The Brattle Group, told the Commission that transmission CIAC "can be a partial solution" when the beneficiaries of an upgrade are clearly identified and willing to fund it. He cautioned that the most cost-effective transmission projects often address multiple regional needs simultaneously — accommodating new load, integrating generation, and improving reliability — which complicates the line between costs that should fall on a specific customer and those that should be shared across the grid.

  Commissioner Kelsey Bagot pressed Brooks on how voluntary CIAC would interact with Virginia's newly approved GS-5 tariff and whether customers could end up paying twice for the same transmission facilities. Brooks acknowledged uncertainty: "The honest answer is I'm probably not sure." He said Dominion and the Commission were better positioned to determine implementation details, but described voluntary CIAC as "a logical next step" beyond the protections already incorporated into GS-5.

  Neil Osnato, founder of Persistence Analytics Group, said the hearing reflects a broader challenge across organized electricity markets as regulators try to assign the costs of serving rapidly growing AI loads. He warned that allowing private funding should not create "a de facto fast lane" that displaces more mature or more system-efficient projects in PJM's planning process. A single large-campus transmission line can alter power flows, contingency requirements, substation configurations, local reliability needs, and future expansion opportunities across the network, he said, making it difficult to isolate one customer's cost responsibility from broader grid effects.

  Osnato framed the core test for regulators: whether a proposal requires customers to "internalize the full incremental infrastructure consequences of its load while preserving PJM's ability to plan the regional system efficiently." His assessment of CIAC's limits was direct: "CIAC can solve direct cost responsibility. It does not, by itself, solve regional cost causation, load verification, or stranded-asset risk."

  He also raised the problem of distinguishing speculative load requests from projects likely to be built, arguing that load at different development stages — requested, studied, contracted, collateralized, site-controlled, permitted, financeable, construction-ready, power-deliverable, and operational — "should not receive the same planning weight."

  ## Shifting costs to large customers

  The hearings also showed that Virginia's recently approved transmission minimum demand charges are already redistributing transmission costs toward large-load customers. Brooks testified, citing Dominion's revised Rider T1 schedules, that the updated allocation shifts roughly 90% of the increased transmission responsibility to the GS-4 large-load class while reducing costs for residential customers. Asked whether the revised figures showed the new charges functioning as intended, Brooks replied, "It certainly seems so from these numbers."

  Separately, Dominion's June rebuttal filing reduced its projected monthly residential bill impact of Rider T1 from approximately $2.90 to $0.94 after incorporating updated load forecasts and the new transmission minimum demand charges.

  ## What it means

  The Amazon proposal places Virginia regulators at the center of a policy question with no established precedent in transmission rulemaking: how to assign infrastructure costs when a single AI campus can drive demand large enough to require new high-voltage facilities whose effects ripple across the regional grid. Experts at the hearing drew a consistent distinction between direct cost responsibility, which CIAC can address, and the broader problems of regional cost causation and stranded-asset risk, which it cannot resolve on its own.

  The SCC has not indicated when it will issue a final order in the Rider T1 proceeding.
body_zh: |
  亞馬遜近日敦促維吉尼亞州公用事業監管機關，允許超大規模資料中心業者自願出資，興建專門服務其園區的輸電基礎設施，並就電網擴建費用如何在所有用電戶之間分攤，向該州主管機關提出新的監管課題。

  這項提案於本月浮出檯面，緣起於維吉尼亞州公司委員會（State Corporation Commission，SCC）就多明尼恩維吉尼亞能源公司（Dominion Energy Virginia）年度「T1費率附加方案」（Rider T1）所舉行的聽證程序——此一機制係多明尼恩向用電戶回收輸電成本的法定管道。亞馬遜主張，大型用電戶應被允許透過「建設援助捐款」（contributions in aid of construction，CIAC）的方式，自行出資承擔專為其項目所建輸電設施的費用。CIAC模式目前已應用於發電機併網及部分配電項目。

  ## 用電戶自行出資輸電的理由

  亞馬遜證人、E9 Insight顧問公司總裁卡麥隆·布魯克斯（Cameron Brooks）在聽證中作證表示，若允許超大規模用電戶就輸電設施提供CIAC，這些業者便能「自行承擔資本風險」，同時透過將擱置資產風險——即昂貴基礎設施若使用率不足所衍生的財務損失——轉嫁給製造新需求的業者，進而降低其他用電戶的費用負擔。

  「是否允許資料中心以自有資本承擔建設費用、自己出錢、自負盈虧，委員會其實……沒有理由不能立即採取行動，」布魯克斯依據Data Center Knowledge所查閱的聽證紀錄如此作證。

  ## 多明尼恩的反對立場

  多明尼恩維吉尼亞能源對此持異議，認為Rider T1程序並非處理此議題的適當場域。該公用事業公司主張，輸電領域的CIAC涉及更廣泛的課題，包括區域輸電組織PJM互聯（PJM Interconnection）、聯邦能源監管委員會（Federal Energy Regulatory Commission，FERC）的管轄權及零售電價設計等，這些問題應另設專案程序處理。

  將CIAC原則應用於輸電領域，比發電機併網更為複雜，原因在於高壓輸電設施往往服務多個用電戶，並在PJM依FERC核准的規劃程序下承擔區域系統可靠度的需求，難以將其視為單一用電戶的專屬資產。

  ## 區域層面的複雜性

  布拉托集團（The Brattle Group）主任約翰尼斯·法芬伯格（Johannes Pfeifenberger）向委員會表示，當輸電升級的受益方明確可辨且願意出資時，CIAC「可作為部分解方」。他同時提醒，最具成本效益的輸電項目往往同時解決多項區域需求——包括容納新增負載、整合發電資源及提升系統可靠度——這使得哪些費用應由特定用電戶負擔、哪些應由電網共同承擔，界線難以釐清。

  委員凱爾西·巴戈特（Commissioner Kelsey Bagot）就自願性CIAC將如何與維吉尼亞州甫通過的GS-5電價方案互動，以及用電戶是否可能就同一輸電設施重複付費，向布魯克斯追問。布魯克斯坦承存在不確定性：「說實話，我可能也不確定。」他表示多明尼恩和委員會更有能力釐清執行細節，但仍將自願性CIAC定性為GS-5已納入保護機制之外「合乎邏輯的下一步」。

  Persistence Analytics Group創辦人尼爾·奧斯納托（Neil Osnato）表示，這場聽證反映出一項更普遍的挑戰——在有組織的電力市場中，監管機關正試圖合理分攤服務AI快速增長負載所產生的費用。他警告，允許私人出資不應形成「事實上的快速通道」，進而排擠PJM規劃程序中更成熟或對系統效益更大的項目。他指出，單一大型園區的輸電線路可能改變整個電網的潮流分布、事故應變需求、變電站配置、在地可靠度需求及未來擴建機會，使得個別用電戶的費用責任難以與電網整體效應切割。

  奧斯納托為監管機關設下核心考驗：一項提案是否要求用電戶「將其負載所帶來的全部增量基礎設施後果內部化，同時保全PJM高效規劃區域系統的能力」。他對CIAC侷限性的評估相當直接：「CIAC能解決直接成本責任的問題。但僅憑CIAC本身，無法解決區域成本歸因、負載核實或擱置資產風險等問題。」

  他也提出如何區分投機性負載申請與可能付諸實施的項目此一難題，主張不同開發階段的負載——包括已申請、已研究、已簽約、已提供擔保品、已取得場址控制權、已獲許可、已具融資能力、已備妥施工條件、已具電力供應能力及已投入運營——「不應在規劃評估中獲得相同的權重」。

  ## 大型用電戶承擔更多費用

  此次聽證亦顯示，維吉尼亞州近期核准的輸電最低需求電費，已開始將輸電費用重新分配至大型用電戶身上。布魯克斯援引多明尼恩修訂後的Rider T1費率表作證，指出更新後的分攤方式將約九成的增量輸電費用責任轉移至GS-4大型負載用電級別，同時降低了住宅用電戶的負擔。當被問及修訂後的數據是否顯示新電費機制發揮了預期功效，布魯克斯回答：「從這些數字來看，看來確實如此。」

  另外，多明尼恩於六月提交的反駁書將Rider T1對住宅月電費的預估衝擊，從約2.90美元降至0.94美元，更新依據為最新負載預測及新版輸電最低需求電費的納入。

  ## 政策意義

  亞馬遜的提案使維吉尼亞州監管機關站上一個在輸電規則制定上毫無先例可循的政策核心：當單一AI資料中心園區所驅動的需求，大到足以要求新建高壓設施，且其影響波及整個區域電網時，應如何合理分攤基礎設施費用。聽證會上的專家一致點出直接成本責任與更深層問題之間的根本差異——CIAC或可解決直接成本責任，但區域成本歸因與擱置資產風險，單憑CIAC並無法獨立化解。

  SCC迄今尚未表示何時將就Rider T1程序做出最終裁定。
