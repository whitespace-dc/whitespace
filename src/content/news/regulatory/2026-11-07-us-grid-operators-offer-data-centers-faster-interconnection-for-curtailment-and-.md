body_en: |
  US electricity markets are conditioning faster data center interconnection on financial guarantees, curtailment obligations, and real-time operational transparency, as regional grid operators build frameworks aimed at reconciling the rapid pace of AI infrastructure buildout with the slower timelines of traditional grid expansion.

  The pattern, described in an analysis published by Utility Dive and written by Seyed Madaeni, CEO and co-founder of Verse, an energy software company specializing in battery dispatch optimization for data centers, has taken distinct forms in Ohio, Texas, the Southwest Power Pool (SPP), and PJM Interconnection — each the administrator of a large regional electricity grid — but shares a common logic: a data center that wants faster or more certain access to power should give the grid greater certainty in return.

  Madaeni frames the current environment as one in which even well-funded projects face a binary choice: wait years for traditional grid upgrades, or build private onsite power plants, the latter carrying its own fuel-supply, equipment, permitting, and community challenges. The new frameworks represent what the analysis describes as a third path.

  ## Ohio requires large customers to pay for capacity they reserve

  Ohio has addressed the financial risk that utilities absorb when they build infrastructure against uncertain demand. After AEP Ohio — one of the state's major electric utilities — proposed a data-center-specific tariff, state regulators approved a settlement requiring large customers to pay for up to 85% of their subscribed capacity even when they consume less than that amount. The agreement also includes longer contract terms and additional collateral requirements.

  In exchange, a connection moratorium that had been in effect was lifted and developers received a defined framework for service, according to the analysis.

  The structure is designed to protect existing electricity customers from paying for substations, transmission equipment, and other infrastructure built against demand that never materializes. It also distinguishes serious development projects from speculative interconnection requests, Madaeni writes.

  ## Texas and SPP trade operational flexibility for faster studies

  The Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), which operates the Texas grid, has set technical and operating requirements for large new loads as part of a new Batch Zero interconnection process. A facility must meet ERCOT's ride-through requirements — the ability to remain connected and operating during grid disturbances — alongside required modeling, stability, and interconnection reviews.

  Batch Zero includes two pathways for developers seeking earlier energization: a controllable-load pathway and a bring-your-own-generation (BYOG) pathway. Under both, developers can elect dispatchable curtailment, agreeing to reduce grid consumption on ERCOT's command in real time when the system is stressed. That reduction can be achieved by curtailing consumption, switching to onsite resources, or coordinating multiple assets.

  The Southwest Power Pool, a regional transmission organization serving parts of the central US, has established a High Impact Large Load framework with similar terms. Developers that accept real-time forecast reporting, ramp-rate limits on how quickly they can change consumption, and remote-disconnect capability can have their interconnection study completed within 90 days, clearing the way for a service agreement shortly afterward.

  ## PJM proposal mostly rejected by FERC

  PJM Interconnection, which operates the grid across all or parts of 13 Mid-Atlantic and Midwestern states, proposed an Interim Resource Adequacy Service framework under which certain new large loads could connect even when the region lacked enough generating capacity to meet all forecast demand at its traditional reliability standard. Loads that did not bring qualifying new generation, storage, or demand-side resources — or did not receive an applicable capacity allocation — would have been required to reduce grid consumption during limited emergency conditions.

  The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), the US agency overseeing interstate electricity transmission, rejected most of the framework in October on jurisdictional grounds, according to the analysis. FERC did, however, encourage states to decide which retail customers are curtailed when the system is stressed, keeping the same underlying trade available through state-level action.

  Madaeni notes that bringing new capacity into PJM does not eliminate transmission and distribution constraints, which still require local upgrades, engineering studies, and regulatory approvals.

  ## FERC directive extends scrutiny to all regional operators

  Beyond the individual market frameworks, FERC in June directed all six regional grid operators under its jurisdiction to justify their existing large-load tariffs or propose reforms. States are also moving in parallel through large-load tariffs and other requirements intended to ensure that data centers pay their share and manage their impact on the grid.

  The resulting landscape is fragmented. Requirements differ significantly from one utility or region to the next, making it difficult for developers to compare potential sites, price the obligations attached to service agreements, and design energy infrastructure from the outset of a project, the analysis says. Developers currently learn a site's specific obligations only after spending time and money on evaluation.

  ## Significance for developers and grid planners

  The spread of performance-based interconnection frameworks marks a shift in how grid operators are managing the surge in large-load requests driven by data center and AI infrastructure growth. Rather than granting access on the basis of promised economic development, the frameworks described in the analysis condition speed and certainty of service on what the load can guarantee: financial commitment, operational dispatchability, or new capacity brought to the grid.

  The analysis calls on utilities and grid operators to publish clear menus of performance-based pathways, specifying what a developer can commit to, what the grid will require, and what level of service each commitment unlocks. That transparency, Madaeni argues, would let utilities distinguish real projects from speculative requests, allow developers to understand the cost of speed before committing to a site, and give regulators a firmer basis for evaluating whether an arrangement protects reliability and existing customers.

  FERC's June directive requiring all six regional operators to justify or propose reforms to their large-load tariffs sets the next formal deadline for how those frameworks will be evaluated at the federal level.

  ## 美國電網業者以削減用電與財務承諾換取資料中心快速併網

  美國電力市場正以財務擔保、削減用電義務及即時運營透明度為前提，提供資料中心更快速的併網機會。各區域電網業者積極建立框架，試圖在AI基礎設施快速擴張的步伐與傳統電網建設的漫長時程之間取得平衡。

  上述趨勢出自能源資訊平台Utility Dive發表的一份分析報告，作者為Verse執行長暨共同創辦人Seyed Madaeni。Verse是一家專門為資料中心提供電池調度最佳化服務的能源軟體公司。這種模式在俄亥俄州、德州、西南電力池（Southwest Power Pool，SPP）及PJM互聯電網——各自管理一大區域電力網路——中呈現出不同樣貌，但共同邏輯一致：資料中心若想更快、更確定地取得電力，就必須回饋電網同等的確定性。

  Madaeni將當前環境形容為一種二選一的困境，即便資金充裕的專案也難逃其中——要麼等待數年以完成傳統電網升級，要麼自建廠區電廠，而後者又帶來燃料供應、設備採購、許可申請與社區溝通等重重挑戰。新框架所代表的，正是這份分析所稱的「第三條路」。

  ## 俄亥俄州要求大用電戶為預訂容量付費

  俄亥俄州針對的是電力公司在面對不確定需求時所承擔的財務風險。AEP Ohio——該州主要電力公司之一——提出資料中心專屬費率方案後，州級監管機構批准了一份和解協議，要求大用電戶在實際用電量低於預訂容量時，仍須支付最多85%的預訂容量費用。協議中還包含更長的合約期限與額外的保證金要求。

  作為交換條件，原本實施中的接連禁令獲得解除，開發商也取得明確的服務申請框架，報告如此指出。

  這套結構旨在保護現有電力用戶，避免他們為了最終未能實現的需求所建設的變電站、輸電設備及其他基礎設施買單。Madaeni寫道，此舉也有助於區分認真推動的開發案與投機性的併網申請。

  ## 德州與SPP以運營彈性換取加速審查

  德州電力可靠度委員會（Electric Reliability Council of Texas，ERCOT）主管德州電網，已針對大型新增負載設立技術與運營要求，作為新「批次零號」（Batch Zero）併網流程的一部分。設施必須符合ERCOT的「穿越」要求——即在電網發生擾動時維持連線運作的能力——並完成所需的建模、穩定性與併網審查。

  批次零號為尋求提前供電的開發商提供兩條路徑：可控負載路徑與自備發電（Bring-Your-Own-Generation，BYOG）路徑。在兩條路徑下，開發商均可選擇接受可調度削減，同意在電網壓力下即時依ERCOT指令降低用電量。削減方式可透過減少用電、切換至廠區自備資源，或協調多項資產來達成。

  服務中部美國部分地區的區域輸電組織西南電力池，已建立「高影響大負載框架」（High Impact Large Load），條款相近。開發商若接受即時預測回報、用電變化速率限制，以及遠端斷線能力，其併網研究可在90天內完成，隨後便可推進服務協議的簽署。

  ## PJM提案大多遭FERC否決

  PJM互聯電網管理大西洋中部及中西部共13個州的全部或部分地區，提出了「臨時資源適足性服務」（Interim Resource Adequacy Service）框架，允許特定新增大負載在區域缺乏足夠發電容量、無法以傳統可靠度標準滿足全部預測需求的情況下仍可併網。若負載未引入符合資格的新發電、儲能或需量側資源，或未獲得相應的容量配額，則在有限度的緊急情況下須減少電網用電量。

  根據報告，聯邦能源管理委員會（Federal Energy Regulatory Commission，FERC）——負責監管美國州際輸電的聯邦機構——以管轄權為由，於十月否決了大部分框架。不過，FERC確實鼓勵各州自行決定在電網壓力下應削減哪些零售用戶，使同樣的基本交換機制得以透過州級行動延續。

  Madaeni指出，為PJM引入新容量並不能消除輸電與配電的瓶頸，這些問題仍需透過地方升級、工程評估與監管審批來解決。

  ## FERC指令將審查範圍擴及所有區域業者

  除各市場個別框架外，FERC已於今年六月要求轄下全部六家區域電網業者說明現行大用電戶費率的合理性，或提出改革方案。各州也同步透過大用電戶費率及其他要求，確保資料中心承擔應有的費用並管控其對電網的衝擊。

  由此形成的監管環境相當分散。不同電力公司或地區之間的要求差異懸殊，使開發商難以比較潛在基地、為服務協議所附帶的義務定價，並在專案初期就規劃好能源基礎設施。報告指出，開發商目前往往要在投入大量時間與金錢評估後，才能得知特定基地的具體義務。

  ## 對開發商與電網規劃者的意義

  以績效為基礎的併網框架日益普及，標誌著電網業者管理資料中心與AI基礎設施成長所帶動的大量大負載申請的方式正在轉變。過去，業者依據承諾帶來的經濟發展來決定是否給予接入許可；如今，框架的設計邏輯是以負載能提供的承諾——財務擔保、可調度削減能力，或引入新容量——來決定服務的速度與確定性。

  該分析呼籲電力公司與電網業者公開清晰的績效導向路徑選單，明確列出開發商可承諾的事項、電網的相應要求，以及各項承諾所對應的服務水準。Madaeni主張，這樣的透明度能讓電力公司區分真實專案與投機申請，讓開發商在確定基地前就能了解加速併網的成本，也讓監管機構有更扎實的基礎，評估相關安排是否確實保障了電網可靠度與現有用戶的權益。

  FERC六月要求六家區域業者說明或提出大用電戶費率改革方案的指令，為這些框架在聯邦層級的評估設定了下一個正式期限。
