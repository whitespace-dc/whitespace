body_en: |
  Norway's national grid operator Statnett halted new grid connection reservations for data centres on November 9, with the government simultaneously announcing plans for a licensing scheme that would restrict which facilities can be built in the country, favouring critical-infrastructure operators over global cloud companies and cryptocurrency firms.

  The pause is expected to end no later than August 1, 2027. Digitalization and Public Governance Minister Torgeir Micaelsen framed the move as matching supply to national need. "We want data centers in Norway, but we can't have all types of data centers," he said at a press conference. "We must therefore control the growth and ensure that Norway gets the data centers we actually need — those that provide the greatest societal benefit."

  ## The scale of the demand problem

  The figures behind the decision illustrate the pressure on the grid. Some 800 megawatts (MW) of data centre capacity is currently connected to the Norwegian grid. Beyond that, 4,200MW has reserved capacity, 9,100MW is in the queue, and requests cover a further 18,000MW — a pipeline of roughly 31,300MW in addition to what is already operating. Norway's national peak electricity demand stands at just over 25,000MW, meaning the combined pipeline of connected, reserved, queued, and requested capacity alone is equivalent to more than twice the country's peak load.

  Energy Minister Terje Aasland said Statnett received applications for approximately 26,000MW of new connections in 2026 alone — more than the country's entire existing generation capacity — with data centres accounting for three-quarters of those requests. Norwegian data centres consumed 3.2 terawatt-hours (TWh) of electricity last year, four times the level recorded three years earlier, according to a Menon Economics report presented by the government.

  Statnett framed its decision primarily as a system-security measure. The operator said it needs time to set requirements covering when data centres must remain connected during grid faults, how quickly their load can change, and how to reduce the risk of grid oscillations. Those requirements will go out for public consultation and require approval from energy regulator RME before they take effect.

  ## What the pause covers — and what it does not

  The halt applies to new connection reservations only. Sites already connected to the grid are unaffected, as are facilities that have already received approval for their technical requirements. Statnett said it will also consider some applications from smaller sites and projects close to commissioning.

  ## The licensing scheme

  The government's plans go further than grid access. Under the proposed framework, operators would need a government licence to establish and run a data centre in Norway, with conditions attached. The scheme is designed to favour facilities serving defence, police and emergency preparedness, health services, and other critical infrastructure including mobile networks and the financial system. The government said it will also consider conditions that support AI computing capacity for Norway, the Nordic region, and Europe.

  Crypto operations will not qualify as socially beneficial under the scheme. Global cloud companies will also face restricted access to land and power. Micaelsen said the plan is "about developing the industry, not winding it down."

  New data centres will be expected to pay for the grid costs they generate. The government said it also wants to discuss how the industry can fund new generation capacity, including gas-fired power with carbon capture and storage and offshore wind. The main licensing principles are due by autumn 2027.

  ## National interest and foreign ownership

  Foreign companies own more than 90 percent of Norway's data centre capacity, according to the Norwegian Technology Council, an independent body that advises the government. The council published a report last month noting that data centres provide few jobs beyond the construction phase, and that revenue from digital services run from Norwegian facilities rarely remains in the country.

  "Data centers are a key infrastructure in our society, but with the pace of development we are seeing now, it becomes necessary to ask the question of scope and how we can align policy so that we get the most out of scarce resources," said Tore Tennøe, the council's director. The body plans to publish a further report later this year with recommendations on regulating the sector.

  Cheap hydropower has historically attracted data centre operators to Norway, which remains a net electricity exporter. The Norwegian Water Resources and Energy Directorate has warned, however, that continued growth in data centre demand could produce a power deficit and push up electricity prices for other consumers.

  Norway had previously imposed a temporary ban on data centres that mine proof-of-work cryptocurrencies. Existing operators with a presence in the country include Microsoft, Nscale, TikTok (operating through Green Mountain), and CoreWeave. Among the projects in development is Nscale's 230MW campus in Narvik, where more than 30,000 Nvidia Rubin GPUs are scheduled for delivery to Microsoft in 2027.

  ## A narrowing Nordic market

  Norway joins a wider Nordic pattern of restrictions on data centre grid access. Denmark's grid operator Energinet paused new connection agreements in March, and a four-tier priority system in force since October 12 places most data centres last unless they serve critical social functions. Sweden had 6,984MW of data centre applications in its transmission queue as of September 1, and its grid operator Svenska kraftnät is considering temporary restrictions on applications with large individual power demands while it reviews how major connections are assessed. Finland has proposed its own four-tier system that would move projects above 10MW further back in the queue.

  The tightening comes as hyperscale operators have been examining Europe as an alternative to the United States. Meta president Dina Powell McCormick said last week that new US builds will be harder and that Meta is looking at other countries, with "every hyperscaler" now asking whether to build outside the US, the Wall Street Journal reported. More than 100 European groups are calling for a moratorium on large-scale data centres, and StackPath counts 22 live or proposed restrictions across 10 countries.

  ## What comes next

  Statnett's technical requirements are subject to consultation and approval by energy regulator RME. The grid reservation pause is set to end by August 1, 2027, before the licensing principles are expected that autumn. A key unresolved question is whether the eventual licensing regime will be applied to projects that already hold grid reservations under the current system.
body_zh: |
  挪威國家電網業者Statnett於11月9日起暫停受理資料中心新增併網預約，政府同步宣布研擬許可證制度，限制境內可興建的資料中心類型，優先支持關鍵基礎設施業者，對全球雲端公司及加密貨幣業者設下門檻。

  暫停期最遲將於2027年8月1日解除。數位化暨公共治理部長托爾蓋爾·米凱爾森（Torgeir Micaelsen）將此舉定調為使供給與國家需求相符。他在記者會上表示：「我們歡迎資料中心進駐挪威，但不能接納所有類型的資料中心。我們必須管控成長速度，確保挪威獲得真正需要的資料中心——也就是能帶來最大社會效益的設施。」

  ## 需求規模問題

  促成這項決策的數字清楚說明了電網所承受的壓力。目前已有約800百萬瓦（MW）的資料中心容量接入挪威電網，此外還有4,200MW已取得預約容量、9,100MW正在排隊等候，另有涵蓋18,000MW的申請需求——合計目前正在運作容量以外，尚有約31,300MW的管線有待消化。挪威全國尖峰用電需求僅略超過25,000MW，意味著已連接、已預約、排隊等候及申請中的容量加總，已超過全國尖峰負載的兩倍以上。

  能源部長特耶·奧斯蘭（Terje Aasland）表示，光是2026年一年，Statnett便收到約26,000MW新增併網的申請——已超過挪威現有全部發電容量——其中資料中心佔申請量的四分之三。根據政府援引的Menon Economics報告，挪威資料中心去年用電量達3.2兆瓦時（TWh），是三年前的四倍。

  Statnett將此決策主要定位為系統安全措施。該業者表示，需要時間制定相關技術要求，涵蓋資料中心在電網故障期間必須維持連接的條件、負載變動的速度限制，以及降低電網振盪風險的方法。上述要求將提交公眾諮詢，並須獲得能源主管機關RME核准後方可生效。

  ## 暫停範圍及例外

  此次暫停僅適用於新增併網預約。已連接電網的既有設施不受影響，已獲技術要求核准的設施亦然。Statnett表示，亦將考量部分小型設施及接近啟用階段的專案申請。

  ## 許可證制度

  政府的規劃遠不止電網接入的層面。依照擬議框架，業者須取得政府許可證方能在挪威設立及營運資料中心，並附帶相關條件。此制度旨在優先支持服務國防、警察與緊急應變、醫療服務，以及行動網路和金融體系等關鍵基礎設施的設施。政府表示，亦將考量支持挪威、北歐地區及歐洲人工智慧運算能力的相關條件。

  加密貨幣業務在此制度下不具社會效益資格。全球雲端公司的土地與電力取得亦將受到限制。米凱爾森表示，此計劃「著眼於發展產業，而非將其關閉。」

  新設資料中心將須承擔其所產生的電網建設費用。政府也表示希望與業界討論如何共同資助新增發電容量，包括結合碳捕捉與封存技術的天然氣發電，以及離岸風電。主要許可原則預計於2027年秋季公布。

  ## 國家利益與外資持有問題

  根據挪威獨立政府諮詢機構「挪威科技委員會」（Norwegian Technology Council）的資料，外資持有挪威資料中心容量逾九成。該委員會上月發布報告指出，資料中心在建設階段之後所提供的就業機會極為有限，且從挪威境內設施所提供的數位服務中所獲得的營收，鮮少留在國內。

  委員會主任托勒·滕諾（Tore Tennøe）表示：「資料中心是我們社會的重要基礎設施，但以目前的發展速度，有必要思考規模的問題，以及如何調整政策，讓我們能從有限資源中獲得最大效益。」該委員會計劃於今年稍晚發布另一份報告，提出對該產業的監管建議。

  廉價的水力發電在歷史上吸引了資料中心業者進駐挪威，挪威目前仍是電力淨出口國。然而，挪威水資源與能源局警告，資料中心需求若持續成長，可能導致電力短缺，並推高其他消費者的電費。

  挪威此前曾暫時禁止採用工作量證明（proof-of-work）機制的加密貨幣挖礦資料中心。目前在挪威設有據點的現有業者包括微軟（Microsoft）、Nscale、TikTok（透過Green Mountain營運）及CoreWeave。正在開發中的專案包括Nscale位於納爾維克（Narvik）的230MW園區，預計將於2027年向微軟交付逾三萬張輝達（NVIDIA）Rubin GPU。

  ## 北歐市場門檻收窄

  挪威加入了北歐各國限制資料中心電網接入的行列。丹麥電網業者Energinet於3月暫停新增併網協議，自10月12日起實施的四級優先制度將大多數資料中心列於最末順位——除非其服務具備重要社會功能。截至9月1日，瑞典輸電網申請佇列中的資料中心申請量已達6,984MW，其電網業者Svenska kraftnät正考慮對大型個別用電需求的申請實施臨時限制，同時檢討重大併網申請的評估方式。芬蘭則提出自己的四級制度，將超過10MW的專案推至較後順位。

  此波收緊趨勢恰逢超大規模雲端業者積極評估以歐洲作為美國的替代建設地點。Meta總裁黛娜·鮑威爾·麥科米克（Dina Powell McCormick）上週表示，美國新建案難度日增，Meta正關注其他國家；《華爾街日報》報導指出，「每家超大規模業者」都在評估是否在美國以外進行建設。逾100個歐洲團體呼籲對大型資料中心實施暫停令，StackPath統計目前10個國家共有22項已生效或擬議中的限制措施。

  ## 後續動向

  Statnett的技術要求須經諮詢程序及能源主管機關RME核准。電網預約暫停期預計最遲於2027年8月1日解除，許可原則預計於當年秋季出爐。目前尚待釐清的關鍵問題是：最終的許可制度是否將適用於現行制度下已取得電網預約的專案。
