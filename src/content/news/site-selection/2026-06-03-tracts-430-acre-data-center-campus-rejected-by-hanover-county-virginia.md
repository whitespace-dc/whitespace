body_en: |
  Local officials in Hanover County, Virginia have rejected a proposal by data center developer Tract to build a large-scale campus on a 430-acre site, handing a victory to community opposition groups that had organized against the project.

  ## A Large-Scale Proposal Turned Down

  The 430-acre footprint would have ranked among the larger data center land assemblies in Virginia — a state that hosts the world's densest concentration of hyperscale data center capacity in its Northern Virginia corridor. Hanover County sits north of Richmond in central Virginia, a region that has drawn growing developer interest as land and power availability tighten in the established Loudoun County and Prince William County markets closer to Washington, D.C.

  Specific capacity details for the Tract project — power draw, server hall square footage, phasing schedule — were not publicly disclosed, but a 430-acre land position suggests an intent to build a multi-building campus with the potential for a substantial multi-phase build-out over time.

  ## Opposition Groups Prevail

  The rejection is a meaningful win for local opposition groups that had mobilized against the development. Community resistance to data center projects has grown alongside the industry's rapid expansion across Virginia; concerns commonly raised at public hearings include traffic generation, strain on electrical transmission infrastructure, water consumption for cooling systems, visual impact on rural and suburban landscapes, and limited job creation relative to the land area consumed.

  Hanover County officials sided with those concerns at the local approval stage — the point in the development pipeline where organized community opposition most frequently succeeds in blocking projects outright.

  ## What It Means for Site Selectors

  The decision is a practical reminder that Virginia's broadly data center-friendly policy environment does not translate uniformly into local approvals across all jurisdictions. While the Commonwealth has enacted legislation to streamline permitting processes and maintain sales tax exemptions for qualifying data center investment, land-use decisions remain local prerogatives, and counties outside Northern Virginia's established data center corridors are showing increasing willingness to reject large-scale proposals.

  For developers treating central Virginia as an overflow market for capacity-constrained Northern Virginia, the vote underscores that community engagement strategy — not just power availability, fiber connectivity, or state-level incentives — has become a determinative factor in site selection outcomes.

  Tract has not publicly indicated whether it plans to appeal the decision, pursue an alternative site in the region, or revise and resubmit a modified project scope.

  ## 大規模開發案遭否決

  這座 430 英畝的開發案，規模堪稱維吉尼亞州迄今最大的資料中心土地開發案之一。維吉尼亞州北部走廊是全球超大規模資料中心密度最高的地區，而漢諾威郡位於里奇蒙以北的中部維吉尼亞——隨著華府近郊勞登郡與普林斯威廉郡的土地與電力供應日趨緊張，開發商近年對此區域的興趣持續升溫。

  Tract 計畫的具體容量細節——包括用電量、機房面積與分期進度——並未見於公開揭露，但 430 英畝的基地規模顯示其長期目標是打造多棟建物、分多期大規模開發的超大型園區。

  ## 反對力量佔上風

  此次否決是當地反對團體長期動員的成果。隨著資料中心產業在維吉尼亞州快速擴張，社區反對聲浪也持續升高；公聽會上常見的疑慮包括：交通衝擊、電力輸配基礎設施承載壓力、冷卻系統用水消耗、對農村與郊區景觀的視覺影響，以及相對於所佔土地偏低的就業創造效益。

  漢諾威郡官員最終在地方審批階段採納了上述顧慮，將開發案予以否決——而此一環節，正是有組織的社區反對力量最常成功阻擋開發案的關鍵節點。

  ## 對選址規劃的啟示

  漢諾威郡的決定再次提醒業界：維吉尼亞州整體友善資料中心的政策環境，並不代表各地轄區的審批態度一致。儘管維吉尼亞州已立法簡化許可程序並延續符合資格的資料中心銷售稅減免，土地使用的核准權仍由地方主導，且北維吉尼亞既有廊帶以外的郡縣愈來愈有意願對大型開發案說不。

  對於將中部維吉尼亞視為北維吉尼亞容量壓力出口的開發商而言，此次投票結果傳達了明確訊號：社區溝通策略——而非僅僅電力供應、光纖連線或州級獎勵措施——已成為影響選址結果的關鍵決定因素。

  Tract 目前尚未公開說明是否計劃提起上訴、在該地區尋找替代用地，或修改計畫規模後重新申請。
