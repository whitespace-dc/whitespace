body_en: |
  Australian biotech startup Cortical Labs has activated Singapore's first biological data centre, housed at the National University of Singapore's Centre for Life Sciences, where computing tasks are processed by living human neurons rather than conventional silicon chips. The facility went live on July 16 in a joint research and development partnership between Cortical Labs, NUS, and data centre operator DayOne, a tie-up first announced in March.

  The Singapore site currently operates 20 units of Cortical Labs' CL1 biological computer. Each CL1 houses at least 200,000 lab-grown neurons mounted on an electrode-fitted silicon chip. Those neurons — derived from blood cells reprogrammed into stem cells — exchange electrical signals with a computer, and their neural activity is translated into raw computing power. Every three days, laboratory technicians feed the cells a cocktail of sugar, micronutrients, and pH buffers; a gas mixer supplies carbon dioxide, oxygen, and nitrogen as a dedicated life-support system.

  Cortical Labs has previously commercialised a biological data centre in Melbourne, which it describes as the first in the world. That facility currently runs 120 CL1 units and counts around 20 paying customers, including corporate research arms and universities working on robotics and gaming applications.

  ## Pricing and energy consumption

  Customers pay US$2,200 (S$2,800) per month to access the computing resources of one CL1, according to Cortical Labs founder and chief executive Chong Hon Weng. That is roughly half the US$4,300 monthly fee charged by major cloud platforms for renting a high-end AI chip for training and running AI models, Chong said during a facility tour on August 7.

  Energy consumption represents the sharpest contrast with conventional hardware. Each CL1 draws 30 watts per unit, including its life-support systems — less than a handheld calculator, according to Chong. By comparison, Nvidia's H100 SXM accelerator can consume up to 700 watts under demanding workloads, and a typical data centre server housing eight H100 chips consumes approximately 10,200 watts including supporting hardware. Because brain cells generate negligible heat, biological data centres require no active cooling infrastructure.

  ## Why Singapore, and why now

  Singapore imposed a temporary moratorium on new data centre construction in 2019, citing the intensity of energy and water demands. Data centres accounted for approximately 5.3 per cent of the country's total electricity consumption in 2019, a share that rose to 7 per cent by 2020 as the Covid-19 pandemic accelerated digitalisation. Chong identified Singapore's position as a major fibre connectivity hub in Asia-Pacific as the commercial rationale for establishing a presence there, while acknowledging that electricity and water constraints make the country a natural target for low-footprint computing alternatives.

  ## Intended applications and technical limits

  Chong said biological data centres are best suited to applications where training datasets are limited and operating conditions are highly unpredictable. He cited humanoid robotics in public spaces, homes, offices, and industrial settings as one use case, arguing that there is insufficient real-world data to train humanoid robots for all required tasks using traditional servers. Cybersecurity anomaly detection, where spotting unusual behaviour must occur without large training datasets, was identified as another candidate application.

  Chong was explicit that conventional silicon chips remain far superior for the fast, precise, and repeatable calculations that underpin large language models such as ChatGPT. The biological approach draws its advantage from the ability to generalise from a small number of examples and adapt when circumstances change — a characteristic Chong likened to human learning.

  ## Scale-up and research agenda

  Cortical Labs plans to expand the Singapore facility to as many as 1,000 CL1 units, subject to regulatory approval and the results of energy efficiency and safety tests. Rickie Patani, a neuroscience professor at NUS's Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine who oversees the biological data centre, said the facility will be used to identify which neuron types and support cells work best together, with the aim of building a scientific basis for manufacturing those cell types at scale for data centre operations. Cortical Labs also intends to use the NUS site to establish the manpower requirements and skill sets needed for commercial deployment in Singapore.
body_zh: |
  澳洲生技新創公司Cortical Labs已在新加坡國立大學（NUS）生命科學中心，啟用新加坡首座生物資料中心。這座設施以活體人類神經元取代傳統矽晶片執行運算工作，於7月16日正式上線，是Cortical Labs、新加坡國立大學與資料中心業者DayOne三方共同研發合作的成果——該合作計畫最早於今年3月宣布。

  新加坡站目前運行20台Cortical Labs的CL1生物電腦。每台CL1至少容納20萬個實驗室培育的神經元，這些神經元被安裝在配有電極陣列的矽晶片上。神經元由血液細胞重新編程為幹細胞後再分化而來，可透過電訊號與電腦進行雙向溝通，其神經活動最終轉化為原始運算能力。每隔三天，實驗室技術人員會為細胞補充含糖分、微量營養素與pH緩衝液的培養液；一套氣體混合系統則持續供應二氧化碳、氧氣與氮氣，作為專屬的生命維持系統。

  Cortical Labs早先已在墨爾本將生物資料中心商業化，並稱其為全球首座。該設施目前運行120台CL1裝置，擁有約20名付費客戶，包括從事機器人及電玩遊戲應用研究的企業研究部門與大學機構。

  ## 收費與能耗

  客戶每月支付2,200美元（約新幣2,800元）即可存取一台CL1的運算資源，Cortical Labs創辦人暨執行長莊漢榮（Chong Hon Weng）表示。他在8月7日的設施參觀活動中指出，這個價格大約是主要雲端平台租用高階AI晶片（用於訓練與執行AI模型）每月收費4,300美元的一半。

  能源消耗是與傳統硬體最鮮明的對比。每台CL1含生命維持系統在內耗電僅30瓦，莊漢榮說，比一台手持計算機還省電。相比之下，輝達H100 SXM加速器在高負載下最高可消耗700瓦，而搭載八塊H100晶片的典型資料中心伺服器（含支援硬體）耗電量約達10,200瓦。由於腦細胞幾乎不產生熱能，生物資料中心毋需主動散熱基礎設施。

  ## 落腳新加坡的考量

  新加坡於2019年以能源與用水需求強度過高為由，暫時禁止新建資料中心。資料中心約占新加坡2019年全國總用電量的5.3%，隨著新冠疫情加速數位化進程，這一比例在2020年攀升至7%。莊漢榮表示，新加坡身為亞太地區主要光纖連網樞紐，是Cortical Labs選擇在當地布局的商業考量；他同時坦承，電力與用水限制使新加坡天然成為低耗能運算替代方案的理想目標市場。

  ## 預期應用與技術限制

  莊漢榮指出，生物資料中心最適合訓練資料集有限、且運作環境高度難以預測的應用場景。他以公共空間、住家、辦公室及工業環境中的人形機器人為例，認為現有真實世界資料不足以讓人形機器人透過傳統伺服器學習所有必要任務。資安領域的異常偵測——即在缺乏大量訓練資料的情況下識別異常行為——也被他點名為另一項潛在應用。

  莊漢榮坦言，傳統矽晶片在快速、精確且可重複的運算上仍遠勝於生物方案，而這正是ChatGPT等大型語言模型的核心需求。生物運算的優勢在於能從少量範例中歸納規律、並在環境改變時靈活應對——莊漢榮將這一特性比擬為人類的學習方式。

  ## 規模擴充與研究議程

  Cortical Labs計畫將新加坡設施擴充至最多1,000台CL1裝置，惟仍有待主管機關核准，以及能源效率與安全性測試結果。新加坡國立大學楊潞齡醫學院神經科學教授帕塔尼（Rickie Patani）負責監督這座生物資料中心，他表示該設施將用於找出哪些神經元類型與支持細胞最能協同運作，以期為資料中心規模化量產相關細胞類型奠定科學基礎。Cortical Labs也計畫藉助新大站點，評估在新加坡商業部署所需的人力規模與技能組合。
