body_en: |
  Meta plans to begin mass production of its custom AI chip, codenamed Iris, in September 2026 and has set a target of doubling its total AI computing capacity to 14 gigawatts (GW) by 2027, according to an internal company memorandum cited by Reuters. The company simultaneously announced a C$13 billion (US$9.17 billion) data center in Alberta, Canada — its first in the country — underscoring the breadth of infrastructure spending behind both milestones. Meta declined to comment on the memo.

  ## Iris chip and the MTIA program

  Iris is the lead product in Meta's fourth-generation Meta Training and Inference Accelerator (MTIA) program, which produces custom silicon for training and running AI models on platforms including Facebook and Instagram. Meta designed the chip internally, co-developed it with semiconductor supplier Broadcom, and will have it fabricated by Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC), according to Commercial Times, citing Reuters' reporting on the internal memo.

  The chip's qualification process was notably compressed. Bug and stability testing for Iris lasted only six weeks and found no major problems, the memo stated — a marked acceleration compared with the MTIA program's historically slow development pace over the past five-plus years. Commercial Times characterized the outcome as evidence that Meta's internal chip engineering capability has matured.

  Despite the Iris rollout, the memo makes clear the chip is not designed to displace Meta's existing graphics processing unit (GPU) fleet. Iris will supplement — not replace — GPUs supplied by Nvidia and AMD. Meta acknowledged in the memo that integrating the latest GPUs into a data center of its scale is a complex and time-consuming undertaking; offloading some workloads to custom silicon is intended to improve overall efficiency and reduce cost.

  ## Computing capacity targets

  Meta deployed roughly 1 GW of new AI computing capacity in the first half of 2026 and expects to add approximately 5.5 GW more in the second half, for a full-year deployment of around 7 GW, according to the memo cited by Commercial Times. The 14 GW target for 2027 would represent roughly double the current year's total deployment. One gigawatt of electricity is sufficient to supply approximately 800,000 homes, according to figures provided in connection with the Alberta announcement.

  Meta's AI infrastructure spending this year is projected to reach as much as US$145 billion, the memo indicated — a substantial portion of what Commercial Times reported as more than US$700 billion in total AI investment expected from large technology companies in 2026.

  ## Alberta data center

  Meta announced it will build a 1 GW data center — designed to scale to 1.8 GW — in Sturgeon County, central Alberta. The facility is Meta's 33rd globally and its first in Canada. The announcement was made in Calgary alongside Alberta Premier Danielle Smith and provincial officials.

  Alberta's appeal rests on several factors: abundant natural gas that trades at a discount to the US benchmark price, a cold climate that lowers cooling costs, and a provincial policy permitting new proponents to build their own power generation rather than draw solely from the existing grid, which is approximately 60% natural gas-powered. There are already 20 small to mid-scale data centers operating in the province, according to The Star.

  Meta said it will fully fund new generation and grid infrastructure for the site. Gary Demasi, Meta's vice-president for data center development, said the company will offset the facility's electricity consumption by investing in clean and renewable energy. The data center will use a closed-loop liquid cooling system, Demasi said, resulting in total water consumption lower than that of a typical golf course.

  The company has entered a long-term tolling agreement with Alberta-based Pembina Pipeline, which last week committed to proceed with its Greenlight Electricity Centre — a new natural gas-fired power generation facility also located in Sturgeon County — expected to enter service in late 2030. Until that plant is operational, Alberta-based power producer Capital Power will supply 250 megawatts of electricity from its existing natural gas-fired fleet. The facility will require approximately 150 million cubic feet of natural gas per day, according to Pembina.

  Alberta Technology Minister Nate Glubish said at the announcement that several other gigawatt-scale data center proposals are in various stages of development in the province. "This is the first of its kind, the first of its size, the first of its scale, but it won't be the last," Glubish said.

  ## Supply chain and cost pressures

  Beyond TSMC and Broadcom for the Iris chip, Meta has secured multi-year supply agreements with Samsung Electronics for memory, SanDisk for flash storage, and Japan's Sumitomo Electric Industries for fiber-optic equipment, according to the internal memo. The contracts are intended to lock in critical components before AI-driven demand can exhaust available supply.

  Morgan Stanley analysis cited by Commercial Times warned that memory and related semiconductor prices are rising faster than the market anticipated — a dynamic the publication described as "chip inflation" that is beginning to register as a macroeconomic issue. Technology companies that cannot secure supply chains early may face materially higher infrastructure build-out costs, the analysis indicated.

  ## Industry significance

  Forrester Research Vice President and Principal Analyst Mike Gualtieri told Commercial Times that companies relying entirely on external chip suppliers will find it difficult to lead in AI. He cited Meta, Google, major cloud providers, and SpaceX as organizations pursuing custom AI chip designs as a means of controlling model training costs and competing on pricing for AI services.

  The Iris production timeline and the Alberta announcement together illustrate a structural shift in the AI infrastructure supply chain. Custom application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) such as Iris represent an expanding competitive front that previously centered primarily on GPUs. Sustained demand for advanced semiconductor manufacturing capacity, high-bandwidth memory, optical networking components, and large-scale dedicated power generation is expected to extend through at least 2030, when the Greenlight Electricity Centre supplying the Sturgeon County site is scheduled to come online.
body_zh: |
  根據路透社引述的Meta內部備忘錄，Meta計劃於2026年9月啟動自研AI晶片Iris的量產，並設定2027年將公司整體AI運算容量倍增至14吉瓦（GW）的目標。Meta同時宣布將在加拿大亞伯達省興建一座造價130億加幣（約91.7億美元）的資料中心——也是該公司在加拿大的第一座——充分彰顯了支撐這兩項里程碑背後龐大的基礎建設投資規模。Meta方面拒絕就該備忘錄置評。

  ## Iris晶片與MTIA計畫

  Iris是Meta第四代「Meta訓練與推論加速器」（MTIA）計畫的主力產品，該計畫專門為臉書（Facebook）、Instagram等旗下平台的AI模型訓練與推論運行打造自製晶片。根據工商時報引述路透社對該內部備忘錄的報導，這款晶片由Meta自行設計、與半導體供應商博通（Broadcom）共同開發，並委由台灣積體電路製造公司（台積電，TSMC）負責晶圓代工。

  Iris的驗證流程壓縮得相當顯著。備忘錄指出，Iris的錯誤排除與穩定性測試僅歷時六週，且未發現重大問題——與MTIA計畫過去五年多來歷來緩慢的開發節奏相比，堪稱大幅提速。工商時報將此結果解讀為Meta內部晶片工程能力已趨於成熟的明證。

  儘管Iris即將推出，備忘錄也明確指出這款晶片的定位並非取代現有的繪圖處理器（GPU）陣容。Iris將作為補充，而非替代輝達（NVIDIA）與超微（AMD）所供應的GPU。Meta在備忘錄中承認，將最新世代GPU整合進其規模的資料中心是一項複雜且耗時的工程；將部分工作負載卸載至自製晶片，目的在於提升整體效率、降低成本。

  ## 運算容量目標

  根據工商時報引述的備忘錄，Meta在2026年上半年已部署約1GW的新增AI運算容量，預計下半年再增加約5.5GW，全年部署量合計約7GW。2027年14GW的目標，大約是今年全年部署總量的兩倍。以亞伯達省公告時所提供的數據為例，1吉瓦的電力足以供應約80萬戶家庭使用。

  Meta今年的AI基礎建設支出預計上看1,450億美元，備忘錄如此顯示——工商時報報導，這在預計2026年大型科技公司逾7,000億美元AI總投資中佔有相當大的比重。

  ## 亞伯達資料中心

  Meta宣布將在亞伯達省中部的史特金縣（Sturgeon County）興建一座容量1GW、設計可擴充至1.8GW的資料中心。這將是Meta全球第33座、也是在加拿大的第一座資料中心。宣布典禮在卡加利（Calgary）舉行，亞伯達省省長丹妮爾．史密斯（Danielle Smith）及省級官員均出席見證。

  亞伯達省的吸引力來自幾個層面：天然氣資源豐富且交易價格低於美國基準報價、寒冷氣候有助於降低冷卻成本，以及省級政策允許新進業者自建發電設施，而非完全仰賴現有電網——該電網目前約有六成由天然氣發電。根據《星報》（The Star）的報導，目前已有20座中小型資料中心在該省營運。

  Meta表示將全額出資興建該場址的新發電與電網基礎設施。Meta資料中心開發副總裁蓋瑞．德馬西（Gary Demasi）表示，Meta將透過投資潔淨能源與再生能源，來抵銷該設施的電力消耗。德馬西指出，資料中心將採用封閉式液冷系統，總用水量將低於一座典型高爾夫球場的用水量。

  Meta已與亞伯達省的彭比納管線公司（Pembina Pipeline）簽訂長期轉供協議。彭比納管線上週剛確認推進其「Greenlight電力中心」計畫——一座同樣位於史特金縣的全新天然氣發電廠，預計於2030年底投入運營。在該廠正式上線之前，亞伯達本地電力業者Capital Power將從其現有天然氣發電機組提供250百萬瓦（MW）的電力。根據彭比納管線的資料，該設施每日約需1.5億立方英尺的天然氣。

  亞伯達省科技部長奈特．葛魯比什（Nate Glubish）在發布會上表示，目前尚有多個吉瓦級資料中心提案正在該省不同開發階段推進。他說：「這是同類中的第一座、這個規模的第一座、這個量級的第一座，但絕對不會是最後一座。」

  ## 供應鏈與成本壓力

  根據內部備忘錄，除了台積電和博通分別負責Iris晶片的製造與共同開發外，Meta還與三星電子（Samsung Electronics）簽署多年期記憶體供應協議、與SanDisk簽署快閃儲存裝置協議、以及與日本住友電工（Sumitomo Electric Industries）簽署光纖設備協議。這些合約的目的在於，趁AI驅動的需求耗盡市場可用供應量之前，預先鎖定關鍵零組件。

  工商時報引述摩根士丹利（Morgan Stanley）的分析警告，記憶體及相關半導體價格的漲幅已超出市場預期——該報告將此現象描述為「晶片通膨」，並指出這股趨勢已開始對總體經濟產生影響。分析指出，無法提前確保供應鏈的科技公司，在基礎建設的建置成本上可能面臨大幅攀升的壓力。

  ## 產業意義

  富瑞研究（Forrester Research）副總裁暨首席分析師麥克．瓜爾提耶里（Mike Gualtieri）告訴工商時報，完全依賴外部晶片供應商的企業，將難以在AI領域取得領先地位。他以Meta、谷歌（Google）、各大雲端服務業者及SpaceX為例，指出這些組織均積極推動自研AI晶片設計，藉此掌控模型訓練成本，並在AI服務定價上取得競爭優勢。

  Iris的量產時程與亞伯達省的建設公告，共同勾勒出AI基礎建設供應鏈正在發生的結構性轉變。以Iris為代表的客製化特殊應用積體電路（ASIC），正在開闢一條此前以GPU為核心的全新競爭戰線。對先進半導體製程產能、高頻寬記憶體、光纖網路元件，以及大規模專用電力設施的持續需求，預計至少將延伸至2030年——也就是為史特金縣場址供電的Greenlight電力中心預定竣工上線的時間點。
