body_en: |
  NVIDIA Chief Executive Jensen Huang told shareholders at the company's 2026 annual meeting that the Vera Rubin platform has entered full production, as the chipmaker reported fiscal year 2026 revenue of $215.9 billion, up 65% year-over-year, with data center revenue reaching $193.7 billion, a gain of 68%.

  The meeting was held in the early hours of June 25 Taiwan time. Huang used the occasion to reinforce what he described as an "AI factory" model, in which data centers have shifted from storing and transmitting information to manufacturing digital intelligence. He said agentic AI, meaning systems capable of executing tasks rather than merely answering questions, has made tokens, the discrete output units of AI computation, into monetizable products.

  ## Product stack and Vera Rubin's role

  Huang outlined three generational positions in NVIDIA's product lineup. Hopper, the current-generation platform, is used primarily for pre-training large AI models. Blackwell, its successor, brought inference workloads into rack-level system architectures. Vera Rubin, the next-generation platform now in full production, is designed specifically for agentic AI workloads.

  Within Vera Rubin, Huang distinguished two components: Vera, a central processing unit (CPU) designed for AI agents, and Rubin, a graphics processing unit (GPU) that handles thinking and computation workloads. He said that if the CPU becomes a bottleneck, GPU idle time translates directly into lost revenue for an AI factory, and that the combined system, including the CPU, GPU, NVLink interconnect fabric, Spectrum-X Ethernet networking, BlueField data-processing units, and the software stack, collectively determines AI factory efficiency.

  ## AI infrastructure demand

  Huang said AI infrastructure is no longer an experimental investment but a new generation of industrial infrastructure being built simultaneously by enterprises, cloud service providers, sovereign governments, and regional AI cloud operators. He said that when AI creates real economic value, tokens become profitable, which drives additional demand for computation, and that AI factory expansion cycles could be measured in decades.

  ## Export controls and China

  Responding to shareholder questions on export restrictions, Huang said the U.S. government has approved licenses for NVIDIA to export its H200 chip to Chinese customers. The company said it has not yet recognized revenue from those shipments, and uncertainty remains over whether China will permit the imports.

  ## Shareholder returns

  NVIDIA returned $41.1 billion to shareholders through stock buybacks and dividends during fiscal year 2026. The company said it plans to continue directing more than 50% of free cash flow to shareholder returns going forward.

  ## Supply chain implications

  The meeting's key signal for the broader industry, according to market analysts cited by Commercial Times, is a shift in competitive focus away from the performance of a single GPU toward the performance-per-watt, cost-per-token, and revenue output of an entire AI factory. That framing, the publication noted, keeps Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company's (TSMC) advanced process nodes and CoWoS advanced packaging central to the supply chain, while also driving demand for server assembly, power systems, liquid cooling, high-speed printed circuit boards, optical communications, and network switching equipment.

  For Taiwan's supply chain, the meeting confirmed that AI capital expenditure priorities remain unchanged and that demand is extending from Blackwell into Vera Rubin and physical AI deployments, keeping order visibility for TSMC, Foxconn, Wistron, Quanta, Wiwynn, and suppliers of cooling, power, and optical communications products under close market attention.

  Huang said the next wave of AI growth will come from robotics, autonomous driving, and industrial systems, as AI infrastructure buildout extends from cloud environments to enterprises, sovereign AI programs, and physical AI deployments.

  ## 黃仁勳宣布Vera Rubin全面量產；輝達2026財年營收達2,159億美元，年增65%

  輝達（NVIDIA）執行長黃仁勳在該公司2026年股東年會上向股東宣布，Vera Rubin平台已正式進入全面量產，同步公布2026財年營收達2,159億美元，年增65%，資料中心營收更高達1,937億美元，年增68%。

  此次年會於台灣時間6月25日凌晨舉行。黃仁勳藉此場合強調他所描述的「AI工廠」模型——資料中心已從過去的資料儲存與傳輸，轉型為製造數位智慧的生產基地。他指出，代理型AI（agentic AI），即能夠主動執行任務而非僅回答問題的系統，已使「Token」——AI運算的離散輸出單位——成為可貨幣化的產品。

  ## 產品布局與Vera Rubin的定位

  黃仁勳說明輝達產品線的三個世代定位。現役平台Hopper主要用於大型AI模型的預訓練；其後繼平台Blackwell則將推論工作負載帶入機架級系統架構；而下一代平台Vera Rubin目前已全面量產，專為代理型AI工作負載所設計。

  在Vera Rubin平台內部，黃仁勳進一步區分兩大元件：Vera是專為AI代理設計的中央處理器（CPU），Rubin則是負責處理思考與運算工作負載的繪圖處理器（GPU）。他表示，若CPU成為瓶頸，GPU的閒置時間將直接轉化為AI工廠的營收損失；而整套系統——涵蓋CPU、GPU、NVLink互連架構、Spectrum-X乙太網路、BlueField資料處理器（DPU）及軟體堆疊——共同決定AI工廠的整體效能。

  ## AI基礎建設需求

  黃仁勳表示，AI基礎建設已不再是試驗性投資，而是一個正在由企業、雲端服務業者、主權政府與區域AI雲端營運商同步興建的新世代工業基礎設施。他指出，當AI創造出真實的經濟價值，Token便成為有利可圖的產品，進而帶動對運算資源的更多需求，AI工廠的擴張週期可能以數十年計。

  ## 出口管制與中國市場

  針對股東提問有關出口限制的議題，黃仁勳表示，美國政府已核准輝達向中國客戶出口H200晶片的相關許可證。輝達表示，目前尚未認列這批出貨的營收，且中國是否允許進口仍存在不確定性。

  ## 股東回報

  輝達在2026財年透過股票回購與股息，共返還股東411億美元。公司表示，未來計畫持續將逾50%的自由現金流用於股東回報。

  ## 供應鏈影響

  根據《工商時報》引述市場分析師的看法，此次年會對整體產業最關鍵的訊號，在於競爭焦點已從單一GPU的效能，轉移至整座AI工廠的每瓦效能、每Token成本與營收產出。報導指出，這樣的定位框架使台積電（TSMC）的先進製程節點與CoWoS先進封裝技術持續居於供應鏈核心地位，同時也帶動伺服器組裝、電力系統、液冷散熱、高速印刷電路板、光通訊及網路交換設備的需求成長。

  對於台灣供應鏈而言，此次年會確認AI資本支出優先順序維持不變，且需求正從Blackwell延伸至Vera Rubin與實體AI部署，台積電、鴻海、緯創、廣達、緯穎，以及冷卻、電力與光通訊產品供應商的訂單能見度，持續受到市場密切關注。

  黃仁勳表示，AI成長的下一波浪潮將來自機器人、自動駕駛與工業系統，隨著AI基礎建設從雲端環境向企業、主權AI計畫及實體AI部署延伸，整體產業規模將持續擴張。
