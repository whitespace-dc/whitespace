body_en: |
  The data center industry's push to secure power capacity for artificial intelligence workloads is overshadowing a more fundamental challenge — whether the infrastructure can sustain reliable performance under the operating conditions AI actually creates, according to a senior engineering executive at Rehlko, a maker of emergency backup power systems.

  Robert Danforth, Director of Engineering, Simulation, and Advanced Development at Rehlko, made the assessment in a Q2 2026 executive roundtable published by Data Center Frontier. Danforth, who joined Rehlko in 2004 and holds more than 10 patents related to mechanical and electrical performance, leads the company's engineering work in digital twin modeling and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) analysis — simulation techniques used to evaluate how fluids, heat, and electrical loads move through a system — applied to backup power infrastructure.

  ## The capacity-performance gap

  "The biggest disconnect right now is that the industry is still talking a lot about capacity, while not talking about performance needed for the future," Danforth said.

  Traditional power systems, he said, were engineered around relatively stable operating conditions with occasional transient events — brief, temporary deviations from normal load. AI changes that equation. Large-scale AI training environments produce highly dynamic, synchronized load patterns, meaning what were once rare disturbances increasingly become part of routine operation.

  Grid constraints, long interconnection timelines — the extended queues utilities require before approving new connections — and pressure to bring capacity online quickly are compounding the problem, Danforth said. "Simply having access to megawatts isn't enough," he said. "The bigger question is whether the infrastructure can deliver stable, reliable performance under real AI operating conditions."

  He said the conversation in the industry is beginning to shift from "How much power do we have?" to "How well does the entire system perform when AI workloads are running at full scale?"

  ## Integration requirements deepen

  Beyond raw power, Danforth said the integration of power systems, cooling, and facility operations is becoming materially more demanding. Historically, those systems could be engineered with some degree of independence. In AI environments, he said, a change in workload behavior can simultaneously affect power delivery, thermal performance, energy storage, controls, and overall facility operations.

  "The question isn't whether a generator, battery system, cooling solution, or control platform performs well on its own," Danforth said. "It's whether all of those systems can work together effectively under highly dynamic conditions."

  He said simulation and digital twin technologies — software models that replicate a physical system's behavior in real time — are drawing growing interest because they allow operators to assess how those cross-system interactions play out before infrastructure is deployed, and to surface potential design challenges early.

  ## Production scale introduces consistency demands

  The shift from pilot AI environments to full-scale production deepens the challenge further, Danforth said. A controlled pilot can confirm that a system handles AI workloads, but production environments require operators to maintain performance and reliability across multiple facilities while workloads continue to evolve and capacity requirements grow.

  "Challenges that can be addressed relatively easily in a single deployment become far more significant when they affect multiple facilities," he said. Infrastructure must be deployed, operated, and maintained through repeatable processes across an entire portfolio of sites, requiring close coordination across design, construction, operations, maintenance, and supply chain functions.

  Danforth said reliability expectations do not diminish as deployments scale. Operators must still deliver uptime across increasingly dense compute environments and more complex power architectures, where each decision carries greater consequence because it affects critical infrastructure at scale.

  ## Industry significance

  Danforth's remarks reflect a shift in how vendors with deep exposure to data center power systems are framing the AI infrastructure challenge, moving from questions of raw capacity to questions of system-level performance under conditions that differ substantially from what most existing infrastructure was designed for.

  He said organizations that design around actual AI operating conditions rather than theoretical capacity will be better positioned as deployments scale, and that long-term success will depend on building repeatable operating models capable of sustaining reliability across a growing portfolio of sites.

  ## 容量與效能之間的落差

  備用電力系統製造商Rehlko旗下一位資深工程主管表示，資料中心產業為人工智慧工作負載搶佔電力容量的浪潮，正在遮蔽一項更根本的挑戰——現有基礎設施究竟能否在AI實際創造的運作環境下維持穩定可靠的效能。

  Rehlko工程模擬暨先進開發總監Robert Danforth在《Data Center Frontier》發布的2026年第二季高階主管圓桌論壇中提出上述評估。Danforth於2004年加入Rehlko，持有逾10項與機械及電氣效能相關的專利，目前主導公司在數位孿生建模與計算流體動力學（CFD）分析方面的工程工作——這兩項模擬技術用於評估流體、熱能與電氣負載在系統中的運動方式——並將其應用於備用電力基礎設施的評估。

  「目前最大的落差在於，業界仍在大談容量，卻沒有充分討論未來所需的效能，」Danforth說。

  他指出，傳統電力系統的工程設計是圍繞著相對穩定的運作條件而建立的，僅偶爾出現短暫的暫態事件——即短暫偏離正常負載的情況。AI的出現徹底改變了這道方程式。大規模AI訓練環境會產生高度動態、同步的負載模式，這意味著過去罕見的擾動如今愈來愈成為日常運作的一部分。

  Danforth說，電網限制、漫長的併網時程——即電力公司在核准新連接前所要求的冗長排隊等候——以及盡快上線的壓力，正在使問題雪上加霜。「光是能取得多少百萬瓦的電力並不夠，」他說，「更關鍵的問題是，基礎設施能否在AI工作負載全負荷運行的真實條件下，提供穩定可靠的效能。」

  他表示，業界的對話正逐漸從「我們有多少電力？」轉向「當AI工作負載全規模運行時，整個系統的表現究竟如何？」

  ## 整合需求日益深化

  除了原始電力之外，Danforth指出，電力系統、冷卻設備與設施營運之間的整合，正變得實質上更加複雜。以往，這些系統或多或少可以獨立設計。但在AI環境中，他說，工作負載行為的任何變化都可能同時影響電力輸送、熱效能、儲能、控制系統以及整體設施的運作。

  「問題不在於發電機、電池系統、冷卻方案或控制平台各自的表現是否良好，」Danforth說，「而在於所有這些系統能否在高度動態的條件下有效協同運作。」

  他表示，模擬技術與數位孿生技術——即在虛擬環境中即時複製實體系統行為的軟體模型——正引發越來越多的關注，因為這些技術讓營運商在基礎設施部署之前，就能評估跨系統互動的實際情況，並及早發現潛在的設計挑戰。

  ## 生產規模帶來一致性要求

  Danforth說，從AI試驗環境轉向全規模生產環境，更進一步加深了這項挑戰。在受控的試驗環境中，或許可以確認某套系統能夠處理AI工作負載，但生產環境要求營運商在工作負載持續演進、容量需求不斷增長的同時，還須跨越多個設施維持效能與可靠度。

  「在單一部署中相對容易解決的挑戰，一旦影響到多個設施，就會變得遠比預期嚴峻，」他說。基礎設施必須透過可重複執行的流程，在整個設施組合中完成部署、營運與維護，這需要設計、施工、運營、維保與供應鏈等各職能之間的密切協調。

  Danforth說，隨著部署規模擴大，可靠度的期望並不會隨之降低。營運商必須在運算密度日益提高、電力架構日趨複雜的環境中確保系統正常運行，而每一項決策所帶來的影響也更為深遠，因為它攸關大規模關鍵基礎設施的穩定運作。

  ## 產業意義

  Danforth的談話反映了深度接觸資料中心電力系統的供應商，在詮釋AI基礎設施挑戰時的思維轉變——從追問原始容量，轉向探討系統在與現有基礎設施設計初衷截然不同的條件下，能否達到整體層面的效能。

  他表示，那些依照AI實際運作條件而非理論容量來進行設計的組織，將在部署規模擴大的過程中佔據更有利的地位；而長期成功的關鍵，在於建立可重複執行的運營模式，使其能夠在持續成長的設施組合中維繫可靠度。
