body_en: |
  Artificial intelligence has moved from a peripheral tool to a central force reshaping high-performance computing (HPC) — the field that uses the world's most powerful systems to run large-scale scientific simulations — as the SC26 conference prepares to open in Chicago on November 15, according to analysts and researchers speaking in a preview podcast published by Data Center Frontier.

  The episode, hosted by DCF Contributing Editor Doug Black, brings together Lois Curfman McInnes, an Argonne Distinguished Fellow and senior computational scientist at Argonne National Laboratory; Addison Snell, founder and CEO of market research firm Intersect 360 Research; and Earl Joseph, CEO of Hyperion Research. SC26 runs November 15–20.

  ## AI moves to the center of scientific computing

  "We're really moving beyond focusing just on bigger machines, faster machines," McInnes said, pointing to growing integration of AI into scientific software development, simulations, and research workflows. Joseph cited new Hyperion Research data showing that 70% of survey respondents are already using AI for code generation, underscoring how rapidly the technology has embedded itself in day-to-day HPC practice.

  The panel discussed the U.S. Department of Energy's Genesis Mission, a federal initiative to connect supercomputers, large-scale AI infrastructure, and commercial cloud resources in support of scientific discovery. Joseph described it as "the first time I've ever seen something that's truly a broad national resource." Snell added that investments in AI infrastructure must be matched by corresponding investments in the science those systems are meant to support.

  ## Financing and upgrade cycles create new pressure

  For data center and infrastructure professionals, the panel identified two compounding economic problems. First, Snell warned that the industry's drive to build quickly has outpaced serious discussion of infrastructure lifecycles and future upgrade paths. Second, Joseph highlighted the difficulty of financing supercomputers that cost $100 million or more when new generations of graphics processing units (GPUs) — the chips that power both AI training and many HPC workloads — arrive approximately every 12 months, compressing the window in which any given system can be considered current.

  The combination creates a financing dilemma: systems are expensive enough to require long capital planning horizons, yet the underlying hardware evolves fast enough to make those horizons risky.

  ## Public opposition and the case for infrastructure

  The panel also addressed growing public opposition to data center development. Joseph argued that the industry has not yet made an adequate case to the public for the societal value of its infrastructure investments — a communications gap he suggested the sector needs to close as projects face local resistance.

  ## SC26 exhibition floor topics

  Looking ahead to the SC26 exhibition floor, the discussion identified several technology areas expected to draw attention: quantum computing's longer-term trajectory; photonics as an interconnect and processing technology; advanced cooling systems designed for high-density AI and HPC hardware; processor sovereignty (the question of which nations control the design and manufacture of key chips); and the challenge of preserving traditional HPC simulation capabilities as hardware architectures are increasingly optimized for AI workloads rather than the broader range of scientific computations HPC has historically run.

  ## Significance

  The SC26 preview reflects a structural shift in how the HPC industry and its research users understand their work. The conference, long organized around raw computational performance, is now grappling with questions that extend well beyond benchmarks: how to fund and refresh expensive systems on compressed timelines, how to integrate AI without crowding out other scientific use cases, and how to maintain public and political support for infrastructure that is growing larger and more expensive. The DOE Genesis Mission, as described by the panelists, represents the most visible current attempt to coordinate those pressures at a national level.

  SC26 opens in Chicago on November 15 and closes November 20.
body_zh: |
  根據分析師與研究人員在《Data Center Frontier》發布的預覽播客中所言，人工智慧（AI）已從邊緣工具一躍成為重塑高效能運算（HPC）的核心力量——HPC是運用全球最強大系統執行大規模科學模擬的領域——而SC26研討會也即將於11月15日在芝加哥正式揭幕。

  這集播客由DCF特約編輯Doug Black主持，邀請到三位重量級來賓：美國阿岡國家實驗室（Argonne National Laboratory）傑出院士暨資深計算科學家Lois Curfman McInnes、市場調研機構Intersect 360 Research創辦人兼執行長Addison Snell，以及Hyperion Research執行長Earl Joseph。SC26研討會將於11月15日至20日舉行。

  ## AI躍居科學運算核心

  「我們正在超越單純追求更大、更快的機器，」McInnes表示，並指出AI已日益深入科學軟體開發、模擬運算與研究工作流程之中。Joseph引用Hyperion Research的最新數據指出，70%的受訪者已將AI運用於程式碼生成，充分顯示這項技術在HPC日常實務中的滲透速度之快。

  論壇也討論了美國能源部（DOE）的「Genesis任務」——一項聯邦層級的倡議，旨在串聯超級電腦、大規模AI基礎建設與商業雲端資源，以支援科學探索。Joseph形容這是「我有史以來第一次見到真正具備全國性廣度的資源」。Snell則補充，AI基礎建設的投資，必須搭配相應的科學研究投入，兩者缺一不可。

  ## 融資難題與升級週期形成雙重壓力

  對於資料中心與基礎建設從業人員而言，論壇點出了兩個相互疊加的經濟難題。首先，Snell警告，業界急於快速建設的衝動，已遠超過對基礎建設生命週期及未來升級路徑的嚴肅討論。其次，Joseph強調，要為造價逾一億美元的超級電腦進行融資，本就困難重重，更何況驅動AI訓練與諸多HPC工作負載的核心晶片——繪圖處理器（GPU）——大約每12個月就會推出新一代產品，大幅壓縮任何現有系統被視為「當代最先進」的時間窗口。

  兩者相加，形成一道融資困境：系統造價之高，需要漫長的資本規劃週期；然而底層硬體的演進之快，又讓這樣的長期規劃充滿風險。

  ## 社會反對聲浪與基礎建設正當性

  論壇也觸及日益升溫的社會反對資料中心開發議題。Joseph認為，業界迄今尚未向社會大眾充分說明其基礎建設投資對社會的具體價值，而這道溝通落差，在各地專案遭遇在地反彈之際，業界必須正視並加以彌補。

  ## SC26展覽現場焦點議題

  展望SC26展覽現場，討論點出了幾個預料將受到矚目的技術領域：量子運算的長期發展軌跡；光子技術在互連與運算處理上的應用；專為高密度AI與HPC硬體設計的先進冷卻系統；處理器主權（即哪些國家掌握關鍵晶片的設計與製造）；以及在硬體架構日益朝AI工作負載最佳化的趨勢下，如何保存傳統HPC模擬能力——亦即HPC歷來所承擔的更廣泛科學運算工作。

  ## 整體意義

  這份SC26預覽報告反映出HPC產業及其研究使用者在理解自身工作上的結構性轉變。這場長期以原始運算效能為核心的研討會，如今正面對遠超過基準測試的更深層課題：如何在壓縮的時程內為昂貴系統籌措資金並更新換代、如何整合AI而不排擠其他科學應用場景，以及如何維繫社會與政治層面對規模日益龐大、成本不斷攀升的基礎建設的支持。誠如與談者所描述，美國能源部的Genesis任務，是目前在國家層級協調應對上述壓力最具代表性的嘗試。

  SC26研討會於11月15日在芝加哥開幕，11月20日閉幕。
