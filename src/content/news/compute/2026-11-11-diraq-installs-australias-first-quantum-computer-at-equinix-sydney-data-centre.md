body_en: |
  Diraq has deployed Australia's first quantum computer inside a commercial data centre, installing an eight-qubit silicon spin system at Equinix's Sydney facility and announcing plans to expand it to 256 qubits within the next year.

  The deployment moves an Australian-built quantum machine out of a research setting and into a facility that supports mainstream computing workloads across banking, healthcare, utilities, manufacturing and retail, the two companies said.

  ## Physical footprint and power

  The system occupies 16 square metres of data centre floor space and draws fewer than 20 kilowatts of power. Diraq says those requirements demonstrate that quantum hardware can operate inside existing data centre environments rather than requiring purpose-built facilities.

  For comparison, Diraq projects that a future utility-scale version of the machine would draw around 300 kW — which it describes as less than a third of the power consumed by a single next-generation artificial intelligence rack — and could occupy as little as five square metres.

  ## Silicon spin technology and manufacturing path

  Diraq builds quantum computers using a silicon spin approach: quantum systems manufactured using semiconductor fabrication processes similar to those used for conventional computer chips. The company says this manufacturing path is already proven at industrial scale and produces a cost curve that improves as qubits are added, allowing customers to expand computing capacity by upgrading the quantum processor with limited changes to surrounding infrastructure.

  Diraq says many competing quantum architectures become more expensive as they scale because they require additional cooling, wiring and control equipment. The company says it is targeting utility-scale systems that would cost at most tens of millions of dollars a year to operate.

  ## Data centre integration

  Equinix, which operates the Sydney facility hosting the system, said the installation creates a pathway for quantum hardware to connect with cloud, artificial intelligence and high-performance computing environments already used by corporate customers.

  "By hosting Australia's first quantum computer in a commercial data centre, we are helping bridge the gap between research and deployment, while creating the foundation for quantum systems to connect seamlessly with the cloud, AI and high-performance computing ecosystems that enterprises rely on today," said Jarrod Nink, managing director for Australia at Equinix.

  Andrew Dzurak, chief executive officer and founder of Diraq, said the silicon spin approach puts broad quantum access "within reach in five years, not decades," citing what he described as a manufacturing path proven at industrial scale and a system designed to run inside existing computing infrastructure.

  ## Drug development economics

  Alongside the deployment, Diraq released a report examining how quantum computing could affect industries including medicine, energy, materials, climate and transport. The company focused particular attention on drug development costs, drawing on an industry study published in Nature Reviews Drug Discovery: it says developing a new medicine can cost more than USD $2 billion and take up to 15 years, while fewer than one in 10 drug candidates reach patients.

  Diraq's modelling projects that a 10 percentage point increase in clinical trial success rates could yield approximately 50 percent more potentially lifesaving medicines reaching patients each year. In a market the size of the United States, that would amount to as many as 25 additional medicines annually, the company said.

  Diraq also says the same improvement could save USD $600 million per medicine by identifying failed drug candidates earlier — provided the quantum computer used to generate that outcome costs less than USD $65 million a year to run. The company uses that threshold to argue that the commercial viability of quantum systems will depend as much on operating costs remaining below the value created for customers as on scientific progress.

  ## Funding and government support

  The Sydney deployment follows support for Diraq's manufacturing plans from Australia's National Reconstruction Fund Corporation. The company has advanced to Stage C of the United States Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency's (DARPA) Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, which carries potential funding of up to USD $300 million. Diraq has also signed a USD $38 million letter of intent with the US Department of Commerce under the CHIPS and Science Act.

  Dzurak cited a Boston Consulting Group (BCG) estimate that quantum computing could unlock USD $3.5 trillion in value at market maturity, and said advanced quantum technologies have already helped compound an estimated USD $19 to $27 trillion in value globally.

  Diraq's near-term milestone is scaling the installed Sydney system from eight qubits to 256 qubits within the next year, while maintaining its DARPA Quantum Benchmarking Initiative engagement, which carries potential funding of up to USD $300 million.
body_zh: |
  Diraq已在商業資料中心內部署澳洲首台量子電腦，於Equinix雪梨設施安裝一套八量子位元矽自旋系統，並宣布計畫在未來一年內將規模擴充至256量子位元。

  此次部署標誌著澳洲自製量子機器正式走出研究環境，進駐一座服務銀行、醫療、公用事業、製造業及零售業等主流運算需求的設施，兩家公司如此表示。

  ## 實體佔地與用電需求

  該系統佔用資料中心機房16平方公尺的空間，耗電不超過20千瓦。Diraq表示，這樣的需求證明量子硬體能夠在現有資料中心環境中運作，無需興建專用設施。

  相較之下，Diraq預估未來公用規模版本的耗電量約為300千瓦——該公司形容這不到單一下一代人工智慧機架耗電量的三分之一——佔地最少可縮至僅5平方公尺。

  ## 矽自旋技術與製造路徑

  Diraq採用矽自旋方式建造量子電腦：運用與傳統電腦晶片相似的半導體製程來製造量子系統。公司表示，這條製造路徑已在工業規模上獲得驗證，且隨著量子位元數量增加，成本曲線持續下降，讓客戶只需升級量子處理器、無需大幅變動周邊基礎設施，即可擴充運算能力。

  Diraq指出，許多競爭的量子架構在擴展規模時成本會隨之攀升，因為需要額外的冷卻設備、配線與控制設備。公司表示，其目標是打造每年運營成本最高僅達數千萬美元的公用規模系統。

  ## 資料中心整合

  負責托管該系統的Equinix雪梨設施表示，此次安裝為量子硬體與企業客戶現有的雲端、人工智慧及高效能運算環境建立了互通的橋梁。

  Equinix澳洲區董事總經理賈洛德．寧克（Jarrod Nink）表示：「透過在商業資料中心托管澳洲首台量子電腦，我們正協助縮短研究與實際部署之間的差距，同時為量子系統奠定基礎，使其能與企業今日仰賴的雲端、人工智慧及高效能運算生態系統無縫整合。」

  Diraq執行長暨創辦人安德魯．朱拉克（Andrew Dzurak）表示，矽自旋方式讓廣泛的量子應用「在五年內，而非數十年後」就能觸手可及，並引用他所描述的已在工業規模驗證的製造路徑，以及專為在現有運算基礎設施中運行而設計的系統作為佐證。

  ## 新藥開發的經濟效益

  與此次部署同步，Diraq發布了一份報告，探討量子運算可能如何影響醫藥、能源、材料、氣候及交通等產業。公司特別聚焦於新藥開發成本，引用《自然評論：藥物探索》（Nature Reviews Drug Discovery）期刊發表的一項產業研究：報告指出，開發一款新藥的成本可能超過20億美元，耗時長達15年，而進入臨床的藥物候選品中，最終能惠及患者的不足十分之一。

  Diraq的模型預測，若臨床試驗成功率提升10個百分點，每年可能有約多50%的潛在救命藥物順利送達患者手中。在美國規模的市場中，這相當於每年最多可多出25種新藥上市，公司如此表示。

  Diraq同時指出，同樣的改善還能透過及早篩除失敗的藥物候選品，為每款藥物節省6億美元——前提是用於實現此成效的量子電腦每年運營成本不超過6,500萬美元。公司以此門檻主張，量子系統的商業可行性，取決於運營成本維持在為客戶所創造的價值之下，這與科學進展同等重要。

  ## 資金來源與政府支持

  此次雪梨部署之前，Diraq已獲得澳洲國家重建基金公司對其製造計畫的支持。公司在美國國防高等研究計畫署（DARPA）量子基準評估計畫中推進至C階段，該計畫潛在資助金額最高達3億美元。Diraq亦依據《晶片與科學法》（CHIPS and Science Act）與美國商務部簽署了一份3,800萬美元的意向書。

  朱拉克引用波士頓諮詢集團（BCG）的估算，指出量子運算在市場成熟時可能釋放3.5兆美元的價值，並表示先進量子技術已協助在全球複合創造約19至27兆美元的估計價值。

  Diraq的近期里程碑，是在未來一年內將雪梨已安裝系統從八量子位元擴充至256量子位元，同時維持其DARPA量子基準評估計畫的合作，該計畫潛在資助金額最高達3億美元。
