body_en: |
  A research team from Penn State University, MIT, and the Superintelligence Foundation has published a study examining whether post-biological superintelligent civilizations could build massive energy-harvesting structures and galaxy-scale data centers around supermassive black holes — a concept the authors call "Dyson Minds" — and proposed observable signatures that could distinguish such structures from natural astrophysical phenomena.

  The findings emerged from the "Dyson Minds 2025 Workshop," held at MIT's Center for Brains, Minds and Machines, where experts in astrophysics, artificial intelligence, engineering, and philosophy convened to assess the physical plausibility of such megastructures. The research was published in the Publications of the Astronomical Society of the Pacific (PASP).

  ## Extending the Dyson Sphere to galactic scales

  The concept builds on the theoretical "Dyson Sphere" — a hypothetical structure enveloping a star to capture its full energy output — and extends it to a far more powerful target: the supermassive black holes (SMBHs) found at the cores of most galaxies. According to the study, civilizations seeking maximum energy gradients would naturally concentrate at galactic centers, where SMBHs release energy through jets and accretion processes that far exceed the combined output of millions of stars.

  Lead author Olivia Curtis said that as humanity begins considering the deployment of data centers in space, the concept of black hole-adjacent megastructures becomes increasingly physically plausible.

  ## Engineering constraints

  Proximity to a supermassive black hole does not necessarily mean destruction, the researchers note. Curtis clarified that black holes are not indiscriminate vacuum cleaners: provided a structure remains outside the event horizon — the boundary beyond which escape is impossible — it will not be pulled in. However, the intense radiation surrounding an SMBH means any harvesting structure must maintain a minimum standoff distance of approximately one parsec, or roughly 3.26 light-years, to avoid being destroyed.

  ## Communication at light-year scales

  Structures spanning several light-years introduce severe communication delays between components positioned on opposite sides of a black hole. The researchers calculated that for transmitting zettabyte-scale data volumes — one zettabyte equals one billion terabytes — physically launching storage drives across the gap would be faster than sending electromagnetic signals.

  If such megastructures exist, the study argues, they would produce detectable signatures: periodic bursts of transmissions or the measurable orbital dynamics of physically propelled storage media.

  ## Implications for telescope data analysis

  The research identifies a specific observational target in existing black hole imaging datasets. Algorithms used by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) — the global array of radio observatories that produced the first direct images of black hole shadows — are designed to synthesize coherent images from raw observational data, a process that may automatically smooth out minor signal perturbations. The study suggests those suppressed anomalies could potentially carry evidence of artificial structures.

  ## Significance for SETI and astrophysics

  The authors situate the search for Dyson Minds within established Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence (SETI) methodology, stipulating that an artificial explanation should remain the last resort after natural causes have been ruled out. The practical value of the work, they argue, lies in its scientific byproducts: examining cosmic data through the lens of possible megastructures may surface previously unrecognized astrophysical phenomena even in the absence of any detection.

  The paper calls for re-examining existing and future observational datasets — including EHT imagery — using algorithms configured to preserve, rather than filter, marginal signal anomalies.

  ## 研究人員提出「戴森心智」：超大質量黑洞周邊的銀河尺度資料中心

  賓州州立大學、麻省理工學院與超級智慧基金會的跨機構研究團隊，近日發表一項研究，探討後生物型超級智慧文明是否可能在超大質量黑洞周邊建造大規模能源收集結構與銀河尺度資料中心——研究人員將此概念稱為「戴森心智」（Dyson Minds）——並提出可與自然天文物理現象加以區分的可觀測特徵。

  這項研究成果源自於在麻省理工學院「大腦、心智與機器中心」舉辦的「2025年戴森心智工作坊」，來自天文物理學、人工智慧、工程學與哲學等領域的專家齊聚一堂，共同評估此類巨型結構在物理層面的可行性。相關論文已發表於《太平洋天文學會出版物》（PASP）。

  ## 從戴森球延伸至銀河尺度

  這一概念以理論上的「戴森球」為基礎——戴森球是一種假想結構，能夠完整包覆一顆恆星並捕獲其全部能量輸出——並將目標延伸至能量更為龐大的天體：位於大多數星系核心的超大質量黑洞（SMBH）。根據這項研究，任何追求最大能量梯度的文明，都會自然而然地聚集在星系中心，因為超大質量黑洞透過噴流與吸積過程釋放的能量，遠遠超過數百萬顆恆星的總輸出量。

  本文第一作者奧利維亞．柯蒂斯（Olivia Curtis）表示，隨著人類開始考慮在太空中部署資料中心，緊鄰黑洞的巨型結構概念在物理層面的可信度也日益提升。

  ## 工程限制條件

  研究人員指出，靠近超大質量黑洞並不必然意味著毀滅。柯蒂斯澄清，黑洞並非來者不拒的宇宙吸塵器：只要結構保持在事件視界——即超越此界限便無法逃脫的邊界——之外，就不會被吸入。然而，超大質量黑洞周圍強烈的輻射環境，意味著任何能源收集結構都必須與黑洞保持至少約一秒差距（parsec）、即大約3.26光年的最小安全距離，否則將面臨被摧毀的風險。

  ## 跨越光年尺度的通訊問題

  橫跨數光年的巨型結構，在黑洞兩側相對位置的各組件之間，將產生嚴重的通訊延遲。研究人員計算指出，若需傳輸澤位元組（zettabyte）級別的資料量——一澤位元組等於十億個兆位元組（TB）——以實體方式發射儲存媒體跨越間隙，反而會比傳送電磁訊號更快。

  研究認為，若此類巨型結構確實存在，將產生可供偵測的特徵：週期性的訊號傳輸爆發，或以實體方式推進的儲存媒體所呈現的可量測軌道動力學。

  ## 對望遠鏡資料分析的意涵

  這項研究在現有黑洞成像資料集中，確認了一個具體的觀測目標。事件視界望遠鏡（EHT）——即產出首批黑洞陰影直接影像的全球無線電天文台陣列——所採用的演算法，旨在從原始觀測資料合成出完整連貫的影像，而這一過程可能會自動平滑掉細微的訊號擾動。研究指出，那些遭到抑制的異常訊號，或許潛藏著人工結構存在的證據。

  ## 對SETI與天文物理學的重要性

  研究作者將搜尋戴森心智的工作，置於既有的「搜尋地外智慧生命」（SETI）研究方法論框架之內，並明確指出，只有在排除自然成因之後，人工解釋才應作為最後的考量選項。他們認為，這項研究的實用價值在於其科學副產品：即便最終未能偵測到任何人工結構，透過「可能存在巨型結構」的視角重新審視宇宙資料，或許能夠揭示過去未曾被認識到的天文物理現象。

  論文呼籲重新審視現有與未來的觀測資料集——包括事件視界望遠鏡的影像——並採用專門設計來保留而非過濾邊緣訊號異常的演算法，以期發現過往可能被忽略的訊息。
