body_en: |
  Former Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger declared current graphics processing units (GPUs) "power inefficient" and "computationally limited" and said the economics of producing AI intelligence "suck," during a keynote at the Ai4 2026 conference in Las Vegas on August 4, arguing that the industry's next phase of growth requires solving fundamental constraints across compute, memory, networking, energy, and cost, not simply deploying more accelerators.

  Sachin Katti, head of compute at OpenAI, shared the stage and said the industry's primary challenge has shifted from demonstrating AI's value to persuading governments, utilities, and manufacturers to invest in every layer of the infrastructure stack at the scale the technology demands.

  ## GPU and memory shortfalls

  Gelsinger, who was ousted as Intel's chief executive in 2025 and is now a general partner at Playground Global, said improvements must span the entire compute stack rather than the chip alone. He described high-bandwidth memory (HBM), the specialized memory architecture used in AI accelerators to move large volumes of data between processor and memory at speed, as "a very inefficient memory" that remains the industry's best available option for AI training and inference. "It's just the best one we have for AI modeling today," he said.

  Asked what business leaders most often misunderstand about AI, Gelsinger said every breakthrough ultimately rests on semiconductors. "There ain't no compute without chips sitting underneath it," he said, describing chips as "the underlying fuel" of AI and arguing that demand for AI-generated intelligence is effectively unlimited. "We see this insatiable demand. Underneath that, you have an almost insatiable demand for compute, for infrastructure, for memory," he said.

  ## Energy as a binding constraint

  Gelsinger identified energy availability as one of the most immediate limits on AI expansion. "In a digital AI economy, economic capacity equals energy capacity," he said, arguing that future AI growth depends as much on expanding electricity generation as on advancing chip design.

  "Nobody's going to build a data center for chips that you can't power up," Gelsinger said. "Nobody's going to put billions of dollars into chips that you don't have power for."

  Katti described energy availability, semiconductor fabrication (fab) capacity, and deployment timelines as intertwined problems that must be solved together. "It's not easy to build data centers quickly in the US. It's not easy to find energy in the US that we need. There's not enough fab and memory capacity to supply the chips we need, and it's not easy to put it all together into usable compute," he said.

  ## Economics require orders-of-magnitude improvement

  Gelsinger's sharpest assessment was economic. Despite unprecedented investment in AI infrastructure, he said the cost of producing intelligence remains far too high and that the required improvement is not incremental. "It doesn't need to get better. It needs to get 10,000 times better," he said.

  He argued those gains will not come from a single breakthrough but from simultaneous advances across semiconductors, memory, networking, power systems, and data center architecture. He also said the economic shift in AI has already benefited chipmakers. "The silicon guys are the big winners," he said. "It has never been this good to be a silicon guy."

  ## OpenAI's deployment timeline target

  Katti said OpenAI is focused on compressing the time required to bring new compute infrastructure into service. "Our vision is: can we cut that down to three quarters instead of three years?" he said. Achieving that timeline would require faster chip manufacturing, standardized data center construction, expanded electricity infrastructure, and more efficient deployment of compute resources across the industry, he said.

  ## Significance for the industry

  The remarks from two senior figures in the AI hardware supply chain reinforce that the industry's primary constraint has shifted from model capability to the physical and economic infrastructure required to run models at scale. For data center operators and infrastructure investors, Gelsinger and Katti's framing suggests competitive advantage in the next phase of AI will depend on efficiency gains across the full hardware and power stack rather than on raw accelerator procurement.

body_zh: |
  前英特爾執行長派特·基辛格（Pat Gelsinger）8月4日在拉斯維加斯Ai4 2026大會的主題演講中，直言現行繪圖處理器（GPU）「電力效率低落」且「運算能力受限」，並批評生產AI智慧的成本「糟透了」。他強調，AI產業若要進入下一階段的成長，必須從根本上突破運算、記憶體、網路、能源與成本等面向的瓶頸，而非只是單純地部署更多加速器。

  OpenAI運算主管薩欽·卡提（Sachin Katti）同台發言，指出產業的首要挑戰已從「證明AI的價值」，轉變為「說服各國政府、公用事業機構與製造商，在技術所需的規模下，對基礎建設堆疊的每一層都投入資金」。

  ## GPU與記憶體的先天限制

  基辛格2025年遭英特爾解除執行長職務，目前擔任Playground Global普通合夥人。他表示，產業所需的改善必須涵蓋整個運算堆疊，而非僅著眼於晶片本身。他將高頻寬記憶體（HBM）——一種用於AI加速器、能在處理器與記憶體之間高速傳輸大量資料的專用記憶體架構——形容為「效率相當低落的記憶體」，卻也坦言它仍是目前業界在AI訓練與推論上最好的選擇。「它就是我們今天在AI建模上所擁有的最佳方案，」他說。

  被問及企業領袖對AI最常見的誤解時，基辛格表示，每一項突破最終都奠基於半導體。「沒有晶片，就沒有任何運算可言，」他說，並將晶片形容為AI的「底層燃料」，同時主張對AI生成智慧的需求實際上是無窮無盡的。「我們看到這種永不饜足的需求，而在它底下，是幾乎同樣永不饜足的對運算、基礎建設與記憶體的需求，」他說。

  ## 能源：最迫切的硬性約束

  基辛格點名能源供應是AI擴張最迫切的限制之一。「在數位AI經濟中，經濟產能等於能源產能，」他說，並主張未來AI的成長，同樣仰賴電力供應的擴充，就像仰賴晶片設計的精進一樣。

  「沒有人會為供不上電的晶片蓋一座資料中心，」基辛格說。「沒有人會把數十億美元砸在你根本沒電可供的晶片上。」

  卡提則描述能源供應、半導體晶圓廠（fab）產能與部署時程，是三個相互糾纏、必須一併解決的難題。「在美國快速興建資料中心並不容易，要在美國找到我們所需的能源也不容易。供應我們所需晶片的晶圓廠與記憶體產能嚴重不足，而要把這一切整合成可供使用的運算資源，同樣困難重重，」他說。

  ## 經濟效益需要數量級的躍進

  基辛格最犀利的批判落在經濟面。儘管AI基礎建設已獲得空前龐大的投資，他仍認為生產智慧的成本遠遠過高，而且所需的改善幅度絕非漸進式的。「不是要變得更好——而是要好上一萬倍，」他說。

  他主張，這樣的進展不會來自單一突破，而必須在半導體、記憶體、網路、電力系統與資料中心架構等各個面向同步推進。他也指出，AI帶來的經濟轉型已讓晶片業者大為受益。「矽晶圓廠商是這波浪潮的最大贏家，」他說。「做晶片從來沒有像現在這麼好的時代。」

  ## OpenAI的部署時程目標

  卡提表示，OpenAI目前的重點在於縮短新運算基礎建設投入使用所需的時間。「我們的願景是：能不能把這個時間壓縮到三個季度，而不是三年？」他說。要實現這一目標，需要更快的晶片製造速度、標準化的資料中心建設流程、更充裕的電力基礎建設，以及在整個產業中更有效率地部署運算資源。

  ## 對產業的深遠意義

  這兩位AI硬體供應鏈重量級人士的發言，進一步印證了業界的首要制約已從模型能力本身，轉移至在規模化運行模型時所需的實體與經濟基礎建設。對資料中心營運商與基礎建設投資者而言，基辛格與卡提的論述暗示：AI下一階段的競爭優勢，將取決於整個硬體與電力堆疊的效率提升，而非單靠採購更多加速器。
