body_en: |
  China's SpinQ and U.S.-based Qolab have separately disclosed major financing rounds totaling more than $200 million between them, marking parallel capital mobilization for superconducting quantum computing on both sides of the Pacific.

  ## SpinQ's $147 million Series D

  SpinQ closed a $147 million Series D funding round with participation from CICC Capital, Shenzhen TopoScend Capital, Shanghai Semiconductor Industry Investment, AVIC Honghua, and Shanghai SEARI Private Equity Investment Management, among others. The company said proceeds would be used to accelerate development of fault-tolerant general-purpose quantum computers, upgrade core manufacturing processes, and expand its global ecosystem.

  SpinQ said it has established a superconducting quantum chip research, development, and pilot production line in Shenzhen, China, supporting design, process development, fabrication, packaging, and testing. The company said it has completed tape-out — the point at which a finalized chip design is committed to fabrication — as well as packaging and validation of its latest 25-qubit and 103-qubit superconducting quantum chips at the facility. Qubits, or quantum bits, are the fundamental unit of quantum information; qubit count and the ability to sustain coherent quantum states without errors are key benchmarks on the path toward practical quantum systems.

  SpinQ described its technology base as a full-stack platform spanning quantum chip design, quantum electronic design automation (EDA) software, control systems, full-system manufacturing, cloud services, and quantum algorithm applications.

  "Quantum computing is a frontier technology that requires long-term commitment, sustained investment, and deep system-level engineering capabilities," said Dr. Xiang Jingen, founder and CEO of SpinQ. "With this new round of funding, we will remain focused on our core goal of building fault-tolerant general-purpose quantum computers, while continuing to advance quantum error correction and key hardware and software technologies."

  ## Qolab's $54.2 million Series B

  California-based Qolab announced the close of a Series B Preferred Stock financing round totaling $54.2 million. UC Investments led the round, which also comprised $12.6 million in convertible securities and a commitment for an additional $10 million in future convertible securities. Other investors include the Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation (WARF), Octave Ventures, and Phoenix Venture Partners.

  Qolab said the funding would support continued development of scalable superconducting quantum computing technologies, expand its strategic semiconductor collaborations, and advance its target of delivering fault-tolerant quantum computing. Fault-tolerant quantum computing refers to systems capable of detecting and correcting the errors that naturally arise in quantum operations, a prerequisite for broad practical use.

  Founded in 2022 by former Google quantum researchers, Qolab is focused on superconducting qubits and improving qubit coherence — the duration a qubit can hold its quantum state before errors accumulate. CEO Alan Ho previously led product and program management for Google's Quantum AI team. CTO John Martinis led the Google Quantum AI Lab team that, in 2019, developed a quantum computer the company said could outperform classical supercomputers on a specific task; Martinis also holds a distinguished professorship at UC Santa Barbara.

  Qolab launched from stealth in summer 2024 after raising $3.5 million that year from the Development Bank of Japan, a state-owned institution.

  "This investment enables Qolab to accelerate development of scalable quantum systems while deepening our collaborations across the University of California ecosystem and the broader scientific community," Martinis said.

  ## Divergent backer profiles, shared engineering goal

  The two rounds reflect investment momentum in superconducting quantum computing from distinct pools of capital. Qolab's Series B draws from U.S. university endowments and venture capital firms, with UC Investments — the investment arm of the University of California system — as lead. SpinQ's Series D includes Chinese state-linked capital alongside semiconductor-sector investors. Despite the different geographies and investor compositions, both companies have identified fault-tolerant quantum computing as their primary near-term engineering objective, and both are building or expanding domestic chip fabrication capacity to reduce reliance on outside production.

  SpinQ said it plans to continue advancing quantum error correction and expanding its global ecosystem following the close of its Series D.

  ## SpinQ完成1.47億美元D輪融資

  量旋科技完成1.47億美元D輪融資，參投方包括中金資本、深圳拓璟資本、上海半導體產業投資、中航弘華，以及上海國睿私募股權投資管理等機構。公司表示，本輪募資將用於加速容錯通用量子電腦的研發、升級核心製造製程，並拓展全球生態系。

  量旋科技指出，公司已在中國深圳建立超導量子晶片研發與中試生產線，涵蓋設計、製程開發、製造、封裝及測試等完整環節。公司表示，最新的25量子位元與103量子位元超導量子晶片已在該產線完成流片——即晶片設計定稿後正式提交製造的關鍵節點——並已完成封裝與驗證。量子位元（qubit）是量子資訊的基本單位，量子位元數量以及在不發生錯誤的前提下維持相干量子態的能力，是通往實用量子系統的重要評量指標。

  量旋科技將自身技術定位為全棧式平台，涵蓋量子晶片設計、量子電子設計自動化（EDA）軟體、控制系統、整機製造、雲端服務，以及量子演算法應用。

  「量子運算是一項需要長期耕耘、持續投入與深厚系統工程能力的前沿技術，」量旋科技創辦人暨執行長向靜軒博士表示。「獲得本輪融資後，我們將持續聚焦打造容錯通用量子電腦的核心目標，同時持續推進量子錯誤更正以及關鍵硬體與軟體技術的突破。」

  ## Qolab完成5,420萬美元B輪融資

  總部位於加州的Qolab宣布完成5,420萬美元B輪特別股融資。本輪由UC Investments領投，另包含1,260萬美元可轉換證券，以及一筆未來1,000萬美元可轉換證券的承諾。其他投資方包括威斯康辛大學研究基金會（WARF）、Octave Ventures與Phoenix Venture Partners。

  Qolab表示，這筆資金將支持可擴展超導量子運算技術的持續研發、深化與半導體業者的策略合作，並推進容錯量子運算的目標。所謂容錯量子運算，是指系統能夠偵測並更正量子操作中自然產生的錯誤，這也是量子電腦走向廣泛實用的必要前提。

  Qolab由前Google量子研究人員於2022年創立，專注於超導量子位元研究，以及提升量子位元相干性——即量子位元在錯誤累積前能夠保持量子態的時間。執行長Alan Ho曾主導Google量子AI團隊的產品與專案管理；技術長John Martinis則曾領導Google量子AI實驗室團隊，於2019年開發出一台量子電腦，Google宣稱該電腦能在特定任務上超越傳統超級電腦；Martinis同時擔任加州大學聖塔芭芭拉分校的特聘教授。

  Qolab於2024年夏季結束隱身模式正式公開亮相，同年已從日本政策投資銀行（一家國有機構）籌得350萬美元種子資金。

  「這筆投資讓Qolab得以加速可擴展量子系統的研發，同時深化我們在加州大學生態系及更廣泛科學社群的合作，」Martinis表示。

  ## 投資方背景各異，工程目標殊途同歸

  這兩輪融資反映出超導量子運算領域吸引了截然不同的資本群體。Qolab的B輪資金來自美國大學捐贈基金與創投業者，由加州大學系統旗下投資部門UC Investments領銜。SpinQ的D輪則包含中國國家關聯資本，以及半導體產業投資機構。儘管地域背景與投資方組成大相逕庭，兩家公司皆已將容錯量子運算列為近期首要工程目標，並正積極建立或擴充本地晶片製造產能，以降低對外部生產的依賴。

  量旋科技表示，完成D輪融資後，公司將持續推進量子錯誤更正技術，並擴展全球生態系布局。
