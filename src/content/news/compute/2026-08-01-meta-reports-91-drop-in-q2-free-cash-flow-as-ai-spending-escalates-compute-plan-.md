body_en: |
  Meta Platforms reported a 91% year-over-year drop in second-quarter free cash flow on Wednesday, falling to $784 million from $8.55 billion a year earlier, as artificial intelligence infrastructure spending consumed capital at a pace that alarmed investors and drove shares down more than 9% on Thursday.

  The decline was the steepest since late 2022, when Meta faced comparable scrutiny over spending on its virtual reality and metaverse ventures. The company simultaneously raised the lower end of its 2026 capital expenditure forecast to $130 billion, narrowing its guidance range to between $130 billion and $145 billion. That range has been revised upward twice since January, when Meta had guided for $115 billion to $135 billion.

  ## The compute strategy question

  On the earnings call, CEO Mark Zuckerberg said a significant portion of Meta's compute — the chips, servers, and data center infrastructure that run AI workloads — would go toward training AI models and supporting the company's core advertising business. He also said Meta planned to build a "large business serving large customers," citing unsolicited offers from outside companies willing to rent compute capacity at "a meaningful premium" over what Meta spent to build it.

  Zuckerberg argued, however, that selling AI-generated outputs would carry substantially higher margins than renting raw computing power. "We believe that there will continue to be a significantly higher margin on selling intelligence rather than selling compute directly, but we think that there's a big opportunity obviously to sell compute as well," he said.

  Pressed by JPMorgan analyst Doug Anmuth, who noted that Meta was simultaneously purchasing capacity from third parties and fielding offers to sell its own, Zuckerberg said the company was intentionally investing ahead of demand, given the lead time between when data centers are funded and when they become operational and generate value.

  Josh Gilbert, lead Asia-Pacific analyst at online investing platform eToro, framed the structural problem: "Meta is spending like a hyperscaler without a hyperscaler's business model. Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon can point their data centre dollars at cloud businesses that sell compute straight back out the door, but Meta doesn't have the same outlet, so every dollar of build-out leans on the ads business." A hyperscaler is a company that operates very large-scale data center infrastructure and sells computing resources to outside customers.

  ## Scale of the buildout

  Reuters reported this month that Meta plans to double its overall computing capacity to 7 gigawatts (GW) this year and to double it again, reaching 14 GW, next year. A gigawatt equals one billion watts; data centers draw power continuously to run servers and cooling equipment. Meta currently has 32 data centers in operation or under construction globally.

  CFO Susan Li told analysts the AI infrastructure industry had "underbuilt historically," making existing capacity "extremely valuable," and expected supply to remain tight "for the foreseeable future." She argued that constraint created opportunities to generate returns through Meta's products, enterprise services, and compute sales.

  Meta's planned 2026 outlays are part of a broader industry surge: Big Tech companies are projected to spend more than $700 billion on AI technology this year, according to figures cited in earnings reporting.

  ## Revenue and earnings

  Second-quarter revenue rose 28% to $60.8 billion, the fastest pace since the fourth quarter of 2021, excluding the first quarter of 2026. Daily active people across Meta's apps reached 3.6 billion, a 3% rise year over year.

  Earnings per share came in at $6.18, below analysts' average estimate of $7.22 compiled by financial data provider LSEG. Li said operating income would have increased 9% year over year absent one-time legal charges and severance costs; it actually fell 8%.

  Luke Stillman, managing director at research firm Madison and Wall, said: "Meta's underlying ad business that's financing everything though is still performing well and is our main focus."

  ## Metaverse parallels

  The combination of large upfront infrastructure spending and uncertain near-term revenue drew comparisons to Meta's earlier metaverse push. Reality Labs, the division responsible for Meta's virtual and augmented reality efforts, has accumulated more than $80 billion in operating losses. The late-2022 period, when Meta's free cash flow was last comparably depressed, coincided with the height of investor concern over those losses.

  Mike Proulx, a senior executive at research firm Forrester, said: "Meta's AI spend was easier to celebrate when margins were expanding. It's harder to celebrate now that the costs are showing up in the numbers." He added that Meta's stated ambitions extend beyond its existing platforms: "The company believes AI can create entirely new businesses."

  Bernstein analyst Mark Shmulik offered a blunt assessment: "The earnings call felt a lot like a good old-fashioned brainstorming session."

  ## Sector context

  Alphabet, Google's parent company, last week reported its first cash-flow-negative quarter on record and raised its own capital expenditure forecast by $15 billion; its shares fell 7%. Microsoft reported a 23% year-over-year drop in free cash flow in the June quarter, but growth in its Azure cloud computing unit and Copilot AI assistant, driven by an established base of corporate customers, alleviated investor concern and sent its shares up more than 13% on Thursday.

  ## Legal exposure

  Meta also disclosed that four U.S. states are seeking $1.4 trillion in penalties over accusations that the company designed Facebook and Instagram to addict young users and misled the public about platform safety. Li warned that youth-related trials scheduled in the United States this year "may ultimately result in a material loss."

  The company has flagged since April that legal and regulatory pressure over youth social media use in the United States and the European Union "could significantly impact" its business and financial results. Meta also recorded severance costs related to the elimination of approximately 10% of its global workforce, around 8,000 employees, in May as part of an AI-focused restructuring.

  ## What comes next

  Pressed by a Wells Fargo analyst on whether Meta planned to use all of its compute internally through 2027, Li declined to specify, leaving open how much capacity the company would direct to outside customers. The company's 2026 capital expenditure guidance, now set at $130 billion to $145 billion, has been raised from the original January forecast of $115 billion to $135 billion, with the latest upward revision narrowing the floor rather than extending the ceiling.
body_zh: |
  Meta Platforms周三公布，第二季自由現金流較去年同期大幅衰退91%，由去年的85.5億美元驟降至7.84億美元。AI基礎設施支出以驚人速度消耗資本，令投資人深感憂慮，公司股價週四隨即重挫逾9%。

  這是自2022年底以來跌幅最大的一次——當年Meta因在虛擬實境與元宇宙事業大舉燒錢，承受了相近程度的質疑聲浪。與此同時，Meta將2026年資本支出預測的下限上調至1,300億美元，指引區間縮窄為1,300億至1,450億美元。這一預測區間自今年1月以來已兩度上修，最初的指引為1,150億至1,350億美元。

  ## 運算策略問題

  法說會上，執行長馬克·祖克柏（Mark Zuckerberg）表示，Meta大部分的運算資源——包括晶片、伺服器及支撐AI工作負載的資料中心基礎設施——將用於訓練AI模型，並支持公司核心廣告業務。他同時透露，Meta計畫打造「一門大型業務，服務大型客戶」，並提及已有外部企業主動洽詢，願意以高於Meta建置成本「相當可觀的溢價」租用運算資源。

  然而，祖克柏主張，出售AI生成的輸出成果，其利潤率將遠高於直接出租原始算力。「我們相信，出售智慧所帶來的利潤率，將持續遠高於直接出售運算資源；但我們認為，出售運算資源顯然也是一大商機，」他說。

  摩根大通（JPMorgan）分析師道格·安穆斯（Doug Anmuth）追問：Meta一方面向第三方購買運算產能，同時又有廠商登門求購，兩者似乎並行不悖。祖克柏對此解釋，公司是刻意超前布局，原因在於資料中心從資金挹注到正式運轉、開始創造效益，中間存在相當長的前置期。

  線上投資平台eToro亞太區首席分析師喬許·吉爾伯特（Josh Gilbert）點出了結構性困境：「Meta花的是超大規模雲端業者的錢，卻沒有超大規模雲端業者的商業模式。微軟、Alphabet和亞馬遜可以把資料中心的投入，直接轉化為對外銷售運算資源的雲端業務，但Meta沒有相同的出口，每一塊錢的建設費用都壓在廣告業務身上。」所謂超大規模雲端業者，是指運營超大型資料中心基礎設施、並對外出售運算資源的企業。

  ## 建設規模

  路透社本月報導，Meta計畫今年將整體運算產能倍增至7吉瓦（GW），明年再翻倍至14吉瓦。1吉瓦等於10億瓦；資料中心須持續耗電以維持伺服器與冷卻設備運行。Meta目前在全球共有32座資料中心投入運營或興建中。

  財務長蘇珊·李（Susan Li）告訴分析師，AI基礎設施產業「歷史上一直處於供給不足的狀態」，使既有產能「極具價值」，預期供給在「可預見的未來」仍將持續吃緊。她認為，供需緊張的格局，為Meta透過旗下產品、企業服務及運算資源出售創造回報提供了機會。

  Meta的2026年支出計畫是整體產業投資熱潮的一部分：根據法說會引用的數據，大型科技公司今年在AI技術上的支出預計超過7,000億美元。

  ## 營收與獲利

  第二季營收年增28%至608億美元，是自2021年第四季以來的最快增速（不含2026年第一季）。Meta旗下各應用程式的每日活躍用戶達36億人，較去年同期成長3%。

  每股盈餘為6.18美元，低於金融數據商LSEG匯整的市場平均預估7.22美元。李表示，若排除一次性法律費用與遣散費用，營業利益將年增9%；但實際數字顯示，營業利益年減8%。

  研究機構Madison and Wall董事總經理盧克·史提曼（Luke Stillman）表示：「為這一切提供資金的Meta廣告業務核心，表現依然穩健，這仍是我們關注的重點。」

  ## 元宇宙對比

  大規模前期基礎設施支出加上近期獲利前景未明，令市場將此次情況與Meta早年的元宇宙豪賭相提並論。負責Meta虛擬實境與擴增實境業務的Reality Labs部門，已累積逾800億美元的營業虧損。2022年底——Meta自由現金流上一次如此低迷之際——正值投資人對這些虧損憂慮最深的時期。

  研究機構Forrester的資深主管麥克·普魯克斯（Mike Proulx）表示：「當利潤率持續擴張時，Meta的AI支出比較容易讓人振奮。如今成本已在數字上顯現，就沒那麼值得慶賀了。」他補充，Meta的雄心不止於現有平台：「公司相信AI能開創全新的業務形態。」

  Bernstein分析師馬克·什穆利克（Mark Shmulik）給出直白評價：「這場法說會感覺很像一場老派的腦力激盪。」

  ## 產業背景

  Google母公司Alphabet上週公布有史以來首次單季現金流轉負，並將資本支出預測上調150億美元，股價隨後下跌7%。微軟6月季度自由現金流年減23%，但Azure雲端運算部門及Copilot AI助理的強勁成長——有賴龐大的企業客戶基礎——緩解了投資人的疑慮，股價週四大漲逾13%。

  ## 法律風險

  Meta同時揭露，美國四個州正以公司刻意將Facebook和Instagram設計成令年輕用戶成癮、並就平台安全性誤導大眾為由，尋求追討高達1.4兆美元的罰款。李警告，今年在美國排定開審的青少年相關訴訟「最終可能造成重大損失」。

  公司自4月起便已提示，美國與歐盟針對青少年社群媒體使用所施加的法律與監管壓力，「可能對業務和財務結果造成重大影響」。Meta亦認列了相關遣散費，此與今年5月以AI轉型為由、裁撤全球約10%員工（約8,000人）的組織重整有關。

  ## 後續展望

  富國銀行（Wells Fargo）一名分析師追問，Meta是否計畫在2027年前將所有運算資源用於內部需求，財務長李對此未作正面回應，未說明公司將有多少產能開放給外部客戶。公司2026年資本支出指引目前定在1,300億至1,450億美元，較今年1月原始預測的1,150億至1,350億美元上調，最新一次上修收窄的是下限，而非上限。
