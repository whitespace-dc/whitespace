body_en: |
  Quantum computing systems approaching the gigaquop threshold — one billion reliable logical operations — will require classical compute, cooling, power, and networking infrastructure on par with conventional high-performance computing environments, QuEra chief commercial officer Yuval Boger wrote in an analysis published November 1, 2026.

  Boger's piece argues that the data center industry needs to begin treating quantum processors as components of a heterogeneous computing stack rather than self-contained machines, a framing with direct implications for how operators plan capacity, power, and cooling.

  ## From qubit counts to quops

  The quantum computing industry has historically measured progress by counting physical qubits, the fundamental building blocks of a quantum processor. Boger argues that metric is insufficient. Physical qubits are error-prone, and what determines practical utility is the number of reliable, error-corrected logical operations a system can execute before errors compromise a calculation.

  The emerging measure is the "quop," shorthand for a quantum operation, a term popularized by physicist John Preskill. Industry usage has coalesced around two thresholds: a megaquop system, capable of approximately one million reliable logical operations, and a gigaquop system, capable of approximately one billion. Boger notes that quops are not a complete performance measure — gate speed, connectivity, parallelism, and workload type remain relevant — but the metric relates more directly to computational capability than raw qubit counts.

  QuEra's own Libra system, listed on the company's public roadmap for 2028, is designed to deliver 256 logical qubits built from more than 10,000 physical qubits at a logical error rate of 10⁻⁶, or one error per million operations. That specification places Libra in the megaquop class.

  ## What a megaquop can and cannot do

  At the megaquop scale, Boger writes, systems may enable meaningful simulations of selected materials and quantum dynamics, and allow testing of quantum algorithms at scales beyond today's hardware. Some chemistry applications may become accessible. However, one million reliable operations is unlikely to be sufficient for large industrial molecules, complete reaction pathways, or the deeper circuits required by many optimization and machine-learning workloads, according to his analysis.

  The gigaquop threshold represents a thousandfold increase. Boger describes this jump as potentially more significant than a conventional performance increment, writing that it could bring a broader range of scientifically and commercially relevant workloads within practical consideration, including problems that may become impractical to solve classically at comparable accuracy and scale. Applications he identifies as leading candidates include catalysts, batteries, superconducting materials, and aspects of drug discovery — workloads in which electron behavior is inherently quantum mechanical and computationally expensive to model with classical methods. A concrete near-term example he cites is computing the electronic structure of small strongly correlated molecules, such as catalytic reaction intermediates, where the number of interacting electrons places exact classical methods out of reach.

  Optimization and certain machine-learning applications may also benefit, Boger writes, though he adds that evidence for broad quantum advantage in those areas remains less established.

  ## The quantum processor as accelerator

  Boger's central infrastructure argument is that quantum processors will not operate as standalone replacements for conventional hardware. Most useful quantum workloads will involve substantial classical computation before, between, and after quantum operations. Conventional CPUs and GPUs will prepare data and problem instances, manage algorithms and error correction, and process results generated by the quantum processor. The quantum processor, Boger writes, therefore becomes a specialized accelerator within a heterogeneous computing environment.

  That framing shifts the capacity-planning question for data center operators. The relevant footprint is not the quantum processor alone but the total infrastructure required to deliver quantum compute: control electronics, classical processing, networking, and environmental systems. Requirements will vary by architecture. Some quantum systems require cryogenic environments; others operate at or closer to room temperature but depend on sophisticated optical, electronic, or environmental systems. Boger states there is unlikely to be a single model for the quantum data center.

  ## Infrastructure and latency considerations

  Workloads that alternate repeatedly between classical and quantum computation could be sensitive to the latency involved in moving information between the two environments, Boger writes. The location and architecture of the classical resources supporting a quantum processor may therefore influence overall application performance, a consideration analogous to how proximity between CPU and GPU affects conventional accelerated workloads.

  On power and cooling, Boger notes that the quantum processor itself may not be the largest consumer of infrastructure resources. Control systems, error correction, readout, and classical processing all contribute to total system requirements. He also identifies a counterpoint: improvements in quantum error correction and hardware efficiency could allow computational capacity to grow faster than associated physical infrastructure demand.

  The useful performance metric for operators, Boger proposes, may ultimately be "useful quantum throughput" — how many workloads a system can execute, how efficiently the quantum resource can be utilized, how quickly jobs can be completed, and how effectively quantum resources can be coordinated with conventional computing.

  ## Planning under uncertainty

  Boger acknowledges a fundamental constraint on forecasting: the most important applications of gigaquop-scale systems may emerge only once sufficient computational capacity is available, making them difficult to predict from the current vantage point. He draws a parallel to historical computing platforms that created uses that were difficult to anticipate when the underlying technology was introduced.

  For data center planners, that uncertainty compounds the infrastructure challenge. Operators must evaluate a technology whose performance is advancing rapidly, whose workloads are still developing, and whose infrastructure requirements differ from those of conventional CPUs and GPUs.

  QuEra's Libra system, targeting megaquop-class performance, is scheduled for delivery in 2028 according to the company's public roadmap.
body_zh: |
  量子運算系統正逐步逼近gigaquop門檻——即十億次可靠的邏輯運算。QuEra首席商務長尤瓦爾・博格（Yuval Boger）在2026年11月1日發表的產業分析文章中指出，這類系統所需的傳統運算、冷卻、電力與網路基礎設施，將與高效能運算環境相當。

  博格的文章主張，資料中心產業必須開始將量子處理器視為異質運算架構中的一個組件，而非獨立自成的機器。這一框架對於業者如何規劃容量、電力與冷卻，具有直接影響。

  ## 從量子位元數到量子運算次數（quop）

  量子運算產業長期以來以實體量子位元（physical qubit）的數量衡量進展，而量子位元正是量子處理器的基本組成單元。博格認為這項指標並不充分。實體量子位元容易出錯，決定實用性的關鍵，在於系統在運算結果被錯誤破壞之前，能夠執行多少次可靠的、經過錯誤修正的邏輯運算。

  新興的衡量標準是「quop」，即量子運算（quantum operation）的縮寫，由物理學家約翰・普雷斯基爾（John Preskill）推廣。業界目前聚焦於兩個門檻：megaquop系統，約可執行一百萬次可靠的邏輯運算；以及gigaquop系統，約可執行十億次。博格指出，quop並非完整的效能衡量標準——量子閘速度、連接性、並行處理能力與工作負載類型仍然重要——但相較於原始量子位元數，此指標更能直接反映運算能力。

  QuEra自家的Libra系統已列入公司2028年的公開路線圖，設計上將以超過一萬個實體量子位元構建256個邏輯量子位元，邏輯錯誤率為10⁻⁶，即每百萬次運算出現一次錯誤。依此規格，Libra屬於megaquop等級。

  ## Megaquop能做什麼，又有什麼局限

  在megaquop規模下，博格寫道，系統或許能針對特定材料與量子動力學進行有意義的模擬，並允許在超越現有硬體規模的條件下測試量子演算法。部分化學應用或許也將因此變得可及。然而，根據其分析，一百萬次可靠運算對於大型工業分子、完整反應路徑，或許多最佳化與機器學習工作負載所需的深層電路而言，仍可能不足。

  gigaquop門檻代表著一千倍的提升。博格將這一躍進描述為潛在影響力遠超常規效能提升的里程碑，認為它可能使更廣泛的、在科學與商業上具有意義的工作負載進入實際考量範圍，包括一些以傳統方式在相近精度與規模下求解可能趨於不可行的問題。他點名的領先應用候選包括催化劑、電池、超導材料，以及藥物開發的部分環節——這些工作負載中，電子行為本質上具有量子力學特性，以傳統方法建模的計算成本極高。他舉出的一個具體近期範例，是計算小型強關聯分子（如催化反應中間體）的電子結構，在這類問題中，相互作用的電子數目使傳統的精確方法難以勝任。

  最佳化與部分機器學習應用或許也能受益，博格寫道，但他補充指出，量子優勢在這些領域廣泛成立的證據目前仍不充分。

  ## 量子處理器作為加速器

  博格在基礎設施面的核心論點是：量子處理器不會以獨立機器的形式取代傳統硬體運作。大多數有用的量子工作負載，在量子運算前後及過程之間，都涉及大量的傳統運算。傳統的CPU與GPU將負責準備資料與問題實例、管理演算法與錯誤修正，並處理量子處理器所產生的結果。因此，量子處理器將成為異質運算環境中的專用加速器。

  這一框架改變了資料中心業者進行容量規劃時的思考方式。相關佔地面積不僅限於量子處理器本身，而是提供量子運算所需的全部基礎設施：控制電子設備、傳統處理器、網路，以及環境系統。各架構的需求將有所不同。部分量子系統需要低溫環境；其他系統雖可在接近室溫的條件下運作，但仍依賴精密的光學、電子或環境系統。博格表示，量子資料中心不太可能存在單一通用模型。

  ## 基礎設施與延遲考量

  對於在傳統運算與量子運算之間反覆切換的工作負載，在兩者之間傳遞資訊所產生的延遲可能帶來影響，博格寫道。因此，支援量子處理器的傳統資源之位置與架構，可能會左右整體應用效能——這一考量類似於CPU與GPU之間的距離如何影響傳統加速運算工作負載的效能。

  在電力與冷卻方面，博格指出量子處理器本身未必是基礎設施資源的最大消耗者。控制系統、錯誤修正、讀出以及傳統處理器，都共同構成整體系統的需求。他也提出了一個反向觀點：量子錯誤修正技術與硬體效率的提升，或許能使運算能力的增長速度超過相關實體基礎設施需求的增速。

  博格提議，對業者而言，真正有用的效能指標或許終究是「有效量子吞吐量」——亦即系統能執行多少工作負載、量子資源能以多高的效率被利用、任務能以多快的速度完成，以及量子資源能多有效地與傳統運算協調配合。

  ## 在不確定中規劃

  博格承認預測工作面臨一項根本性的限制：gigaquop規模系統最重要的應用，或許只有在足夠的運算能力實際到位之後才會浮現，因此從當前的視角難以預判。他援引歷史上的運算平台作為類比——這些平台所催生的用途，在底層技術問世之初同樣難以預料。

  對資料中心規劃者而言，這種不確定性使基礎設施挑戰更加複雜。業者必須評估一項效能快速演進的技術，而其工作負載仍在發展之中，基礎設施需求也與傳統CPU及GPU有所不同。
