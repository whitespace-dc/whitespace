body_en: |
  New US export controls that took effect November 19 have shut Chinese artificial intelligence companies out of overseas data centre computing, a channel that supplied roughly 30% of their processing capacity, while domestic alternatives remain constrained by manufacturing bottlenecks and high costs.

  ## The rules and their reach

  The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security announced the controls on November 16. They prohibit data centres outside the United States from providing significant computing power to Chinese clients unless the operator holds a US government licence. Chinese companies had grown increasingly reliant on renting capacity abroad after Washington began restricting direct chip exports in 2022, making the new rules a direct hit on a workaround the industry had built over three years.

  Before the restrictions took effect, the computing power Chinese firms could access through overseas data centres was projected to nearly triple, from the equivalent of approximately one million Nvidia H100 graphics processing units at the end of 2026 to 2.8 million units a year later, according to the Washington-based Institute for Progress think-tank.

  ## Chinese companies signal disruption

  Tencent president Martin Lau said on a November 18 earnings call that reduced access to advanced computing power could lengthen the development timeline for a future generation of the company's Hunyuan AI model. "It sets everyone back in China," Lau said.

  Alibaba chief executive Eddie Wu said the following day that the company could scale back its long-term data centre expansion target. The uncertainty has reached lenders: bankers have paused work on a proposed $480 million loan to pay for Nvidia chips at a Malaysian data centre that would serve ByteDance, the Chinese owner of TikTok.

  At least two of Nvidia's server partners have suspended deliveries to customers in Southeast Asia, according to Benjamin Ho, lead research strategist for Hong Kong and China equities at Maybank Investment Banking Group. Ho said model iteration and product roll-outs will slow significantly and the effect will become more severe in coming months.

  ## Domestic chip supply already near its limits

  The restrictions fall on a domestic chip market operating near maximum capacity. China's leading semiconductor manufacturer, SMIC (Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation), is running at 98% capacity, and buyers including AI startup DeepSeek face wait times of more than a year for the latest domestically available chips, Ho said.

  Large technology companies including Baidu, Tencent and Alibaba are best positioned to weather the scarcity because of early investments in domestic data centres and hardware, Ho said. Pure AI startups such as Moonshot AI and MiniMax are most exposed because they depend on renting computing capacity from their larger rivals.

  ## Nvidia's position in China

  Access to Nvidia chips inside China has also grown less certain. Nvidia has confirmed it stopped shipping its H200 processors to China despite holding some US export licences. Beijing has withheld approval for a shipment of approximately 600,000 H200 chips to enter from Hong Kong, according to a November 5 Reuters report.

  Nvidia accounted for 55% of China's AI chip market in 2025, according to Sigrid Wang, lead technology analyst at Hutong Research, an independent Chinese advisory firm. "Were Nvidia to stop supplying China with its H20 and H200 chips altogether, Chinese chipmakers would have a huge gap to fill," Wang said.

  Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia chief executive on November 23. Huang had been one of the most prominent advocates in Washington for continued chip sales to China, but analysts say his return changes little for Chinese buyers. Nvidia's board has established a new committee to oversee national security risks and compliance with export controls, according to George Chen, a partner who leads digital practice at The Asia Group, a Washington-based consultancy. "Nvidia will surely double down on compliance because it won't want to be accused again of being too cosy with China," Chen said.

  Tan Kong Yam, an emeritus professor of economics at Singapore's Nanyang Technological University, said the episode points to a structural vulnerability. "The most important lesson for China is that it cannot depend on the goodwill of individual American corporate leaders, regardless of their commercial influence or willingness to engage with China," Tan said.

  ## Home-grown chip alternatives remain costly

  Chinese companies including Huawei, Baidu, Alibaba and Cambricon have made progress in designing AI processors, but China's capacity to manufacture them at scale remains limited. Huawei has built what are called supernodes, clusters that connect many chips at high speed to allow them collectively to train and run large AI models. Wang said Huawei's Cloudmatrix 384 can rival Nvidia's GB200 NVL72 system on some performance measures, but carries a price of $8 million, putting it out of reach for smaller developers. Connecting more processors to compensate for lower individual chip performance draws further on the already constrained domestic supply.

  The manufacturing bottleneck could deepen if the US Congress passes the MATCH Act, a bill that would press allied countries including the Netherlands and Japan to restrict exports to China of equipment used to manufacture advanced chips, according to Guo Shan, a partner at Hutong.

  ## What it means for China's AI industry

  The combined restrictions are turning domestic chip and infrastructure development from a contingency plan into a central priority for Chinese AI companies. A Chinese government adviser on AI policy, speaking anonymously because he was not authorised to speak to the media, said the curbs create a window for domestic capability to grow. "Instead of bemoaning how the US curbs will handicap us, we would rather see them as giving us a window period to grow ourselves," the adviser said.

  The Institute for Progress had projected that overseas data centre capacity available to Chinese firms would reach the equivalent of 2.8 million H100 chips by the end of 2027; under the new rules, accessing that capacity requires US government licences that are not guaranteed.
body_zh: |
  美國商務部新一輪出口管制措施自11月19日起正式生效，封鎖中國人工智慧企業向海外資料中心租用算力的管道，一舉切斷其約三成的算力來源，而國內替代方案受製造瓶頸與高昂成本雙重箝制，紓困空間極為有限。

  ## 法規內容與影響範圍

  美國商務部工業安全局（BIS）於11月16日公布上述管制措施。新規禁止美國境外的資料中心在未持有美國政府許可證的情況下，向中國客戶提供大量算力。自2022年華盛頓開始限制晶片直接出口後，中國企業愈來愈依賴向海外租用算力，此番新規等於正面打擊了這個行業歷經三年所建立的變通管道。

  在限制生效之前，中國企業可透過海外資料中心取得的算力，原本預計將從2026年底相當於約100萬張輝達H100繪圖處理器的規模，在一年後暴增至280萬張——根據總部位於華盛頓的智庫「進步研究所」（Institute for Progress）的估算，幾乎三倍於前。

  ## 中企示警，影響已現

  騰訊總裁劉熾平於11月18日法說會上表示，取得高階算力的管道受限，可能拉長公司下一代混元（Hunyuan）AI模型的開發時程。他說：「這讓中國所有業者都倒退一步。」

  阿里巴巴執行長吳泳銘隔日表示，公司可能縮減長期資料中心擴建目標。不確定性已延伸至金融圈：有關籌措一筆4.8億美元貸款、用於購買輝達晶片以支援馬來西亞一座將服務字節跳動（TikTok母公司）之資料中心的計畫，相關銀行業者已暫停作業。

  馬來亞銀行投資銀行集團（Maybank Investment Banking Group）香港及中國股票首席研究策略師Benjamin Ho指出，輝達旗下至少兩家伺服器合作夥伴已暫停對東南亞客戶的交貨。他表示，AI模型迭代與產品推出速度將大幅放緩，且衝擊將在未來數月持續加重。

  ## 國內晶片供應已達極限

  上述限制落在一個早已逼近產能天花板的國內晶片市場上。Benjamin Ho指出，中國首要半導體製造商中芯國際（SMIC）的產能利用率已達98%，包括AI新創公司深度求索（DeepSeek）在內的買家，等待最新可取得的國產晶片已超過一年。

  他表示，百度、騰訊、阿里巴巴等大型科技公司因早年積極布建國內資料中心與硬體設備，在此次供應緊縮中處於最有利的應對位置。反觀月之暗面（Moonshot AI）、MiniMax等純AI新創公司，由於高度依賴向上述科技大廠租用算力，承受的風險敞口最大。

  ## 輝達在中國市場的處境

  輝達晶片在中國境內的供應前景同樣充滿變數。輝達已確認停止向中國出貨其H200處理器，儘管持有部分美國出口許可證。據路透社11月5日報導，北京已拒絕批准約60萬顆H200晶片經香港入境的申請。

  根據獨立中國研究機構胡同研究（Hutong Research）首席科技分析師Sigrid Wang的數據，2025年輝達在中國AI晶片市場的佔有率達55%。她表示：「若輝達全面停止向中國供應H20與H200晶片，中國晶片製造商將面臨一個龐大缺口亟需填補。」

  黃仁勳於11月23日重新就任輝達執行長。他一向是華盛頓最力主持續對中國銷售晶片的倡導者之一，但分析師認為他的回歸對中國買家的處境改變有限。總部位於華盛頓的顧問公司亞洲集團（The Asia Group）數位業務合夥人George Chen表示，輝達董事會已成立新的委員會，負責監督國家安全風險與出口管制合規事務。他說：「輝達肯定會加倍嚴格執行合規，因為它不會想再度被指責與中國走得太近。」

  新加坡南洋理工大學經濟學榮譽退休教授Tan Kong Yam指出，這一事件揭示了中國的結構性脆弱。他說：「對中國而言，最重要的一課是，不能依賴個別美國企業領導人的善意，無論其商業影響力多大、與中國互動的意願多強。」

  ## 國產晶片替代方案成本高昂

  華為、百度、阿里巴巴及寒武紀（Cambricon）等中國企業在AI處理器設計上已取得進展，但中國大規模製造這些晶片的能力仍十分有限。華為建構了所謂的「超節點」（supernode）——以高速互連方式將大量晶片串聯成叢集，使其能夠共同訓練並執行大型AI模型。Sigrid Wang表示，華為的CloudMatrix 384在部分效能指標上可媲美輝達的GB200 NVL72系統，但售價高達800萬美元，令規模較小的開發者望而卻步。以串連更多處理器來彌補單一晶片效能不足的做法，更進一步消耗了本已吃緊的國內供應。

  胡同研究合夥人Guo Shan表示，若美國國會通過《MATCH法案》，要求荷蘭、日本等盟國限制對中國出口用於製造先進晶片的設備，這一製造瓶頸恐將進一步惡化。

  ## 對中國AI產業的意義

  多重限制疊加之下，國內晶片與基礎設施的自主發展，正從應急備案轉變為中國AI企業的核心優先要務。一位因無對外發言授權而要求匿名的中國政府AI政策顧問表示，此次管制措施為國內能力的成長創造了一個窗口期。他說：「與其哀嘆美國管制將如何束縛我們，我們更願意將其視為給予我們自我壯大的窗口期。」

  進步研究所原先預估，可供中國企業使用的海外資料中心算力，將在2027年底達到相當於280萬張H100晶片的規模；在新規施行後，取得這些算力需要美國政府的許可授權，而此類授權並非必然獲批。
