body_en: |
  Nvidia has begun soliciting orders from Chinese clients for its Vera central processing unit (CPU), informing prospective buyers the chip could ship as soon as August, three sources familiar with the discussions told Reuters.

  The approach signals a significant shift in strategy for the world's most valuable company, which has watched its advanced-chip sales in China effectively collapse over the past year. Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang said in October that the company's market share in China had "effectively fallen to zero," driven by US export controls on advanced graphics processing units (GPUs) and Beijing's push to develop domestic semiconductor alternatives.

  ## Stalled GPU sales drive the pivot

  Nvidia's H200, its second-most powerful AI GPU, has not made a single delivery to China despite Washington licensing approximately 10 Chinese companies to purchase the chip. Chinese officials have withheld the domestic approvals needed to complete those transactions, as Beijing seeks to support homegrown chip suppliers.

  With GPU sales blocked, Nvidia has moved to pitch Vera — its first stand-alone CPU designed for agentic AI, meaning systems that perform tasks autonomously without continuous human direction. Unlike GPUs, CPUs are not subject to the same level of US export restrictions, making the Vera sales push less legally complex for Nvidia.

  ## Vera specifications and pricing

  Vera is now in full production, Nvidia said. The company claims the chip runs up to 1.8 times faster than comparable processors from rivals. It is built on Arm architecture, placing Nvidia in direct competition with Intel and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), which have long dominated the server CPU market using x86 architecture.

  A single Vera processor will cost "well north" of $20,000 before bulk discounts, according to semiconductor research firm SemiAnalysis. A fully configured rack housing 256 Vera chips would run approximately $10 million, depending on memory configuration, SemiAnalysis said. Most chips are initially going into large, ready-to-install racks preferred by hyperscale cloud operators; simpler two-processor servers are expected to ramp up later, SemiAnalysis added.

  Nvidia unveiled Vera in March, with Huang saying he expected the chip to become the company's next multibillion-dollar product line. The company said at the time that cloud operators including Alibaba and ByteDance were collaborating with it to deploy Vera, though Nvidia did not indicate then whether ordering had begun. Alibaba and ByteDance did not respond to requests for comment; Nvidia declined to comment on the Reuters report.

  ## Chinese client interest and constraints

  At least one major Chinese cloud company plans to place an initial order for more than 300 servers, each containing two Vera CPUs, one source said. The company intends to deploy those systems for testing before committing to larger official orders, the source added.

  Chinese clients plan to deploy Vera initially only in their overseas data centres, one source said — an approach that would sidestep domestic regulatory approval requirements.

  Whether early interest converts to large-scale adoption remains uncertain. One source cited concerns around software ecosystem compatibility and the difficulty of migrating workloads built around domestic AI chips to a new architecture.

  ## CPU shortage and the shift to inference

  The Vera push comes as the global AI industry transitions from model training — the computationally intensive phase of building AI systems — toward inference computing, the process of running trained models to answer queries. GPUs face greater competition from CPUs and custom chips in inference workloads, a shift that has tightened the global CPU market.

  Intel has notified Chinese customers of server CPU delivery lead times of up to six months, Reuters reported in February. AMD flagged last month that the global CPU market is "tight," with demand outpacing forecasts and supply constraints expected to persist.

  ## Competitive and financial stakes

  Nvidia's entry into the CPU market puts it in direct competition with Intel and AMD, which are racing to scale server CPU supply for AI data centres. Nvidia expects to generate $20 billion in revenue from Vera chip sales by the end of its current fiscal year, which closes at the end of January.

  The company has told Chinese clients that orders can begin now, with availability expected as soon as August.
body_zh: |
  輝達（Nvidia）已開始向中國客戶洽詢其Vera中央處理器（CPU）的訂單，並告知潛在買家最快今年八月即可出貨。三名熟悉相關討論的消息人士向路透社表示，此舉源於GPU交付幾近停擺，輝達在中國市場被迫展開策略調整。

  這一動向標誌著全球市值最高企業的重大策略轉變。過去一年，輝達在中國的先進晶片銷售已幾近崩潰。輝達執行長黃仁勳去年十月表示，受美國出口管制限制先進繪圖處理器（GPU）銷售、以及北京積極扶植本土半導體替代方案等因素影響，輝達在中國的市佔率「實際上已降至零」。

  ## GPU銷售停滯促成策略轉向

  輝達次強的AI GPU——H200，儘管美國政府已核發許可證、准許約十家中國企業購買，至今仍未完成任何一筆對中國的交付。中國官方遲遲未給予完成交易所需的國內審批，原因在於北京有意扶植本土晶片供應商。

  GPU銷售受阻之際，輝達轉而主推Vera——這是該公司首款專為代理式AI（agentic AI）設計的獨立CPU，即能在無需人類持續指導下自主執行任務的系統。與GPU不同，CPU所受的美國出口管制限制相對較少，使Vera的銷售推進在法規層面對輝達而言較不複雜。

  ## Vera規格與定價

  輝達表示，Vera目前已進入量產階段。輝達宣稱該晶片的運算速度比競爭對手的同級處理器快達一點八倍。Vera採用Arm架構，使輝達直接挑戰長期主導伺服器CPU市場的英特爾（Intel）與超微（AMD）——後兩者長久以來以x86架構稱霸市場。

  據半導體研究機構SemiAnalysis指出，單顆Vera處理器在享有批量折扣前，售價將「遠超過」兩萬美元。配備256顆Vera晶片的整機架，依記憶體配置不同，售價約為一千萬美元。SemiAnalysis表示，大多數晶片初期將進入超大規模雲端業者青睞的大型整機架；較簡單的雙處理器伺服器預計稍後才會放量。

  輝達於今年三月發布Vera，黃仁勳表示預期該晶片將成為公司下一條數十億美元級的產品線。輝達當時表示，阿里巴巴和字節跳動等雲端業者正與其合作部署Vera，但未說明訂單是否已開始接受。阿里巴巴與字節跳動未回應置評請求；輝達則拒絕就路透社的報導發表評論。

  ## 中國客戶興趣與限制

  一名消息人士表示，至少一家中國大型雲端企業計畫先下單逾三百台伺服器，每台配備兩顆Vera CPU。該公司打算先將這批系統用於測試，再決定是否擴大正式訂購規模。

  另一名消息人士指出，中國客戶初期計畫僅將Vera部署於境外資料中心——此舉可繞過中國國內的監管審批要求。

  然而，初期興趣能否轉化為大規模採用，目前仍存在不確定性。一名消息人士提及，外界對軟體生態系相容性的疑慮，以及將原先圍繞國產AI晶片建構的工作負載遷移至新架構的難度，都是潛在的障礙。

  ## CPU短缺與推論運算的轉型

  Vera的銷售攻勢，恰逢全球AI產業正從模型訓練——建構AI系統中運算量最密集的階段——轉向推論運算，即執行已訓練模型來回應查詢的過程。在推論工作負載方面，GPU面臨來自CPU及客製化晶片愈來愈激烈的競爭，這一趨勢已使全球CPU市場供需趨緊。

  路透社今年二月報導，英特爾已通知中國客戶，伺服器CPU的交貨前置時間最長達六個月。超微上個月也示警，全球CPU市場「供不應求」，需求持續超出預期，供應瓶頸料將延續。

  ## 競爭格局與財務目標

  輝達進軍CPU市場，使其直接與英特爾和超微正面交鋒——後兩者正競相擴大AI資料中心伺服器CPU的供應規模。輝達預計，在截至明年一月底的本財年結束前，Vera晶片銷售將創造兩百億美元的營收。

  輝達已告知中國客戶，訂單現已開放接受，最快八月即可出貨。
