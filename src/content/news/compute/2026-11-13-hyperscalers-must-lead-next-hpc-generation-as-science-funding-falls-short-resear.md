  ## Hyperscalers Must Lead Next HPC Generation as Science Funding Falls Short, Researcher Says

  The scientific community can no longer serve as the primary financial driver of high-performance computing (HPC), and cloud hyperscalers — large-scale public cloud providers such as Google, Amazon Web Services, Microsoft, and Oracle — must fill that role, according to a keynote address at the ISC High Performance conference in Hamburg, Germany.

  Daniel Reed, Presidential Professor in Computational Science at the University of Utah and chair of the U.S. National Science Board, made the case in a paper co-authored with Jack Dongarra of Oak Ridge National Laboratory and computer scientist Dennis Gannon. The paper, titled "Reinventing High Performance Computing: Challenges and Opportunities," outlines six principles the authors say must guide HPC's next phase.

  "The leading edge HPC market is too small, the procurements are too infrequent, the funding is too small, and the financial risk to vendors is too high, while the size and scale of the hyperscaler and deep learning markets are too large to ignore," Reed said.

  ## Why the traditional funding model is breaking down

  Historically, national governments and research institutions have been the primary buyers of supercomputing capacity, ordering large bespoke systems on multi-year procurement cycles. Reed's argument is that this model no longer generates enough sustained investment to push hardware vendors toward the next tier of performance.

  By contrast, the hyperscalers spend heavily on computing infrastructure on an annual basis — dwarfing the periodic government budgets allocated for supercomputer builds, according to the paper. That spending scale, Reed argues, positions the cloud operators as the only entities with the financial leverage to drive next-generation hardware development.

  HPC on demand is already an established part of commercial practice, Reed noted, and AI and machine learning (ML) workloads delivered on demand are rapidly joining it. Second-tier cloud providers and specialty colocation operators are also entering the AI/ML market, offering not just on-demand compute but physical infrastructure capable of handling the power and cooling demands of building and training large language models (LLMs) — the deep-learning systems that underpin conversational AI tools — and running inference engines, the systems that deliver AI outputs to end users.

  ## The six maxims

  Reed, Dongarra, and Gannon propose six principles to guide HPC's development:

  **Semiconductor constraints require new approaches.** Conventional central processing units (CPUs) and graphics processing units (GPUs) are approaching the limits of what scaling can achieve. The authors argue that chiplet designs — modular chip components that can be assembled into larger processors — combined with standardized HPC architectures offer a path forward.

  **End-to-end hardware and software co-design is essential.** Future systems should be designed around specific applications, including custom processors built to run particular software workloads. The authors call for significant government research investment alongside hardware vendors and hyperscalers, citing the U.S. Department of Energy's recent investment in new cooling technologies as one example of this model.

  **Prototyping at scale is required.** New silicon and software approaches must be built and tested at real-world scale before they can be validated. That demands willingness to absorb risk, which Reed says governments and major cloud players must accept.

  **The HPC application space has expanded significantly.** The range of problems HPC is applied to has grown. The authors point to the high-profile use of HPC in designing COVID-19 vaccines as a moment that raised commercial awareness of the technology's potential and accelerated its adoption in everyday business contexts.

  **Cloud economics have reshaped the supply chain.** Annual hyperscaler hardware spending far exceeds the sums governments periodically allocate for supercomputer procurement. The authors argue that future HPC hardware development will require collaboration among governments, businesses, cloud operators, and smartphone chip developers.

  **Societal implications require ongoing oversight.** HPC already touches daily life through applications such as weather forecasting and pharmaceutical development; AI is expected to deepen that reach. The authors call for continuous ethical oversight of the technology's impact.

  ## What the shift means for the industry

  The paper's framing marks a significant reorientation of where HPC's center of gravity is expected to sit. If the hyperscalers become the principal funders and developers of leading-edge compute infrastructure, the design priorities of that infrastructure — optimized for commercial AI and ML workloads — will shape what is available to scientific users, rather than the reverse.

  Reed's position is that commoditization drove the computing industry to its current state, but the next generation of HPC is likely to be purpose-built for specific problem classes rather than general-purpose. The paper calls for new technologies, new funding perspectives, and a clearer understanding of how future development will be financed.

  Reed and his co-authors argue that governments and businesses will need to collaborate directly with cloud operators and chip developers to shape the hardware roadmap for the next era of high-performance computing.

  ## 研究員示警：科研資金不足，超大規模業者須接棒引領下一代HPC

  在德國漢堡舉行的ISC高效能運算大會上，猶他大學教授Daniel Reed發表主題演講，主張科學界已無法繼續扮演高效能運算（HPC）的主要資金推手，必須由Google、亞馬遜雲端服務（AWS）、微軟、甲骨文等超大規模公有雲業者來填補這個角色。

  身兼美國國家科學委員會主席的猶他大學計算科學總統級講座教授Daniel Reed，與橡樹嶺國家實驗室的Jack Dongarra及電腦科學家Dennis Gannon共同撰寫了這篇論文，題為〈重塑高效能運算：挑戰與機遇〉，提出六大原則，作者認為這些原則必須引導HPC進入下一個發展階段。

  Reed表示：「高端HPC市場規模太小、採購頻率太低、資金太少、供應商面臨的財務風險太高；反觀超大規模業者及深度學習市場的規模之龐大，實在不容忽視。」

  ## 傳統資金模式為何走到瓶頸

  長久以來，各國政府及研究機構一直是超級電腦運算能力的主要購買者，以多年一度的採購週期訂製大型客製化系統。Reed的論點是，這套模式已不再能產生足夠且持續的投資，無法推動硬體供應商邁向下一個效能層次。

  相較之下，超大規模業者每年在運算基礎設施上的投入極為可觀，遠超政府定期撥付的超級電腦建置預算。Reed在論文中主張，正是這種資金規模，讓雲端業者成為唯一具備財務槓桿、足以推動下一代硬體發展的角色。

  Reed指出，隨選HPC早已是商業實務中行之有年的一環，隨選交付的人工智慧（AI）與機器學習（ML）工作負載也正快速跟進。次級雲端供應商與專業主機代管業者亦陸續進軍AI／ML市場，提供的不僅是隨需運算，更包括能夠承載大型語言模型（LLM）建置與訓練所需的供電及冷卻基礎設施——LLM正是支撐對話式AI工具的深度學習系統——以及負責將AI運算結果交付給終端使用者的推論引擎。

  ## 六大原則

  Reed、Dongarra與Gannon提出六大原則，作為引導HPC發展的方向：

  **半導體限制需要新思維。** 傳統中央處理器（CPU）與繪圖處理器（GPU）的效能提升空間已逐漸觸頂。作者主張，結合小晶片設計——也就是可組裝成更大處理器的模組化晶片元件——以及標準化HPC架構，將是突破之道。

  **端對端軟硬體協同設計不可或缺。** 未來系統應圍繞特定應用場景設計，包括為特定軟體工作負載量身打造的客製化處理器。作者呼籲政府在硬體廠商與超大規模業者之外，挹注更多研究資金，並以美國能源部近期對新型冷卻技術的投資為例，說明此一合作模式的具體樣貌。

  **大規模原型驗證不可省略。** 新型矽晶片與軟體方案必須在接近實際規模的環境中建置並測試，才能完成驗證。這需要各方願意承擔風險，Reed認為政府與主要雲端業者都必須接受這一現實。

  **HPC應用空間已大幅擴展。** HPC所解決的問題類型已大為增加。作者特別提及HPC在新冠肺炎疫苗研發中備受矚目的應用，認為這一里程碑提升了商業界對該技術潛力的認知，並加速了其在日常商業情境中的普及。

  **雲端經濟學重塑供應鏈。** 超大規模業者的年度硬體支出，遠超政府定期撥付的超級電腦採購款項。作者主張，未來HPC硬體的發展需要政府、企業、雲端業者與智慧型手機晶片開發商攜手合作。

  **社會影響需要持續監督。** HPC早已透過天氣預報、藥物研發等應用深入日常生活；AI的發展預料將進一步加深這種影響。作者呼籲對該技術的社會衝擊進行持續的倫理審視。

  ## 這波轉變對產業意味著什麼

  這篇論文的論述框架，標誌著HPC重心所在的一次重大重新定位。若超大規模業者成為頂尖運算基礎設施的主要資金方與開發者，那麼這套基礎設施的設計優先順序——以商業AI與ML工作負載為優化目標——將反過來決定科學使用者所能取用的資源，而非由科學需求主導設計方向。

  Reed的立場是，商品化推動了運算產業走到今日的格局，但下一代HPC很可能是針對特定問題類型量身打造，而非通用設計。論文呼籲引入新技術、開拓新的資金視野，並更清晰地釐清未來發展的財務支撐模式。

  Reed及其共同作者主張，政府與企業必須直接與雲端業者及晶片開發商攜手合作，共同形塑下一個高效能運算時代的硬體發展藍圖。
