body_en: |
  Space Forge reported on December 31, 2025, that its ForgeStar-1 satellite had successfully demonstrated autonomous plasma generation in orbit using a microwave plasma-enhanced chemical vapour deposition (CVD) system — the most significant milestone yet disclosed by the British company in its program to manufacture high-purity semiconductor crystals in low Earth orbit.

  The feat required simultaneous fine control of microwave power, gas flow, chamber pressure, timing, and exhaust aboard an automated satellite system. ForgeStar-1 had launched on June 23, 2025. Space Forge, which raised £22.6 million ($30.5 million) in a Series A round described as the largest in British space technology history, said the demonstration validates the core manufacturing process it is developing commercially. The round was led by the NATO Innovation Fund and included the World Fund and the British Business Bank.

  ## What space-grown crystals offer chip makers

  The scientific case for orbital semiconductor manufacturing rests on the behavior of materials in microgravity. On Earth, gravity-driven convection currents and buoyancy introduce irregularities as crystals form. Absent those forces in orbit, crystals grow with greater uniformity and lower dislocation density — a measure of how precisely atoms fit together in a crystal lattice, and a key determinant of semiconductor performance.

  Space Forge lead CVD engineer Dr. Andrew Griffiths said the company's process can yield "a three, four, or even a five-time order of magnitude improvement in the purity of the crystal, specifically the dislocation density." CEO Joshua Western told the UK In-Orbit Servicing, Assembly, and Manufacturing (ISAM) conference in Belfast in 2025 that the gain equates to "roughly one hundred million chips per flight," with end-system benefits including "up to a 60 percent reduction in size, weight, and power constraints."

  Space-grown crystals are not designed to replace terrestrial production outright. When returned to Earth, a single orbital batch seeds approximately ten subsequent generations of higher-quality crystals grown in conventional ground-based facilities, multiplying the practical benefit from a small initial quantity.

  ## Target: compound semiconductors, not silicon

  Space Forge is not targeting silicon CMOS — the mainstream technology underpinning data center processors — which Dr. Griffiths described as "extremely mature" and governed by process scaling rather than material purity. The company's stated focus is compound and wide-bandgap semiconductors, a category that includes materials used in high-power electronics, where material purity places "hard limits on performance," Dr. Griffiths said. He also identified diamond as a material of particular interest, noting its "unmatched thermal properties" and the potential for "even small volumes of ultra-high-quality material" to have significant impact.

  ## Science dating to Skylab

  The scientific foundation for in-space crystal growth spans more than five decades. On January 24, 1974, during NASA's Skylab IV mission — part of a program that conducted nearly 300 discrete experiments between 1973 and 1979 — silver crystals were grown at one-gram and five-gram sizes in orbit through electrochemical reaction. Observations showed those crystals exhibited a more perfect microcrystalline structure than equivalents grown on Earth or in centrifuges, with researchers noting a greater uniformity of size and structure attributable to the absence of convective currents and buoyancy effects. A 1976 report documented potential "uses for massive single crystals of semiconductors and optical materials."

  Dr. Griffiths said more than a hundred documented experiments on in-space crystal growth have been conducted since the 1970s and 1980s by U.S., Russian, European, and Chinese programs.

  ## The downmass constraint

  The primary barrier to commercial orbital manufacturing is not the science of crystal growth but the logistics of returning material to Earth. Dave Barnhart, CEO and co-founder of in-space services company Arkisys Inc., described downmass — the capacity to bring cargo from orbit to the ground safely — as "the major limiting factor," noting that current capability is "limited to kilograms, with unresolved challenges around safe re-entry, landing, and cost." High-volume orbital manufacturing will remain constrained, he said, "until downmass capacity increases by an order of magnitude."

  Space Forge addresses re-entry with a proprietary heat shield called Pridwen — named after the shield of King Arthur in Arthurian legend — that radiates accumulated heat rather than ablating (burning away material on atmospheric contact), as conventional shields do. The system has undergone microgravity testing on a parabolic flight. Sierra Space's Dream Chaser spaceplane has been cited as a potential future contributor to downmass capacity, though that vehicle has not yet flown.

  For semiconductor crystal applications, the current downmass constraint is less acute than it would be for bulk manufacturing: the business model depends on small quantities of high-value material, with crystal batches returned in heat-shielded capsules, some using cost-effective inflatable designs.

  ## Broader orbital manufacturing landscape

  Barnhart characterized in-space manufacturing as "still in its nascent experimental phase" and framed the central commercial question as identifying industries seeking "access to the extreme physics of space." He said the most realistic near-term beneficiaries are biological and pharmaceutical companies, where microgravity enables novel crystal growth, cell culture formation, and protein structuring that cannot be replicated on Earth.

  Capital in that adjacent sector has been substantial: Varda Space Industries, a space-based pharmaceutical manufacturer with backing from billionaire investor Peter Thiel, has raised $329 million in total funding. Orbital Medicine has raised $270 million.

  Space Forge's December 2025 plasma generation demonstration is the most recently disclosed operational milestone for ForgeStar-1; no commercial crystal production timeline has been provided in available reporting.
body_zh: |
  Space Forge 於 2025 年 12 月 31 日宣布，其 ForgeStar-1 衛星已成功在軌道上利用微波電漿增強化學氣相沉積（CVD）系統，自主完成電漿生成——這是這家英國公司迄今公開披露的，在低地球軌道製造高純度半導體晶體計畫中最重要的里程碑。

  這項成就需要在全自動衛星系統上同時精密控制微波功率、氣體流量、腔室壓力、時序與排氣。ForgeStar-1 於 2025 年 6 月 23 日發射升空。Space Forge 在 A 輪融資中募得 2,260 萬英鎊（約 3,050 萬美元），被稱為英國太空科技史上規模最大的 A 輪，公司表示此次示範驗證了其正在商業化開發的核心製造流程。本輪融資由北大西洋公約組織創新基金（NATO Innovation Fund）領投，世界基金（World Fund）及英國商業銀行（British Business Bank）亦參與投資。

  ## 太空生長晶體為晶片製造商帶來的優勢

  在軌道製造半導體的科學依據，源自材料在微重力環境下的行為特性。在地球上，重力驅動的對流與浮力效應會在晶體形成過程中引入不規則性。在軌道上去除這些力的干擾後，晶體生長的均勻性更高，位錯密度（dislocation density）也更低——位錯密度衡量的是原子在晶格中排列的精確程度，是決定半導體性能的關鍵指標。

  Space Forge CVD 首席工程師安德魯·格里菲斯（Andrew Griffiths）博士表示，該公司的製程可在晶體純度上實現「三到五個數量級的提升，尤其是位錯密度方面」。執行長喬書亞·韋斯頓（Joshua Western）在 2025 年於貝爾法斯特舉行的英國在軌服務、組裝與製造（ISAM）研討會上表示，這項提升相當於「每次飛行約一億顆晶片的產能效益」，最終系統效益包括「尺寸、重量及功耗限制最高可降低 60%」。

  太空生長晶體並非旨在全面取代地面生產。每批從軌道帶回地球的晶體，可作為種晶，在傳統地面設施中再衍生約十個世代的更高品質晶體，從而以少量初始材料成倍放大實際效益。

  ## 目標：化合物半導體，而非矽晶片

  Space Forge 的目標並非矽 CMOS——這是支撐資料中心處理器的主流技術。格里菲斯博士形容矽 CMOS 技術「極為成熟」，其發展受製程微縮而非材料純度所主導。該公司明確聚焦於化合物半導體與寬能隙半導體，這類材料廣泛應用於高功率電子產品，其中材料純度對性能設下「硬性限制」，格里菲斯博士說。他也特別點名鑽石為重點研究材料，指出其「無與倫比的導熱特性」，以及「即便是小量的超高品質材料，也能帶來顯著影響」的潛力。

  ## 可追溯至天空實驗室的科學淵源

  太空晶體生長的科學基礎已有逾五十年歷史。1974 年 1 月 24 日，在 NASA 天空實驗室 IV 號（Skylab IV）任務期間——該計畫於 1973 年至 1979 年間共進行近 300 項個別實驗——銀晶體在軌道上透過電化學反應，分別以一克與五克的規模生長成形。觀察結果顯示，這些晶體比在地球或離心機中生長的同類晶體具有更完美的微晶結構，研究人員指出其尺寸與結構的均勻性更高，歸因於對流及浮力效應的缺失。1976 年的一份報告記載了「大型塊狀半導體及光學材料單晶的潛在用途」。

  格里菲斯博士表示，自 1970 至 1980 年代起，美國、俄羅斯、歐洲及中國的太空計畫已記錄了逾一百項太空晶體生長實驗。

  ## 下行載荷的瓶頸

  商業軌道製造的主要障礙，並非晶體生長的科學，而是將材料帶回地球的後勤問題。在軌服務公司 Arkisys Inc. 的執行長兼共同創辦人戴夫·巴納特（Dave Barnhart）將下行載荷（downmass）——即將貨物從軌道安全返回地面的能力——形容為「主要的限制因素」，並指出目前的能力「僅限於公斤級，且在安全再入大氣層、著陸及成本方面仍有未解難題」。他表示，在下行載荷能力提升一個數量級之前，大規模軌道製造將持續受到限制。

  Space Forge 以一款名為「普里德文」（Pridwen）的專有隔熱盾應對大氣再入問題——這個名稱取自亞瑟王傳說中亞瑟王的盾牌。與傳統隔熱盾採用燒蝕（ablating，即在接觸大氣時燃燒消耗材料）的方式不同，普里德文以輻射方式散發累積的熱能。這套系統已在拋物線飛行中進行過微重力測試。賽艾羅太空（Sierra Space）的「追夢者」（Dream Chaser）太空飛機曾被引述為未來可能的下行載荷補充選項，但該飛行器目前尚未飛行。

  就半導體晶體應用而言，當前的下行載荷限制比大規模製造所面臨的問題更為可控：其商業模式依賴少量高價值材料，晶體批次以隔熱艙返回，部分採用成本效益較高的充氣式設計。

  ## 更廣泛的軌道製造版圖

  巴納特將太空製造定性為「仍處於萌芽實驗階段」，並將核心商業問題界定為找出哪些產業正在尋求「進入太空極端物理環境的機會」。他表示，近期最切實可行的受益者是生物與製藥公司，因為微重力環境能實現地球上無法複製的新型晶體生長、細胞培養形成及蛋白質結構化。

  鄰近領域的資本投入相當可觀：由億萬富翁投資人彼得·泰爾（Peter Thiel）支持的太空製藥公司 Varda Space Industries 已累計募資 3.29 億美元；Orbital Medicine 則已募資 2.70 億美元。
