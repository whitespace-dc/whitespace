body_en: |
  The U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) has awarded $20 million to five new research teams as part of its National Quantum Virtual Laboratory (NQVL), a federally coordinated program aimed at building shared infrastructure for quantum sensing, networking, and computing. Each team will receive $4 million over two years to refine development plans and prepare for an implementation phase.

  The five newly selected teams join four chosen in a prior round, bringing the NQVL design cohort to nine projects in total. Participating researchers span 20 U.S. states. Federal partners include NASA, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and multiple Department of Energy national laboratories. Industry participants named in the program include Nvidia, Honeywell, IonQ, and Quantinuum.

  ## What the NQVL is and how it works

  The NQVL, which NSF launched to create a shared, federated environment for quantum development, is organized around a distributed model rather than a single centralized facility. Mike England of NSF's Office of Legislative and Public Affairs told Data Center Knowledge that no single institution can complete this work alone. "The National Quantum Virtual Laboratory draws upon the national pool of talent to build a federated suite of testing platforms that will in turn enable prototypes for future quantum sensing, networking and computing capabilities," England said. "No individual lab or university can accomplish this work on its own."

  Participating institutions are expected to share resources and coordinate as a single unit while translating fundamental research into functional quantum devices and systems, according to NSF.

  ## The five newly funded projects

  Two of the five new projects have the most direct downstream relevance to data center infrastructure.

  ASPEN-Net, led by the University of Oregon across testbeds in three states, is developing a scalable quantum networking platform designed to route data at high speeds over long distances entirely via light. The project uses phase-stable optical memories to eliminate the latency and heat generated by conventional optical-to-electronic signal conversion, a bottleneck in today's data center interconnects.

  The Accelerating Fault-Tolerant Quantum Logic (FTL) project, led by UCLA, addresses the resource overhead required to make quantum processors reliable. Current experimental quantum systems require a large number of physical qubits — the basic unit of quantum information — to suppress processing errors. The FTL team is designing what it describes as a "60 logical-qubit quantum computer" that optimizes hardware layouts and reduces error rates, with the goal of moving quantum hardware toward standard enterprise server environments.

  The remaining three projects in this funding round focus on hardware components:

  - **Erasure Qubits and Dynamic Circuits for Quantum Advantage:** Develops error detection and correction techniques using superconducting hardware to stabilize quantum processing.
  - **Distributed-Entanglement Quantum Sensing of Chemical Properties:** Builds ultra-precise quantum sensors, including protein-based qubits designed to operate inside solid materials and living cells.
  - **Quantum Photonic Integration and Deployment:** Produces portable, chip-scale quantum sensors engineered to operate outside of controlled laboratory conditions.

  ## NSF's rationale for an integrated approach

  England said the projects should not be viewed as competing priorities. "Each of these areas is critical to deliver on the promise of quantum technologies and accelerate progress towards the quantum future," he said. NSF intends to involve researchers, practitioners, and industry users throughout development to help ensure the technologies address real-world problems and reduce barriers to future commercial applications.

  The program also aligns with a White House Executive Order on quantum innovation, which calls for strengthening U.S. leadership in quantum technologies by accelerating research, expanding public-private collaboration, and moving discoveries toward practical deployment.

  ## What these investments could mean for data center operators

  NSF and the researchers involved frame the long-term potential of NQVL in terms relevant to data center infrastructure, though the program's own documentation acknowledges that commercial deployment remains years away. Quantum networking, the researchers note, could enable faster and more efficient data transfer. Advances in fault-tolerant quantum computing could eventually move specialized quantum processors into enterprise compute environments alongside conventional hardware. Quantum sensors offer potential gains in energy efficiency and real-time processing.

  Research from AWS cited by Data Center Knowledge suggests that high-performance data center infrastructure could itself accelerate quantum computing by enabling large-scale hardware-calibrated simulations. JPMorgan, OQC, and AMD are separately reported to be planning quantum AI data centers for financial applications.

  The nine NQVL teams are currently in the design phase, preparing implementation plans before any broader deployment. NSF has not announced a timeline for when the implementation phase would begin or what additional funding it might carry.

  ## NSF撥款2000萬美元　五支新團隊加入國家量子虛擬實驗室

  美國國家科學基金會（NSF）已向五支新研究團隊各撥款400萬美元，總計2000萬美元，資助期限為兩年。這些團隊將在此期間完善開發計畫，並為後續實作階段做好準備。這項資助隸屬於「國家量子虛擬實驗室」（National Quantum Virtual Laboratory，NQVL）計畫——一個由聯邦政府統籌協調、旨在為量子感測、量子網路與量子運算建立共享基礎設施的計畫。

  五支新入選團隊加入先前一輪已選定的四支團隊，使NQVL設計階段的計畫總數達到九個。參與研究人員橫跨美國20個州。聯邦合作夥伴包括美國太空總署（NASA）、美國國家標準暨技術研究院（NIST），以及能源部旗下多個國家實驗室。計畫中點名的業界參與者則有輝達（Nvidia）、霍尼韋爾（Honeywell）、IonQ與Quantinuum。

  ## NQVL是什麼，又如何運作

  NQVL由NSF創立，旨在為量子技術開發建立一個共享的聯合環境。此計畫採分散式架構，而非單一集中設施。NSF立法暨公共事務辦公室的麥克‧英格蘭（Mike England）向《資料中心知識》（Data Center Knowledge）表示，沒有任何單一機構能夠獨力完成這項工作。「國家量子虛擬實驗室匯聚全國人才，建立一套聯合測試平台，進而為未來的量子感測、量子網路與量子運算能力催生原型，」英格蘭說，「沒有任何一個實驗室或大學能獨自完成這項工作。」

  依據NSF的規劃，參與機構須共享資源、以整體協同方式運作，同時將基礎研究轉化為可實際運用的量子元件與系統。

  ## 五個新獲資助的計畫

  五個新計畫中，有兩個與資料中心基礎設施最具直接的下游關聯性。

  由奧勒岡大學（University of Oregon）主導、橫跨三州測試平台的「ASPEN-Net」計畫，正在開發一套可擴展的量子網路平台，設計目標是讓資料能以高速、純光訊號的方式進行長距離傳輸。此計畫採用相位穩定光學記憶體，以消除傳統光電訊號轉換所帶來的延遲與熱能，而這正是當前資料中心互連的一大瓶頸。

  由加州大學洛杉磯分校（UCLA）主導的「加速容錯量子邏輯」（Accelerating Fault-Tolerant Quantum Logic，FTL）計畫，則著眼於提升量子處理器可靠性所需付出的資源成本。現有的實驗性量子系統必須使用大量物理量子位元（qubit，量子資訊的基本單位）來抑制運算錯誤。FTL團隊正在設計他們所稱的「60邏輯量子位元量子電腦」，藉由優化硬體佈局、降低錯誤率，最終目標是讓量子硬體得以進入標準企業伺服器環境部署。

  本輪資助的另外三個計畫聚焦於硬體元件：

  - **消除型量子位元與動態電路以實現量子優勢：** 利用超導硬體開發錯誤偵測與修正技術，以穩定量子運算過程。
  - **分散糾纏量子感測化學特性：** 建構超高精度量子感測器，包括設計用於在固態材料與活細胞內運作的蛋白質量子位元。
  - **量子光子整合與部署：** 製造可攜式晶片級量子感測器，工程目標是在受控實驗室以外的環境中正常運作。

  ## NSF採整合式方法的理由

  英格蘭表示，不應將這些計畫視為相互競爭的優先項目。「這些領域每一個都是兌現量子技術承諾、加速邁向量子未來不可或缺的關鍵，」他說。NSF計畫在整個開發過程中納入研究人員、從業者與產業使用者，以確保相關技術能切中實際問題，並降低未來商業應用的門檻。

  此計畫亦呼應白宮有關量子創新的行政命令——該命令要求藉由加速研究、擴大公私合作，並推動研究成果走向實際應用，來鞏固美國在量子技術領域的領導地位。

  ## 這些投資對資料中心營運者意味著什麼

  NSF及參與研究人員以與資料中心基礎設施相關的角度，勾勒出NQVL的長期潛力，但計畫本身的文件也承認，商業部署仍需數年時間。研究人員指出，量子網路有望實現更快速、更高效的資料傳輸；容錯量子運算的進展，最終可能讓專用量子處理器與傳統硬體並肩進入企業運算環境；量子感測器則在能源效率與即時處理方面帶來潛在效益。

  《資料中心知識》引述AWS的研究指出，高效能資料中心基礎設施本身也可能加速量子運算的發展，因為它能支援大規模的硬體校準模擬。此外，摩根大通（JPMorgan）、OQC與超微半導體（AMD）據報也正分別規劃面向金融應用的量子AI資料中心。

  目前，NQVL的九支團隊均處於設計階段，正在準備實作計畫，尚未進入更大規模的部署。NSF迄今未宣布實作階段的啟動時程，也未說明屆時將投入多少額外資金。
